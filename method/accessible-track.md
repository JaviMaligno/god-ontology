# Accessible Track

The accessible track expresses the project's beliefs in ordinary language, in the first person, for a listener with no metaphysical background: why the author believes in God, what kind of God, why there is such a thing as the good, why that good is objective, and how all of this touches human experience. It runs parallel to the hard ontology and is derived from it. Provenance: author brief of 2026-09-02, recorded in [S46](../sessions/2026-09-02-accessible-track-and-experiential-register.md).

## Two registers, one ontology

The project keeps two registers of exposition over one set of commitments.

| Register | Task | Where | Language |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| argumentative | build, test, and record the ontology | `ontology/`, `sessions/`, `writing/treatise-outline.md` | English, with bilingual notes |
| accessible | say the same things plainly and connect them with lived experience | `writing/accessible/` | Spanish first; English versions may follow |

The choice of Spanish for the accessible register follows the author's use: the questions it answers are the ones people ask the author in conversation. The register also serves the author's own understanding, self-recognition, and expression (C557): an answer must satisfy the author as a reader of their own position, which is why every answer is shown to the author and revised on review. The treatise remains an English-first work.

## Rules

1. **Downstream fidelity.** An accessible answer may not claim more than the ledger supports. Where the ledger says `preferred`, the answer says “I think the best account is…”; where it says `provisional` or `open`, the answer says “I don't know yet” or “this is where I have least to offer.” Status is expressed in plain words, never hidden by a confident tone.
2. **Provenance.** Every answer carries, in a closing table or note, the ledger items and sessions it rests on. When one of those items changes status, the answer is revised.
3. **First person and honesty.** The answers are the author's beliefs, not a neutral survey. They distinguish explicitly: what has been established within the project, what is preferred, what is believed beyond the result, and what is not known. They do not present a preference as a proof (S09).
4. **No proof claims.** The project does not expect to demonstrate its ultimate choices. The accessible answer to “Can you prove it?” is “No, and I don't think anyone can; here is why I find this the most reasonable and satisfying account.”
5. **Question-driven gap filling.** When a plain question cannot be answered faithfully because the ontology lacks the material, the shortfall is recorded in the [question bank](../writing/accessible/banco-de-preguntas.md) as `abierta` and becomes a research item. It enters the active queue under the priority discipline of the research method, where explicit authorial direction ranks first. The gap is filled in the argumentative register, under the author's criteria and along the author's line, and only then translated. S47 and S48 are the first two examples.
6. **Experience is a register, not a proof.** The experiential approach — art, music, prayer, religious experience — is taken seriously as the fourth evidential layer of the research method. The ontology says what it can and cannot support (C466–C471): experience can be veridical of standing presence or of exercised regard, is defeasible first-person evidence of presence or relation, and is never evidence for the specific architecture.
7. **The hemisphere image is a metaphor.** The picture of an analytic left hemisphere and an experiential right one is used only as a figure for the two registers and is flagged as such wherever it appears. It is not an empirical premise (R181; C183–C184).
8. **No repetition of the treatise.** The accessible answers are not a summary of the treatise and do not follow its order. They follow the order in which people ask.
9. **Author review loop.** Every batch of answers, new or revised, is shown to the author in the reply, not only written to the file. Each review is recorded as a session that lists the corrections, classifies each as language, argument, structure, or gap, and gives the ledger basis of the change (S62 is the first).
10. **The author's framing of the two registers.** The author frames both registers as communication: metaphysics, mathematics, and science communicate certain kinds of knowledge through logic; art, music, and religion communicate feelings and experiences; all of it is communication among subjects and with $G$ (C547). This is a framing consistent with asymmetric communication (C295–C296) and the complementarity of the registers (C469), not a premise of the ontology.

## Translation register

The following equivalences keep the plain language faithful. They are guides, not fixed phrases.

| Technical term | Plain rendering |
| --- | --- |
| necessary entity / necessary ground | something that could not have failed to exist, and on which everything else depends |
| contingent | something that exists but could have not existed, or could have been otherwise |
| brute fact | a fact with no explanation at all: “it just is” |
| weak explanatory preference (C457) | it is more reasonable to keep asking than to stop at “it just is,” though stopping is not absurd |
| necessitation lemma (C458) | if the ground could not fail to exist, and it produced the world automatically, then the world could not have been otherwise either |
| impersonal necessary structure | a set of rules or a mathematical structure from which everything follows without anyone choosing |
| necessary agent | someone, not something: a conscious being who chooses which world exists |
| generative structure $S$ | the rules of the game: laws, constants, starting conditions, what kinds of things there are |
| actuality-complete omniscience (ACO) | knows everything that actually happens, without having to wait to find out |
| agent-causal eternalism (E+) | the whole history is real at once from God's point of view, and still contains our real choices |
| asymmetric co-production | God sets up the game and keeps it in being; we make real moves that God does not make for us |
| intervention, miracle, providence | God acting directly in the world; a direct act that ordinary causes do not exhaust; the overall shape of the story |
| agency-preservation presumption | a standing reason not to override our freedom when the same end can be reached without doing so |
| ontological omnipresence | there is no place, thing, or moment that God is not holding in being and knowing |
| universal personal availability | anyone can be addressed by God and can address God; that is possible for everyone |
| anchors $P$, $S$, $R$ | what can suffer and enjoy; what can choose; what can know and be known truthfully |
| objective fit ($Fit_*$) | what actually suits a being given what it is, whether or not anyone believes it |
| due-good privation | evil is a good that ought to be there and is missing or broken; it is not a rival substance |
| good-oriented profile (C463) | everything established about God points toward the freedom of conscious beings and truthful relation with them |
| the P-gap (C464) | what has not been established: God's orientation toward what we feel, including our suffering |
| qualified omnipotence ($OMP_C$ preferred, $OMP_Q$ provisional) | God can do with the created order anything that makes sense to do, and nothing created can limit that; S73 provisionally extends all concrete reality into dependence on God, while genuinely new action kinds still keep the unrestricted claim open |

## Workflow

1. Take a question from the bank, or add one.
2. Locate the ledger items that bear on it. If they suffice, write the answer and record provenance. If they do not, mark the question `abierta` and propose the gap as a session under the research method.
3. After any session that changes a cited item, revise the affected answers.
4. Keep the plain answers, the question bank, and the ontology documents in separate files; do not paste formal notation into the accessible register.

## Files

- [Question bank](../writing/accessible/banco-de-preguntas.md): the questions the track aims to answer, their status, and their links to the ledger and the challenge register.
- [Answers in plain language](../writing/accessible/preguntas-en-lenguaje-comun.md): the first-person answers, Spanish first.
