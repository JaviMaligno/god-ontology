# Framework Challenge Register

This register accumulates high-pressure questions for a later hardening pass after the main architecture is sufficiently complete. A queued challenge is not an objection already answered or a reason to interrupt the current development order.

## Status vocabulary

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `queued` | Preserved for later analysis |
| `scoped` | Main variants and affected commitments identified |
| `tested` | Applied adversarially to the relevant modules |
| `resolved` | Answered without a current need to revise the framework |
| `reopens` | Requires revision of one or more commitments |

## CH01 — Divine self-copy

### Main variants

1. **Exact divine duplicate:** a numerically distinct $G'$ shares every essential and modal feature of $G$, including necessity, aseity or independence, ultimate grounding status, and qualified omnipotence.
2. **Created qualitative duplicate:** $G'$ shares intrinsic cognitive and power properties but is existentially dependent on $G$.
3. **Derivative near-copy:** $G'$ is an extraordinarily powerful, knowledgeable, non-spatial created agent but lacks ultimacy, aseity, or an unrestricted maximal action domain.
4. **Personal multiplication without being multiplication:** several divine persons belong to one divine being. This requires a separate person/being ontology and must not be smuggled in as an answer.

### Preliminary pressure

The exact version appears to combine:

\[
CreatedBy(G,G')
\Rightarrow
Depends(G',G)
\]

with:

\[
ExactDivineDuplicate(G',G)
\Rightarrow
Independent(G')\land UltimateGround(G').
\]

If dependence and aseity or ultimate independence are incompatible, “a created exact divine duplicate” is an incoherent task and therefore no counterexample to qualified omnipotence. This is only a preliminary diagnosis: the framework must later test which properties are genuinely essential to $G$, whether derivative necessity is possible, and whether two maximal agents could have coherent overlapping action domains.

The second and third variants remain more serious live possibilities. They may not threaten $U_G$ or $U_D$ because a dependent being is not a second ultimate ground, but they can expose whether current language about omnipotence, omnipresence, or “Godlike” agency is too coarse.

### Dependencies for the later test

- C74–C76: qualified omnipotence and coherent action descriptions;
- C78–C83: ground uniqueness, divine-agent uniqueness, and plural maximal agents;
- C98–C103: what creation and existential dependence mean;
- the future account of divine essence, personhood, and derivative necessity.

## CH02 — Explanatory coverage of physical features

The present authorial criterion is generative:

> A physical feature need not receive an independent creative reason if it is a consequence of features of $S$ that do receive adequate reasons.

The later challenge must distinguish:

1. strict consequences of $S$;
2. stochastic but structurally licensed outcomes;
3. agent-originated consequences;
4. intervention-dependent features;
5. brute residual parameters or events not explained by any of the above.

Generative derivation may provide metaphysical explanation without yet providing moral justification. The goodness branch must later ask whether foreseeable costs of the selected package require countervailing reasons even when they are not separately chosen.

## CH03 — Knowledge of another subject

S20 derives universal personal availability only at a minimal level: $G$ individuates each actual conscious subject, knows the actual truths about that subject, and can address or respond directly. The challenge asks whether this remains an external third-person profile even when propositionally exhaustive.

The later test must distinguish:

1. knowing every true proposition about $A$;
2. knowing that conscious states belong to $A$'s first-person perspective;
3. acquaintance with $A$ as a subject;
4. empathy or knowledge of what $A$'s experience is like;
5. phenomenal identity with, or literal sharing of, $A$'s experience;
6. actual reciprocal second-person engagement.

The framework should neither assume that these collapse nor infer that a distinction automatically limits omniscience. It must decide which relations have truthmakers, which count as kinds of knowledge, which are coherent for numerically distinct subjects, and which are exercises rather than standing capacities.

## CH04 — Normative fit and divine ultimity

S21 provisionally distinguishes an objective normative relation from a separately existing superior agent or substance. That distinction answers one formulation of the ultimity objection but does not yet ground the normative relation.

The later test must compare at least:

1. unrestricted divine choice as the good-maker;
2. divine nature as archetype or standard;
3. primitive necessary normative structure;
4. relational fit grounded in conscious natures, capacities, and relations;
5. a layered account assigning different roles to nature, reason, will, and created subjects.

Each option must explain why cruelty, deception, destruction, flourishing, agency, truth, and relationship receive their claimed valence; how value can guide divine choice in grounding order; and whether the account preserves both contingency and $G$'s existential ultimity.
