# Session: Internal Time and Atemporal Knowledge

**Date:** 2026-08-31
**Session ID:** S02
**Primary question:** How can a creature's temporally internal deliberation coexist with complete atemporal knowledge?
**Dependencies:** E+, ACO, agent causation, modal accessibility, truthmaking
**Starting ledger items:** C08, C10–C14, C22–C23

## Proposal developed in conversation

The word `open` should be disambiguated into epistemic, causal, modal, and alethic openness. E+ can combine:

1. limited creaturely knowledge from within time;
2. absence of sufficient determination by the prior event-history;
3. more than one metaphysically admissible continuation;
4. a single tenselessly true actual outcome known by God.

It therefore need not claim that the future proposition lacks a truth value. The internal/external distinction prevents an inference from tenseless truth to prior causal determination, but it does not itself prove freedom. Agent causation and the reality of modal alternatives must do that work.

## Initial formal scaffold

Let $\mathcal H(S)$ be the histories compatible with generative structure $S$, and let $H^*$ be the actual complete history. Define $h\equiv_{<t}h'$ as sameness of the relevant creaturely causal past before $t$. It deliberately excludes a requirement that atemporal divine knowledge have identical content in both histories.

\[
Alt(A,t,h)=\{h'\in\mathcal H(S):h'\equiv_{<t}h\text{ and }Act(A,t,h')\neq Act(A,t,h)\}.
\]

A candidate libertarian condition combines agent origination, non-empty alternatives, and the absence of a sufficient prior event-cause.

Actuality-complete knowledge is initially constrained by:

\[
K_G^{h}(p)\quad\text{iff}\quad h\vDash p.
\]

This does not yet explain the cognitive mechanism. It says that the necessary or invariant epistemic capacity can have contingent content: across admissible histories, God's knowledge covaries with what the creature actually does. Timelessness excludes learning or updating within a history; it does not entail that every known content is necessary across histories.

## Principal objection

The proposal may appear to secure freedom by defining alternative histories so that divine knowledge is allowed to vary. The next argument must therefore show why atemporal knowledge-content is downstream in grounding or explanation from the contingent truth, even though neither term is temporally earlier. It must also test whether such contingent relational content remains available if immutability, simplicity, or stronger necessity claims are later derived.

## Status

The conversational protocol is accepted. The fourfold openness framework and knowledge-content covariance remain provisional. The internal/external distinction as a clarification, and the separation between invariant epistemic capacity and contingent knowledge-content, are preferred.
