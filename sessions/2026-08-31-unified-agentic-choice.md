# Session: Unified agentic choice

**Date:** 2026-08-31
**Session ID:** S06
**Primary question:** Can a necessary agent and a temporal created agent share one coherent logic of libertarian choice?
**Dependencies:** agent causation, power-grounded modality, E+, asymmetric co-production
**Starting ledger items:** C05, C08, C33, C48, C50

## Starting position

The necessary agent and created agents are already assigned originating powers. A proposed demand for an antecedently sufficient contrastive reason threatened to turn every exercise of those powers into a determined output. The author rejects importing that demand: it would assume the determinism that the agent-causal model is meant to test.

The correction is to use one positive choice structure for both kinds of agent. Reasons guide and explain without necessarily entailing. Indeterminism supplies openness but not authorship; conscious agentive sourcehood supplies the positive settling relation.

## Definitions

| Term/symbol | Definition used in this session |
| --- | --- |
| $C_A^-$ | All action-relevant conditions held fixed for $A$, excluding the token settling and its consequences |
| $\mathcal O_A(C_A^-)$ | Options that $A$ can originate under those conditions |
| $Settles(A,a)$ | $A$ exercises its power so that option $a$ becomes actual |
| $ForReasons(A,a,R_A)$ | $A$ acts for a set of reasons $R_A$ without those reasons necessarily entailing $a$ |
| Sourcehood | The agent, rather than prior events or chance alone, is the originating source of the token act |

## Unified schema

\[
\mathcal O_A(C_A^-)=\{a:CanOriginate(A,a,C_A^-)\}.
\]

For distinct live options $a$ and $b$:

\[
|\mathcal O_A(C_A^-)|\geq 2,
\]

\[
C_A^-\not\models Settles(A,a),
\qquad
C_A^-\not\models Settles(A,b).
\]

The actual act satisfies:

\[
Originates(A,a)\land ForReasons(A,a,R_A).
\]

Reasons remain causally and explanatorily relevant:

\[
Supports(R_A,a)\not\models Settles(A,a).
\]

The act is therefore neither a deterministic output of $C_A^-$ nor a merely unowned random event. The agent's exercise of originating power is the terminal source of the token difference.

## Cosmic and temporal application

For $G$, the context contains its invariant nature, agentive powers, reasons, and structures it can actualize. Choice need not be a temporal process:

\[
\Box E(G)\not\models
\bigl(Settles(G,S_1)\rightarrow\Box Settles(G,S_1)\bigr).
\]

For a created agent, $C_A^-$ includes a temporal causal past, bodily and cognitive powers, accessible reasons, character, attention, emotion, habits, and circumstances. These can constrain the option set and alter salience or inclination without becoming a sufficient cause of the act.

The ontological differences are substantial: $G$ is necessary and atemporal in the model, while created agents are contingent, dependent, and temporally deliberative. The core relation of choice remains the same: an agent settles among originable alternatives for reasons.

## Adversarial tests

### Does identical antecedent information require an identical act?

No such principle has been established. Imposing it would define every agent as a deterministic transition system. In the intended libertarian sense, two histories may share $C_A^-$ and differ because the agent originates different admissible acts.

### Is the difference merely random?

No. Randomness supplies at most non-entailment or a distribution over outcomes. The model additionally requires a conscious bearer of the power, ownership of the act, responsiveness to reasons, and the originating relation. These are primitive commitments requiring later philosophy-of-mind analysis, not consequences of probability.

### Does necessary existence make every divine act necessary?

No transfer rule from the necessity of a bearer or power to the necessity of each exercise has been established. Treating the exercise as necessary would erase the very agentive power attributed to $G$ rather than derive a contradiction in contingent divine choice.

### Must every choice have a sufficient contrastive reason?

The model accepts intelligible explanation: $A$ settled $a$ for $R_A$. It rejects the stronger demand for an antecedent condition that already entails $a$ rather than $b$. That stronger demand is incompatible with originating alternatives and cannot be assumed as a neutral premise.
