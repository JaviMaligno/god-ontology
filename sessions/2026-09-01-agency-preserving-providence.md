# Session: Agency-preserving intervention and providence

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Session ID:** S19
**Primary question:** Does the agency-oriented reason for creation constrain divine intervention?
**Dependencies:** C18, C35–C46, C55–C66, C71–C76, C104–C112, S04, S18
**Target depth:** F1 reintegration of creative reason, intervention, and providence

## Starting position

If a central reason for creating is to realize finite autonomous sourcehood, then divine action after creation should normally remain coherent with that reason. This does not yield a prohibition on intervention and does not yet invoke goodness. It yields a **defeasible, reason-relative presumption** against unnecessarily replacing created sourcehood.

## Explanatory humility

The S18 reason profile makes the preferred ontology intelligible but does not claim access to $G$'s ultimate actual motive. The project distinguishes:

\[
PlausibleReason(G,S)
\]

from:

\[
KnownUltimateReason(G,S).
\]

Only the first is claimed. The same humility applies to particular interventions: the model can identify which effects fit or burden the preferred rationale without pretending to know every divine purpose.

## What lower control means

“Lower control” concerns authorship, not physical magnitude, visibility, frequency, or miraculousness. Define the agency-impact profile of intervention $I$ on agent $A$ at $t$:

\[
Impact_A(I,A,t)=
\langle
\Delta Feas_I,
\Delta Orig_I,
\Delta Capacity_I,
SourceStatus_I
\rangle.
\]

The dimensions need not collapse into one scalar. In particular, option closure and source override both remove token autonomy but do so differently:

- option closure reduces executable alternatives to one;
- source override sufficiently settles the act even if several actions remain executable.

A physically extensive intervention directed at a non-agentic process may have no negative agency impact. A subtle intervention that determines one choice may remove sourcehood for that token act.

## The defeasible presumption

Let $AimEquivalent(I_1,I_2)$ mean that two interventions adequately serve the same relevant divine purpose. Let $Preserves_A(I_1,I_2)$ mean that $I_1$ preserves more of the relevant created sourcehood than $I_2$ without introducing another known defeating cost. Then:

\[
\begin{aligned}
Reason_{\mathrm{sourcehood}}(G)
&\land AimEquivalent(I_1,I_2)\\
&\land Preserves_A(I_1,I_2)\\
&\Rightarrow Favors_G(I_1,I_2).
\end{aligned}
\]

This is a ceteris-paribus favoring relation, not a necessitating rule. A countervailing reason may support $I_2$, and the project need not already know that reason. What would be incoherent is to treat created sourcehood as a central rationale while assigning no explanatory cost at all to its systematic and unnecessary replacement.

## Intervention classes under the presumption

| Intervention effect | Initial agency assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Restores damaged capacities or originable alternatives | supports the agency-oriented rationale |
| Protects the stable arena or agents from external destruction | may support the rationale without controlling their choices |
| Changes information, salience, incentives, or opportunities without settling the act | compatible by default; assess cumulative manipulation |
| Alters a non-agentic target with no downstream agency effect | agency-neutral |
| Closes options for one token act | removes token autonomy; requires a countervailing reason |
| Overrides the source while leaving physical options | removes token authorship; requires a countervailing reason |
| Permanently destroys an agent's general originating power | strong burden under the preferred rationale |

Intervention can therefore increase, preserve, reduce, or remove agency. Nonintervention has no automatic privilege: allowing an external process to destroy an agent's capacities can conflict with the rationale more than intervening to protect them.

## Cumulative context control

Formal alternatives at isolated moments may be insufficient. Repeated manipulation of information, incentives, emotional salience, or opportunities could leave $|Orig_I(A,t)|>1$ at every step while making the agent's long-run authorship increasingly engineered.

The later F2 analysis should therefore distinguish token autonomy from an **agency ecology**: the diachronic conditions under which a subject develops, understands reasons, forms commitments, and exercises powers across a life. This issue is recorded but not solved at F1.

## Providence after creative reason

Providence is now more than the extensional set of divine contributions, but it remains no additional causal force. Define the agency-oriented providential architecture schematically:

\[
Prov_A=
\langle
OEC(G,\mathcal C_0,S^*),
\mathcal R_G,
\mathbf I^*,
H^*
\rangle.
\]

Its elements have different roles:

- OEC and $S^*$ establish the arena and its powers;
- $\mathcal R_G$ supplies the preferred rationale and defeasible intervention constraints;
- $\mathbf I^*$ contains actual additional divine contributions;
- $H^*$ is asymmetrically co-produced by the structure, secondary causes, created agents, and interventions.

This yields **qualified agency-respecting providence**:

1. $G$ retains the power to intervene and even override sourcehood;
2. providence need not pre-program every outcome;
3. created agents genuinely affect which history obtains;
4. the agency-oriented rationale normally favors preserving that contribution;
5. actual interventions may pursue plural purposes not yet known or evaluated.

The architecture therefore rejects both exhaustive divine outcome control and a deistic inference from respect for agency.

## Atemporal responsiveness

An intervention belongs to the resulting complete history; it is not a later edit of an already actual block. It may nevertheless be responsive in created causal order to an actual condition, request, action, or threat:

\[
Actual(p,H^*)
\land RespondsTo(I,p)
\land PartOf(I,H^*).
\]

ACO includes $p$, $I$, and their actual relations without temporal learning by $G$. This does not reinstate pre-actual selection counterfactuals: the reason for the actual intervention can include an actual truthmaker rather than a purported truth about what a merely possible libertarian agent would have done.

The exact dependence between an actual creaturely condition and an atemporal divine response remains a later F2 problem. Self-consistency is required when the response itself changes the conditions surrounding the action.

## Generative explanation of physical features

The author does not require a separate creative reason for every physical detail if that detail is a consequence of a reason-supported generating structure:

\[
ReasonSupported(S^*)
\land
S^*\models f
\Rightarrow
DerivativelyExplained(f).
\]

The entailment may later be generalized to stochastic or dispositional generation, where $S^*$ explains the admissibility and distribution rather than one necessary outcome. Agent-originated and intervention-dependent features receive their contrastive explanation from their respective sources.

This criterion concerns explanatory coverage. It does not yet show that every foreseeable consequence is good or justified; that is a separate evaluative burden.

## Adversarial tests

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Respect for agency entails never intervening | Rejected; intervention may preserve, restore, or enable agency |
| Smaller physical intervention is always less controlling | Rejected; source impact and physical magnitude are independent |
| Any influence on reasons destroys freedom | Rejected under C42 and C56; influence is not sufficient settlement |
| Formal alternatives guarantee meaningful long-run autonomy | Open; cumulative context control motivates the agency-ecology problem |
| A countervailing reason can justify any override | Not automatically; invoking an unspecified reason preserves possibility but does not provide an explanation |
| Providence becomes identical to the agency motive | Rejected; the motive constrains one dimension of a broader architecture |
| Derivation from $S$ morally justifies every consequence | Rejected; metaphysical explanation and evaluative justification are distinct |

## Decisions

| Decision | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claim knowledge of $G$'s ultimate actual reason | rejected | The reason profile is a plausible intelligibility model, not epistemic access to divine motivation |
| Adopt a defeasible agency-preservation presumption | preferred | It provides cross-act coherence between creative purpose and later intervention |
| Identify lower control with smaller or rarer physical action | rejected | Relevant control concerns originable alternatives, capacities, and sourcehood |
| Treat intervention as necessarily opposed to agency | rejected | It may restore, protect, or enable agency |
| Integrate the reason profile into providence | accepted | Providence now connects creation, rationale, interventions, and the co-produced history |
| Infer goodness from this coherence | rejected | Normative value and correct balancing of reasons remain separate |
| Accept generative explanation of derived physical features | preferred | Direct reasons can attach to $S$ while its consequences inherit explanatory coverage |

## Literature and novelty notes

- [Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I.22](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1022.htm) treats providence as compatible with contingency and secondary causes. The present project does not inherit his stronger universal-causality account of creaturely acts.
- The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on divine providence](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2021/entries/providence-divine/) surveys the tension between libertarian freedom, divine control, secondary causation, and the free-will defense.
- [Katherin Rogers, “Can Libertarian Free Will be Reconciled with Divine Providence?”](https://doi.org/10.14428/thl.v9i2.85953) combines eternalism, created libertarian input, divine action, and substantial but non-absolute providence.

The general compatibility project is established. The local contribution is a typed derivation from the S18 creative rationale to a defeasible intervention policy, using the existing distinctions among feasible options, originable alternatives, source override, non-agent targets, and atemporal actual-history membership. Novelty remains unassessed beyond that synthesis.
