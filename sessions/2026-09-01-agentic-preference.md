# Session: Constructive standard and agentic preference

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Session ID:** S09
**Primary question:** What must the comparison with impersonal necessity accomplish if the project does not expect metaphysical demonstration?
**Dependencies:** C04–C05, authorial direction, spiral method
**Starting status:** necessary agent preferred; impersonal necessity remains a comparison alternative

## Starting position

The author does not expect either an agentic or impersonal ultimate foundation to be demonstrable in the sense of uniquely resolving the fundamental mystery of reality. The goal is to determine which ontology makes more sense, is more satisfying, coheres better with the whole architecture, and survives serious objections.

The agentic hypothesis is already the selected working direction. The comparison must explain that preference and identify genuinely strong reasons that could overturn it. It must not treat every coherent alternative as resetting the project to neutrality or discard downstream work without a substantive defeat of C05.

## Comparative standard

Use five questions:

1. Is the hypothesis internally coherent?
2. What does it explain or unify?
3. Which primitives and brute stopping points does it require?
4. How well does it fit the project's other commitments and authorial judgment?
5. Is there a substantive defeater strong enough to require revision?

The resulting judgment may be abductive, coherence-based, and preferential. It must not be presented as deduction.

## Preliminary cost audit

| Cost or advantage | Necessary agent | Impersonal necessary structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fundamental ontology | Requires necessary subjectivity, powers, and reason-guided agency | Begins with a less personal base but requires a complete modal-generative structure |
| Contingent actuality | Terminates in an agent's non-necessitated settling for reasons | Terminates in brute realization, chance, a selection law, or universal realization |
| Created agency | Reuses agency as a causal kind already present at the foundation | Must ground or permit irreducible agency from an impersonal base |
| Explanatory form | Supplies authorship and purposive intelligibility without necessitating the outcome | Supplies structural or probabilistic production without authorship |
| Principal risk | Primitive agency may be judged obscure, anthropomorphic, or explanatorily idle | Apparent simplicity may conceal brute modality, emergence, or unexplained selection |

Fairness requires the same anti-necessitating standard for both hypotheses. The impersonal view is not rejected merely because it lacks a sufficient contrastive reason, since the agentic view also terminates in a non-necessitated exercise. The claimed advantage is reason-guided sourcehood and explanatory unification, not complete elimination of primitive facts.

## Reversal conditions

C05 should be demoted or rejected only if a serious case establishes at least one of the following:

- necessary agenthood or contingent divine exercise is incoherent;
- agent causation cannot perform the causal or explanatory work assigned to it;
- an impersonal model grounds contingency and created agency with materially lower cost and equal or greater explanatory reach;
- the agentic hypothesis merely renames brute selection without adding genuine sourcehood or reason-guidance;
- major evidence or a later commitment conflicts with the agentic foundation.

These are possible defeaters, not current conclusions.
