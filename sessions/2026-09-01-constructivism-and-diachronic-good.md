# Session: Constructed value, sacrifice, and diachronic good

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Session ID:** S24
**Primary question:** Can creature-constructed value, sacrifice, and justified priority for long-term goods be represented within the emerging welfare architecture?
**Dependencies:** C127–C153, S21–S23
**Target depth:** F1 comparison and integration with local F2 notation

## Starting position

The dimension-relative notation needs an explicit temporal parameter. The project also needs to compare its relational divine-source candidate with the possibility that $G$ has no native conception of good and that evaluative concepts or truths are constructed by created autonomous subjects.

The author's adequacy condition is substantive: the final account must explain why at least some sacrifices and long-term commitments can defeat short-lived pleasures. It must do so without declaring every sacrifice, distant project, or delayed outcome good merely because it is costly or temporally extended.

## Corrected welfare notation

Let $d$ be a welfare dimension and $\tau$ a temporal scope: a moment, interval, project horizon, or whole life.

\[
PTGF_d(x,A,C,\tau)
\]

states that $x$ is pro tanto good for $A$ in dimension $d$, context $C$, and temporal scope $\tau$. Use $PTBF_d$ for pro tanto bad-for. Reserve:

\[
GF(x,A,C,\tau)
\]

for an explicitly generic or all-things-considered assessment. Until a defeat or aggregation structure is justified, unqualified $GF$ must not be inferred from one dimension.

The dominance relation becomes:

\[
x\succ^P_{A,C,\tau}y
\quad\text{iff}\quad
\bigl(\forall d\in D_A\;x\succeq^d_{A,C,\tau}y\bigr)
\land
\bigl(\exists d\in D_A\;x\succ^d_{A,C,\tau}y\bigr).
\]

This correction is substantive rather than cosmetic: the same event can be bad phenomenally at $t_0$, good for functioning over interval $I$, and unresolved all things considered across a life.

## What “constructed by subjects” can mean

| Variant | Constructed item | Consequence for $G$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| semantic construction | evaluative vocabulary, classifications, or linguistic practice | $G$ may lack native evaluative vocabulary while objective subject-affecting facts still obtain |
| attitudinal construction | value for $A$ is constituted by $A$'s actual or coherent evaluative standpoint | $G$ knows the standpoint through ACO; objectivity is standpoint-relative |
| procedural or constitutivist construction | valid norms are outputs of, or commitments inherent in, suitable rational agency | if the procedure applies to agency as such, $G$ appears eligible unless independently excluded |
| intersubjective construction | norms arise through recognition, negotiation, convention, or agreement among subjects | norms exist only where the relevant plurality and practice obtain |
| creature-only global construction | all evaluative truth is constituted by created autonomous valuers | divine goodness prior in grounding order to created valuation becomes inapplicable or false |
| projectivism or fictionalism | evaluative discourse expresses or coordinates attitudes without corresponding normative facts | $G$ can know the practice while there may be no value truth for ACO to recognize |

These variants are not equivalent. The claim that humans invented the word “good” is compatible with welfare realism. The claim that no suffering is bad for its subject until autonomous agents construct a norm is much stronger.

## Can $G$ lack a conception of good?

Because $G$ is atemporal, “before creatures” must mean prior in grounding order, not at an earlier time. Define the candidate:

\[
NoNativeNorm(G)\quad\text{iff}\quad
\neg\exists v\;PrimitiveEvaluativeStandpoint(G,v).
\]

If creatures construct a normative practice $N$ in $H^*$, ACO still entails:

\[
Constructs(A,N,H^*)\Rightarrow K_G(Constructs(A,N,H^*)).
\]

Thus $G$ cannot remain ignorant of an actual created evaluative concept. The coherent thesis is that $G$ has no **native or constitutive evaluative standpoint** and only knows the creaturely construction as part of actuality. Knowledge need not entail endorsement.

This alternative has real architectural consequences:

1. creation is not antecedently morally good, bad, or justified by value;
2. divine goodness and omnibenevolence are inapplicable unless $G$ later adopts or participates in creaturely norms;
3. the problem of evil becomes initially a creature-standpoint challenge to $G$, not a violation of a stance-independent norm;
4. ACO includes constructed normative truths only if the construction makes them truth-apt;
5. divine reasons for creation must remain non-evaluative or proto-evaluative reasons of intelligibility.

There is also a scope pressure. The current ontology treats $G$ as a conscious autonomous agent. A rule stating:

\[
AutonomousSubject(A)\Rightarrow EligibleValueConstructor(A)
\]

includes $G$. A creature-only theory must instead restrict construction to finite, dependent, embodied, vulnerable, social, or otherwise specifically created agents. Such a restriction may be defensible, but it is an additional thesis rather than a consequence of autonomy alone.

## The infant, animal, and manipulation pressure

Pure autonomy-based construction risks denying welfare to infants, some disabled humans, and non-human animals that have phenomenal valence but cannot construct reflective norms. Social construction can assign them status, but then whether pain is bad *for the sufferer* depends on another community's practice.

The same theory must handle corrupted construction. Manipulation, addiction, indoctrination, fear, and adaptive preference can shape what an agent endorses. Idealizing the construction procedure may exclude these cases, but the criteria of adequate information, coherence, freedom from manipulation, and equal standing can reintroduce unconstructed normative standards.

For these reasons, two versions currently outperform pure actual-attitude construction:

- **agency constitutivism:** some norms follow from what it is to act and remain an agent;
- **hybrid construction:** phenomenal welfare or other subject-affecting facts are not constructed, while moral, institutional, and coordination norms are constructed by agents.

Both remain open. The hybrid fits the typed normative bridge especially well because value constitution can differ across prudential, moral, and institutional domains.

## Sacrifice is not one evaluative claim

Let $s$ be an act by which $A$ knowingly accepts cost $c$ for end, subject, relation, or project $g$, with beneficiary set $B$ and evaluative horizon $\tau$:

\[
Sacrifice(A,s,c,g,B,C,\tau).
\]

$B$ may contain $A$, contemporaries, later subjects, or several of these; $\tau$ may extend beyond $A$'s life. This extension was made explicit in S25.

At least three assessments must remain distinct:

1. $c$ can remain $PTBF_d(c,A,C,t_0)$; accepting pain or loss does not make the token pleasant or harmless.
2. $s$ can instantiate agentic excellence through informed sourcehood, self-governance, fidelity, courage, or care.
3. $s$ may improve $A$'s diachronic welfare, may reduce it for the sake of another, or may be pointless or destructive.

Sacrifice therefore has no intrinsic positive sign. A fitting sacrifice requires at least informed and non-manipulated authorship, a worthwhile target under the relevant evaluative theory, causal suitability, and proportionality between cost, probability, alternatives, and target. These conditions do not yet supply a complete moral theory, but they prevent costliness from manufacturing value.

## Defeasible diachronic priority

The long-term case is grounded first in temporally extended agency. A subject who forms, revises, and pursues projects over time is not merely a sequence of unrelated momentary appetites. Let $g$ be a stable project or commitment, $p$ a conflicting transient pleasure, $B$ the affected or intended beneficiaries, and $\tau$ the relevant horizon. The project adopts a defeasible priority condition:

\[
DP(g,p;A,B,C,\tau)
\Rightarrow
g\triangleright_{A,B,C,\tau}p,
\]

where $\triangleright$ is normative defeat rather than numerical superiority. $DP$ requires, provisionally:

1. reflective and sufficiently informed endorsement of $g$;
2. absence of source-undermining manipulation;
3. stability across relevant temporal perspectives rather than one compulsive episode;
4. realization or preservation of agency, identity, relationship, understanding, or another justified dimension;
5. due consideration of $A$'s future welfare rather than arbitrary temporal location;
6. proportional cost and no decisive undefeated harm to other subjects.

This explains why studying, caregiving, maintaining a promise, tolerating treatment, or resisting addiction can defeat a short-lived pleasure. The reason is not that later automatically means better. The stable commitment expresses the temporally extended agent and can preserve the conditions of future authorship. A long-term plan for cruelty, domination, or self-destruction does not gain priority merely by lasting.

No lexical ordering has yet been adopted. Phenomenal suffering can defeat a project when the cost is extreme, nonconsensual, futile, or disproportionate. Conversely, a momentary pleasure does not automatically defeat a life-organizing commitment merely because it is presently intense.

## Constructivism under the diachronic test

| View | Can it ground diachronic priority? | Principal cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| actual-attitude construction | only if the current attitude happens to favor the future | cannot criticize coherent present bias, implanted preference, or future indifference |
| idealized Humean construction | sometimes, through a coherent evaluative standpoint | may permit fully coherent agents who simply do not care about future selves or others |
| agency constitutivism | yes, if diachronic integration is constitutive of effective self-government | must show that rejecting long-term integration undermines agency rather than merely one style of life |
| intersubjective construction | yes for promises, roles, trust, and shared projects | social approval can itself be oppressive or short-sighted |
| hybrid welfare plus constructivism | yes: future valence and capacity matter independently, while commitments and institutions add constructed reasons | must explain where the boundary between grounded and constructed value lies |

The author's adequacy condition weighs against pure current-attitude construction. Agency constitutivism and the hybrid are the strongest constructivist candidates at this stage.

## Adversarial tests

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| If value is constructed, anything can become good | Not for procedural or constitutivist views, but they owe non-circular constraints on valid construction |
| If $G$ has ACO, $G$ must already possess the concept of good | ACO requires knowledge once the creaturely concept exists; it does not by itself supply a native evaluative standpoint in grounding order |
| If autonomous subjects construct value, $G$ does too | Correct under the unrestricted rule; creature-only construction needs an independently justified scope restriction |
| Sacrifice is good because it denies pleasure | Rejected; deprivation is a cost, and value depends on authorship, target, causal fit, and proportionality |
| Long-term projects are always superior | Rejected; duration alone does not justify cruelty, obsession, domination, or futile suffering |
| Partial ordering cannot explain self-control | Answered provisionally by normative defeat: a justified stable commitment can defeat a transient attraction without assigning both a common number |
| Prioritizing future agency smuggles in the value of agency | Live and explicit; the project has architectural reasons to value sourcehood, but their prudential and moral force still requires grounding |

## Decisions

| Decision | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Index pro tanto welfare by dimension and temporal scope | accepted correction | Dimension and time are load-bearing in every conflict case |
| Reserve unqualified $GF$ for generic or all-things-considered use | accepted | Prevents a local benefit from masquerading as a total verdict |
| Consider creature-constructed normativity | open alternative | It could preserve autonomy and avoid external normative entities, but has several distinct versions and costs |
| Interpret “$G$ has no good” as no native evaluative standpoint | preferred formulation of the alternative | Literal ignorance conflicts with ACO after evaluative concepts are actual |
| Treat actual-attitude construction as sufficient | disfavored | It cannot adequately ground error, manipulation, non-autonomous welfare, sacrifice, or long-term priority |
| Treat agency-constitutivist and hybrid construction as the strongest constructivist candidates | provisional comparison | They can preserve subject connection and criticize transient or corrupted attitudes |
| Treat sacrifice as intrinsically good | rejected | Bad targets, disproportion, coercion, and futility are counterexamples |
| Adopt defeasible diachronic priority for qualified stable commitments | preferred | It grounds self-government and long-term thought without making duration or deferral sufficient for value |

## Literature and novelty notes

- The 2025 revision of the [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on metaethical constructivism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/constructivism-metaethics/) distinguishes Kantian, Humean, social, and hybrid forms and emphasizes the arbitrariness-versus-unconstructed-constraint dilemma.
- [Sharon Street, “What is Constructivism in Ethics and Metaethics?”](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1747-9991.2009.00280.x), distinguishes constructivism from realism, ideal-response reduction, and expressivism through the practical standpoint.
- The 2026 [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on well-being](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/well-being/) discusses temporal distribution, sacrifice, and conflicts among components of welfare.

The constructivist families, diachronic welfare, pro tanto value, normative defeat, and the distinction between prudential and moral sacrifice are established materials. The project-specific work is their integration with $G$'s atemporality, ACO, created autonomy, agentic sourcehood, and typed normative bridge. No novelty claim is made.
