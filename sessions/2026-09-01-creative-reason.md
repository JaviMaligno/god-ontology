# Session: Creative reason without necessitation

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Session ID:** S18
**Primary question:** What reasons can make creation and the selection of $S$ intelligible without determining them?
**Dependencies:** C05–C07, C13, C48–C60, C95–C100, S06, S16–S17, U15–U16, U19, U21
**Target depth:** F1 reason architecture with selective formalization

## Starting position

The author already prefers an agency-oriented line: a conscious originating agent may reasonably choose to create other autonomous agents, analogously to the religious image of creatures resembling their creator. The task is to ground this more carefully, permit several concurrent reasons, explain non-agentic domains, and avoid turning reasons into a deterministic choice function.

The result is an **agency-oriented plural reason profile**. It is a selected abductive explanation, not a deduction from bare agenthood and not yet a theory of divine goodness.

## Three levels of creative explanation

| Level | Question | Preferred answer at F1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creation | Why actualize anything contingent? | To make finite otherness and contingent modes of existence actual rather than merely possible |
| Architecture | Why a generative order containing agents? | To realize finite analogues of conscious sourcehood and permit genuinely non-divine contributions to an actual history |
| Specification | Why this exact $S$? | Several reasons support a class of structures; $G$ settles one eligible structure without a uniquely determining antecedent reason |

The levels need not be supported by one reason. A teleological reason may favor sourcehood, an expressive reason may favor finite analogues of the creator, a relational reason may favor actual other subjects, and structural or aesthetic reasons may favor intelligible order and variety.

## The central agency-oriented profile

### Actual finite otherness

MKO includes possibilities, but knowing a possible creature is not identical to that creature actually existing. Creation can therefore realize a mode of actuality that divine modal knowledge alone does not instantiate:

\[
KnowsPossible(G,x)\not\Rightarrow Exists(x).
\]

This does not add information that an ignorant $G$ needed. It adds a numerically distinct existent and, with it, actual relations and truthmakers that would otherwise not obtain.

### Analogous sourcehood

$G$ is already modeled as a conscious, reason-guided originating source. A created autonomous agent is not a duplicate of $G$: it is temporal, dependent, limited, and embedded in $\mathcal C_0$. It can nevertheless instantiate an analogous causal form:

\[
\operatorname{Analogous}
(Sourcehood_{\mathrm{finite}},Sourcehood_G).
\]

MKO and the relevant creative power, rather than the analogy alone, establish the candidate possibility. Being an agent does not deductively entail wanting such analogues. The proposed reason is that realizing finite sourcehood expresses or extends a mode central to $G$'s own agency while producing genuine otherness rather than another divine token.

### Asymmetric co-production

If the purpose includes other originating sources, a completely scripted history would fail to instantiate the relevant property. The selected structure must enable acts whose contrastive content is attributable to created agents:

\[
Reason_{\mathrm{sourcehood}}(G)
\Rightarrow
Favors_G\bigl(Enables(S,AutonomousAgents)\bigr).
\]

This is a favoring relation, not a necessitating implication. It explains why the project prefers a generative structure over a fully determined history without claiming that agency is the only possible creative aim.

## Why non-agentic reality belongs

A reason to include agency is not a command to maximize agency in every entity or at every moment. A non-agentic domain can:

1. supply a shared, stable arena in which different agents encounter the same reality;
2. provide regularities that make evidence, prediction, deliberation, and reasons-responsiveness possible;
3. give actions persistent consequences rather than reducing history to disconnected choices;
4. instantiate genuine secondary causation, so not every event is an act of either $G$ or a creature;
5. support embodied, temporal, and developmentally limited forms of agency.

Thus:

\[
Favors_G(Agency)
\not\Rightarrow
\forall x\in\mathcal C_0\,Agent(x).
\]

Some non-agentic structures might also be favored for independent expressive, structural, or aesthetic reasons. This pass does not claim that every actual physical feature is instrumentally required for agency, and it supplies no general justification of suffering.

The same point applies to local agency restriction. An architecture-level reason for autonomous agents does not make every token act inviolable. It does create a defeasible reason against overriding sourcehood: a source-overriding intervention would require some countervailing purpose if the agency-oriented profile is retained.

## Reason-guided selection without an optimizer

Let $\Sigma_G$ be the structures independently within $G$'s power, and let $Profile_M(S)$ contain the modal and structural features of $S$ available through MKO. Define an eligible set:

\[
\mathcal E_G=
\left\{
S\in\Sigma_G:
Coherent(S)
\land Supported_G(Profile_M(S))
\land \neg Defeated_G(S)
\right\}.
\]

The project does not assume that all structures are totally ordered on one scale, that a unique best structure exists, or that rationality is a single-valued maximizing function. Several structures may be tied, incomparable, or differently supported by plural reasons:

\[
\exists S_i,S_j\in\mathcal E_G
\quad
S_i\neq S_j.
\]

The actual specification is then:

\[
S^*\in\mathcal E_G
\land
Settles(G,S^*;\mathcal E_G).
\]

Reasons explain why $S^*$ is eligible and attractive, while $G$ supplies the contrastive settling:

\[
\mathcal R_G(S^*)
\not\Rightarrow
\Box Actualizes(G,S^*).
\]

This is not sheer arbitrariness. The choice is constrained and guided by intelligible considerations. It does leave a residual fact—why this eligible structure rather than another—that terminates in agent causation instead of a further antecedently sufficient selector. Demanding such a selector would reproduce the deterministic principle rejected in C60.

The no-creation option also remains metaphysically live. The reason profile may favor creation without requiring it:

\[
\Diamond Actualizes(G,S)
\land
\Diamond\neg\exists S\,Actualizes(G,S).
\]

If later work on perfect rationality or goodness makes the reasons conclusive and requiring, this claim must be revisited.

## Principle-guided rather than outcome-selected creation

The creative rationale initially ranges over $Profile_M(S)$: laws, powers, constraints, types of possible agents, and admissible continuations. It does not rely on pre-actual truths about which libertarian choices merely possible individuals would make:

\[
ReasonsFor(G,S)
\not\Leftarrow
WouldChoose(A,a,C).
\]

This fits the author's proposal that $G$ may select principles or a generating set rather than every consequence. $S^*$ should be individuated up to ontological rather than syntactic equivalence; equivalent formulations do not constitute different creative choices merely because their descriptions differ.

Once $\mathcal C_0$ and $H^*$ obtain, ACO includes the actual agents and their choices. Their token acts contribute to the actual history but are not smuggled backward as pre-actual selection counterfactuals.

## Experiment, play, and epistemic motives

A literal motive of creating in order to discover which antecedently true outcome will occur conflicts with the current model: before actuality there is no such token truth, and ACO involves no temporal learning.

An enactive or playful motive remains coherent. Actualizing, relating to, or appreciating a possibility is not identical to representing it modally, just as performing a work is not identical to knowing its score. This can supplement the reason profile, but it is not needed for the central argument and is not yet preferred.

## Adversarial tests

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Bare agenthood does not entail a desire to create other agents | Correct; the profile is an abductively selected motive, not a deduction |
| A reason for agency should make everything autonomous | Invalid; favoring a property in an architecture does not require universal maximization |
| Choosing among equally supported structures is arbitrary | There is residual contrastive settling, but it remains reason-guided; eliminating all residue would eliminate libertarian choice |
| A perfectly rational being must choose the uniquely best structure | Open; no unique total ordering or perfection premise has been established |
| God must know each free outcome before selecting $S$ | Rejected; structural reasons range over modal profiles, while actual token knowledge is actuality-indexed |
| Creation must satisfy a divine deficiency or epistemic need | Unsupported; actualization and relation differ from modal knowledge without implying prior lack |
| Agency-oriented creation already proves divine goodness | Rejected; the purpose and its implementation still require evaluation |

## Decisions

| Decision | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use a plural, layered reason architecture | accepted | Creation, architecture, and exact specification answer different contrastive questions |
| Prefer actual finite otherness and analogous sourcehood as the organizing reasons | preferred | They connect divine agency to autonomous created agency and asymmetric co-production |
| Prefer a generative $S$ over a completely scripted history under this motive | preferred | Created sourcehood must contribute genuine contrastive content |
| Require universal or continuously exercised creaturely agency | rejected | Agency can be a local and architecture-level aim among plural reasons |
| Require one uniquely best structure or total value ordering | rejected as a present premise | Neither minimal rationality nor the current ontology establishes it |
| Let the exact choice terminate in reason-guided agentic settling | accepted | This applies C55–C60 consistently to $G$ |
| Use pre-actual selection counterfactuals to justify $S$ | rejected | It conflicts with C13, C50, and the actuality-indexed epistemic architecture |
| Treat experimentation as divine learning | rejected | There is no temporally acquired truth; enactive play remains a separate open motive |

## Literature and novelty notes

- [Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I.19.3](https://www.sacred-texts.com/chr/aquinas/summa/sum022.htm) distinguishes God's necessary willing of the divine good from non-necessary willing of creatures. His derivation relies on perfection and goodness not yet adopted here.
- [Robert Adams, “Must God Create the Best?”](https://andrewmbailey.com/rma/1972Adams-MustGodCreate.pdf) is a classic argument against the requirement that perfect creation always select the best possible world.
- The 2025 [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on divine freedom](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/divine-freedom/) maps alternatives involving a unique best world, several equal or incomparable unsurpassable worlds, and no best world, as well as reasons-responsive and alternate-possibility accounts of divine freedom.
- [Luke Wilson, “A perfectly free God cannot satisfice”](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11098-025-02317-9) argues that satisficing requires brute preferences if perfect freedom excludes all non-rational influences. This is a live objection if the project later adopts perfect freedom or a strong rational-purity thesis.

Plural non-necessitating reasons and divine selection among alternatives are established topics. The potentially useful local synthesis is the chain:

\[
\text{finite otherness}
\rightarrow
\text{analogous sourcehood}
\rightarrow
\text{generative }S
\rightarrow
\text{asymmetric co-production},
\]

integrated with MKO profiles, the rejection of pre-actual selection counterfactuals, and the same reason-guided settling used for creaturely agents. No novelty claim is made before a deeper literature comparison.
