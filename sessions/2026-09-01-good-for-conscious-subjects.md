# Session: Good-for and conscious subjects

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Session ID:** S23
**Primary question:** Can the project develop a usable account of what is good or bad for a conscious subject before defining goodness in general?
**Dependencies:** C33, C55–C66, C104–C120, C127–C141, S18–S22
**Target depth:** F1 conceptual architecture with local F2 comparative notation

## Starting position

The absence of a complete theory of goodness does not prevent narrower progress. The present pass treats **good-for** as the first target: how well a subject's life or some part of it goes for that subject. It does not yet reduce good-for to another non-evaluative property, identify it with morality, or infer divine goodness.

The method is constraint-first. Begin with cases in which experiential valence, desire, agency, truth, functioning, relationships, and temporal development come apart. A viable account must preserve the distinctions before attempting aggregation.

## Definitions

| Term/symbol | Definition used in this session |
| --- | --- |
| $WBearer(A)$ | $A$ is a candidate bearer of welfare: its life can go better or worse for it. The present inquiry begins with conscious subjects without claiming that no non-conscious entity can have another kind of good. |
| $WProf(A,C)$ | The welfare-relevant profile of $A$ in context $C$, provisionally including phenomenal valence, embodied or psychological functioning, agency, epistemic contact, relationships, and diachronic integrity or development. |
| $\tau$ | The temporal scope of assessment: a moment, interval, project horizon, or whole life. |
| $PTGF_d(x,A,C,\tau)$ | $x$ benefits $A$ in dimension $d$, under context $C$ and temporal scope $\tau$, without yet settling conflicts with other dimensions. |
| $PTBF_d(x,A,C,\tau)$ | $x$ harms $A$ in dimension $d$, under context $C$ and temporal scope $\tau$, without yet settling conflicts with other dimensions. |
| $x\succeq^d_{A,C,\tau}y$ | $x$ is at least as good for $A$ as $y$ with respect to dimension $d$, context $C$, and temporal scope $\tau$. |
| $GF(x,A,C,\tau)$ | Reserved for an all-things-considered or explicitly generic good-for assessment. No complete aggregation rule is adopted here. |

## Why good-for can precede good simpliciter

“Good for $A$” has a bearer and a subject-relative direction that impersonal value lacks. It can therefore be investigated without assuming that everything valuable is reducible to welfare or that every welfare improvement is morally required.

The project can initially use comparative judgments:

\[
x\succ^d_{A,C,\tau}y
\]

without assigning numerical value. A safe multidimensional dominance relation is:

\[
x\succ^P_{A,C,\tau}y
\quad\text{iff}\quad
\bigl(\forall d\in D_A\;x\succeq^d_{A,C,\tau}y\bigr)
\land
\bigl(\exists d\in D_A\;x\succ^d_{A,C,\tau}y\bigr).
\]

This establishes some comparisons when $x$ is no worse in any admitted dimension and better in at least one. It intentionally leaves mixed cases partially ordered. Lack of an overall verdict is not equivalent to equal value.

## Candidate dimensions

The following are a research profile, not a final objective list:

| Dimension | Initial content | Evidential strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| phenomenal valence | suffering, pain, enjoyment, affective quality, and the experienced character of life | strongest initial anchor; not exhaustive |
| functioning | bodily and psychological conditions enabling the subject's continued life and characteristic capacities | strong but sensitive to disability, variation, and context |
| agency | effective authorship, practical access, non-manipulated options, and capacity to pursue or revise projects | architecturally supported; more than option count |
| epistemic contact | truth, understanding, non-deception, and accurate relation to the world and self | plausible; can conflict with comfort |
| relationship | attachment, recognition, reciprocity, trust, and participation with other subjects | plausible and inherently relational |
| diachronic integrity | development, memory, projects, commitments, identity, and the shape of a life through time | needed to avoid evaluating isolated moments as whole lives |

No dimension is unrestricted. More options can burden rather than benefit; painful functioning can be harmful; a relationship can be abusive; some truths can damage a subject; and “development” can disguise coercive normalization. The profile supplies questions for evaluation rather than automatic positive labels.

## Desire and endorsement

Current desire is neither necessary nor sufficient for good-for. A subject may want what is based on deception, addiction, manipulation, transient compulsion, or ignorance, and infants or some animals can fare well or badly without reflective endorsement.

For a capable agent, however, informed and non-manipulated endorsement is important evidence and can itself contribute to authored well-being. A purported objective benefit that the subject could not value from any adequately informed and undistorted standpoint faces an alienation defeater. This is a constraint on objective-list reasoning, not a reduction to actual preference.

## Case matrix

| Case | What it establishes |
| --- | --- |
| pleasant deception or an experience machine | positive phenomenal valence may coexist with epistemic loss and frustrated real projects; hedonism is not yet sufficient |
| painful surgery | a local phenomenal bad can be instrumentally connected to later functioning and agency; temporal scope matters |
| addictive satisfaction | desire fulfillment and pleasure can occur alongside impaired agency and diachronic damage |
| restored mobility or communication | functioning and agency may improve together even if effort or pain increases |
| coerced “self-improvement” | capacity gain does not automatically compensate for source override, humiliation, or imposed identity |
| chosen sacrifice | an act may reduce narrow personal welfare while fitting commitments, relationships, or moral reasons; good action and good-for remain distinct |
| sadistic enjoyment | positive phenomenal valence for one subject can coexist with harm to another and moral badness; the final prudential status of that valence remains part of the anchor test |

These cases resist a single immediate scalar. They do not show that comparison is impossible. Some interventions dominate alternatives, some require contextual trade-offs, and some remain incommensurable at the present depth.

## Real-world application

Metaphysics identifies candidate bearers, dimensions, and grounding relations; it cannot determine unaided how pain, trauma, social isolation, disability, coercion, attachment, or restored capacity actually affect different subjects. Psychology, neuroscience, medicine, ethology, and social inquiry must inform $WProf(A,C)$ and the causal consequences of $x$.

Empirical description does not by itself derive normative valence. Conversely, a purported normative account that systematically ignores reliable evidence about subjects is not adequately connected to the world it evaluates. The eventual theory therefore needs a typed interface: empirical evidence helps establish the descriptive profile and expected effects, while the metaphysical account explains why those effects count as benefits, harms, or neither.

## Connection to the normative bridge

For finite welfare, the four bridge roles now receive an initial allocation:

1. **Existential grounding:** $G$ grounds the existence of the subject, the created arena, and its basic capacities, conditional on the existing creation architecture.
2. **Value constitution:** provisionally located in the fit between an actual state or relation and $WProf(A,C)$; the ultimate ground of that fit remains open.
3. **Recognition:** if a good-for fact obtains, ACO includes it. Divine knowledge tracks the fact and does not by itself constitute it.
4. **Institution:** divine acts can alter $C$ by establishing contingent roles, promises, permissions, or ends; this can change some relational or deontic facts without determining every welfare fact by fiat.

The actual token fact can be asymmetrically co-grounded:

\[
GroundsExistence(G,A,C)
\land Occurs(x,A,C)
\land Fits_d(x,WProf(A,C),\tau)
\Rightarrow PTGF_d(x,A,C,\tau).
\]

$G$ supplies the existence of the relata and arena; created causes or agents can supply $x$; the fit relation supplies its prudential valence. Thus a creature can originate an act that benefits or harms without $G$ becoming the contrastive author of its content.

The strongest live form of “normativity emerges from $G$” would make the principles of fit necessarily dependent on $G$'s necessary nature while allowing their actual finite instances to depend on contingent created profiles and histories. That proposal is coherent with the current architecture but not yet earned: it must identify which independently supported feature of $G$ grounds which evaluative relation instead of redescribing the divine nature as good.

## Adversarial tests

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Without defining goodness, every pro tanto judgment is arbitrary | Too strong; comparative and bearer-specific constraints can rule out reductions and establish dominance before a complete grounding theory exists |
| The profile is only an arbitrary objective list | Live pressure; each dimension needs case support, an account of subject connection, and a possible defeater rather than inclusion by stipulation |
| Pleasure is the only feature intrinsically connected to consciousness | Strong reason to treat valence as an anchor, not yet a reason to ignore deception, authorship, relationship, or temporal shape |
| Whatever realizes a designed capacity is good for the creature | Rejected; capacities can conflict, be manipulated, or be specified through a degrading design, and functioning excellence differs from welfare |
| Divine creation of the profile makes every result of that profile good | Rejected; existential sourcehood does not settle evaluative fit, and created acts can damage the very subject and capacities they presuppose |
| A partial order evades difficult trade-offs | It postpones only unsupported aggregation; dominance handles clear cases, while mixed cases receive explicit later burdens |

## Decisions

| Decision | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Begin with good-for before defining good simpliciter | accepted | It narrows the bearer, relation, and cases without reducing all value to welfare |
| Treat conscious subjects as the first welfare bearers | preferred scope | Phenomenal valence supplies the clearest subject-affecting anchor; other bearers remain open |
| Separate pro tanto and all-things-considered good-for | accepted | Benefits and harms can coexist across dimensions and time |
| Use a plural welfare profile without a total scalar | provisional | It captures current cases while avoiding premature commensuration |
| Equate current desire or pleasure with total good-for | rejected | Deception, addiction, manipulation, and diachronic damage provide counterexamples |
| Treat informed non-alienated endorsement as evidence and sometimes a constituent | preferred | Preserves the subject connection without making preference infallible |
| Treat finite welfare facts as asymmetrically co-grounded | provisional | Integrates value with creation and creaturely causal authorship without divine token determination |
| Ground principles of normative fit directly in $G$'s necessary nature | open candidate | Promising for the author's direction, but no independently established divine feature yet supplies the required valence |
| Treat empirical inquiry as necessary for applying the welfare account | accepted | Actual profiles and effects require evidence, although evidence alone does not derive their normative valence |

## Literature and novelty notes

- The 2026 [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on well-being](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/well-being/) treats good-for as how a life goes for its bearer, distinguishes hedonist, desire, and objective-list families, and discusses temporal shape, variabilism, and the separation between welfare and morality.
- [Aristotle's *Nicomachean Ethics*](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.html) is a central precedent for locating human flourishing in characteristic rational activity and a whole life; the project does not import his function argument or identification of virtue with welfare.
- [Guy Fletcher, “A Fresh Start for the Objective-List Theory of Well-Being”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0953820812000453), develops a more discriminating taxonomy and addresses arbitrariness concerns for objective-list accounts.

Plural dimensions, pro tanto value, partial comparison, and hybrid subject/object constraints are established families. The project-specific contribution is their integration with phenomenal and agentive consciousness, asymmetric co-production, ACO, and the typed divine normative bridge. No novelty claim is made.
