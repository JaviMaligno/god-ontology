## Result

The audit finds no omitted traditional divine attribute that must precede the current work. The main inherited gap is the bridge from a necessary agent with creative capacity to an intelligible act of creation. The new material-production objection belongs to the same module.

Call this the **creation bridge**, with two axes:

1. **ontological production:** how material substances and created structure depend on or arise from $G$;
2. **creative reason:** why a necessary agent creates anything contingent and why it actualizes this kind of structure.

Goodness, reasons for intervention, and a fuller providence account depend on this bridge and should follow it rather than fill it prematurely.

## Why the creation bridge is next

The architecture distinguishes:

\[
CanCreate(G)
\]

from:

\[
HasReason(G,\mathcal C)
\]

and from:

\[
Good(\mathcal C).
\]

Power explains availability of an act, not why the act is exercised. A creative reason makes the exercise intelligible without yet showing that the reason or outcome is morally or ultimately good.

The same agentic logic already used for $G$ and created agents constrains the result:

\[
Reasons_G(\mathcal C)
\not\Rightarrow
\Box Actualizes(G,\mathcal C).
\]

Reasons may be teleological, epistemic, aesthetic, agency-oriented, relational, or plural. At F1 they must support choice without becoming an antecedently sufficient choice function.

## Two-axis question set

### Ontological production

1. Does creation involve grounding, efficient production, constitution, manifestation, or more than one relation?
2. Must producer and product share a substance type?
3. Can numerically distinct uncreated and created matter instantiate one material type without making $G$ spatial?
4. Does divine modification preserve a genuinely contingent creation distinct from $G$?

### Creative reason

1. Why create anything rather than exercise no creative power?
2. Why actualize a generative structure rather than specify a complete deterministic history?
3. Why include autonomous agents?
4. Why permit non-agentic entities and local removals or restrictions of agency?
5. Can several reasons jointly favor a structure without necessitating it?
