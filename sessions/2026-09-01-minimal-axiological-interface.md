# Session: The minimal axiological interface

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Session ID:** S27
**Primary question:** What is the smallest value structure needed to define good and evil, explain sacrifice and long-horizon concern, and relate value to $G$ without developing a comprehensive ethics?
**Dependencies:** C127–C185, S21–S26
**Target depth:** F1 closure with local F2 typing

## Correction to the S26 boundary

Longtermism is partly internal to the required interface because sacrifice, species survival, and projects extending beyond an agent all depend on the standing and defeasible priority of temporally distant goods. The system of goodness must positively favor durable and transpersonal goods over conflicting transient benefits under suitable conditions. What does not follow automatically is a complete **strong-longtermist** doctrine, far-future dominance across a wide class of decisions, or comprehensive population maximization. S28 makes this authorial correction explicit.

The project therefore includes a minimal long-horizon thesis derived from its metaphysics:

1. temporal distance alone does not erase or discount an otherwise identical value relation;
2. future subjects and future instances of agency, consciousness, and relation can matter now;
3. durable, generative, or transpersonal goods receive defeasible priority over otherwise comparable transient benefits;
4. effects beyond the acting agent's life can therefore defeat immediate self-interest;
5. no total aggregation or maximizing rule follows solely from this preference.

## Ontological economy: three domains and one bridge

The proposed minimum does not add a free-standing catalogue of ethical entities. It uses three domains already present in the ontology:

| Domain | Existing ontological basis | Why it is load-bearing for value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $P$ — conscious valence | phenomenal consciousness and states that feel good or bad for a subject | without it, unendorsed suffering has no direct negative status |
| $S$ — sourcehood | conscious reason-guided origination, capacities, and diachronic agency | without it, determined contentment, manipulation, or servitude can exhaust the good |
| $R$ — truthful recognition and relation | plurality of subjects, personal availability, knowledge, attention, and possible reciprocity | without it, deception, exploitation, other-regarding sacrifice, and relation with $G$ lack an irreducible evaluative dimension |

Call this ontological anchor domain:

\[
\mathcal A_{min}=\{P,S,R\}.
\]

The single specifically axiological addition is a necessary relation of **objective fit**:

\[
Fit_*(x,a,B,C,\tau),\qquad a\in\mathcal A_{min},
\]

stating that $x$ realizes, protects, restores, or appropriately responds to anchor $a$ for bearer or beneficiary $B$ in context $C$ and horizon $\tau$. Its negative counterpart $Misfit_*$ covers frustration, corruption, destruction, or inappropriate response.

This is not yet a reduction of value to non-evaluative facts. The project accepts that no list of physical or psychological descriptions alone yields the normative bridge. Its economy claim is narrower: one typed fit relation applied to an independently developed ontology can do the work that would otherwise require several unrelated value primitives.

## Minimal good and bad

Dimension-relative judgments become:

\[
PTGood_a(x,B,C,\tau)\quad\text{iff}\quad Fit_*(x,a,B,C,\tau),
\]

\[
PTBad_a(x,B,C,\tau)\quad\text{iff}\quad Misfit_*(x,a,B,C,\tau).
\]

The existing $PTGF_d$ and $PTBF_d$ are subject-relative applications within this wider scheme. Generic:

\[
Good(x,C,\tau)
\]

means that $x$ has undefeated objective fit across the anchors and bearers relevant to the stated context and horizon. It does not imply that every dimension is positive or that a complete scalar sum has been computed. A painful treatment or costly sacrifice may contain a genuine $PTBad_P$ while remaining good under a broader undefeated relation.

This account keeps “good” objective while allowing finite subjects to approximate it. Their judgments do not constitute $Fit_*$; evidence, deliberation, experience, and criticism give fallible access to its instances.

## Minimal evil

The word “evil” has two useful scopes:

\[
Evil^{broad}(x,C,\tau)
\]

marks objective badness through misfit with one or more central anchors, including minor and severe cases, while:

\[
Evil^{grave}(x,C,\tau)
\]

is reserved for severe, non-trivial destruction, corruption, or violation of conscious life, sourcehood, or truthful relation.

Evil is an evaluative status, not presently a second fundamental substance opposed to $G$. S28 refines this through due-good privation: evil qua evil is absence, frustration, or corruption of a good due to a bearer, capacity, or relation, rather than every missing possible good. Severe pain can remain a positive phenomenal occurrence that bears or manifests damaged $P$; domination can positively occur while corrupting $S$; betrayal can occur while depriving $R$ of truthful recognition. The positive vehicle and the privative evaluative structure are compatible.

The natural/moral division is causal rather than a division between two value predicates:

\[
MoralEvil(x,A,C)
\Rightarrow
Evil(x,C)\land CulpablyOriginates(A,x,C),
\]

whereas non-agentic or natural evil lacks culpable agent origination of the relevant token, even when agents later affect its course. This integrates evil with the existing typed causal architecture. Divine existential grounding of the arena does not by itself make $G$ the contrastive author of a creature's moral evil.

## Long-horizon standing from E+

In the preferred E+ model, later subjects in the actual history are not less actual because they occur after the deliberator's temporal frame. A temporal agent lacks full epistemic access to them, while ACO includes whichever later subjects and effects obtain in the complete history.

Define **long-horizon standing**:

\[
FutureBearer(B,t')\land a\in\mathcal A_{min}
\Rightarrow EligibleForFit(B,a,t'),
\]

with no discount produced merely by $t'>t$. Before a future individual's identity is available to the deliberator, the agent can reason about structural bearer descriptions and conditions under which conscious, agentive, relational lives will exist. This requires no pre-actual counterfactual truth about what a future libertarian agent would choose.

Long-horizon standing is the metaphysical core of the project's moderate longtermism. It establishes eligibility and possible importance, not numerical dominance. Epistemic uncertainty, causal attenuation, present harms, rights, proportionality, alternative futures, and the quality of future lives remain defeaters or comparison factors.

## Sacrifice

The earlier structure can now be explained through anchors. A sacrifice can be fitting when:

1. $A$ knowingly accepts a real cost in $P$, $S$, or another good and acts from sincere belief, trust, hope, or faith directed toward the protected good;
2. the act protects or realizes an undefeated anchor for $A$ or beneficiaries $B$;
3. the link between act and protected good is sincerely believed or trusted without culpable disregard of accessible counterevidence; knowledge and complete or accurate information are not required;
4. the cost is proportionate under uncertainty and available alternatives;
5. the act is not produced by source-undermining coercion or manipulation.

The sacrifice's goodness does not arise from pain or deprivation. It arises from the agent's sourceful response to a good whose bearer or horizon can extend beyond that agent. This covers sacrifice for contemporaries, descendants, knowledge, institutions, a form of life, or the continued possibility of autonomous subjects.

## Species continuity

Species survival is derivative but not merely instrumental in a trivial sense. It can preserve a temporally extended network of future bearers and relations:

\[
SpeciesContinuity(H,\tau)
\Rightarrow
PreservesConditions(H,\mathcal A_{min},\tau).
\]

Its value depends on what continuity makes possible: conscious lives, agency, truthful relation, inherited projects, cultural memory, and further goods. No conscious species-superorganism is required. Extinction can be gravely bad through irreversible prevention of this open field of future instantiation even when no single present person experiences all of the loss.

The implication is one-way. Biological continuation that predictably consists only in extreme suffering, systematic destruction of agency, or corrupted relation is not made good merely by being continuation. The same anchors that explain the value of survival constrain the means and forms by which it is pursued.

## Relation of objective fit to $G$

The preferred working realization is **essentially G-grounded fit**:

\[
EssentialGround(G,Fit_*).
\]

This does not mean that $G$ chooses the content of fit arbitrarily. It means that the necessary fit relation is grounded in $G$'s independently developed character as conscious rational source and ultimate relational ground, if that bridge can be completed without circularly calling those features good. A free-standing necessary structural relation remains the fallback if the grounding bridge fails.

Only the conditional fit structure need be necessary. MKO can include its application to possible conscious and agentive types, while actual value instances require actual bearers and enter ACO with $H^*$. Thus $G$-grounding does not make every possible person actual or assign welfare to a non-instantiated individual.

The roles remain typed:

1. $G$ existentially grounds actual bearers, capacities, and relational fields;
2. $Fit_*$ supplies objective evaluative content;
3. ACO recognizes every actual instance of fit and misfit;
4. divine acts may institute contingent norms and relations;
5. divine goodness would require $G$'s dispositions and acts to respond perfectly to the relevant fit relations.

Only the first and third roles are established by the prior ontology. The second is now preferred provisionally; the fourth is permitted; the fifth remains an unearned attribute. Thus the interface makes “$G$ is good” substantive without pretending already to demonstrate it.

Once created subjects exist, $G$ need not share their pressure for self-preservation for fit to apply to divine relations with them. Creation, intervention, protection, permission of suffering, and personal availability can be evaluated by how they bear on $P$, $S$, and $R$. This is the point at which the problem of evil becomes internal to the architecture rather than a borrowed objection.

## Adversarial compression

| Challenge | Present result |
| --- | --- |
| Why these three anchors rather than an arbitrary list? | Each blocks a distinct collapse already exposed by the ontology: suffering into endorsement, agency into experience, and other-regarding or divine relation into solitary welfare. Minimality remains defeasible. |
| Does $Fit_*$ merely rename good? | It would if left untyped. Its content is constrained by specific bearers, capacities, causal effects, context, and horizon, but the normativity of fit remains one admitted primitive bridge. |
| Does long-term preference imply a complete strong-longtermist theory? | No. The system positively favors durable or transpersonal goods over conflicting transient benefits under suitable conditions, while aggregation, maximization, and general far-future dominance require further premises. |
| Is extinction bad for a nonexistent person? | The present claim concerns irreversible loss of conditions for future anchor instantiation; exact person-affecting rights and population comparisons are unnecessary at F1. |
| Does grounding fit in $G$ make anything $G$ does good? | No. Grounding a standard differs from satisfying it, and arbitrary willing does not constitute its content. |
| Does evil refute the current $G$? | Not yet deductively: full goodness and qualified omnipotence remain unearned. Evil now supplies a later integration test for any stronger divine-attribute claim. |

## Decisions

| Decision | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Include defeasible long-term preference within the axiological interface | accepted correction | The goodness system must favor durable or transpersonal goods over conflicting transient benefits under suitable conditions |
| Adopt a complete strong or maximizing longtermism | not required | Some stronger conclusions may follow, but the present preference does not by itself establish far-future dominance or a population calculus |
| Use $\mathcal A_{min}=\{P,S,R\}$ as the minimum anchor domain | preferred at F1 | Conscious valence, sourcehood, and truthful recognition each prevent a distinct explanatory collapse |
| Add one objective relation $Fit_*$ | provisional/preferred | It gives the anchors normative direction without multiplying primitive goods, while admitting a real is–ought bridge |
| Treat good as undefeated contextual fit | preferred | It preserves mixed cases and partial ordering without a total scalar |
| Treat evil as a fundamental rival substance | rejected | Severe badness can be phenomenal occurrence, privation, agency corruption, or relational violation |
| Distinguish broad, grave, moral, and non-agentic evil | accepted | Scope and causal authorship answer different questions |
| Ground species value in future anchor instantiation | preferred | This explains transpersonal continuity without a species super-subject or unconditional biological supremacy |
| Prefer essentially G-grounded fit | provisional | It best preserves divine ultimity and non-arbitrariness, but still owes a non-circular bridge from established divine features |
| Attribute goodness or omnibenevolence to $G$ now | rejected as premature | The standard is substantive; its perfect divine satisfaction has not been demonstrated |
| Close the active ethical branch after this pass | accepted | The five outputs required by S26 now have an F1 structure; further ethics remains available when ontologically load-bearing |

## Literature and novelty notes

- The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on the concept of evil](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/concept-evil/) distinguishes broad and narrow evil and the established natural/moral division; it also surveys substance and privation accounts.
- The 2025 [entry on theological voluntarism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/voluntarism-theological/) explains why accounts often restrict divine will to some normative roles in order to preserve substantive divine goodness and intelligible choice.
- The 2026 [entry on intergenerational justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-intergenerational/) treats future standing, temporal discount, unknown identities, asymmetric power, and non-identity.
- Greaves and MacAskill's [case for strong longtermism](https://globalprioritiesinstitute.org/hilary-greaves-william-macaskill-the-case-for-strong-longtermism/page/9/) defines a substantially stronger priority thesis than the minimal long-horizon standing adopted here.

Plural value, fittingness, broad/grave and natural/moral evil, future-generation standing, and strong longtermism are established materials. The project-specific work is their compressed integration with $PCon$, sourcehood, personal relation, E+, ACO, typed causation, and divine grounding. No novelty claim is made.
