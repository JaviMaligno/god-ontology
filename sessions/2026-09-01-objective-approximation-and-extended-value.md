# Session: Objective approximation, finite construction, and extended value

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Session ID:** S25
**Primary question:** Does an objective but only approximable good outperform global construction, and how should the account represent different conscious capacities and goods extending beyond the agent's life?
**Dependencies:** C127–C164, S21–S24
**Target depth:** F1 comparison with local F2 distinctions and notation

## Authorial direction

The author presently finds most satisfactory a concept of good toward which temporal subjects can improve without any guarantee of complete attainment. More than one theory may remain admissible when the architecture genuinely underdetermines the choice, but the project should not preserve a flat list indefinitely: it should select a working preference after applying discriminating tests.

Three corrections guide this pass:

1. capacity to construct a norm differs from needing norms because of vulnerability, dependence, coordination, or self-preservation;
2. animals, children, and adults should not be treated as having one undifferentiated level of consciousness;
3. long-term good can concern contemporaries and subjects living after the acting agent has died.

## Construction capacity, pressure, exercise, and endorsement

Four predicates prevent a single appeal to agency from doing too much:

\[
CapConstruct(A,N),\qquad PressureConstruct(A,N,C),
\]

\[
Constructs(A,N,C),\qquad Endorses(A,N,C).
\]

An agent may possess the capacity to formulate a norm, lack a practical need to do so, encounter reasons to construct one, construct it, and still refuse to endorse it. None of these relations entails the next without further premises.

Finite temporal subjects face a distinctive cluster of pressures:

- vulnerability to pain, deprivation, coercion, and death;
- dependence on other subjects and shared environments;
- limited information and control;
- conflicts among agents and among one agent's temporal perspectives;
- the need to coordinate, preserve projects, and maintain agency through time.

These facts can explain why creatures construct personal rules, moral practices, and institutions. Self-preservation is one important source of practical pressure, but not the whole source: care, recognition, truth, shared projects, and concern for others can also motivate norm formation.

If $G$ is necessarily existent, atemporal relative to creation, and not threatened with loss of existence, then $G$ may lack the creaturely pressure generated by self-preservation while retaining $CapConstruct$. This claim is conditional because full divine invulnerability has not been independently derived. It also establishes only absence of one pressure. It does not establish $NoNativeNorm(G)$: truth, recognition of conscious subjects, creation, relationship, and fitting response may supply stronger reasons for a divine evaluative standpoint.

The distinction therefore improves the coherence of creature-only construction but does not yet make it the best account.

## Three candidates after consolidation

| Candidate | What is objective or constructed? | Principal strength | Principal burden |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| global finite construction | evaluative truth depends ultimately on finite attitudes, procedures, or practices | explains the practical genesis and creature-relative grip of norms | struggles with unrecognized suffering, corrupted construction, cross-practice error, and sacrifice beyond self-preservation |
| objective regulative good | at least some good-making facts set a standard independently of actual endorsement | makes error, learning, criticism, sacrifice, and moral progress intelligible | must explain the ontology and epistemic access of the standard without merely naming it |
| objective-anchor hybrid | phenomenal harms and some relational or agentic goods are objective; subjects construct more specific personal, moral, and institutional norms under those constraints | preserves objective criticism while explaining why concrete norms depend on finite histories and coordination | must justify the boundary between anchor and construction and the ultimate ground of the anchors |

The **objective-anchor hybrid** is now the preferred working direction. This preference does not yet choose among every metaphysical realization of objective value. Necessary structural or relational truth, fit grounded in $G$'s necessary nature, and a suitably constrained divine-source account may be different realizations of the same functional architecture. They remain live only where they make different predictions or explanations.

$NoNativeNorm(G)$ remains admissible as a conditional competitor, not a coequal default. Its strongest explanatory resource is the absence of finite normative pressure in $G$; its decisive deficit is that absence of need does not establish absence of other sufficient reasons to evaluate.

## Objective approximation without reifying the good

“Approaching the good” is epistemic and practical. It does not require the good to be a spatial target, a Platonic substance, or another agent ontologically above $G$. Let $J_1$ and $J_2$ be evaluative judgment systems available to a temporal subject or community. Write:

\[
Closer_{Good}(J_2,J_1\mid E,C)
\]

when $J_2$ is better supported than $J_1$ relative to evidence $E$ and context $C$ by the project's admitted truth-conducive tests. The relation is comparative and partial; no numerical distance from perfect goodness is assumed.

Candidate indicators include:

1. correction of identifiable factual or inferential error;
2. resistance to manipulation, arbitrary exclusion, and self-serving distortion;
3. coherence across relevantly similar cases and temporal perspectives;
4. better representation of the experiences, interests, agency, and relations of affected subjects;
5. survival under adversarial criticism responsive to a broader evidential base;
6. capacity to explain sacrifice, moral error, and concern beyond the evaluator's own lifetime.

These indicators are defeasible evidence of approximation, not what makes the objective good true. Otherwise consensus or method would silently become the constructor of the standard. Persistent disagreement therefore weakens confidence and may block a ranking; it does not by itself prove that no objective answer exists.

Finite agents need never reach a complete or final evaluative theory. Limited information, situated perspectives, changing circumstances, and incomplete access to other subjects' experience make fallibility structural. Approximation permits genuine improvement without promising convergence, a unique complete ordering, or epistemic infallibility.

## Selection without premature closure or permanent pluralism

Candidate accounts pass through four stages:

1. **admissibility:** internal coherence and compatibility with the rest of the ontology;
2. **discrimination:** performance on cases where candidates diverge;
3. **working preference:** the most satisfactory surviving account directs subsequent development;
4. **residual underdetermination:** multiple realizations remain only when the available discriminators do not decide among them.

The immediate discriminators are phenomenal suffering, animals and children, manipulation, evaluative error, sacrifice, posthumous commitments, future subjects, divine agency, and eventually evil and divine goodness. A retained alternative must identify what future argument or case could distinguish it from the preferred model. Mere logical consistency is insufficient for equal status.

## Conscious capacities form a profile, not one ladder

“Level of consciousness” can be useful informally, but one scalar would conflate several capacities. Use the profile:

\[
CProf(A,t)=\langle PCon,Access,Self,Temporal,Source,Norm\rangle_{A,t},
\]

where the components concern:

- phenomenal experience and valence;
- flexible access for reasoning and action;
- representation of oneself as a subject;
- representation of oneself across time;
- conscious participation in originating agency;
- competence to understand, construct, negotiate, and answer to norms.

Animals, infants, children, adults, and cognitively impaired humans may instantiate these dimensions in different combinations and degrees. These categories are heterogeneous, developmental, and context-sensitive; they are not themselves capacity values.

The profile supports different role thresholds:

| Role | Initial sufficient or required basis |
| --- | --- |
| phenomenal welfare bearer | capacity for valenced experience is the present sufficient anchor |
| bearer of projects and diachronic interests | some temporal self-representation, memory, anticipation, or stable relation is additionally relevant |
| autonomous norm constructor | practical access, a degree of self-governance, and normative or social competence are additionally required |
| responsible moral agent | sufficient understanding, control, epistemic opportunity, and sourcehood are additionally required |

These thresholds prevent the capacity to suffer from being confused with the capacity to legislate or bear responsibility. Differences in construction or responsibility do not directly settle degrees of moral status. In particular, lower reflective agency supplies no inference that a subject's pain matters less. The grounding and possible equality or gradation of moral status remain a separate F2 problem.

## Diachronic and transpersonal value

Sacrifice and priority must include beneficiaries beyond the acting agent and a horizon beyond that agent's life:

\[
Sacrifice(A,s,c,g,B,C,\tau),
\]

\[
DP(g,p;A,B,C,\tau)
\Rightarrow
g\triangleright_{A,B,C,\tau}p.
\]

$B$ may contain $A$, contemporaries, later subjects, or a mixture; $\tau$ may extend past $A$'s death. The expanded condition retains sincere orientation, non-culpable attention to accessible reasons, non-manipulation, causal suitability, temporal robustness, proportionality, and undefeated effects on affected subjects. S28 clarifies that information is a defeasible input rather than a universal threshold.

This creates an important typing distinction. A costly act may:

- be prudentially good or bad for $A$;
- realize $A$'s autonomous commitment or fidelity;
- benefit members of $B$;
- preserve a relationship, institution, body of knowledge, ecosystem, or project whose value outlasts $A$.

A posthumous effect need not improve $A$'s later phenomenal welfare, because there may be no later subject-stage of $A$ to benefit. Its justification can instead be commitment-relative, relational, moral, or good-for later subjects. This is exactly why self-preservation cannot exhaust the ground of normativity.

At the time of choice, future beneficiaries may not yet exist or be individually identifiable. The temporal agent can deliberate over structural descriptions, probability distributions, or classes of possible beneficiaries without claiming pre-actual truths about which libertarian choices those future agents would make. In the complete E+ history, whichever later subjects actually exist and whichever effects obtain are included in ACO. Internal deliberative uncertainty and atemporal actuality remain distinct.

Future location alone neither discounts a subject's interests nor makes them lexically dominate present interests. Uncertainty, causal attenuation, rights, distribution, agency, replaceability, and non-identity effects require separate treatment. The present extension licenses concern beyond one's life; it does not adopt maximizing longtermism.

## Adversarial results

| Objection | Present result |
| --- | --- |
| If norms arise from survival pressure, $G$ has no norms | Does not follow. The premise explains one construction pressure, while $G$ may have other evaluative reasons or an objective standpoint. |
| If there is objective good, it must be another thing above $G$ | Does not follow. Objectivity fixes truth or correctness independently of actual endorsement; its metaphysical realization remains open. |
| “Closer to good” presupposes the answer | Partly. Every proposed indicator needs justification and can be contested; this is why approximation is a partial epistemic relation rather than a metric or proof. |
| Adults have more consciousness, so their welfare matters more | Rejected inference. Capacity profiles affect the kinds of interests, authorship, and responsibility involved; moral status requires an additional argument. |
| A sacrifice after death cannot be good for the agent | Often correct prudentially, but incomplete: it may express the agent's commitment or benefit contemporary and future subjects. |
| Future people do not yet exist, so they cannot matter | Their identity and existence create genuine problems for person-affecting theories, but current choices can still shape whether and how later conscious lives occur. |
| Concern for the far future swamps every present good | Rejected. Temporal extension supplies standing, not automatic priority or a scalar maximization rule. |

## Decisions

| Decision | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distinguish capacity, practical pressure, construction, and endorsement | accepted | Finite need cannot be inferred from agency alone, and capacity does not entail exercise |
| Use finite vulnerability and self-preservation as a partial explanation of constructed norms | provisional | It explains practical genesis but cannot ground other-regarding or self-consuming sacrifice by itself |
| Treat objective good as a fallibly approximable regulative standard | preferred direction | It preserves error, criticism, learning, and concern beyond actual endorsement without requiring perfect access |
| Prefer an objective-anchor hybrid at the functional level | preferred | It combines non-constructed welfare anchors with historically constructed personal, moral, and institutional specifications |
| Decide the ultimate metaphysical ground of objective anchors now | open | Divine nature, necessary relational structure, and divine-source fit need a later discriminator rather than premature verbal selection |
| Retain all coherent candidates as equally active | rejected | Coherence is an admissibility threshold; working preference follows comparative performance |
| Replace a single consciousness scale with a capacity profile | accepted | Welfare, norm construction, and responsibility draw on different combinations of capacities |
| Infer moral status directly from reflective or agentic level | rejected | The value of suffering and welfare is not identical to normative competence |
| Extend sacrifice and diachronic priority to beneficiary sets and posthumous horizons | accepted correction | Long-term concern can be contemporaneous, intergenerational, and later than the agent's life |
| Give future interests automatic lexical priority | rejected | Temporal location alone supplies neither discount nor dominance |

## Literature and novelty notes

- The 2026 [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on moral realism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-realism/) separates the minimal claim that some moral claims are true from stronger claims about mind-independence and from any one substantive moral theory.
- The [entry on moral epistemology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-epistemology/) treats disagreement, distortion, evidential method, and the possibility of moral knowledge; disagreement alone does not decide the ontological question.
- The [entry on animal consciousness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consciousness-animal/) emphasizes both multidimensional conceptual disputes and uncertainty about the distribution of conscious capacities. The [entry on animal moral status](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/) cautions against sharp human/non-human boundaries based on supposedly unique capacities.
- The 2026 [entry on intergenerational justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-intergenerational/) frames obligations to later people, limited future knowledge, and the non-identity problem.

Objective realism, fallible moral knowledge, capacity-based moral-status debates, intergenerational concern, and realist–constructivist hybrids are established. The project-specific contribution remains their typed integration with ACO, E+, finite normative pressure, conscious sourcehood, and extended sacrifice. No novelty claim is made.
