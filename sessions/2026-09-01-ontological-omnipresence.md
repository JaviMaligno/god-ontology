# Session: Ontological omnipresence

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Session ID:** S15
**Primary question:** What positive relation makes a non-spatial $G$ present throughout creation?
**Dependencies:** C11–C12, C71–C75, C79–C86, C88–C89, S11–S14
**Target depth:** F1 definition, actual and modal scope, and edge cases

## Dependency note

The open question of how a non-material substance grounds material substances does not block this pass. Omnipresence requires non-containment by created space and immediate relations to its contents. It does not require a completed account of whether production crosses substance categories.

## Three dimensions of immediate presence

Let $D(\mathcal C^*)$ contain every actual entity, event, relation, and region admitted by the ontology of the actual created order. Define:

\[
Present_O(G,x)
\quad\text{iff}\quad
GroundReach(G,x)
\land
EpistemicAccess(G,x)
\land
CausalAvailability(G,x).
\]

| Dimension | Content | Existing basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grounding reach | $x$, or the arena and powers enabling $x$, depends existentially on $G$ | existential sovereignty and asymmetric co-production |
| Epistemic access | $G$ knows the complete actuality of $x$ without created signals, sensory reception, or inference | ACO and exhaustive epistemic access |
| Causal availability | Every coherent direct divine action concerning $x$ lies within $G$'s power without a necessary created intermediary | qualified omnipotence and direct divine contribution |

Grounding reach is typed. For an agent-originated act, $G$ grounds the agent, power, and arena without becoming the contrastive author of the act's content.

Causal availability is also a capacity. It does not imply that $G$ acts, intervenes, or repeatedly maintains an entity at every created time.

## Why use the conjunction?

Knowledge alone could describe exhaustive remote observation. Power alone could be mediated or analogous to a distant ruler's authority. Grounding alone could belong to an impersonal foundation. Their conjunction expresses one immediate ontological, epistemic, and agentive relation using attributes already developed by this project.

The definition therefore adds a mode of integration rather than a new unexplained power.

## Actual omnipresence

\[
OP_A(G)
\quad\text{iff}\quad
\forall x\in D(\mathcal C^*)\;Present_O(G,x).
\]

$OP_A$ is preferred conditional on ACO, existential sovereignty, qualified maximal power, uniqueness, and non-spatiality. It does not distribute spatial parts of $G$ across regions. Under E+, the relation holds atemporally to the entities and events throughout the complete history rather than being reacquired at successive times.

## Empty regions

The definition remains neutral between substantival and relational accounts of space:

- if an unoccupied region exists as a spatial entity or structural relatum, it belongs to $D(\mathcal C^*)$;
- if “empty region” only denotes the absence of occupants within a relational structure, there is no extra entity to include;
- in either case, $G$ knows all actual spatial facts and can act directly with respect to the relevant structure.

Omnipresence therefore does not depend on reifying every coordinate as an object.

## Modal scope

Actual omnipresence concerns what exists. A distinct modal capacity is:

\[
OP_M(G)
\quad\text{iff}\quad
\Box\forall\mathcal C
\left(
Actualizes(G,\mathcal C)
\rightarrow
\forall x\in D(\mathcal C)\;Present_O(G,x)
\right).
\]

This says that the presence architecture accompanies every possible actualization by $G$. It does not make $G$ presently related to non-actual entities. MKO knows their possibility; ontological presence relates to whatever is actual in the relevant pointed model.

## Personal presence

Ontological presence does not yet entail universal second-person relation. Personal availability might additionally require that $G$ can attend and respond to any conscious agent as a subject. Exercised mutual or shared attention also depends on the creature and on divine willingness.

The capacity may later be supported by divine personhood, while its universal exercise may involve providence and goodness. It remains outside minimal $OP_A$ so those later conclusions are not imported.

## Literature comparison

The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on omnipresence](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/omnipresence/) records the established knowledge, power, and cause-of-being family of accounts, literal occupation alternatives, the world-as-body proposal, and stronger personal-presence models. [Joseph Jedwab, “God's Omnipresence”](https://doi.org/10.24204/ejpr.v8i2.61) defends a classical account, while [Eleonore Stump's treatment of union and presence](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199277421.001.0001) motivates the distinction between general omnipresence and significant personal presence.

The project's formulation is a local synthesis of its independently developed typed grounding, ACO, direct intervention capacity, non-continuous dependence, and E+ semantics. It is not presented as a new general theory of presence.

## Later boundary refinement in S31

[S31](2026-09-02-transcendence-immanence-and-ex-deo.md) names $OP_A$'s universal profile **relational immanence** and combines it with **constitutive transcendence**. Immediate grounding, epistemic access, causal availability, and intervention require neither spatial entry nor shared divine–created substance. Omnipresence nevertheless does not by itself exclude a separately posited overlap model; creator–creation constitutive disjointness carries that additional preferred condition.
