# Session: Ontological production and material continuity

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Session ID:** S17
**Primary question:** How can a non-material $G$ produce or ground a material created domain?
**Dependencies:** C04–C07, C55–C60, C68, C73, C84–C89, C95–C97, S14, S16
**Target depth:** F1 comparison with one load-bearing formal distinction

## Starting position

Physical immateriality remains preferred, but it cannot be defended merely by repeating that $G$ grounds matter. The project needs a positive relation and a fair comparison with material continuity.

The strongest initial material-continuity proposal says that $G$ contains uncreated matter of the same type as created matter while the two are numerically distinct. Analysis shows that this formulation divides into two importantly different models:

1. the instances merely share a material type, without any divine material token constituting the created token;
2. divine material substance is transferred, divided, modified, or expressed as creation.

Only the second supplies literal material continuity. It is therefore a creation *ex deo* model rather than merely a same-type model.

## Definitions

Let $\mathcal C_0$ be the created ontological basis: the instantiated spacetime, material domain, structural constraints, and basic created powers from which ordinary secondary developments proceed.

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Material transformation | Production in which a prior material patient or input persists through, or is transformed into, the effect |
| Type continuity | Producer and product instantiate the same qualitative material kind without sharing a numerically identical material constituent |
| Token continuity | Some numerically identical substrate or constituent of the producer becomes, constitutes, or persists in the product |
| Originative existential causation (OEC) | A causal exercise whose immediate terminus is the existence of a numerically distinct entity or domain, rather than a changed state of a prior material patient |
| Principle of Material Causality (PMC) | Every concrete object with an originating or sustaining efficient cause also has a corresponding originating or sustaining material cause |

## Preferred F1 account

Creation has three typed explanatory dimensions:

1. **specification:** $S$, known within MKO and selected in the creative act, supplies determinate structural content;
2. **origination:** $G$ exercises an agentive power whose terminus is the existence of $\mathcal C_0$;
3. **dependence:** $\mathcal C_0$ is existentially dependent on that exercise while remaining numerically and substantially distinct from $G$.

The central agentic distinction is:

\[
C_G^-\not\Rightarrow_{\mathrm{causal}} Act_G(\mathcal C_0),
\]

while:

\[
Act_G(\mathcal C_0)
\Rightarrow_{\mathrm{causal}}
Exists(\mathcal C_0)\land Instantiates(\mathcal C_0,S).
\]

The antecedent state and reasons do not sufficiently settle which creative act $G$ performs. Once the contentful act occurs, however, it may sufficiently cause its selected effect. Freedom of the cause does not require indeterminacy between the act and its immediate terminus.

A provisional definition is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
OEC(G,\mathcal C_0,S)\quad\text{iff}\quad&
Originates(G,Act_G(\mathcal C_0))\\
&\land Specifies(S,\mathcal C_0)\\
&\land \neg\exists m\,MaterialInput(m,\mathcal C_0)\\
&\land Distinct(G,\mathcal C_0)\\
&\land GroundsExistence(G,\mathcal C_0).
\end{aligned}
\]

`GroundsExistence}` describes the asymmetric dependence of the obtained effect; OEC adds the source, exercise, structural content, and absence of a transformed patient. It is not a second event layered on top of creation.

There is also no temporal transition from a state containing “nothing” to a state containing creation. Relative to E+, the single creative exercise and its complete effect stand in an atemporal explanatory relation. Created temporal processes begin within $\mathcal C_0$.

## Comparative assessment

| Model | Real material continuity | Preserves ultimacy of $G$ | Preserves strong creator–creation distinction | Fits non-spatiality | Main burden |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OEC without material input | no | yes | yes | yes | Defend the coherence of existential causation without a material patient |
| Same material type, distinct tokens | no | possibly | yes | difficult if “matter” retains physical or spatial content | Adds divine matter but still must explain the new token |
| Creation from divine substance (*ex deo*) | yes | possibly | weakened and theory-dependent | difficult but not formally impossible under a non-physical notion of substrate | Divine partition, modification, overlap, or expression; contingency and identity of the effect |
| Independent uncreated substrate | yes | no, unless ultimacy is reassigned to a composite basis | yes | $G$ may remain non-spatial but is not the whole ground | Explains neither the substrate nor its coordination with $G$ |

### Why type continuity is dominated

Sharing a type is not a production relation. Two fires can be of the same type without either providing the fuel of the other. If no constituent of $G$ becomes a constituent of $\mathcal C_0$, the proposed model still lacks a material cause in the relevant sense and must posit a power to produce a new material instance. It therefore retains the OEC burden while adding divine materiality.

If a constituent of $G$ does become or constitute created matter, the model moves to token continuity. This answers the PMC objection directly, but the created world is then made from divine substance. The model must explain whether $G$ loses parts, acquires a new modification, contains creation as a proper part, or remains wholly present in a derivative expression. These are viable pantheist, panentheist, emanationist, or material-monist directions, but they are not small amendments to the present ontology.

### The PMC objection

Felipe Leon formulates the contemporary objection directly: concrete things with efficient originating or sustaining causes also have material causes; classical creation affirms an efficiently caused universe without one.

The project does not presently accept PMC as a universal modal principle. Its ordinary support comes from transformations inside an already material causal order. Extending it to the origin or ontological dependence of that whole order may be correct, but it is not licensed merely by those intraworld cases. Nor may the project answer by appealing circularly to omnipotence or by defining creation as possible.

PMC therefore remains a substantive defeater condition:

\[
\Box PMC
\Rightarrow
\neg\Diamond OEC(G,\mathcal C_0,S).
\]

At F1, no contradiction in OEC has been identified independently of PMC. This is a coherence result and an abductive preference, not a demonstration that ex nihilo creation is possible.

## Creation, dependence, and secondary causation

The preferred account concerns the ontological basis $\mathcal C_0$, not the direct production of every later entity or event. Created substances and agents can contribute genuinely within the resulting order:

\[
OEC(G,\mathcal C_0,S)
\not\Rightarrow
\forall e\in H^*\,Originates(G,e).
\]

This preserves asymmetric co-production and avoids occasionalism. It also fits C72: one atemporal creative exercise can ground the complete created arena without a sequence of repeated maintenance interventions. Whether later persistence requires a stronger conservation relation remains separable.

## Decisions

| Decision | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distinguish material transformation from existential production | accepted | A material patient belongs analytically to the former, not automatically to every possible kind of production |
| Prefer OEC as the F1 account of creation | preferred | It preserves ultimacy, non-spatiality, contingency, creator–creation distinction, and secondary causation |
| Treat same-type but token-distinct divine matter as an explanatory improvement | rejected | Type resemblance supplies no material input or productive relation |
| Retain creation from divine substance | open but disfavored | It genuinely answers the continuity demand but revises identity, composition, and transcendence commitments |
| Accept PMC as universally necessary | open | It is the strongest live objection, but its modal scope is not established by ordinary material transformations |
| Identify OEC with grounding alone | rejected | Grounding without source, exercise, content, and effect conditions would merely rename the burden |

## Literature and novelty notes

- [Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I.44.2](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1044.htm#article2) distinguishes particular material transformation from the production of beings as beings and applies the latter even to primary matter.
- [Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I.45.1](https://inters.org/Summa-Theologiae-Pars-I-q-45) treats creation as production without a material cause and not as transformation from “nothing” construed as a substrate.
- [Felipe Leon, “The Problem of Creation Ex Nihilo”](https://philpapers.org/rec/FELTPO-20) gives PMC a recent explicit formulation against classical creation.
- The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on creation and conservation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/creation-conservation/) distinguishes the same-action thesis, sole-source dependence, and occasionalism. This supports keeping global dependence separate from divine authorship of every event.
- [Daniel Soars, “Creation in Aquinas: ex nihilo or ex deo?”](https://doi.org/10.1111/nbfr.12603) shows that the border between emanation, dependence, and creation from God remains contested even within interpretation of Aquinas.
- Recent [grounding-panentheist work](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034412525100978) illustrates that treating divine grounding as ontological overlap is a substantive additional theory, not a neutral consequence of the word “grounding.”

The project-specific contribution is presently organizational: it combines OEC with $S$-specification, unified agentic settling, E+, non-continuous global dependence, and asymmetric co-production. No novelty claim is made for creation without material input.
