# Session: Ontological priority and epistemic boundaries

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Session ID:** S26
**Primary question:** How deep should the project go into ethics, and what authority should empirical science have over a metaphysical ontology?
**Dependencies:** C01–C03, C127–C177, S21–S25
**Target depth:** Methodological F1 and a stopping rule for the evaluative branch

## Authorial correction

Ontology is the project's primary aim. Ethics can eventually be developed further, but it should presently be pursued only far enough to support several ontologically relevant questions:

- what good and evil minimally mean;
- how sacrifice can be intelligible and sometimes justified;
- how value can extend beyond one individual, including survival of the species;
- how evaluative reality relates to $G$;
- how those notions bear on creation, intervention, providence, and suffering.

Detailed normative theory becomes optional after this interface is adequate. Moral-status taxonomies, population ethics, aggregation, longtermism, political or institutional ethics, and extensive applied cases should not direct the main sequence unless they expose a contradiction or become indispensable to one of the listed questions.

Science is informative because the ontology concerns a universe that includes the physical order studied by science. It is not automatically definitional or blocking. Scientific inquiry is a fallible, model-mediated route to knowledge pursued by subjects located within the created physical world; the project also asks about that world's metaphysical ground, modal structure, and possible reality beyond the range of physical measurement.

## An axiological interface rather than a complete ethics

Call the required module the **minimal axiological interface**. Its job is to connect the ontology to evaluative claims without expanding into a complete moral system.

The interface is adequate when it can do five things:

1. identify at least some objective anchors for benefit, harm, good, and evil;
2. distinguish good-for a subject, agentic excellence, moral or relational value, and the evaluation of $G$;
3. explain why some sacrifice and concern beyond the individual can be fitting;
4. represent species survival and transgenerational continuity without making biological persistence an unconditional supreme value;
5. state how value is grounded by, dependent on, recognized by, instituted by, or applicable to $G$.

This does not require a complete ordering of every good, a decision procedure for every conflict, or a comprehensive account of moral status and population ethics. Formal detail is warranted only where the divine or general ontology depends on it.

## Species survival as a boundary case

The survival of a species extends the evaluative horizon beyond any present individual. It cannot be reduced to the later prudential welfare of the deciding agent. Its candidate value can arise through several connected bearers and goods:

- the lives and welfare of future conscious subjects;
- continuation of agentive capacities and relations;
- preservation of cultures, knowledge, projects, and forms of life;
- maintenance of the conditions under which new subjects and goods can exist.

The species need not itself be a conscious super-subject for its continuation to matter. The value may be relational, transpersonal, and opportunity-preserving. Conversely, mere replication of a biological lineage is not automatically good regardless of the quality of lives, coercion, catastrophic cost, or destruction of other bearers. Species survival is therefore a required adequacy case, not a lexical axiom.

This case is particularly useful to the ontology because it demonstrates that self-preservation cannot exhaust normativity. An agent may sacrifice its own survival for descendants, other agents, or the continuation of the conditions of agency itself.

## Epistemic roles of science

The project distinguishes four roles:

| Role | Legitimate use | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| empirical input | report observed regularities, structures, organisms, cognition, and causal behavior | observations arrive through theories, instruments, and finite perspectives |
| realization constraint | constrain claims about how a metaphysical power or subject is physically realized in the actual universe | a constraint on actual physical realization need not define the metaphysical kind |
| compatibility test | reject a model that entails an empirical claim robustly contradicted by evidence | the contradiction and the bridge from metaphysics to the empirical prediction must both be shown |
| analogical or abductive clue | inform metaphysical comparison through order, intelligibility, emergence, causation, or consciousness | scientific success does not select one metaphysical interpretation by itself |

The key relation is underdetermination rather than independence:

\[
EmpiricalModel(E) + Bridge(O,E) \Rightarrow Constraint(O).
\]

A scientific result constrains ontology when an explicit bridge shows that the ontology entails an empirical consequence. Without that bridge, absence from a physical model is not evidence that a metaphysical category is meaningless or impossible. With a valid bridge and robust contrary evidence, the ontology cannot protect itself merely by labeling the disputed claim metaphysical.

Science therefore has defeasible evidential authority within its domain. It is neither the definition of reality nor irrelevant to it.

## Three boundaries

1. **Semantic boundary:** scientific operational definitions serve scientific inquiry; they do not automatically fix the meanings of cause, substance, possibility, agency, consciousness, good, or God in the ontology.
2. **Domain boundary:** empirical science primarily accesses the created order from within it. Claims about the ground of that order may have physical implications, but they need not be physical hypotheses themselves.
3. **Defeater boundary:** a scientific finding becomes a metaphysical defeater only through a demonstrated conflict with a commitment or its consequences. Mere non-detection, current explanatory completeness, or methodological exclusion is not enough.

These boundaries preserve the original motivation of the project: rich scientific description can explain phenomena without exhausting the question of why the fundamental order exists or has its structure. They also prevent metaphysics from becoming insulated from everything learned about the actual universe.

## Revised depth and stopping rule

The evaluative branch remains at F1, with local F2 only at the five interfaces above. It stops being part of the active spine once the project can state a coherent minimal account of:

\[
Good, Evil, Sacrifice, TranspersonalContinuity, NormBridge_G.
\]

Further ethical development moves to the optional backlog unless:

- an ontological commitment depends on it;
- a challenge reveals a contradiction in creation, divine attributes, agency, intervention, or providence;
- the author explicitly chooses to deepen it.

After this closure pass, priority returns to the ontological trunk and its remaining load-bearing gaps, especially the ground of objective fit only insofar as it bears on $G$, the OEC/PMC production challenge, and the blocker analysis for qualified omnipotence.

## Decisions

| Decision | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Give ontology priority over ethics | accepted governing priority | The intended work is a metaphysical ontology, not a comprehensive ethical theory |
| Develop a minimal axiological interface | accepted | Good, evil, sacrifice, species survival, and relation to $G$ are required for the ontology to address divine action and suffering |
| Treat detailed ethics as part of the active spine | rejected | It becomes optional once the interface is adequate unless a load-bearing dependency reopens it |
| Use species survival as a required transpersonal adequacy case | accepted | It extends value beyond individual self-preservation and connects present action to future agents and conditions of agency |
| Treat biological continuation as lexically supreme | rejected | Persistence alone does not settle quality, coercion, cost, or conflicts with other value bearers |
| Treat science as informative and defeasibly constraining | accepted | The ontology includes the actual physical universe and must remain answerable to evidence where it has empirical consequences |
| Let scientific categories define metaphysical categories automatically | rejected | Operational success and metaphysical exhaustiveness are different claims |
| Let current non-detection or methodological exclusion veto a metaphysical claim | rejected | A genuine defeater requires an explicit bridge to a conflicting empirical consequence |
| Ignore robust scientific conflict by calling a claim metaphysical | rejected | Metaphysics cannot evade evidence when it actually entails the contradicted physical claim |
