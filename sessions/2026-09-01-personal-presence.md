# Session: Personal presence beyond ontological omnipresence

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Session ID:** S20
**Primary question:** What personal relation to created subjects follows before divine goodness is developed?
**Dependencies:** C55–C67, C74–C76, C90–C94, C119–C120, S07, S15, S19
**Target depth:** F1 distinction and derivation

## Starting position

Ontological omnipresence already places every actual relatum within $G$'s grounding reach, epistemic access, and direct causal availability. For a conscious created subject, this supports more than impersonal coverage, but it does not by itself establish actual attention, reciprocity, love, or communion.

The preferred F1 result is **universal personal availability**: $G$ can relate directly and intentionally to every actual conscious subject as a subject. Stronger exercised relations divide among providence, creaturely co-production, and the later goodness branch.

## A tiered model

| Tier | Relation | Present assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P0 | Ontological presence to any created relatum | already preferred under $OP_A$ |
| P1 | Direct personal availability to a conscious subject | preferred conditionally in this session |
| P2 | Exercised divine regard, address, or response | contingent act within providence |
| P3 | Reciprocal or shared attention | co-produced relation requiring creaturely participation |
| P4 | Stable communion, love, or union | deferred to goodness and fuller personhood |

The tiers are cumulative only in the relevant case. P0 applies to non-agents; P1–P4 require a subject capable of the corresponding relation.

## Minimal personal availability

Let $Subject(A,t)$ mean that $A$ is a conscious subject at created time $t$. Define:

\[
Avail_P(G,A,t)
\]

as the conjunction of:

1. $G$ identifies $A$ as the bearer of an actual perspective and conscious states;
2. $G$ is cognitively accessible to any actual address by $A$ without sensory reception or inference;
3. $G$ can intentionally address or respond to $A$ without a necessary created intermediary;
4. neither spatial distance nor temporal succession in $G$ is required for that relation.

Universal personal availability is:

\[
UPA(G)\quad\text{iff}\quad
\forall A\forall t
[Subject(A,t)\rightarrow Avail_P(G,A,t)].
\]

The support is cumulative:

- minimal divine agency supplies subjectivity, intellect, will, and intentional action;
- ACO supplies exhaustive access to actual truths about $A$, including actual attempts to address $G$;
- ontological presence supplies direct rather than spatially mediated epistemic and causal reach;
- qualified omnipotence provisionally includes every coherent direct act of communication or response available to $G$.

$UPA(G)$ is therefore preferred conditional on the existing attribute package. It is stronger than impersonal omnipresence but weaker than a claim that $G$ actually enters a reciprocal relation with every subject.

Availability is relative to the recipient's coherent capacities. It does not require linguistic address, identical manifestation, or reciprocal awareness from an infant, non-human animal, impaired subject, or other limited consciousness. $G$ can stand in a subject-directed relation appropriate to $A$ even where P3 shared attention is unavailable.

## The epistemic gap

ACO is presently formulated as knowledge of every actual truth. Personal relation raises a further question:

\[
PropositionalCompleteness(G,A)
\stackrel{?}{\Rightarrow}
KnowsAsSubject(G,A).
\]

The implication is not assumed. Knowing every proposition about a pain, intention, or perspective may still differ from phenomenal identity, acquaintance, empathy, or second-person knowledge. The current derivation needs only subject-sensitive individuation and accessibility for direct intentional relation. A stronger claim that $G$ experiences another subject's states from within, or possesses irreducible interpersonal knowledge, remains open and is added to the challenge register.

## Capacity and exercise

Preserve the distinction already used for power:

\[
Avail_P(G,A,t)\not\Rightarrow Exercises_P(G,A,t).
\]

Actual personal regard or response requires a contingent divine act or standing intention. Its mere possibility follows neither from goodness nor from universal exercise. When exercised, it belongs to the providential architecture and to $H^*$ rather than constituting another kind of omnipresence.

An actual creaturely address and divine response can stand in created causal order without temporal acquisition by $G$:

\[
Addresses(A,G,t)
\land RespondsTo(G,A,t')
\land PartOf(Addresses,Responds,H^*).
\]

ACO includes the events and relation together. This inherits C120's unresolved F2 question about exact atemporal responsiveness.

## Reciprocity and co-production

Shared attention minimally requires mutual awareness rather than unilateral divine cognition:

\[
SharedAtt(G,A,x,t)
\Rightarrow
Attends(G,x)
\land Attends(A,x,t)
\land MutualAwareness(G,A,t).
\]

The creature's awareness, attention, and willingness are not supplied merely by $UPA(G)$. If they are libertarian acts, G cannot both determine them sufficiently and preserve their token sourcehood. Significant reciprocal presence is therefore naturally co-produced: G can make relation available and contribute to it, while the creature can attend, respond, refuse, or remain unaware.

This result also distinguishes two absences:

- **ontological absence** is excluded by $OP_A$;
- **experienced or reciprocal absence** remains possible when personal availability is unmanifested, unrecognized, or unanswered.

The distinction will matter later for hiddenness, religious experience, and goodness, but those questions are not settled here.

## Adversarial tests

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Omniscience already entails every form of personal presence | Rejected; complete truth-knowledge does not automatically yield exercised regard, acquaintance, or reciprocity |
| Personal availability imports divine goodness | Rejected at P1; coherent personal capacity follows from agency and direct access, while benevolent exercise and communion remain evaluative |
| Atemporality prevents genuine response | Not established; actual condition and response can be related within $H^*$ without learning, though the exact grounding relation remains open |
| G's awareness alone guarantees shared attention | Rejected; mutual awareness includes a creaturely cognitive contribution |
| Universal personal availability forces every agent into relationship | Rejected; availability leaves exercise, recognition, and creaturely response unsettled |
| Every conscious subject must receive personal presence in the same form | Rejected; availability is indexed to the subject's receptive capacities, while reciprocity may be unavailable |
| Personal presence must be spatial or sensory | Rejected under created-space aspatiality; direct cognitive and causal relation need not use bodily co-location |

## Decisions

| Decision | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distinguish P0–P4 rather than use one undifferentiated personal-presence predicate | accepted | Separates capacity, exercise, reciprocity, and evaluative union |
| Prefer universal personal availability $UPA(G)$ | preferred conditional | Integrates divine agency, ACO, direct causal availability, and qualified power |
| Infer universal exercised personal attention | not inferred | Requires a further claim about divine will and possibly goodness |
| Treat shared attention as co-produced | preferred | Mutual awareness includes a creaturely contribution and may involve free attention or response |
| Reduce knowing a subject to ACO's current propositional form | open | Interpersonal acquaintance or phenomenal access may require a richer epistemology |
| Treat loving communion as part of minimal omnipresence | rejected | It introduces value, will, mutuality, and fuller personhood |

## Literature and novelty notes

- The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on omnipresence](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/omnipresence/) surveys the established analysis through knowledge, power, and cause of being. This supplies precedent for P0 rather than for the full P1–P4 sequence.
- [Eleonore Stump, “Omnipresence, Indwelling, and the Second-Personal”](https://doi.org/10.24204/ejpr.v5i4.204) distinguishes general omnipresence from presence with and to a person and develops joint attention as a stronger personal relation.
- Stump's chapter [“Union, Presence, and Omnipresence”](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199277421.003.0004) treats joint attention as necessary for significant personal presence.

The capacity/exercise/reciprocity/value partition is a project-specific integration of established distinctions with ACO, E+, qualified power, and agentic co-production. No novelty claim is made.
