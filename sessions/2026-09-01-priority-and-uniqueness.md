The project first aims to organize, formalize, and assess arguments already present in the original conversation. Divine unity belongs to that inherited material and is also conceptually prior to omnipresence.

It was stated there in the compressed form “necessary + simple → unique.” The next substantive pass should reconstruct the strongest version rather than merely repeat that slogan, using standard historical arguments where they fit this ontology.

## Two uniqueness theses

Let $\mathcal C$ denote the total contingent order:

\[
U_G\quad\text{iff}\quad
\exists!g\,UltimateGround(g,\mathcal C),
\]

\[
U_N\quad\text{iff}\quad
\exists!g\,NecessaryBeing(g).
\]

| Thesis | Content | Immediate role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $U_G$ | Exactly one being is the ultimate ground of the total contingent order | Fixes the subject of created-order attributes |
| $U_N$ | Exactly one necessary being exists simpliciter | Excludes every additional necessary reality |

$U_N$ is more globally exclusive, but neither formula by itself entails the other without bridging assumptions. The reconstruction must say which conclusion each argument supports and must not silently import full simplicity or perfection.

## Why unity precedes omnipresence

Omnipresence of one subject would require a form such as:

\[
\forall x\in\mathcal C\;PresentTo(g,x).
\]

By contrast:

\[
\forall x\in\mathcal C\;\exists g\;PresentTo(g,x)
\]

allows a plurality of locally limited beings whose coverage is collectively exhaustive. This is not the omnipresence of any individual. The project must therefore identify the relevant subject before choosing among spatial, causal, epistemic, and grounding accounts of presence.
