# Session: Spatiality and immateriality

**Date:** 2026-09-01
**Session ID:** S14
**Primary question:** What follows about $G$'s spatial and material mode from grounding created spacetime?
**Dependencies:** C04–C05, C68, C73, C80–C81, S10–S13
**Target depth:** F1 derivation, scope distinctions, and targeted precedent check

## Method correction

Historical arguments are comparison material, not a mandatory derivation path. This pass begins with the project's own commitments and consults the literature afterward. It does not use omnipresence, perfection, full simplicity, or unrestricted immutability as premises.

## Three levels

Let $ST_{\mathcal C}$ be created spacetime:

| Level | Claim | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Created-space aspatiality | $G$ is not circumscribed by or fundamentally constituted through a region of $ST_{\mathcal C}$ | preferred from grounding asymmetry |
| Physical immateriality | $G$ is not a body, field, or physical system constituted under $S_{\mathrm{phys}}$ | preferred relative to the created order |
| Absolute fundamental non-spatiality | No spatial order circumscribes or fundamentally extends $G$ | preferred abductively, not deduced |

These conclusions have different strengths and must not be collapsed into the single word “immaterial.”

## Created-space result

Define $CircumscribedBy(x,\Sigma)$ as the relation in which $x$'s basic mode of existence is spatially bounded, extended, or individuated by spatial order $\Sigma$. It includes a dependence condition:

\[
CircumscribedBy(x,\Sigma)
\;\Rightarrow\;
ModeDepends(x,\Sigma).
\]

Grounding asymmetry gives:

\[
UltimateGround(G,\mathcal C)
\land
ST_{\mathcal C}\subseteq\mathcal C
\;\Rightarrow\;
\neg ModeDepends(G,ST_{\mathcal C}),
\]

and therefore:

\[
\neg CircumscribedBy(G,ST_{\mathcal C}).
\]

If created spacetime constituted $G$'s extension, boundaries, or spatial identity, then the ultimate ground would depend in its basic mode of existence on part of its own contingent effect. This conflicts with grounding asymmetry and existential sovereignty.

The conclusion concerns constitutive or circumscriptive location. It allows derivative relations such as immediate knowledge of a spatial entity, power to act there, grounding of its existence, or personal availability to an agent there.

## Physical immateriality

Use a local rather than maximally general conception of matter:

\[
Material_{\mathcal C}(x)
\;\Rightarrow\;
CircumscribedBy(x,ST_{\mathcal C})
\land
ConstitutedUnder(x,S_{\mathrm{phys}}).
\]

Because $G$ grounds and actualizes both the physical structure and its spacetime:

\[
\neg Material_{\mathcal C}(G).
\]

This establishes that $G$ is not a created-order body, field, or physical system. It does not settle every historical definition of matter or prove that every possible concrete entity must be spatial.

## Absolute non-spatiality

The stronger thesis is:

\[
ASP_G\quad\text{iff}\quad
\neg\exists\Sigma\,CircumscribedBy(G,\Sigma).
\]

Grounding $ST_{\mathcal C}$ alone does not entail $ASP_G$. The alternatives remain intelligible:

| Alternative | Coherence | Cost in this architecture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Uncreated external space | coherent description | Adds a fundamental arena that either conditions $G$ or exists without explanatory work |
| Higher-dimensional divine arena | coherent description | Relocates rather than resolves the spatial question and adds dimensions or topology |
| Intrinsic divine geometry | coherent description | Adds primitive extension, metric, or part-like relations to $G$ |
| Ubiquitous occupation of created space | developed in recent literature | Makes presence a location relation and reopens materiality, distribution, and mereology questions |

None is contradictory. None is needed for reason-guided agency, ACO, actualization, grounding, or intervention. $ASP_G$ is therefore preferred abductively for economy and explanatory fit.

## What does not follow

Non-spatiality does not make $G$ structureless. The model still distinguishes subjecthood, knowledge, reasons, will, power, and modal capacities. Whether those distinctions are identities, aspects, powers, or real metaphysical constituents belongs to the optional simplicity module.

Atemporality supports the fit of $ASP_G$ but is not sufficient by itself. An atemporal entity could be modeled as occupying an entire four-dimensional region. The grounding and dependence analysis performs the main work.

## Literature comparison

The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on omnipresence](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/omnipresence/) distinguishes bodily containment from presence through knowledge, power, causation, or alternative occupation relations. It also surveys higher-dimensional models and ubiquitous entension, confirming that aspatiality is a substantive choice rather than a trivial consequence of the word “God.”

[Ross Inman, “Omnipresence and the Location of the Immaterial”](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198806967.003.0008) develops a taxonomy of location models and examines ubiquitous entension together with its implications for materiality. [William Lane Craig, “God and Space”](https://doi.org/10.3390/rel15030276) directly compares divine aspatiality with spatial extension. These works are precedents for the distinction, not premises of the project argument.

## Reopened production question

The author identifies a burden left by physical immateriality: the model must explain how one substance can ground or produce a substance of a materially different kind. A same-matter model might reduce that discontinuity.

Four versions must be compared:

1. $G$ is made of matter belonging to the created physical order;
2. $G$ is constituted by uncreated matter of the same type as, but numerically distinct from, created matter;
3. $G$ and creation share a prior necessary material substrate;
4. created matter is a modification, expression, or part of $G$'s substance.

The first conflicts with $G$'s ultimacy over the total created order. The second is the strongest creator–creation version of the author's suggestion: it reduces the discontinuity between substance types while preserving numerical distinction, but still owes an account of producing new material instances and must reconcile divine matter with non-spatiality. The third makes the substrate more fundamental than $G$. The fourth is a coherent material-monist or panentheist alternative, but it changes creation from actualization of something distinct into transformation or articulation of divine substance and must recover contingency, agency, and non-spatiality.

Physical immateriality remains preferred, in line with the author's intuition, but the cross-category grounding relation is now an explicit open burden rather than a solved transition. This does not block omnipresence because that attribute depends on non-containment and immediate relations, not on a completed theory of substance production.
