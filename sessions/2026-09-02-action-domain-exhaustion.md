# Action-Domain Exhaustion and the Identity Handoff

**Session ID:** S44

**Date:** 2026-09-02

**Primary question:** Do the established created-order power ranges exhaust the divine task domain, and what remains before unrestricted qualified omnipotence can be assessed?

**Priority correction:** The recurring identity gap is promoted to the next trunk module at F2

**Target depth:** F2 classification and conditional created-domain closure; no premature promotion of unrestricted $OMP_Q$

## Outcome

The three established ranges exhaust **pure created-directed tasks** at F2, conditional on a created-action normalization principle. A task of that kind either originates a root created basis, settles an eligible created-order outcome without fixing a concrete past, or asks for an intervention relative to a fixed prefix and preservation profile. These are covered respectively by $CP_G^{elig}$, $CC_G^{elig}$, and $HI_G^{elig}$.

This closure does not yet establish unrestricted qualified omnipotence. Divine-task descriptions also include:

1. **self-executive acts**, such as selecting, intending, attending, addressing, or refraining;
2. alleged **self-reconstituting acts**, whose result would change the essential identity of $G$;
3. **mixed constitutive acts**, in which a created effect is claimed to overlap constitutively with $G$;
4. actions concerning a distinct non-created concrete relatum, if such a relatum is genuinely possible.

Self-reconstitution that destroys the independently established identity of $G$ does not describe an action of the same $G$. The current architecture already supports the self-executive acts it uses, especially selection, intention, and refraining from creation, but it has not established a universal range over every independently eligible self-executive type. Necessary abstract truths are constraints on task coherence rather than causal patients. A distinct non-created concrete relatum is absent from the preferred ontology but has not been shown impossible in every rival ontology.

The defensible result is therefore **ontology-relative qualified maximality** over the currently typed domain, not unrestricted $OMP_Q$. Identity is now load-bearing in deciding whether a proposed case is modification, persistence, replacement, numerical recreation, or loss of the original agent. It becomes the next trunk priority.

## Why task exhaustion needs a normal form

The existing definition is:

\[
OMP_Q(G)\leftrightarrow
\forall a[Task_G(a)\rightarrow StrongCan(G,a)].
\]

That quantifier cannot be assessed by accumulating examples. Every task must first receive a source, patient, result, and preservation profile:

\[
NF_G(a)=\langle s,p,q,\pi,\sigma\rangle,
\]

where:

- $s$ is the agent whose action is described;
- $p$ is the primary patient or domain, if any;
- $q$ is the contrastive result assigned to $s$;
- $\pi$ states what must remain fixed;
- $\sigma$ assigns any other constitutive source roles.

For $Task_G(a)$, $s=G$. The remaining fields prevent several grammatical descriptions from masquerading as one task. Creating an agent who freely chooses $b$, making that agent choose $b$, and supplying circumstances in which the agent may choose $b$ have different $q$ and $\sigma$. Likewise, modifying $x$, replacing $x$, and preserving numerical identity through a change have different $p$ and $\pi$.

## Exhaustion of pure created-directed tasks

Define:

\[
PureCreated_G(a):=
Task_G(a)\land CreatedDirected(a)
\land \neg MixedConstitution(a,G).
\]

`CreatedDirected` means that the divine contribution specified by $q$ terminates in a created bearer, structure, event, relation, law profile, or existential boundary. It does not require every constituent of the complete outcome to be strongly produced by $G$.

The created-action normalization principle is:

\[
CAN_G:\quad
PureCreated_G(a)\rightarrow
RootForm(a)\lor TypeOutcomeForm(a)\lor FixedPrefixForm(a).
\]

The forms are ordered by what the task holds fixed:

| Form | Characteristic question | Covering range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $RootForm(a)$ | Can $G$ originate this complete fundamental created basis? | $CP_G^{elig}$ |
| $TypeOutcomeForm(a)$ | Can $G$ directly settle this eligible kind of created outcome or boundary? | $CC_G^{elig}$ |
| $FixedPrefixForm(a)$ | Can $G$ do so while this concrete past, identity, law, and source profile is preserved? | $HI_G^{elig}$ |

These forms need not be extensionally disjoint. A fixed-prefix intervention also instantiates an operation type; the point is exhaustive normalization, not a partition into unrelated powers.

Under $CAN_G$ and the established range bridges:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&CP_G^{elig}\land CC_G^{elig}\land HI_G^{elig}\land CAN_G\\
&\qquad\rightarrow
\forall a[PureCreated_G(a)\rightarrow StrongCan(G,a)].
\end{aligned}
\]

### Coverage checks

- creating a complete root order has $RootForm$;
- producing a derivative entity or event has $TypeOutcomeForm$, or $FixedPrefixForm$ when its causal history is preserved;
- sustaining a created bearer is an identity-preserving temporal outcome;
- varying, masking, restoring, or removing a created power is a constitutive outcome;
- law replacement and local nomic exception are type or fixed-prefix outcomes with different preservation profiles;
- annihilation is a terminal-boundary outcome;
- divine communication is a fixed-prefix contribution whose creaturely uptake may remain autonomously sourced;
- created relations and collective structures are outcomes involving several relata rather than a new power category;
- mediated production through a creature is represented by the created causal structure and its source-role profile.

$CAN_G$ is an F2 analytic bridge. A defeater would need to exhibit an independently eligible created-directed action whose divine contribution cannot be represented as root origination, created-outcome settlement, or history-relative settlement. No such pure case has been identified. Strict constitutive *ex deo* does not refute $CAN_G$ because it is a mixed case by definition.

## Self-directed descriptions

Self-directed language divides into two importantly different families.

### Self-executive action

\[
SelfExec_G(a):=
Task_G(a)\land AgentOf(a,G)
\land ExerciseOf(a,Power_G)
\land Preserves(a,E_G^{ind}).
\]

Selection, intention, attention, address, and refraining can be acts of $G$ without making $G$ into a created patient or changing its essential constitution. The architecture already relies on:

- $G$ selecting a complete created specification through $AOR$;
- $G$ intentionally exercising $OEC^+$;
- the accessible no-creation option, which includes refraining from creation;
- $G$ intentionally performing an intervention in the relevant $HI_G$ witness;
- divine address or disclosure as the divine contribution to communication.

These are positive power data. They justify a typed range:

\[
SE_G^{est}:=
\{a:SelfExec_G(a)\land EstablishedExerciseType(a)\}.
\]

They do not justify:

\[
\forall a[SelfExec_G(a)\rightarrow StrongCan(G,a)]
\]

until the independently eligible self-executive types are themselves exhaustively characterized. This is a smaller residual gap than the earlier untyped domain, but it is real.

### Self-reconstitution

Let:

\[
SelfReconstitutes_G(a):=
Result(a,q)\land q\text{ alters a feature in }E_G^{ind}.
\]

Some such descriptions fail $Task_G$ because the requested result removes the numerical or essential agent whose action is being attributed. Examples include the same necessary $G$ making itself never exist, becoming an externally created being while retaining underivedness, or ceasing to be $G$ while the same $G$ later acts.

This exclusion is legitimate only where the relevant identity feature was established independently. It cannot be generalized into “every inability of $G$ is essential.” Identity therefore functions as a constraint on who performs the task, not as a convenient substitute for a range argument.

## States and relations are not automatically further actions

Some remaining descriptions name states or relations rather than exercises of power:

- $CreatorOf(G,C)$ obtains through the creation of $C$;
- $Grounds(G,C)$ obtains through the grounding relation already represented in the complete history;
- ACO's knowledge of actual contingent truth is actuality-dependent knowledge, not an additional act that produces the truth;
- being related to a possible specification through MKO is an epistemic relation, not creation of that specification.

When producing a relation requires a divine contribution—for example, addressing a creature—that contribution is classified under self-executive action and, as to its created terminus, under $HI_G$. The resulting relation may be co-produced if another autonomous source must respond.

This yields:

\[
StateOrRelation(r)\not\Rightarrow \exists a\,[Task_G(a)\land Produces(a,r)].
\]

The distinction prevents $OMP_Q$ from becoming a demand that $G$ strongly produce every truth about $G$.

## Abstract and modal relata

Logical, mathematical, modal, and identity truths enter $Coherent(a,N_L,N_M,N_G)$ and the constitutive-admissibility conditions. On the current account they are not concrete causal patients that resist $G$.

Consequently, “make a contradiction true” and “make $x$ numerically distinct from itself in the same respect” do not identify eligible exercises over a recalcitrant abstract object. Whether abstracta depend ontologically on $G$ remains a separate meta-modal question; either answer would need a further bridge before becoming a causal action type.

## Distinct non-created concrete relata

Suppose a distinct relatum $L$ satisfies:

\[
Concrete(L)\land \neg Created(L)\land L\neq G.
\]

If $L$ is genuinely possible and an action on $L$ passes $Task_G$, the created-order ranges do not cover it. Three outcomes are possible:

1. $L$ is dependent on $G$ in another sense and a broader constitutive range can be derived;
2. $L$ is co-fundamental but within $G$'s power;
3. $L$ imposes an independent range boundary, defeating sole divine ultimity or unrestricted $OMP_Q$.

The preferred sole-ground architecture contains no such $L$, but actual absence does not demonstrate modal impossibility. This is why closure relative to the present ontology is stronger than unrestricted closure across every rival ontology.

## The mixed *ex deo* case

A strict non-destructive *ex deo* proposal gives a created effect a divine constitutive contributor while $G$ remains the same underived subject. It is created-directed, yet its preservation conditions also concern divine constitution:

\[
MixedED_S(a):=
CreatedResult(a)\land DivineConstituent(a,G)
\land PreserveIdentity(a,G).
\]

It therefore cannot be classified by created outcome alone. If $MixedED_S$ is independently coherent, a complete identity and overlap account must show how the same $G$ can enter the new constitutive relation without partition, depletion, self-destruction, or multiplication of ultimate subjects. Only then can $Task_G(a)$ and its covering power range be assessed. $OMP_Q$ must not be used to prove the coherence that makes the task eligible.

## Ontology-relative maximality

Let $\mathcal O_*$ be the currently preferred ontology and let:

\[
Task_G^{\mathcal O_*}(a)
\]

mean that $a$ is a task licensed by its independently accepted kinds, identity conditions, source roles, and relata. Then:

\[
OMP_Q^{\mathcal O_*}(G):=
\forall a[Task_G^{\mathcal O_*}(a)\rightarrow StrongCan(G,a)].
\]

The present result supports the created-directed fragment and the established self-executive fragment. It does not yet establish the whole formula because the self-executive vocabulary is not closed and the mixed identity cases remain unresolved. More importantly:

\[
OMP_Q^{\mathcal O_*}(G)\not\Rightarrow OMP_Q(G)
\]

without a reason to think $\mathcal O_*$ contains every independently possible non-created relatum and action type.

Ontology-relative qualification is useful because every quantified modal claim depends on a background account of actions, individuals, and possibility. It is not allowed to trivialize omnipotence: unexplained failures within $\mathcal O_*$ remain counterexamples, and the rival-relatum question stays visible.

## Why identity is now the next trunk priority

Identity has recurred independently in:

1. the transworld sameness of $G$ in modal power witnesses;
2. essential constraints on $Task_G$;
3. persistence through bodily and power change;
4. the bearer of temporally separated conscious episodes;
5. the boundary between modification and replacement;
6. numerical recreation across an existence gap;
7. fission and duplicate cases;
8. strict *ex deo* and divine self-copy challenges.

This is sufficient dependency density to promote the issue. The next module should not begin with resurrection alone. It should first define a general identity framework and then specialize it:

\[
IdGround_K(x,t_1,t_2,c),
\]

where $K$ is the kind of entity and $c$ records continuity, constitution, origin, source, and gap conditions. The framework must distinguish:

- numerical identity from exact qualitative similarity;
- synchronic individuation from diachronic persistence;
- ordinary continuous persistence from intermittent existence;
- identity conditions from evidence or reidentification criteria;
- essential kind-preservation from preservation of every property;
- creaturely identity from the identity conditions of underived $G$.

The target is F2: enough to select a preferred account for embodied agents and determine whether $GapIdentity_C$ is available, while leaving a full general metaphysics of identity unnecessary unless the comparison exposes a deeper blocker.

## Adversarial tests

### Does created-target coverage already include every divine action?

No. Choosing whether to create is an exercise by $G$ without a prior created patient, and some mixed descriptions concern both created and divine constitution.

### Can every self-directed impossibility be removed through essence?

No. Only independently established identity and essence conditions restrict $Task_G$. An unexplained inability to perform an otherwise eligible self-executive action remains a power gap.

### Is knowledge of a truth another action that must be strongly produced?

Not as such. Knowledge may be a state or relation grounded by the truth and the knower's epistemic capacity. Attention, inquiry, or disclosure can be actions and must be typed separately.

### Do abstract necessities limit divine power?

They constrain which descriptions identify possible actions. They are not thereby agents or resistant causal patients. A theory on which abstracta are created or sustained would need to supply an action relation rather than infer one from necessity alone.

### Does the absence of another non-created concrete entity close that range?

Only relative to the current ontology. It does not prove that no rival coherent ontology contains such a relatum.

### Does identity merely postpone hard power cases?

It would if identity were defined by whatever $G$ cannot do. The next module must instead compare independently motivated identity grounds and apply them symmetrically to ordinary persistence, duplication, gaps, and divine cases.

## Literature and precedent

- [P. T. Geach, “Omnipotence”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031819100060381) distinguishes power over all things from ability to perform every describable act. The present created-directed/complete-task distinction is compatible with that warning but retains a broader qualified-action question.
- [Kenneth Pearce and Alexander Pruss, “Understanding Omnipotence”](https://writings.kennypearce.net/omnipotence.pdf) show why unrestricted quantification over action types and simple compatibility with an agent's nature each generate counterexamples. This supports independent task typing and the refusal to close self-executive range by definition.
- [Kenny Pearce, “Infinite Power and Finite Powers”](https://writings.kennypearce.net/infinitePower.pdf) emphasizes the unresolved range of “all” in all-powerful and distinguishes general power from an inventory of particular finite powers. The present normalization theorem remains a local range argument rather than a definition of infinite power.
- [Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 25](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1025.htm) treats contradictions and failure as lacking the relevant character of possible effects rather than as resistant things. The project uses the task-typing lesson without importing pure act, maximal perfection, or Aquinas's full derivation.

The created-action normal form and its integration with $CP_G^{elig}$, $CC_G^{elig}$, $HI_G^{elig}$, source-indexed agency, E+, and mixed *ex deo* are project-specific as a synthesis. No novelty claim is warranted before a broader comparison of action ontologies and contemporary accounts of divine power.
