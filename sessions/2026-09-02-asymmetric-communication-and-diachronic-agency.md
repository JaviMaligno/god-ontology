# Session: Asymmetric communication and diachronic agency

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S35
**Primary question:** Can cumulative control undermine meaningful agency while each token choice retains originable alternatives and creaturely sourcehood?
**Secondary clarification:** What symmetry, if any, is required for genuine communication between \(G\) and a much more limited created subject?
**Dependencies:** C08–C10, C33, C39–C46, C55–C60, C115–C126, C134–C139, C283–C293, CH03, CH15, S19, S20, S29, S34
**Target depth:** F2 proposal for the diachronic agency gap, with an accepted refinement of divine–creature communication

## Result in one sentence

Genuine divine–creature communication may be radically asymmetric in understanding and expressive power while remaining minimally reciprocal in participation; similarly, genuine token sourcehood may coexist with weakened diachronic authorship when another agent controls the developmental ecology, adaptively neutralizes deviation, and blocks effective routes of recognition, resistance, or self-revision.

## Author clarification: reciprocity without equal capacity

The author explicitly accepts \(ASA\) and prefers leaving the exact form of mutual interaction open. A relation between \(G\) and a finite subject need not display equal comprehension, expressive range, precision, immediacy, or communicative bandwidth.

The useful distinction is:

\[
Reciprocity\neq CapacitySymmetry.
\]

For an actual communicative episode, minimal reciprocity requires two real contributions:

1. one subject intentionally addresses or makes content available to the other;
2. the other subject achieves some genuine uptake and orientation or response.

It does not require:

\[
Grasp(A,G)=Grasp(G,A),
\]

or:

\[
CommCap(A)=CommCap(G).
\]

\(G\)'s acquaintance with \(A\) may be exhaustive and direct, while \(A\)'s cognition of \(G\) is partial, accommodated, analogical, uncertain, or mediated through created signs and experiences. A creaturely response can nevertheless be genuinely its own and genuinely directed toward \(G\).

This yields four levels:

| Level | Relation |
| --- | --- |
| divine address | \(G\) intentionally makes content or availability directed toward \(A\) |
| creaturely uptake | \(A\) receives enough of that address to orient toward its source or content |
| asymmetric communication | address and uptake occur, with unequal cognitive and expressive capacities |
| fuller communion | communication is joined by sustained recognition, volitional openness, and evaluative fit |

Whether every purported revelation, prayer, experience, or sign meets the uptake condition is a separate epistemic question. The ontology establishes a coherent relational type, not an empirical identification rule.

## The cumulative-agency problem

Token freedom is already represented by:

\[
FreeTok(A,a,t)\rightarrow
\left|
\mathcal O_A(C_A^-(t))
\right|\geq 2
\land Originates(A,a,t)
\land \neg PriorSufficientCause(C_A^-(t),a).
\]

Suppose this holds at every decision time. It still does not follow that the agent governs the development of its life. Another subject \(X\) could repeatedly alter information, salience, incentives, relationships, habits, opportunities, and recovery paths so that locally free deviations are continually redirected toward a target profile.

The result is not hidden determinism. Each token act may remain genuinely originated by \(A\). The possible deficit concerns a different bearer and temporal scale:

- token sourcehood concerns why this act rather than another occurs at \(t\);
- diachronic authorship concerns how far \(A\)'s own exercises help form, maintain, and revise the practical subject acting across interval \(\tau\).

## Dynamic context

Let the action-relevant context evolve as:

\[
C_A^-(t+1)=
F\bigl(
C_A^-(t),
a_t,
Sec_t,
I_t
\bigr),
\]

where \(a_t\) is \(A\)'s own act, \(Sec_t\) contains ordinary secondary causes, and \(I_t\) contains interventions. This equation assigns no deterministic reading to \(F\); it records which contributions shape the next context.

The diachronic agency ecology is a structured profile:

\[
AE(A,\tau)=
\langle
TS,OR,CP,EA,RR,SF,RC
\rangle_{A,\tau},
\]

where:

- \(TS\): persistence of token sourcehood;
- \(OR\): a usable range of originable options across time;
- \(CP\): relevant cognitive, affective, bodily, and executive capacities;
- \(EA\): sufficient epistemic access for recognizing action-relevant features, without requiring certainty or complete information;
- \(RR\): reflective and practical revisability of acquired motives, habits, and commitments;
- \(SF\): non-redundant self-formative contribution by the agent's own earlier acts;
- \(RC\): viable resistance, correction, or exit routes relative to external control.

These dimensions form a profile rather than a single score. Severe impairment in one dimension cannot automatically be compensated by abundance in another.

## No global self-creation requirement

No created subject authors its initial constitution, embodiment, powers, family, language, or starting environment. Requiring authorship of every cause of one's character would therefore make created autonomy impossible by definition and generate a regress.

The proposed condition begins only when the relevant capacities exist. A mature or developing agent must have genuine pathways through which its own acts can affect later governing states:

\[
\begin{aligned}
SelfForms(A,\tau)\rightarrow
\exists t_1,t_2,h_a,h_b\bigl[
&t_1<t_2\in\tau\\
&\land h_a,h_b\in Adm(C_A^-(t_1))\\
&\land Originates(A,a,t_1,h_a)\\
&\land Originates(A,b,t_1,h_b)\\
&\land GovState(A,t_2,h_a)\neq GovState(A,t_2,h_b)
\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\]

This is a modal comparison between histories containing different originable acts. It does not assert a pre-actual truth about which act \(A\) would select.

For children or temporarily impaired subjects, present agency may be limited while the ecology is prospectively agency-supporting. Protection, education, rehabilitation, and scaffolding can be assessed partly by whether they cultivate later capacities for sourcehood and revision.

## Capacity and historical achievement

The apparent fork between a possible revision route and actual self-formation should not be forced into one predicate. It separates three standings:

\[
\begin{aligned}
FreeTok(A,a,t)&:\text{ freedom of this settlement},\\
RobustCap(A,t)&:\text{ present capacity for continuing self-government},\\
DiaAuth(A,\tau)&:\text{ authorship actually realized across a history}.
\end{aligned}
\]

\(RobustCap\) requires more than a remote metaphysical possibility. At least one revision or resistance route must be actual, live for this agent, within its effective powers, and not systematically controlled or neutralized by the alleged controller:

\[
\begin{aligned}
RobustCap(A,t)\rightarrow\exists r\bigl[
&ActualRoute(r,A,t)\\
&\land PracticallyAccessible(r,A,t)\\
&\land CanOriginatePursuit(A,r,t)\\
&\land \neg CapturedRoute(X,r,A,t)
\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\]

\(DiaAuth\), by contrast, is historically realized only when actual earlier exercises contribute to later governing states. An instantaneously created mature agent may therefore possess \(FreeTok\) and \(RobustCap\) before it has accumulated \(DiaAuth\). A historically self-forming agent may retain substantial \(DiaAuth\) even after illness or confinement later damages \(RobustCap\).

This division also types manipulation more exactly:

- attempted capture concerns the controller's aim and interventions;
- capacity impairment concerns damage to live revision, resistance, or correction routes;
- realized diachronic capture concerns the extent to which actual self-formative contributions are neutralized or displaced;
- token override remains a separate sufficient settlement of a particular act.

The proposal dissolves the either/or question while preserving a substantive threshold: bare logical or metaphysical availability of an inaccessible escape does not establish robust capacity, and robust capacity without exercise does not manufacture a self-authored past.

## Influence, steering, capture, and override

Four levels should remain distinct.

| Level | Characterization | Effect on agency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| influence | changes reasons, evidence, salience, or opportunity while leaving uptake and response to \(A\) | compatible with and often necessary for agency |
| steering | intentionally arranges context to favor an outcome without settling it | burdens neutrality but need not impair authorship |
| governance capture | controls high-leverage developmental inputs, counteracts deviations, and restricts recognition, resistance, or revision | can weaken diachronic authorship while token acts remain free |
| option closure or source override | leaves one feasible option or sufficiently settles the token act | removes token freedom for that act |

Governance capture by \(X\) over interval \(\tau\) toward target profile \(q\) requires a conjunction such as:

\[
\begin{aligned}
Capture(X,A,\tau,q)\rightarrow{}&
Targets(X,A,q,\tau)\\
&\land ControlsHighLeverageInputs(X,A,\tau)\\
&\land AdaptivelyNeutralizesDeviation(X,A,q,\tau)\\
&\land RestrictsRevisionRoutes(X,A,\tau).
\end{aligned}
\]

No single conjunct is sufficient. Intent without relevant impact is attempted manipulation, not achieved capture. Similar impairment can also arise from addiction, disease, trauma, or oppressive systems without one controlling subject; that is agency degradation but not necessarily agential capture.

### Adaptive neutralization

The clearest cumulative pattern is not one irresistible command. It is an attractor maintained over time. When \(A\) freely deviates, \(X\) changes later inputs so that the practical system is pushed back toward \(q\). Across a sufficiently rich range of admissible deviations:

\[
Deviation_A(d)
\rightarrow
Counteracts_X(d,q),
\]

while no individual counteraction need determine \(A\)'s next choice. The life trajectory may thus be robustly controlled by \(X\) even though its constituent choices are not individually authored by \(X\).

This does not make control all-or-nothing. The breadth of controlled inputs, duration, opacity, adaptive responsiveness, and availability of independent correction paths all matter.

## Creation is not automatically manipulation

\(G\) necessarily supplies an initial created nature and arena; before the subject exists there is no prior creaturely practical standpoint to bypass or override. Initial constitution is therefore not governance capture merely because it strongly influences later action.

Two further claims remain possible:

1. a created subject can genuinely possess originating power it did not create;
2. an architecture can instantiate only thin token freedom if it systematically withholds the developmental conditions needed for robust use, understanding, and revision of that power.

Predictability is also insufficient for capture. In the present ontology, neither accurate knowledge nor high probability causes the act. Capture concerns causal control of the agent's developmental ecology and correction paths.

## Agency-supporting intervention

Intervention may improve the ecology by:

- restoring cognitive, bodily, or affective capacities;
- disclosing relevant truth without determining uptake;
- reopening options or escape routes;
- protecting the agent from another controller;
- enabling reflection, learning, repentance, or revision;
- preserving a stable arena in which choices can accumulate into projects and character.

The same physical event may support one dimension and burden another. Rescue can remove an immediate option while preserving the subject's future sourcehood. Education can shape values while increasing epistemic access and revisability. Evaluation therefore remains vectorial and context-sensitive.

## Consequence for providence

The S19 presumption should be strengthened:

\[
AgencyRespecting(I)
\not\equiv
PreservesTokenAlternatives(I).
\]

Where two policies adequately serve the same relevant aim, the agency-oriented creative rationale favors the one that better preserves the wider \(AE(A,\tau)\) profile. This remains defeasible; it neither proves divine goodness nor prohibits intervention.

Providential control and creaturely authorship may coexist when \(G\) establishes the arena, sustains capacities, communicates reasons, and responds to actual events without adaptively capturing the creature's governing standpoint. Conversely, merely leaving two formal options at every instant is not enough to establish agency-respecting providence.

## Atemporal responsiveness

A condition-sensitive series of divine interventions can belong timelessly to \(H^*\) while its members occur responsively in created causal order. The cumulative test asks about the actual and modal causal structure of that series, not about \(G\) learning what happened.

No rejected selection counterfactual is required. The model may state:

\[
Actual(p,t)\land Policy_G(p,I)\rightarrow RespondsTo(I,p),
\]

and compare admissible policy structures without positing a prior truth that a libertarian creature would choose \(a\).

The harder problem of self-consistent responsive loops remains open: an intervention responding to \(p\) may alter the conditions under which \(p\) occurs. That problem should be treated separately from cumulative capture.

## Literature and novelty notes

- [John Christman, “Autonomy and Personal History”](https://doi.org/10.1080/00455091.1991.10717234) is direct precedent for making the history through which preferences and values develop relevant to autonomy.
- [Alfred Mele, “Free Will and Moral Responsibility: Manipulation, Luck, and Agents' Histories”](https://doi.org/10.1111/misp.12105) treats manipulation and agential history as relevant to freedom and responsibility.
- The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on personal autonomy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/personal-autonomy/) maps the established divide between internal, historical, and relational conditions of self-government.

The historical relevance of manipulation is not novel. The local contribution is the typed integration with libertarian token sourcehood, intervention classes, atemporal actual-marker semantics, and a non-scalar agency ecology. The attractor-like notion of adaptive neutralization is presently a project formulation rather than a novelty claim.
