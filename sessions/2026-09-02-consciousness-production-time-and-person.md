# Session: The production, temporal status, and bearer of consciousness

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S36
**Primary question:** When and how is consciousness produced, is it temporal or atemporal, and how is it connected to the person?
**Dependencies:** C05, C08–C14, C28, C47–C67, C126, C134–C139, C212–C220, C223–C232, C283–C296, S01–S07, S22, S29, S30, S34
**Target depth:** F1 partition with selected F2 temporal constraints; metaphysical realization remains open

## Result in one sentence

Consciousness has no single temporal or productive profile: the preferred model distinguishes an underived atemporal divine conscious subject, a temporally existing created bearer and its developing conscious capacity, and temporally located conscious episodes that are tenselessly parts of the complete history without thereby being atemporal; the bearer relation connects an episode to a person while leaving the bearer's reductive, emergent, hylomorphic, dual-aspect, or dual-substance realization open.

## Why the question divides

The phrase “when consciousness is produced” can ask at least five questions:

1. when a conscious subject begins to exist;
2. when that subject acquires or first manifests a capacity for consciousness;
3. when a particular conscious episode begins;
4. what makes a bodily or other physical process realize an experience;
5. what makes that experience belong to this person.

These cannot be answered by one date or one causal arrow.

## Bearer, capacity, episode, and content

Use four typed items:

| Item | Symbol | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| persisting subject or bearer | $A$ | the entity whose experience it is |
| conscious capacity | $CapCon(A,t)$ | a power or organized disposition to undergo conscious states |
| conscious episode | $c$ | an actual token experience with phenomenal character |
| intentional or phenomenal content | $Content(c)$ | what is presented, felt, imagined, or thought in the episode |

Then:

\[
PCon(A,t)\leftrightarrow
\exists c[
Bears(A,c,t)
\land Phenomenal(c)
\land OccursAt(c,t)
].
\]

The bearer relation is not yet a theory of substance. It says that the episode is presented to and integrated as the state of $A$, rather than floating free or belonging to an inner homunculus.

The minimum asymmetry is:

\[
ConsciousAt(A,t)\rightarrow ExistsAt(A,t),
\]

while:

\[
ExistsAt(A,t)\not\Rightarrow ConsciousAt(A,t).
\]

Sleep, anesthesia, coma, and other gaps motivate the second formula. Whether the persisting bearer is an organism, a non-reductive embodied subject, a form–matter unity, a dual-aspect individual, or a non-physical substance remains open.

## Three temporal statuses

### 1. Divine consciousness

If conscious reason-guided agency is essential to the necessary agent $G$, its basic conscious capacity is neither generated by an earlier cause nor acquired at a first created time:

\[
EssentialConsciousAgent(G)
\rightarrow
\neg\exists t\,BeginsToBeConscious(G,t).
\]

This is conditional on the selected necessary-agent model. It does not infer every classical doctrine of divine consciousness.

$G$ may nevertheless stand in contingent intentional and acquaintance relations to the actual world. The capacity can be essential while the actual-world content known through ACO and ASA depends on which history is actual.

### 2. Created conscious capacity

For an actual embodied creature, the capacity is instantiated and may develop within created time:

\[
BeginsCapCon(A,t_c)
\leftrightarrow
CapCon(A,t_c)
\land
\forall t<t_c\,\neg CapCon(A,t).
\]

This formula defines an onset relative to a chosen capacity predicate. It does not tell us which physical, developmental, or metaphysical condition satisfies that predicate.

Phenomenal, access, self, and agentive consciousness may have different onset profiles. Therefore “the beginning of consciousness” remains ambiguous unless the layer is specified.

### 3. Token conscious episodes

Created experiences occur at times or across intervals:

\[
OccursDuring(c,[t_1,t_2])
\rightarrow TemporalToken(c).
\]

An episode may itself present succession through a temporally thick phenomenal field. Its objective duration, experienced duration, remembered duration, and report time need not coincide.

## Eternalism does not make the episode atemporal

In E+, a temporal episode is tenselessly part of the complete actual history:

\[
PartOf(c,H^*)\land LocatedAt(c,[t_1,t_2]).
\]

This means there is no external moment at which $c$ is first absent from and later added to the completed history. It does not mean:

\[
OccursAtEveryTime(c),
\]

or:

\[
AtemporalMode(c).
\]

The distinction is:

- **temporal location:** where the created episode occurs inside $H^*$;
- **tenseless actuality:** the episode is an actual constituent of the complete history;
- **atemporal divine knowledge:** $G$ knows the episode without learning it in sequence;
- **atemporal mode of existence:** an entity is not temporally located at all.

Only the first three apply to an ordinary created conscious episode, and they apply in different respects.

## Production has several causal levels

The actual provenance of a created conscious episode may contain:

\[
\langle
ExistGround,
CapacityGround,
RealizationBase,
EpisodeCause,
SubjectContribution
\rangle.
\]

### Existential production

$G$ ultimately grounds or produces the created subject and its arena:

\[
Created(A)\rightarrow DependsExistentially(A,G).
\]

This does not by itself state whether $G$ directly produces every conscious token.

### Capacity production

The conscious capacity may arise through:

1. the standing generative structure $S$ and ordinary biological development;
2. a basic psychophysical law or power included in $S$;
3. a strongly emergent subject-level power when organized conditions obtain;
4. a direct divine contribution $I_P$ at some developmental point;
5. generation or embodiment of a distinct non-physical subject;
6. activation or organization of more fundamental experiential properties.

The current ontology does not select among these.

### Episode realization

Let $RB_A(c,t)$ be the bodily, neural, affective, sensorimotor, environmental, and possibly further base relevant to episode $c$. Each theory must specify a bridge:

\[
RealizationBridge(RB_A(c,t),A,c).
\]

Calling this “emergence” is not yet an explanation. Weak emergence, strong emergence, constitution, identity, manifestation of a power, and interaction with a distinct subject are different relations.

### Token causation and content

Not every conscious state is originated by the person. Perception, pain, dreams, involuntary emotion, and manipulated experience may be produced by external or antecedent causes while still belonging to $A$.

In conscious free action, the subject can additionally originate a token exercise whose conscious contents participate causally:

\[
AgCon(A,a,\delta)
\land Originates(A,a,\delta).
\]

The bearer of an experience and the source of its occurrence or content are therefore separate roles.

## How consciousness connects to the person

The preferred minimum is a **bearer–presentation–integration** relation:

\[
PersonalState(c,A,t)\rightarrow
Bears(A,c,t)
\land PresentedTo(c,A)
\land IntegratedWith(c,Life_A,t).
\]

- $Bears$ assigns the token to the subject;
- $PresentedTo$ supplies its first-personal character;
- $IntegratedWith$ relates it to the subject's embodied capacities, memory, action, or continuing organization where those exist.

Integration need not mean explicit memory, report, self-concept, or access consciousness. A pain can belong to an infant or animal without reflective self-representation.

This relation supplies the truthmaker for ASA's subject index:

\[
Bears(A,c,t)\land PresentedTo(c,A)
\rightarrow GraspsAsPresentedTo(G,c,A).
\]

ASA does not create or transfer the state. It tracks the actual bearer relation.

## Does the person precede the first conscious episode?

Four principal models remain:

| Model | When the person or subject exists | Main pressure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| consciousness-constituted person | begins with first consciousness or the relevant psychological capacity | must explain gaps, development, and continuity |
| capacity-bearer | exists when the entity has the appropriately grounded capacity, even if no episode is occurring | must define capacity without circular or remote possibility |
| organismic or hylomorphic bearer | the persisting living subject can precede conscious manifestation | must explain why and when its organized life grounds subjectivity |
| distinct-substance bearer | a non-physical subject is produced or united with a body | must explain individuation, timing, interaction, and persistence |

The project presently prefers only the weaker thesis that actual experiences require a bearer when they occur and that a bearer may persist through non-conscious intervals. It does not yet settle whether the bearer before the first conscious episode already counts as a person.

This distinction is ontological before it is ethical. No conclusion about moral status, abortion, death, resurrection, or afterlife follows without additional premises.

## Could a created conscious subject be atemporal?

Creation means ontological dependence, not necessarily temporal beginning:

\[
Created(x)\not\Rightarrow\exists t\,BeginsToExist(x,t).
\]

The actual human subjects modeled so far are temporal and embodied. A numerically distinct created atemporal conscious subject is not excluded merely by the word “created”; it would be an atemporally dependent being. Such a model would require:

- individuation from $G$ without spatial or temporal separation;
- a distinction between necessary and contingent existence;
- an account of its relation to created time;
- consistency with divine uniqueness and qualified omnipotence;
- an explanation of whether it has changing or timeless conscious content.

This is preserved as a modal edge case rather than incorporated into the actual human ontology.

## What science can and cannot determine

Empirical work can constrain:

- which neural and bodily structures covary with report, discrimination, integration, wakefulness, and behavior;
- when candidate capacities develop or disappear;
- whether a proposed realization base is causally sufficient under an explicit metaphysical bridge;
- whether conscious processing is temporally punctate, recurrent, or extended.

It cannot by operational definition alone decide:

- why the selected organization has phenomenal character;
- whether realization is identity, constitution, emergence, or interaction;
- what ultimately individuates the subject;
- whether personhood begins with organism, capacity, or episode.

The onset date therefore depends jointly on an empirical profile and a metaphysical criterion of consciousness.

## Constraints on a later realization choice

A satisfactory model must explain:

1. **phenomenal presence:** why there is something it is like for $A$;
2. **subject unity:** why multiple contents belong to one bearer;
3. **temporal unity:** how episodes compose a stream or life without requiring uninterrupted consciousness;
4. **causal efficacy:** how $AgCon$ participates without duplicating a complete physical cause;
5. **individuation:** why this experience belongs to $A$ rather than another subject;
6. **creation dependence:** how subject, capacity, and episode fit OEC and secondary causes;
7. **ASA truthmaking:** what grounds the first-personal index known by $G$;
8. **graded development:** why animals, infants, adults, and impaired agents can differ across conscious layers without becoming one linear moral ranking;
9. **empirical compatibility:** which observations would genuinely favor or defeat the model.

## Adversarial checks

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| If an episode is eternally part of $H^*$, it is an atemporal experience | rejected; tenseless actuality and temporal location are compatible |
| If $G$ knows an experience atemporally, the creature experiences it atemporally | rejected; the knower's mode and the known event's temporal position differ |
| If the person is unconscious at $t$, no person exists at $t$ | rejected as a general inference; bearer persistence and occurrent consciousness differ |
| Every conscious episode is freely originated by its bearer | rejected; bearing, causal production, and agentive origination are distinct |
| A neural correlate by itself establishes that consciousness is identical to it | rejected; correlation or realization evidence needs a metaphysical bridge |
| “Emergence” by itself explains how experience is produced | rejected; the emergence relation and its powers or laws must be specified |
| A persisting bearer must be a Cartesian soul | rejected; bearer language remains neutral among several realization models |
| Created consciousness must begin at a time | rejected as a definition; creation is dependence, although actual embodied creatures are temporal |
| One onset date covers phenomenal, access, self, and agentive consciousness | rejected; the layers may develop or occur differently |

## Decisions and open positions

| Decision | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Distinguish bearer, capacity, episode, and content | preferred F1 | They answer different production and identity questions |
| Treat actual creaturely conscious episodes as temporally located | preferred | They occur within embodied histories even under E+ |
| Infer atemporality of an episode from atemporal knowledge | rejected | Epistemic and event-temporal modes differ |
| Model $G$'s basic conscious agency as underived and atemporal | preferred conditionally | It follows if conscious agency belongs essentially to the necessary agent |
| Let a bearer persist through some non-conscious intervals | preferred | It explains ordinary gaps without identifying person and current stream |
| Decide whether the pre-conscious bearer is already a person | open | Competing identity models attach the boundary to different conditions |
| Select the production or realization bridge | deferred but now prioritized | Existing commitments constrain but do not determine reduction, emergence, hylomorphism, dual aspect, or dual substance |
| Admit created atemporal consciousness as the actual human model | rejected | Human agents are presently modeled as temporal and embodied |
| Preserve a created atemporal subject as a modal edge case | open | Dependence does not semantically entail temporal onset |

## Literature and novelty notes

- [David Chalmers, “Strong and Weak Emergence”](https://consc.net/papers/emergence.pdf) distinguishes derivational or weak emergence from strong emergence involving further fundamental principles; this prevents “emergence” from functioning as one unanalyzed bridge.
- [Tim Bayne and David Chalmers, “What Is the Unity of Consciousness?”](https://consc.net/papers/unity.html) distinguish multiple forms of unity and examine the relation between unified phenomenal fields and subjects of experience.
- [Eric Olson, “Was I Ever a Fetus?”](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/772/) challenges accounts that make persistence depend solely on psychological continuity and illustrates why person, organism, capacity, and conscious episode must be separated.
- [Holly Andersen, “The Development of the ‘Specious Present’ and James' Views on Temporal Experience”](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/10721/) surveys the view that experienced presence can have non-zero duration, supporting the distinction between objective, phenomenal, and reported timing without selecting one temporal-consciousness theory.

The component distinctions are established. The local synthesis is their integration with E+, ACO/ASA, the OEC production hierarchy, embodied agent causation, and the explicit possibility of an atemporally created but dependent subject. No novelty claim is made at this stage.
