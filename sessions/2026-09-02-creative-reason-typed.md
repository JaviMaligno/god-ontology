# Session: Creative reason at F2 — typed reasons, eligibility, and the missing P-reason

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S53
**Primary question:** Can the agency-oriented plural reason profile of S18 be given a formal structure sufficient for the claims that now rest on it — good-orientation (C409), the diachronic agency-preservation presumption (C447), and the source cover (C420) — without turning reasons into a choice function, and what does that structure reveal about the P-gap?
**Dependencies:** C55–C60, C96–C97, C104–C115, C187–C189, C405–C406, C409–C411, C420, C443–C451, S06, S18–S19, S27, S45–S46, S49, S51
**Origin:** S51 identified creative reason as the weakest load-bearing transition; the author approved the queue
**Target depth:** F2 for the reason architecture; the actual divine motive remains unknown (C113)

## Starting position

S18 gives a plural, layered profile at F1: creation for actual finite otherness, an architecture for finite analogues of conscious sourcehood, exact specification by reason-guided settling among an eligible set. Its formal apparatus is thin: $Supported_G$, $Defeated_G$, and $Favors_G$ are undefined; the relation between reasons and the value anchors of S27 is not stated; and “favoring rather than necessitating” is asserted rather than modelled. Three later results rest on it. S51 therefore marked it as the weakest link.

The session keeps every S18 decision and gives the profile the structure the later claims need. It does not add goodness, perfection, or a maximizing norm.

## Definitions

| Term/symbol | Definition used in this session |
| --- | --- |
| creative reason $r$ | A triple $\langle a,\mu,\ell\rangle$: an **anchor or target** $a$ the reason is directed at, a **mode** $\mu\in\{\text{favoring},\text{justifying},\text{requiring}\}$, and a **level** $\ell\in\{\text{creation},\text{architecture},\text{specification}\}$ |
| target | An anchor of $\mathcal A_{min}=\{P,S,R\}$ or a non-axiological feature (intelligibility, variety, expression) |
| $\mathcal R_G$ | $G$'s reason profile: the set of creative reasons attributed to $G$ by the architecture |
| $Adm_r$ | The admissibility set of $r$: structures whose modal profile does not conflict with $r$'s target |
| $\mathcal E_G$ | The eligible set: $\bigcap_{r\in\mathcal R_G^{req}}Adm_r\cap\{S:Coherent(S)\}$, where $\mathcal R_G^{req}$ are the requiring reasons at the architecture and specification levels |
| $\succ_r$ | The attraction relation induced by a favoring reason: $S\succ_r S'$ iff $Profile_M(S)$ realizes or enables $r$'s target more fully than $Profile_M(S')$ |
| $\succ_G$ | The partial order on $\mathcal E_G$ generated by the favoring reasons: $S\succ_G S'$ iff $S\succeq_r S'$ for every favoring $r$ and $S\succ_r S'$ for some |
| $Und_G$ | The undominated set: $\{S\in\mathcal E_G:\neg\exists S'\,S'\succ_G S\}$ |
| $Settles(G,S^*;Und_G)$ | $G$'s reason-guided agentic settling on one member of $Und_G$ (C109) |

## Step 1: modes, and why favoring is not necessitating

The three modes answer different questions. A **requiring** reason filters: structures conflicting with its target are not eligible. A **favoring** reason orders: it makes some eligible structures more attractive without excluding the rest. A **justifying** reason makes an act intelligible and defensible without making it more attractive than its alternatives; it is what makes non-creation and creation both intelligible (C111).

This gives “favoring rather than necessitating” a model. Necessitation would require that $\succ_G$ be total on $\mathcal E_G$ with a unique maximum; the project denies both (C108). A partial order generated by plural reasons typically leaves an undominated set with more than one member, and $G$'s settling selects within it:

\[
|Und_G|\geq 2\;\Rightarrow\;\mathcal R_G\not\Rightarrow\Box Actualizes(G,S^*).
\]

The residue is exactly the one C109 and C406 accept: a reason-guided exercise, not a selector. Wilson's objection that satisficing requires brute preferences does not apply, because settling within $Und_G$ is not satisficing under a maximizing norm that the agent declines to apply; it is sourcehood where no norm selects.

## Step 2: the levels, formally

| Level | Options | Reason mode | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| creation | $\{Create,\neg Create\}$ | justifying and favoring | both remain live (C111); settling |
| architecture | kinds of order | requiring for the organizing reasons | the eligibility filter: every eligible $S$ enables what the requiring reasons target |
| specification | members of $\mathcal E_G$ | favoring | $\succ_G$ partial; settling within $Und_G$ (C109) |

The one refinement of S18 is at the architecture level. S18 said the agency reason *favors* a generative $S$. If it merely favored, some eligible structures would be fully scripted and C106 would be a preference among eligible options rather than a consequence. The session records the requiring reading: given that $G$ creates for actual finite otherness and analogous sourcehood, enabling autonomous agents is an admissibility condition, not an attraction:

\[
r_S=\langle S,\text{requiring},\text{architecture}\rangle\in\mathcal R_G
\;\Rightarrow\;
\forall S\in\mathcal E_G\,[Enables(S,AutonomousAgents)].
\]

C106 then follows rather than being preferred separately, while non-creation stays live at the creation level. This is a refinement, not a reversal: the requiring mode holds conditionally on the organizing reasons, which are themselves abductively preferred (C105) and not known to be the actual motive (C113).

## Step 3: typing the S18 profile by anchor

Every reason S18 attributes to $G$ has a target that is either an anchor of $\mathcal A_{min}$ or a non-axiological feature:

| S18 reason | Target | Mode | Level |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| actual finite otherness | $R$ (actual other subjects with whom relation is possible) | favoring at creation; requiring at architecture | creation, architecture |
| analogous sourcehood | $S$ | requiring | architecture |
| generative rather than scripted $S$ | $S$ | requiring | architecture |
| non-agentic domains: shared arena, regularity, consequences, embodiment | $S$ and $R$ (conditions of agency and of truthful encounter) | requiring | architecture |
| intelligible order and variety | non-axiological | favoring | specification |
| enactive play or appreciation | expressive; open | favoring, not preferred | creation |

The typing shows two things at once. First, C409 is secured formally: the established profile's targets are $S$ and $R$, so every established creative disposition is fit-directed toward those anchors by construction, not by a separate inspection. Second, **no reason in the established profile has $P$ as its target.** There is no reason directed at the conscious valence of created subjects: none that creation be for the sake of their felt good, none that the architecture protect it, none that specification favor it.

## Step 4: the P-gap is located in the reason profile

S45 found the P-gap by inspecting dispositions; the present typing shows why it is there. The gap is not a failure of S45's inference; it is the absence of a **P-reason** in S18's profile:

\[
\neg\exists r\in\mathcal R_G\,[target(r)=P]
\;\Rightarrow\;
\neg GoodOrient_P(G)\ \text{is derivable}.
\]

This relocates the question. To earn $GoodOrient_P$ is to earn a P-directed creative reason and its exercise. Two routes are visible and neither is taken here:

1. **From otherness as for-the-sake-of.** If “actual finite otherness” is read not only as making other subjects actual but as making them actual *for their own sake*, the reason acquires a P-target: a subject made for its own sake is made with regard to what its life is like for it. This reading is natural and is what the accessible register believes; it is not entailed by C105 as stated, which speaks of otherness and sourcehood, not of the other's good.
2. **From relation.** Truthful relation ($R$) with a valenced subject might be argued to include regard for its valence, since recognizing a subject as a subject includes recognizing what its states are like for it. This gives cognitive regard (which $ASA$, C284–C286, already secures) but not yet conative orientation.

Both routes are candidates; both would be abductive additions to $\mathcal R_G$ at the same evidential level as C105. The session records them as the exact content of open question 9 and does not adopt either. What it establishes is the dependency:

\[
GoodOrient_P(G)\;\Leftrightarrow\;\exists r\in\mathcal R_G\,[target(r)=P]\land Exercised(r).
\]

The accessible register's belief that God is good “also in what we feel” is, in the ontology's terms, the belief that $\mathcal R_G$ contains a P-reason. That is a sharper thing to believe than before.

## Step 5: securing the downstream claims

**Good-orientation (C409).** Follows from Step 3 by typing, for $S$ and $R$; the P-gap is Step 4.

**Diachronic agency-preservation presumption (C447).** The requiring reason $r_S$ targets sourcehood. S49 showed that sourcehood has a diachronic level. A reason targeting sourcehood targets it at both levels, since a life whose profile is funnelled instantiates token sourcehood without the authorship the target names. Hence the admissibility set of $r_S$ excludes curator policies at the intervention level, and C447 follows from the same reason as C115 without a new premise:

\[
r_S\in\mathcal R_G^{req}\;\Rightarrow\;Excluded_{Adm}(Funnel)\land Excluded_{Adm}(StandardInstallation).
\]

**Source cover (C420).** The non-agentic-domain reason requires a real field of consequences; a policy of preventing every harmful outcome of created choice conflicts with a requiring reason, not merely with a favored one. This strengthens the cover's status from a conflict with a preference to a conflict with an admissibility condition, while the threshold residue remains: token prevention does not violate the filter, only a policy does.

**Agency-preservation presumption (C115).** Same derivation as C447 at the token level.

## Step 6: what the structure does not do

- It does not supply the actual divine motive (C113); $\mathcal R_G$ is an abductively attributed profile.
- It does not make the eligible set computable or the undominated set a singleton.
- It does not import goodness: reasons are typed by anchors because the anchors are the project's account of what finite subjects need, not because $G$ is assumed to be good.
- It does not decide whether the enactive or expressive motive belongs to the profile.
- It does not introduce pre-actual counterfactuals: attraction ranges over $Profile_M(S)$ (C110).
