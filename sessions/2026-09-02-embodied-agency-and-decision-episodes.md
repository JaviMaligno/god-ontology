# Session: Embodied agency and decision episodes

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S29
**Primary question:** Is the consciousness-and-agency model compatible with bodily and neural processes preceding conscious awareness, and could the deciding subject be irreducible to a simple mind/body division?
**Dependencies:** C55–C66, C134–C139, S07, S22
**Target depth:** Local F2 repair of agentive consciousness; metaphysical realization remains open

## Authorial objection

Ordinary and experimental descriptions sometimes suggest that the body begins or even completes a decision before the consciously reporting mind becomes aware of it. A model that locates freedom in one punctual conscious event would therefore be fragile. The project must distinguish bodily preparation, conscious awareness, causal settlement, execution, and later narrative ownership.

The objection also exposes a metaphysical question. The acting subject might be neither a detachable mind nor a mere body part. It may be the unified embodied agent whose mental, neural, affective, physiological, and sensorimotor processes are constituents or realizations of one exercise. This possibility must remain available without prematurely adding a third substance.

## The agent, not a conscious homunculus, is the source

Keep the whole-agent attribution already present in S22:

\[
Originates(A,a,\delta),
\]

where $A$ is the temporally persisting subject and $\delta=[t_0,t_m]$ is a decision-and-action episode ending in movement or another public act at $t_m$. Neither a conscious image nor a neural subsystem is automatically a second agent inside $A$.

The created agent's realization base during the episode can be represented as:

\[
RB_A(\delta)=
\langle N_A,Phys_A,Aff_A,SM_A,Con_A\rangle,
\]

for neural, wider physiological, affective, sensorimotor, and conscious contributions. The notation does not assert that these are exhaustive or ontologically independent components. It prevents “the mind” from being treated as the only causal site and “the body” from being personified as a separate chooser.

## From an instantaneous decision to a distributed episode

Distinguish at least five events or relations:

| Stage | Function | What it does not establish by itself |
| --- | --- | --- |
| preparation or bias | changes readiness, salience, motor availability, or relative attraction | that one outcome is already unavoidable |
| conscious access | makes an option, reason, urge, or intention available to the subject | that consciousness began the process or settles it alone |
| agentive settlement | the integrated exercise makes one live option actual | that a report can locate its exact instant |
| motor execution | implements the selected action through bodily control | that movement onset is the time of choice |
| retrospective ownership | the subject interprets or reports what occurred | that the report created or accurately timed the decision |

Let $t_r$ be the onset of a readiness signal, $t_w$ reported awareness of intention, $t_s$ causal settlement, and $t_m$ movement onset. Then:

\[
t_r<t_w<t_m
\not\Rightarrow
t_s=t_r.
\]

More generally:

\[
Prepares(q,A,a,t)
\not\Rightarrow
Settles(q,a,t).
\]

Prediction above chance, correlation, or early motor preparation does not by itself show that the outcome was sufficiently settled. Conversely, the framework must concede the genuine defeater:

\[
C^-_{A,t_w}\models Settled(A,a)
\]

where the sufficient base excludes any later integrated agentive contribution. If that obtains, the subsequent conscious state witnesses, interprets, or endorses an already fixed token; the act is not consciously free under the current libertarian model.

## A revised role for consciousness

Consciousness need not be the first cause in a conscious free act. It must participate non-epiphenomenally in the control or settlement episode before the option becomes irrevocably fixed. Its role can include apprehending reasons, evaluating an urge, sustaining attention, endorsing a developing intention, inhibiting or vetoing a tendency, revising a plan, or guiding execution where control remains open.

Define:

\[
ConsciousFree(A,a,\delta)
\Rightarrow
EmbodiedUnity(A,\delta)
\land Originates(A,a,\delta)
\land |\mathcal O_A(C^-_{\delta})|\geq2
\land \neg(C^-_{\delta}\models Settled(A,a))
\land \exists p,t\in\delta\,[ACon(A,p,t)\land CausalControl(p,A,a,\delta)].
\]

$CausalControl$ requires more than a feeling of authorship after settlement. It does not require consciousness to operate without neural or bodily realization, nor to monopolize causation. The whole agent can act through a bodily process in which conscious and non-conscious contributions are mutually integrated.

This yields three categories that should remain distinct:

1. **organismic behaviour:** a bodily process produces movement, perhaps reflexively or automatically;
2. **embodied agentive action:** the integrated subject originates or controls the act, possibly with substantial non-conscious contribution;
3. **conscious reason-guided free action:** embodied sourcehood includes causally relevant conscious access before or during settlement.

Whether fully non-conscious embodied agentive action can satisfy minimal libertarian freedom remains open. The project's analogy with $G$ depends on the third category for paradigm reason-guided agency, not on every movement of a conscious organism being consciously chosen.

## Does this require something beyond mind and body?

The framework now requires a **subject-level unity**, but it does not yet determine its metaphysical realization. The main options are:

| Model | Meaning of the unified agent | Current pressure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| reductive organismic or neural identity | agent-level predicates reduce in principle to bodily organization | must preserve genuine sourcehood rather than only event determination |
| non-reductive embodied subject | the living subject is realized by bodily systems but has irreducible agent-level powers | naturally fits whole-agent causation; owes an account of realization and causal non-competition |
| dual-aspect account | mental and physical descriptions disclose aspects of one underlying subject | avoids two competing agents; must specify the common bearer |
| hylomorphic or form–matter account | the living subject is one substance constituted through organized matter and form | offers strong unity; requires translating form and power into the current ontology |
| substance dualism | a non-physical subject acts through a body | gives a distinct bearer; faces interaction and individuation burdens |

The phrase “beyond mind and body” is therefore admissible if it means that the agent is not exhausted by treating mental episodes and bodily parts as independent inventories. It is not yet evidence for a numerically third component. The minimum commitment is:

> Mental and bodily processes belong to one subject whose integrated exercise, rather than any isolated part, is the candidate source of action.

## Empirical bridge and limits

The relevant empirical findings constrain the model only through explicit bridge premises:

\[
NeuralMarker(n,t)<AwarenessReport(t_w)
\land Bridge(n,Settlement)
\Rightarrow
Constraint(ConsciousFree).
\]

The bridge from a marker to completed settlement is the disputed step. The checked primary literature supports caution:

- [Libet et al. (1983)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6640273/) found a readiness potential preceding the reported time of conscious intention in arbitrary self-initiated movement.
- [Matsuhashi and Hallett (2008)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19046374/) used real-time probes and estimated conscious intention substantially earlier than conventional retrospective clock reports, showing that report method affects timing.
- [Schurger, Sitt, and Dehaene (2012)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3479453/) modeled the readiness potential in arbitrary movement as thresholded accumulation of spontaneous neural fluctuations rather than as a completed unconscious decision.
- [Schultze-Kraft et al. (2016)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4743787/) found that subjects could still cancel movements after readiness-potential onset, until a later point of no return.
- [Maoz et al. (2019)](https://elifesciences.org/articles/39787) found the expected readiness potential for arbitrary decisions but not for deliberate consequential choices, limiting generalization from purposeless motor tasks.

These results do not prove libertarian sourcehood. They show that “neural activity precedes reported awareness” is not equivalent to “the body has deterministically completed the decision before consciousness.” A future result establishing sufficient pre-conscious settlement for a relevant token would remain a genuine empirical defeater for its classification as consciously free.

## Relation to divine agency

The analogy between created agency and $G$ concerns unified sourcehood, apprehension of reasons, live possibilities, and efficacious settlement. It does not require $G$ to reproduce temporal embodiment, neural preparation, affective regulation, motor execution, or retrospective awareness. Those belong to the created realization of agency.

This asymmetry also explains why the created-agent model needs an episode while divine settling can remain atemporal. The common ontology is the subject-level power; the temporal and bodily implementation is not projected onto $G$.

## Decisions

| Decision | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attribute action to the unified embodied agent rather than a conscious homunculus | preferred | Bodily, neural, affective, and conscious processes can be contributions of one subject |
| Treat every neural precursor as a completed decision | rejected | Preparation, prediction, settlement, and execution are distinct relations |
| Require consciousness to be the first causal event | rejected correction | Consciousness can enter later while still contributing before or during settlement |
| Permit consciousness to be only a retrospective witness in a consciously free act | rejected | This would abandon the earlier non-epiphenomenal condition |
| Model temporal choice as an episode rather than a punctual reportable instant | preferred | It accommodates distributed preparation, control, veto, settlement, and execution |
| Identify the unified subject with a third substance now | deferred | Non-reductive embodied, dual-aspect, hylomorphic, reductive, and dualist realizations remain open |
| Treat sufficient pre-conscious settlement as irrelevant | rejected | If established through a valid empirical–ontological bridge, it defeats conscious freedom for that token |
