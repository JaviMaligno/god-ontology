# Session: Explanatory stopping points and the necessitation lemma

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S47
**Primary question:** Can the ordinary answer to “Why do you believe in God?” — that a reality which merely exists because it exists is an unsatisfying stopping point, and that of the two ways beyond it the agentic one is more satisfying — be given a formal backbone consistent with C04–C05 and the S09 constructive standard?
**Dependencies:** C01–C05, C08, C55–C60, C108–C109, S09
**Origin:** Question-driven gap filling requested by the author in the 2026-09-02 brief for the accessible track (S46)
**Target depth:** F2 for the contingency/ground junction; the C05 comparison remains abductive and preferential

## Starting position

C04 holds a necessary foundation of contingent reality as a provisional hypothesis and C05 prefers to model that foundation as an agent. S09 fixed the comparative standard and audited costs at F1. The development map planned an F2 pass on contingency and necessary ground “after comparative criteria are clear.” Those criteria are now clear, and the accessible track needs the first two public questions — why seek anything beyond brute existence, and why an agent rather than a structure — to rest on an explicit argument rather than on a summary of a preference.

The author's ordinary formulation contains two distinct moves, and the session keeps them separate:

1. Stopping at “everything just exists” is admissible but unsatisfying; it is more interesting, and more continuous with scientific curiosity, to ask what could lie beyond. If nothing does, that must be accepted.
2. Beyond brute existence there are two families: an impersonal necessary structure from which everything follows, and a conscious agent who chooses which universe exists. The agentic family is preferred because it does not deliver determinism and because it connects with what we are as conscious agents.

## Definitions

| Term/symbol | Definition used in this session |
| --- | --- |
| $\mathcal C$ | The contingent order: the totality of contingent facts and entities, including $S$, $H^*$, and created agents |
| $Cont(\phi)$ | $\phi\land\Diamond\neg\phi$: $\phi$ obtains and could have failed to obtain |
| $\Box E(N)$ | $N$ exists necessarily |
| $Ground(N,\phi)$ | $\phi$ depends existentially or explanatorily on $N$ (typed as in C32) |
| $NecGround(N,\phi)$ | $Ground(N,\phi)\land\Box(E(N)\land K_N\to\phi)$, where $K_N$ is any necessary condition internal to $N$: a necessitating sufficient ground |
| $NNProd(N,\phi)$ | $Ground(N,\phi)\land\neg\Box(E(N)\land K_N\to\phi)$: non-necessitating production |
| $CExp(e,\phi,\psi)$ | $e$ explains why $\phi$ rather than the available alternative $\psi$ (contrastive explanation, C33) |
| Strong PSR | Every contingent fact has a sufficient explanation entailing it |
| Weak explanatory preference (WEP) | Other things being roughly equal, prefer the account that leaves fewer, or less fundamental, contingent facts unexplained; an unexplained contingent totality is a cost, not a contradiction |

## Move 1: why not stop at brute existence?

The project does not adopt a strong PSR. A strong PSR is known to threaten modal collapse: if every contingent fact has an entailing explanation, and the ultimate explainer is necessary, the explained facts inherit necessity. The author's stance is also weaker than a PSR: brute existence is admitted as a coherent stopping point that would have to be accepted if true.

What the author actually uses is a defeasible methodological preference, here named WEP. It is the same principle that keeps a scientist asking why after a successful description (C01–C03) and it is continuous with the project's motivation: description does not settle why the fundamental structure obtains. WEP is:

- comparative, not existential: it ranks accounts, it does not assert that an explanation must exist;
- defeasible: coherence, cost, and fit can outweigh it (S09 criteria);
- explicitly labeled as preferential, so that “I find brute existence unsatisfying” is recorded as authorial judgment rather than disguised deduction.

Under WEP, brute contingency is the worst-ranked stopping point on the explanatory dimension alone, because it leaves the largest and most fundamental contingent fact — that anything contingent obtains at all, and this rather than that — without any account. It is not thereby refuted.

## Move 2: the necessitation lemma

Suppose a necessary $N$ grounds the contingent order. In any normal modal logic (axiom K plus necessitation):

\[
\Box E(N)\land\Box\big(E(N)\land K_N\to\phi\big)
\;\vdash\;
\Box\phi .
\]

Hence, if $Cont(\phi)$, no necessary being is a necessitating sufficient ground of $\phi$:

\[
Cont(\phi)\land\Box E(N)\;\Rightarrow\;\neg NecGround(N,\phi).
\]

Therefore any necessary ground of a genuinely contingent order must produce it non-necessitatingly:

\[
Cont(\mathcal C)\land\Box E(N)\land Ground(N,\mathcal C)
\;\Rightarrow\;
NNProd(N,\mathcal C).
\]

The lemma is formal and neutral between impersonal and agentic $N$. Its force is to convert the informal “an impersonal ground gives determinism” into a precise dilemma: a necessary impersonal structure either necessitates its output, in which case nothing is contingent, or it does not, in which case the selection of the actual output needs a separate account.

## Move 3: the four stopping points

| Stopping point | What it says about $\mathcal C$ | What remains unexplained | Cost against project commitments |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **B — brute contingency** | $\mathcal C$ obtains; nothing grounds it | the existence and character of the whole contingent order | ranked lowest by WEP; otherwise coherent |
| **Nec — necessitarianism** | nothing is contingent; $\mathcal C$ and every event in it are necessary | nothing, at the price of denying contingency | denies the contingency datum, alternative possibilities, and C08; makes MKO trivial |
| **IN — impersonal necessity** | $\Box E(N)$, $N$ non-agentive; by the lemma $NNProd(N,\mathcal C)$ | which output obtains: by chance (no contrastive explainer), by a selection law (itself necessary, collapsing into Nec, or contingent, reopening the regress), or by plenitude (every possibility realized) | chance leaves the contrast brute; plenitude erases “this rather than that” and inflates ontology; agency must still emerge from an impersonal base |
| **AN — necessary agent** | $\Box E(N)$, $N$ a conscious reason-guided agent; $NNProd(N,\mathcal C)$ through agentic settling (C109) | why this exercise rather than another, beyond the agent's reasons and sourcehood | requires necessary subjectivity and the coherence of non-necessitating agency (C55–C60); terminates in an exercise attributable to a subject |

Under IN and AN the terminus is a non-necessitated fact in both cases. The difference is the kind of terminus:

\[
IN:\;NNProd(N,\mathcal C)\land\neg\exists e\,CExp(e,\mathcal C,\mathcal C'),
\qquad
AN:\;NNProd(N,\mathcal C)\land CExp\big(Exercise(N,\mathcal C,\mathbf R),\mathcal C,\mathcal C'\big),
\]

where $\mathbf R$ is the plural reason profile of S18 and $Exercise$ is the agentic settling that C109 already treats as the point at which explanation stops without a further sufficient selector. Plenitude is the one impersonal variant that supplies an answer to “why this rather than that” — it answers “both” — but does so by abandoning the contrast rather than explaining it.

## Why this is the formal core of C05, and not more

The comparison yields a comparative advantage, not a proof:

1. **Explanatory shape.** Only AN offers a contrastive, reason-guided, non-necessitating explanation of the actual contingent order. IN offers either no contrastive explanation or plenitude; B offers none; Nec removes the explanandum.
2. **Unification.** AN places consciousness and agency at the foundation, where the project already needs them for C08 and C57; IN must ground or permit irreducible agency from an impersonal base (S09 cost audit).
3. **Freedom.** Nec and the necessitating reading of IN exclude libertarian creaturely agency; AN and chance-IN permit it. This matches the author's remark that the agentic option “does not give rise to determinism.”
4. **What the objector keeps.** The impersonal theorist may say that a brute chance terminus is no worse than a brute exercise, and that “reason-guided sourcehood” is a label placed on the same stop. The project's answer is the one already recorded in S06 and S09: the exercise belongs to a subject, is responsive to reasons, and is the same causal kind the project needs for created freedom; the advantage is authorship and intelligibility, not the elimination of a primitive. This is where the choice is preferential, and the ledger says so.

Reversal conditions from S09 still apply. In addition, the lemma exposes a specific defeater: if non-necessitating agentic settling were shown to be indistinguishable in explanatory content from chance, AN would lose its distinctive advantage and the comparison would return to unification and freedom alone.

## Literature and novelty notes

Sources are cited from memory; no online verification was possible in this session, and the citations should be checked in the literature pass.

- The modal-collapse argument against a strong PSR is due to Peter van Inwagen, *An Essay on Free Will* (Oxford, 1983), ch. 6, and is discussed in the Stanford Encyclopedia entry on the principle of sufficient reason. C458 is the same inference applied to a necessary ground rather than to the PSR.
- Timothy O'Connor, *Theism and Ultimate Explanation* (Wiley-Blackwell, 2008), argues that a necessary being can explain a contingent order only by non-necessitating agent causation. C460 is the closest possible precedent and the project's argument should be read as a reconstruction within its own premises, as the research method requires for named authors.
- Alexander Pruss and Joshua Rasmussen, *Necessary Existence* (Oxford, 2018), defend necessary being through weaker-than-PSR explanatory principles; WEP belongs to that family but is explicitly comparative and authorial.
- Necessitarianism and plenitude are treated in the Spinoza and Leibniz literature; the four-way table is an expository compression, not a novelty claim.

Classification: established components (modal collapse, O'Connor's agentic necessary being, weak explanatory principles); project-specific compression into a four-way stopping-point comparison with an explicitly preferential residue.
