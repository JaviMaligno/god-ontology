# Fixed-History Intervention Range

**Session ID:** S43

**Date:** 2026-09-02

**Primary question:** Does $G$ retain access to every independently eligible intervention when a concrete created past, identity profile, law profile, and source-role profile are held fixed?

**Secondary correction:** Can recreation restore the same numerical individual rather than merely produce a duplicate?

**Target depth:** F2 treatment of $HI_G$ and closure of the created-order power decomposition; leave unrestricted action-domain exhaustion separate

## Outcome

Numerical recreation is a coherent conditional option in the present architecture. A qualitatively matching later entity is not automatically the original, but neither is it automatically a different individual. If the independently selected identity theory permits intermittent existence or supplies a gap-spanning individual ground, $G$ may recreate the same $x$. The project will reserve **duplicate reconstruction** for a later $y\neq x$ and **numerical recreation** for the return of $x$.

$HI_G$ can also receive F2 support, conditional on a substantive **local prefix-composability** bridge. An exact created past cannot alter $G$'s underived essential powers; independently admissible history constraints already filter out conflicts involving identity, laws, promises, causal structure, and sourcehood; and $RI$ prevents temporal or spatial remoteness from creating a new access barrier. Where a task passes those filters, a relevant complete history containing the same $G$'s intentional intervention witnesses the ability.

This is not a deductive theorem from type-level $CC_G$ alone. The composability bridge remains an explicit F2 premise open to a rival account of holistic modal constraints. With it, the three named created-order power ranges are now supported:

\[
NamedRange_C(G):=CP_G^{elig}\land CC_G^{elig}\land HI_G^{elig}.
\]

The next pass must test whether these types exhaust created-order tasks and then classify tasks beyond the created order before unrestricted $OMP_Q$ can be assessed.

## Correction: reconstruction and recreation

S41 and S42 correctly denied that qualitative duplication is sufficient for numerical identity. Their wording nevertheless leaned too far toward treating every post-cessation production as a new individual.

### Duplicate reconstruction

\[
DupRecon(G,x,y,t_2):=
QualMatch(y,x)\land y\neq x\land Originates(G,y,t_2).
\]

This produces a later individual with the relevant qualitative, structural, psychological, or bodily profile. Similarity, including perfect qualitative similarity, does not establish identity.

### Numerical recreation

Let $J_1$ and $J_2$ be disjoint temporal intervals. A discontinuous career has:

\[
Life_H(x)=J_1\cup J_2,
\qquad \sup J_1<\inf J_2.
\]

Then:

\[
NumRecreate(G,x,J_1,J_2):=
GapIdentity_C(x,J_1,J_2)
\land \neg ExistsBetween(x,J_1,J_2)
\land DirectlyRestoresExistence(G,x,J_2).
\]

$GapIdentity_C$ is deliberately independent of divine power. It may ultimately be grounded by the same embodied form, an individual essence or haecceity, continuity of the power-bearing subject, a first-personal persistence condition, or another theory. The current thin hylomorphic-powers model does not yet choose among these or demonstrate that any one permits a literal existence gap.

Therefore:

\[
QualMatch(y,x)\not\Rightarrow y=x,
\]

and:

\[
GapIdentity_C(x,J_1,J_2)\rightarrow
Poss_C^{ind}(SameIndividualReturns(x,J_1,J_2))
\]

is admissible as a conditional schema. The second formula records compatibility, not a completed identity theory.

### Final and interval annihilation

S42's terminal-boundary account describes **final annihilation**:

\[
FinalAnn(G,x,t_1):=Ann(G,x,t_1)\land
\forall t>t_1\;\neg ExistsAt(x,t).
\]

Numerical recreation requires a different temporal profile:

\[
GapCessation(G,x,t_1,t_2):=
ExistsBefore(x,t_1)
\land \forall t\in[t_1,t_2)\;\neg ExistsAt(x,t)
\land DirectExitBoundary(G,x,t_1).
\]

If $GapIdentity_C$ holds and $x$ exists again at $t_2$, the event is numerical recreation. If a matching $y\neq x$ exists instead, it is duplicate reconstruction. Both use existential-boundary control, but only final annihilation gives $Life_H(x)$ an ultimate upper bound.

This terminology makes the user's intuition explicit: calling the second case recreation is most natural when the same individual returns. A distinct qualitative successor should normally be called reconstruction, replacement, or duplication.

## The fixed-history problem

Type-level constitutive control says that $G$ can settle each independently eligible kind of created-order outcome. It does not yet say that the power remains exercisable while an exact obtaining past is preserved.

Let:

\[
P_t(H^*):=H^*\restriction_{<t}
\]

be the complete created prefix before the intervention boundary. The relevant comparison is between complete histories:

\[
H_0=P_t\oplus C_0,
\qquad
H_I=P_t\oplus I_t\oplus C_I.
\]

They share the same created past and differ at or after the intervention boundary. If the intervention occurs, $H_I$ is the actual history. $H_0$ was not first made actual and later edited.

## Intervention task type

Refine the S04 intervention tuple as:

\[
i=\langle \tau,x,q,t,\pi,\sigma\rangle,
\]

where:

- $\tau$ is the operation type: root/boundary, nomic, physical, circumstantial, power-directed, or act-directed;
- $x$ is the target;
- $q$ is the requested created-order outcome;
- $t$ is the created manifestation boundary;
- $\pi$ is the preservation profile;
- $\sigma$ assigns the required contrastive source roles.

The intervention source is fixed as $G$. The tuple does not state that $G$ can perform it.

## Preservation is not one undifferentiated condition

Write:

\[
\pi=\langle \pi_-,\pi_{\leftrightarrow},\pi_+\rangle.
\]

The components are:

1. $\pi_-$: past facts preserved by exact prefix identity;
2. $\pi_{\leftrightarrow}$: conditions spanning the boundary, such as numerical identity, an enduring law, a promise, or a causal relation;
3. $\pi_+$: requested future conditions not already contained in $q$.

This separation blocks ambiguous tasks such as “replace the law while preserving numerically the same globally timeless law” or “annihilate $x$ while preserving $x$ continuously.” Such tasks may be reformulated by preserving earlier law instances or a qualitative law pattern instead.

## Independent history compatibility

History compatibility must not contain the conclusion that $G$ can intervene. Define:

\[
\begin{aligned}
HistComp_C(P_t,q,\pi,\sigma):=\exists H\,[{}&Adm_C(H)\\
&\land H\restriction_{<t}=P_t\\
&\land Embeds(H,q,\pi)\\
&\land SourceStructureCompatible(H,\sigma)].
\end{aligned}
\]

The witness contains the requested neutral outcome and a mutually consistent allocation of creaturely or open source roles. It does not instantiate the requested divine performance. $HistComp_C$ therefore establishes compossibility of the worldly terms rather than divine ability.

An intervention task is independently eligible when:

\[
\begin{aligned}
HIElig_G(i\mid P_t):={}&Task_G(i)
\land HistComp_C(P_t,q,\pi,\sigma)\\
&\land RoleComp(G,\tau,q,\sigma)
\land EssenceComp(E_G^{ind},i).
\end{aligned}
\]

$RoleComp$ excludes action descriptions that require $G$ and a creature each to be the exclusive source of the same contrast. It does not say that $G$ has the power.

## Essential power retention

The created prefix cannot constitutively modify an underived, constitutively transcendent $G$:

\[
\begin{aligned}
Retain_G(\tau,P_t):={}&EssentialTypeCapacity(G,\tau)\\
&\land Created(P_t)
\land \neg Constitutes(P_t,G).
\end{aligned}
\]

Given $UPSS$, $CT$, and the independently earned type-level powers:

\[
EssentialTypeCapacity(G,\tau)\land P_t\subseteq\mathcal C
\rightarrow Retain_G(\tau,P_t).
\]

A created past can make a compound task inconsistent—for example, by fixing an identity that the requested outcome denies. It cannot drain, damage, spatially isolate, or metaphysically reconstitute the power-holder. Those task conflicts are already handled by $HistComp_C$, $RoleComp$, and $EssenceComp$.

## Local prefix composability

Retention still does not yield performance in the specified circumstances. The substantive bridge is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
LPC_G:\quad HIElig_G(i\mid P_t)
&\land TypeCan(G,\tau,q)
\land Retain_G(\tau,P_t)\\
&\rightarrow \exists H_I\,RelevantHIWitness(H_I,G,i,P_t).
\end{aligned}
\]

where:

\[
\begin{aligned}
RelevantHIWitness(H_I,G,i,P_t):={}&Adm_C(H_I)\\
&\land H_I\restriction_{<t}=P_t\\
&\land SameEssentialAgent(H_I,G)\\
&\land IntentionallyPerforms(H_I,G,i)\\
&\land Embeds(H_I,q,\pi,\sigma).
\end{aligned}
\]

$LPC_G$ is supported by five already preferred features:

1. $E^+$ represents the alternative as a complete history rather than an edit;
2. $Poss_C^{ind}$ and $HistComp_C$ independently filter holistic constitutive conflicts;
3. $G$ and the relevant power remain numerically and essentially the same across the witness;
4. $CT+RI$ combine independence from created constitution with access to every created target;
5. $CC_G$ supplies the relevant operation type without requiring ordinary exercise.

The bridge is still substantive. Modal facts need not always recombine freely. A rival may reject $LPC_G$ by proposing a holistic constraint that makes the neutral history admissible and the operation type available elsewhere while preventing the same $G$ from exercising it with this prefix. At F2, such a brute source-sensitive discontinuity is less satisfactory than $LPC_G$ unless independently motivated.

## Ability conclusion

A relevant witness must represent $G$'s intentional performance rather than the outcome's accidental occurrence. Under the agent-relative modal standard already used in S33 and S40:

\[
RelevantHIWitness(H_I,G,i,P_t)
\rightarrow CanIntervene(G,i\mid P_t).
\]

Therefore:

\[
HI_G^{elig}:=
\forall i,P_t
[HIElig_G(i\mid P_t)
\rightarrow CanIntervene(G,i\mid P_t)]
\]

is preferred at F2, conditional on $LPC_G$ and the earlier conditional modal-ultimity architecture.

The result concerns metaphysical effective ability: there is an accessible history in which $G$ intentionally performs the intervention. It does not require a temporal deliberative procedure, practical uncertainty, or luck on $G$'s part.

## Laws and exceptions

A law intervention has two readings:

\[
LawException(e,L):=L\text{ remains governing while }e
\text{ has an additional non-ordinary cause},
\]

and:

\[
LawReplacement(L_0,L_1,t):=
GovernsBefore(L_0,t)\land GovernsAfter(L_1,t).
\]

Whether one numerically identical law can vary over time depends on the law ontology. $HI_G$ does not decide that question by vocabulary. The preservation profile must say whether it fixes a law token, its earlier applications, or merely a qualitative regularity.

## Autonomous and joint outcomes

If $A$'s response $a$ essentially includes $A$'s originating settlement:

\[
SourceReq(a)=A
\rightarrow \neg StronglyProduces(G,a\text{ as }A\text{-free}).
\]

$G$ can perform the divine part of an eligible interaction:

\[
Address(G,A,m)\land Responds(A,m,a).
\]

The complete joint outcome remains co-produced. The ability to address does not entail the ability to guarantee a free uptake while preserving its sourcehood. If $G$ instead overrides the response, the physical or behavioral outcome may be produced, but the task type and agency status change.

## Atemporal action and temporal manifestation

$CanIntervene(G,i\mid P_t)$ does not locate a new divine decision after $P_t$ in a divine timeline. The complete $H_I$ includes a created manifestation at $t$; $G$'s contribution is one atemporal settlement, while any autonomous creaturely contributions remain separately sourced. The temporal index belongs to the created effect or relation:

\[
ManifestAt(i,t)\not\Rightarrow BeginsToActAt(G,t).
\]

This also handles a response to a creaturely free act. If the act lies in the preserved prefix, ACO includes it in the actuality-dependent content known by $G$; the intervention may occur later in created causal order without divine temporal learning.

## No meta-temporal revision

The following is not an eligible E+ task:

\[
Actual(H^*)\land PreserveActuality(H^*)
\land MakeActual(H_I\neq H^*).
\]

It demands two incompatible complete histories to bear the unique actual marker in the same respect. A counterfactual or causal comparison may hold the past fixed and compare continuations, but if the intervention is exercised, $H_I$ is the one complete co-produced history bearing the actual marker. Past-directed or retrocausal effects require their own history-compatible profile; they cannot rewrite an already actual past from a later meta-time.

## Total annihilation as a history-sensitive task

For $t_*$ after an exact prefix:

\[
HIElig_G(TotalAnn,t_*\mid P_{t_*})
\rightarrow CanIntervene(G,TotalAnn\mid P_{t_*}).
\]

The witness history simply terminates at $t_*$. It need not preserve future conditions because no later created times exist. A demand both to terminate created time and preserve a later created event fails $HistComp_C$.

## Adversarial tests

### Does exact past identity secretly fix the future?

Not in the preferred agentic architecture. S07 already rejects the inference from complete prior reasons, powers, and circumstances to one unique agentic settlement. Law–Miller–Rasmussen likewise formulate robust libertarian alternatives while holding past facts and laws fixed. $HistComp_C$ still excludes prefixes that genuinely entail the requested outcome's negation through an independently accepted constitutive constraint.

### Does a possible continuation automatically establish divine ability?

No. The relevant witness must contain the same $G$ intentionally performing the action through the retained operation type. $LPC_G$ supplies this additional source-sensitive bridge.

### Does retained power automatically establish local composability?

No. This is why $LPC_G$ remains explicit. Essential retention removes acquisition, damage, and exhaustion stories; independent history compatibility and role compatibility remove worldly conflicts; the remaining inference is an abductive rejection of brute source-specific discontinuity.

### Does $HI_G$ force universal divine concurrence?

No. It concerns accessible alternative histories. $GSDA$ continues to assign ordinary actual derivative effects to secondary causes and direct divine exercises only to the root and actual interventions.

### Can a promise remove divine power?

A contingent policy or promise can make an action unavailable under a task that preserves that commitment without changing the underlying type capacity. An independently essential commitment may instead exclude the action from $Task_G$. The ontology need not decide divine goodness to retain this distinction.

### Can the same person be recreated twice simultaneously?

Not without revising numerical identity. If two later bearers are mutually distinct, at most one can be identical to the earlier $x$. A branching task therefore triggers a uniqueness or fission problem inside $GapIdentity_C$ rather than a power problem.

## Literature and precedent

- [Wolfgang Schwarz, “Ability and Possibility”](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/154663153/FPV_SchwarzPI2020AbilityAndPossibility.pdf) analyzes ability through accessible possible worlds whose domain is fixed by relevant circumstances and requires the performance, rather than mere outcome occurrence. This supports the relevant-witness form without determining the project's accessibility relation.
- [Law, Miller, and Rasmussen, “How We Could Have Libertarian Free Will Even if God Were a Total Know-It-All About the Future”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-philosophy/article/how-we-could-have-libertarian-free-will-even-if-god-were-a-total-knowitall-about-the-future/F4EB5F85998531F3135D184B69548DF3) explicitly formulate robust alternatives while holding past and laws fixed and defend their compatibility with eternalism and divine knowledge. They do not supply the divine-intervention bridge, but they support the consistency of its shared-prefix background.
- [Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 105](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1105.htm) combines genuine creaturely operation with divine ability to produce effects directly and distinguishes miracles by their relation to the ordinary causal order. The project uses the capacity distinction without adopting his universal concurrence.
- [Lynne Rudder Baker, “Persons and the Metaphysics of Resurrection”](https://people.umass.edu/lrb/files/bak07percM.pdf) makes numerical identity, rather than mere similarity, an explicit adequacy condition for resurrection and compares several possible identity grounds. This supports reopening numerical recreation while leaving its ground theory-dependent.
- [Kenny Pearce, “Infinite Power and Finite Powers”](https://writings.kennypearce.net/infinitePower.pdf) emphasizes that claims to possess all powers require a justified range. The present result remains typed and does not infer unrestricted omnipotence from created-order coverage.

The potentially distinctive element is the integration of exact E+ prefixes, source-indexed libertarian acts, independently admissible neutral histories, essential power retention, and a local composability bridge. Each component has nearby precedent; no novelty claim is warranted before a systematic intervention and ability comparison.
