# Session: A good-oriented divine profile

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S48
**Primary question:** When someone asks “Is your God good?”, what can the established ontology honestly license, and can the non-circular bridge left open in S27 (C195) be made more explicit without importing perfection or a divine-nature standard by stipulation?
**Dependencies:** C55–C67, C104–C126, C127–C133, C186–C196, S18–S21, S27–S28
**Origin:** Question-driven gap filling for the accessible track (S46)
**Target depth:** F1 with local F2 notation; omnibenevolence remains unearned

## Starting position

C133 records that full divine goodness and omnibenevolence are not earned. S27 prefers, provisionally, that objective fit $Fit_*$ is essentially grounded in $G$ (C195), on the condition that the bridge can be completed “without circularly calling those features good.” The accessible track cannot answer “Is God good?” with either “yes, obviously” or “the project has no view.” It needs to know exactly which part of goodness the established profile earns, which part is belief with a stated status, and where the remaining burden lies.

## Step 1: the non-circular grounding route

The three anchors were selected in S27 because each blocks a collapse already exposed by the ontology, not because of a prior theory of the good. The divine features listed below were each established in sessions that explicitly refused to assume goodness:

| Anchor | Kind from which its fit conditions derive | Divine feature established without evaluative premises | Status of the feature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $S$ — sourcehood | conscious reason-guided origination | $G$ is the paradigm necessary agent: constitutive sourcehood, intellect, will (C55–C60, C67) | preferred/accepted |
| $R$ — truthful recognition and relation | knowledge that tracks truth; availability for address and response between subjects | ACO knows every actual truth without distortion (C11); $G$ is universally personally available (C122) | accepted; preferred |
| $P$ — conscious valence | states that feel good or bad for a subject | $G$ is a conscious subject (C57, C61); $G$ existentially grounds every actual valenced subject (C73) and modally knows every possible valenced kind (MKO, C126). Divine valence is not attributed. | preferred; valence open |

Hence:

\[
Inst_{\Box}(G,K_S)\land Inst_{\Box}(G,K_R)\land
\big(GroundsExist(G,K_P)\land Knows_M(G,K_P)\big),
\]

with no evaluative predicate on the left-hand side. This supplies the missing content of C195: $Fit_*$ can be grounded in $G$ because the kinds whose natures fix the fit conditions for $S$ and $R$ are necessarily instantiated by $G$, and the kind fixing $P$'s conditions is existentially and modally grounded in $G$. The route says what “grounded in $G$'s character as conscious rational source and ultimate relational ground” (S27) refers to. It does not say that these features are good; it says that the fit relation takes its content from natures that $G$ has or grounds.

The route is provisional for the same reason C195 was: it presupposes that fit conditions are fixed by the natures of the anchor kinds (relational objectivity, S21, S25) rather than by an independent Platonic standard or by bare divine choice. Both alternatives remain the recorded fallbacks.

## Step 2: what the established profile already exhibits

$Disp^{est}(G)$ currently contains at least:

| Established disposition or exercise | Session | Anchor | Fit relation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Creating for the sake of actual finite otherness and analogous sourcehood; preferring a generative $S$ to a scripted history | S18; C104–C106 | $S$ | realizes $S$ for created bearers |
| Defeasible presumption to preserve or restore created sourcehood in intervention | S19; C115–C117 | $S$ | protects $S$ |
| Universal personal availability: capacity to recognize, receive address, address, and respond | S20; C122 | $R$ | makes $R$ available to every subject |
| Actuality-complete knowledge that tracks truth and does not distort it | S03, S05; C11 | $R$ | truthful recognition of every actual subject |
| Non-necessitated, reason-guided creation with plural non-requiring reasons | S18; C108–C111 | $S$ (divine) | exemplifies $S$ |

Each row is an instance of $Fit_*$ toward $S$ or $R$. Therefore:

\[
GoodOrient_S(G)\land GoodOrient_R(G)
\]

is earned in the architecture, in the sense of the attribute map: it follows from several current commitments taken together, none of which was adopted on evaluative grounds. In plain language: everything the project has managed to establish about $G$'s dispositions runs toward the agency of conscious subjects and toward truthful relation with them.

## Step 3: what is not earned, and why the gap is exactly the problem of evil

No row in Step 2 concerns $P$. The ontology has not established any divine disposition toward the valence of created subjects: neither that $G$ aims at their felt good nor that permitted suffering is fit-preserving. This is the **P-gap**:

\[
GoodOrient_P(G)\;\text{— open.}
\]

The gap is not an accident of incomplete work. It is where the problem of evil enters the architecture (S27: “the point at which the problem of evil becomes internal”). C115 explains a presumption against replacing agency; it does not explain permitted pain. C206–C207 explain what evil is; they do not explain why a good-oriented ground permits it. Until the P-gap is treated, the accessible answer must say so.

Omnibenevolence is further away than $GoodOrient_P$. It requires universality over anchors, bearers, contexts, and acts, together with a no-misfit guarantee; the project has neither the divine-valence account nor the theodicy nor the perfection premise this would need, and R03 forbids importing the last.

## Step 4: the plain-language license

The accessible track may therefore say, in the first person and with the status marked:

1. *What has been established:* every trait of $G$ the argument reaches is oriented toward the freedom of conscious beings and toward truthful relation with them. This is a result, not a hope.
2. *What is believed beyond the result:* that the same orientation extends to what conscious beings feel, including their suffering. The project holds this as belief with a recorded burden; it is where faith, in the S28 sense of practical orientation under incomplete evidence, does explicit work.
3. *What is not claimed:* that $G$ is perfectly good in every respect, or that the world is the best possible (C132).

## Adversarial tests

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| The anchors were chosen to match $G$, so the derivation is circular | The anchors were selected in S27 to block specific collapses in the theory of finite good; the divine features were established earlier and independently. Circularity would require an evaluative premise in either selection, and none is present. |
| A ground of fit that instantiates the kinds automatically satisfies fit | Rejected (R30). Instantiating the kind of sourcehood grounds what sourcehood-fit is; whether a given act protects created sourcehood is a further fact, which S19 argues case by case. |
| Good-orientation toward $S$ and $R$ is trivial: any creator of agents “aims at agency” | Not trivial: a creator could script every event (rejected R04), override sourcehood freely, or withhold availability. C106, C115, and C122 are substantive and could have failed. |
| The P-gap makes the whole account empty, because suffering is what people ask about | The gap is real and is recorded as the next load-bearing item. The account is not empty: it locates precisely which claim the objection targets and forbids answering it by fiat. |
| “Good-oriented” smuggles in omnibenevolence through language | The two are distinguished formally; R180 forbids the inference. |

## Literature and novelty notes

Verified online in the 2026-09-02 literature pass; exact sources, links, and qualifications are recorded in [the provenance checklist](../research/provenance-checklist.md).

- Robert M. Adams, *Finite and Infinite Goods* (Oxford, 1999), grounds excellence in resemblance to God; the present route differs by grounding fit in kinds $G$ instantiates or grounds, without a resemblance premise or a prior claim that $G$ is excellent.
- Mark Murphy, *God's Own Ethics* (Oxford, 2017), directly treats creaturely existence and welfare as giving God justifying but non-requiring reasons. The P-gap is the project's reconstruction of the remaining gap between such reasons and a requiring norm.
- Classification: established close precedent with a project-specific derivation and reason profile. The literature pass does not support treating the partial orientation as an otherwise unlabeled gap.
