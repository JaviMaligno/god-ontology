# Session: The ground of necessary structure

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S88
**Primary question:** What grounds necessary structure: the logical and identity truths, mathematical structure, and the type-level constitutive profiles that fix what can be created (open question 5, C350–C351)? The author's direction after S86: the reading on which that structure is $G$'s own way of being fits the architecture better, the rival reading already says the structure is not apart from $G$, and the register should give a decided answer.
**Dependencies:** S38 (C343–C351, C355); S57/S60 (C529, C542); S05 (C12, C48, C51–C54); S30 (C224–C227, $MCS$); S32 (C264); S47 (C457); S51 (C492); S73 ($UCD_G$, C586–C594); S86 (C697); C55–C60, C363

**Target depth:** F2 for the grounding thesis and its exclusions; the participation relation itself is left at the same depth as $Fit_*$'s realization (C195), a preferred provisional bridge

## Outcome

Necessary structure is grounded in $G$'s nature, not in $G$'s will, not in $G$'s knowledge, not in $G$'s power, and not in a realm apart from $G$. The thesis, **essential-structure grounding** ($ESG$), says: the logical and identity truths hold of $G$'s nature first and of every dependent as a consequence, since a dependent shares the form of being of that on which it depends; and the space of instantiable profiles, including the mathematical structure of possible plurality, is the space of ways $G$'s being can be participated by dependents. The structure is not made, because it is not the result of any exercise; it is not apart from $G$, because it is how $G$ is; and it is not ours, because we abstract it from a created order made according to it. This is the exemplarist reading, restated without divine simplicity, divine ideas as a second realm, or a logic of participation the project has not built.

The two readings the register offered (S86) converge under $ESG$: “authorless” is true, because no will authored the structure; “from $G$” is true, because the structure is $G$'s nature as participable. The rival that survives as a fallback is primitive necessity: structure that is nothing apart from $G$ but is grounded in nothing. It is coherent, and it is disfavored by the same weak explanatory preference that prefers one terminus for concreta (C457, C592): it leaves the form of everything as a second unexplained fundamental. The rival that is rejected is a Platonic realm as an independent stopping point, and the rival that is rejected outright is voluntarism, on which necessary truths are willed and thereby contingent.

$ESG$ is proposed preferred at F2, abductively, at the same evidential level as universal concrete dependence (C591), of which it is the structural counterpart. It changes no clause of the criterion of possibility, which stays free of every $G$-clause (C529, C542): $ESG$ says why the criterion holds, not what it says.

## Step 1: what has to be grounded, and what the ledger already forbids

Three kinds of item are in question (C52, C350): logical and identity truths; mathematical structure; and the type-level constitutive profiles that fix which created kinds are possible without a token of them existing. S38 left their ultimate ground open among three options: necessary structural fact, grounding in independently established features of $G$, or a mixed account (S38, “What exists on this account”).

The ledger already forbids four answers.

1. **Voluntarism.** A settlement has live alternatives (C55); a necessary truth has none; so a willed necessary truth is a contradiction in terms. If $G$ willed that two and two make four, it could have willed otherwise, and the truth would be contingent. C363 already blocks the transfer of necessity from bearer to exercise; the reverse transfer, from exercise to necessity, is the same error. Necessary structure is not an exercise of $G$'s will.
2. **Cognitive constitution.** MKO tracks admissibility; divine representation does not make represented possibilities true (C355; C12's basis excludes cognitive truthmaking). So necessary structure is not constituted by $G$'s knowing it. Divine conceptualism in its strong form, on which abstracta *are* divine thoughts and are true because thought, reverses the project's grounding order.
3. **Power constitution.** Possibility must not be defined through what $G$ can create (C274, C529): that was S33's circularity, and S38 removed it. Necessary structure is not grounded in $G$'s power.
4. **A rival concrete or co-ultimate ground.** Necessary truths are constraints, not substances, agents, or causes (C264, C351, C429); nothing here reintroduces a second concrete terminus.

What remains open after these exclusions is the choice among: $G$'s *nature* as the ground; primitive necessity; and an independent abstract realm.

## Step 2: essential-structure grounding

Let $Nat(G)$ be $G$'s nature, the independently established essence profile $E_G^{ind}$ together with whatever $G$ essentially is beyond what the project has enumerated. Write $Dep(x,G)$ for existential dependence (C586) and $Profile(\pi)$ for a type-level constitutive profile (C350). $ESG$ has two clauses.

**$ESG_1$ (form of being).** The logical and identity truths are true in virtue of $Nat(G)$ and hold of every dependent as a consequence of its dependence:

\[
\Box\,[\,TrueOf(\lambda,G)\ \text{in virtue of}\ Nat(G)\,]\land\Box\forall x\,[Dep(x,G)\rightarrow TrueOf(\lambda,x)]
\]

for each logical or identity truth $\lambda$. The grounding relation is Finean “true in virtue of the nature of” (S38's literature), not causation or production: nothing is made to be non-contradictory; being non-contradictory is part of what it is to be, and $G$ is the underived case of being. A dependent could not fail to share the form of being of its source, since to depend existentially on $G$ is to be a way of being that $G$'s being supports; a dependent that violated identity or non-contradiction would not be a way of being at all.

**$ESG_2$ (participable profiles).** A type-level constitutive profile is admissible iff it is a way $G$'s being can be participated by a dependent:

\[
\Box\forall\pi\,[Adm_C(\pi)\leftrightarrow Participable(\pi,Nat(G))],
\]

where $Participable(\pi,Nat(G))$ holds when a bearer instantiating $\pi$ would be a coherent dependent of $G$: coherent in itself ($ESG_1$), coherent in its powers and their compossibility, and coherent as dependent (the created-dependence clause $DepSat_C$ that $Adm_C$ already contains). The left-hand side is the criterion S38 and S57 built and S60 reconciled; the right-hand side is its ground. The biconditional is not a redefinition: $Adm_C$ keeps its clauses and its criterion independence (C343, C529), and $ESG_2$ says that what makes those clauses the right ones is that they spell out what it is to be a possible dependent of $G$.

**Mathematical structure** falls under $ESG_2$. Number, order, and quantity are the structure of possible plurality: of there being many distinct dependents, each a way of participating one source. That structure is grounded in $G$'s nature as participable *in many ways*, which is the exemplarist thesis in its minimal form: one nature, many possible imitations, and the many on the side of the dependents (Aquinas, ST I q.15 a.2; q.44 a.3). The created order instantiates that structure, and finite knowers abstract it from the created many, which is why mathematics is discovered by abstraction and yet is not ours (S86, C697).

## Step 3: why $ESG$ is preferred over the two survivors

**Against an independent abstract realm.** A Platonic realm is not a concrete rival (C264), so $UCD_G$ does not reach it (C593), and the objection to it is not that it competes with $G$. The objection is explanatory. The project prefers one underived terminus for concreta on grounds of explanatory unity, well-founded dependence, the absence of a positive role for a second terminus, and the costs of plural ultimacy (C592). Every one of those grounds applies to structure: an independent realm of necessary truths is a second fundamental with no dependence on the first, no positive role the first cannot play, and a brute concordance between what the realm permits and what $G$'s nature admits. The concordance is the decisive cost. On an independent-realm view, it is a further unexplained necessity that the structure of possible dependents happens to match what $G$'s nature can support; on $ESG$ there is one fact, not two.

**Against primitive necessity.** Primitive necessity is the honest fallback: structure that is grounded in nothing and is not a realm, because it is nothing apart from what it is true of. It is coherent, and the project keeps it as the fallback in the same way that a free-standing necessary structural relation is the fallback for $Fit_*$ (C195). It is disfavored by the weak explanatory preference (C457) exactly as a brute necessary concretum is (C592): it leaves the form of everything as a second unexplained fundamental beside $G$. The author's observation is correct that this reading already concedes the point that matters, that the structure is not apart from $G$; $ESG$ adds only that it is not ungrounded either.

**Why $ESG$ does not cost what the rejected options cost.** $ESG$ attributes no will, no cognition, and no power to the grounding relation, so it inherits none of the four exclusions of Step 1. It does not make $G$ complex: the many profiles are not parts or ingredients of $G$ ($MCS$, C224), and a power or a participable way is an aspect, not a constituent (C492); the multiplicity is on the side of the dependents. It does not make creation necessary: $ESG$ concerns which dependents are possible, and $G$'s settling among eligible structures, or on none, remains free (C111, C363, C522).

## Step 4: the bootstrapping objection

The standard objection to grounding necessary truths in $G$ is that $G$'s nature is itself described using logic and identity, so the truths would have to be prior to the nature that grounds them. The reply is that the objection reads grounding as production. $ESG_1$ says the logical truths hold *in virtue of* $G$'s nature, and a nature is not prior to itself: “$G$ is self-identical” is true in virtue of what $G$ is, and requires no prior fact of identity that $G$ then satisfies. This is Fine's direction, essence to modality, applied at the terminus. The truths about $G$ itself, that $G$ exists necessarily and is the underived source, are grounded in $Nat(G)$ in the same way. There is no regress because nothing in $Nat(G)$ is produced, and no circle because “in virtue of” is not “caused by.”

A second form of the objection asks whether $ESG_2$ secretly reintroduces divine power. It does not: $Participable(\pi,Nat(G))$ is a constitutive relation between a profile and a nature, and it can hold of a profile whose instantiation $G$ never selects and could fail of a profile $G$ might be imagined to select. The bridge from admissibility to capacity is still $CP_G^{elig}$ with its own premises (C372–C376); $ESG$ is upstream of it and adds no premise to it.

## Step 5: the order of the argument

The author's worry in S86 was that the argument to $G$ uses necessary truths, so that grounding them in $G$ would make the argument circular. It does not, for the reason S03 already gave for knowledge and history: the order of explanation is not the order of discovery. We reason with logic to the conclusion that the contingent order has an underived source; $ESG$ then says what that logic is, ontologically. A ruler is used to measure the building before one learns what rulers are made of. The argument's premises are the contingency data and the weak explanatory preference (C01–C05, C457), not any claim about the ground of logic; $ESG$ is a downstream result that explains a tool, not an upstream premise that the tool presupposes.

## Adversarial tests

### Does $ESG_2$ restore the S33 circularity?

No. C343 anticipated this: independence in the criterion is independence from divine creative capacity, knowledge, actual choice, and creaturely representation, “not yet absolute ontological independence from $G$.” $ESG$ supplies exactly the ontological dependence C343 left room for while touching no clause of the criterion (C529, C542).

### Does $G$'s nature then contain all of mathematics?

$G$'s nature is participable in many ways; the many ways are the profiles, on the side of the dependents. What $G$ has is one nature and, in MKO, complete knowledge of its participability (C12, C126). This is the exemplarist answer and it is compatible with $MCS$; it is not compatible with absolute divine simplicity, which the project has not adopted (C227), and it does not need it.

### Is a necessary truth about nothing, such as a theorem about numbers no created order instantiates, grounded in $G$?

Yes, under $ESG_2$: it is true of the structure of possible plurality, which is a way $G$'s being can be participated whether or not it is. Uninstantiated profiles were exactly C350's burden, and this is where it is discharged.

### Could a naturalist accept $ESG$ with the structure as the fundamental item and drop $G$?

That is the position C03 records as a competitor. Against it the project has only what it has against every impersonal terminus: a structure explains how what exists must be, not that anything exists (C458–C460); the contingency argument is unaffected by where structure is grounded. $ESG$ presupposes $G$; it is not an argument for $G$.

### Does $ESG$ make the impossible impossible “because of $G$”?

In the sense that a square circle is not a way of participating $G$'s being, yes; in the sense that $G$ forbids it, no. Impossibility is the absence of a participable profile, not a prohibition (C347, C353).

## Literature and precedent

Recorded from memory; to be verified in the author's next literature pass. Aquinas, ST I q.15 a.2 (many ideas, one essence known as imitable) and q.44 a.3 (God as exemplar cause) are the precedent for $ESG_2$; Descartes's creation of the eternal truths is the voluntarist position R281 rejects; Leibniz's location of the eternal truths in the divine understanding is the conceptualist neighbor from which $ESG$ differs by grounding in nature rather than in intellect. Brian Leftow, *God and Necessity* (2012), defends a theistic ground of modal truth and confronts the problem of truths about God; Michael Bergmann and Jeffrey Brower, “A Theistic Argument against Platonism (and in Support of Truthmakers and Divine Simplicity)” (2006), state the bootstrapping objection that C709 answers and argue the aseity–sovereignty pressure against Platonism that Step 3 uses; Thomas Morris and Christopher Menzel, “Absolute Creation” (1986), are the theistic-activist position that $ESG$ declines by refusing production. Kit Fine, “Essence and Modality” (1994), supplies the direction of grounding. The project-specific element is the pairing of $ESG$ with a criterion of possibility that contains no $G$-clause, so that the ground and the criterion are kept apart. No novelty claim.
