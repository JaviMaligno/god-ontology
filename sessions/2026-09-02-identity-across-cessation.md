# Session: Identity across the cessation of the realization base

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S47
**Primary question:** What secures the identity of a post-mortem subject with its embodied predecessor, under each open realization model of the subject, without adopting primitive haecceities by stipulation; and can the gappy existence of one individual be coherent?
**Dependencies:** C08, C33, C53, C55–C60, C122, C183–C184, C212, C219, C223, C390–C396, C405, S03, S05, S29, S44, S45
**Origin:** Author's instruction of 2026-09-02 to open CH21 while CH03 is completed locally
**Target depth:** F2 for the persistence conditions of created subjects; realization model not selected

## Starting position

S44 showed that continuation is coherent under every open realization model and relocated the burden to the identity relation $Ident(B,B^+)$. S45 sharpened the target: a created subject is a $Substance_T$, an identity unit that bears powers, whose material profile is instantiated by its realization base; re-realization would be re-instantiation of $\Pi_{phys}$ by the same identity unit (C405). CH21 forbids settling identity by stipulation and asks specifically about the replica objection, gappy existence, and the Parfitian claim that identity is not what matters.

The session's method is to ask what persistence conditions the ontology is already committed to for created subjects within a life, and then to test whether those conditions extend across a gap. If the within-life account is reductive — persistence as physical or psychological continuity — the gap is fatal or nearly so. If it is not, the gap is a different problem from the one usually posed.

## Definitions

| Term/symbol | Definition used in this session |
| --- | --- |
| $Ident(B_1,B_2)$ | Numerical identity of subjects at different times or in different histories |
| $PhysCont(B_1,B_2)$ | Continuity of the realization base: the same organism, brain, or functional organization, or a suitably connected succession of them |
| $PsyCont(B_1,B_2)$ | Psychological continuity: overlapping chains of memory, intention, character, and other psychological connections |
| reductive persistence | $Ident(B_1,B_2)$ is constituted by, or analyzable as, $PhysCont$, $PsyCont$, or a combination, possibly with a non-branching clause |
| simple persistence | $Ident(B_1,B_2)$ is not analyzable in terms of continuity relations; continuity relations are evidence for, and normal realization conditions of, persistence, not its constitution |
| identity unit | The $Substance_T$ subject that bears powers and originates acts (C223, C405); “source” when the emphasis is on origination |
| replica | A subject qualitatively identical to $B$ at some time but numerically distinct |
| gappy existence | One individual located at temporally disconnected intervals of created time |

## Step 1: what the ontology already requires within a life

Three commitments fix the persistence conditions of created subjects before any question about death arises.

**De re identity across histories (C53, S03).** The structural test for created freedom uses two histories $h_a,h_b$ that share a past and differ in what the same agent $A$ does at $t$. The agent in $h_a$ and the agent in $h_b$ have different psychological futures and, downstream, different physical ones. If $Ident$ were constituted by $PsyCont$ or $PhysCont$, the two agents would not be the same agent, and “$A$ could have done otherwise” would become “a different agent does otherwise.” Libertarian sourcehood as the project uses it therefore requires that the identity of an agent not be constituted by the continuity relations its acts produce.

**Whole-agent attribution (C212, S29).** Acts are attributed to the unified temporally persisting subject, not to a stage, a brain, or a psychological chain. Within a life the realization base changes continuously and psychological connectedness can be interrupted by amnesia, radical conversion, or the agent's own originating discontinuities. The subject remains the same source through these; that is why S29 could distinguish preparation, settlement, and retrospective ownership without changing the subject.

**Source-indexed sourcehood (C55–C60).** Sourcehood attaches to the subject that originates the act. If persistence were a matter of continuity chains, sourcehood would attach to chains, and an agent whose chain forked would have originated acts of two later subjects. The project's account of contrastive authorship (C33) requires one source.

Together these entail that the project is already committed to **simple persistence** for created subjects:

\[
Ident(B_1,B_2)\;\not\equiv\;PhysCont(B_1,B_2)\lor PsyCont(B_1,B_2)\lor\ldots
\]

This is a derivation, not a stipulation of haecceities. It says that the identity unit is prior to the history it produces, which is exactly what a source must be. Continuity relations retain their full evidential and realization roles: they are how persistence is normally realized and how it is known.

The empirical bridge (C183–C184) is respected. Amnesia, dissociation, and split-brain phenomena are disruptions of $PsyCont$ or of the base's integration; they are evidence about the subject's condition, not evidence that a different subject has appeared, unless a bridge premise identifies subjects with chains, which is precisely the reductive thesis at issue. Fission cases are treated in Step 4.

## Step 2: what simple persistence does to the gap

Under reductive persistence, gappy existence is the familiar problem: the chain is broken, and any later subject connected to $B$ only by $G$'s act is at best a replica. Under simple persistence the question changes. There is no continuity relation whose interruption ends $B$, because no continuity relation constituted $B$'s persistence in the first place. The interruption of the realization base ends the realization of $B$; whether it ends $B$ depends on whether the identity unit can be unrealized (persistence route) or re-realized (re-realization route). Neither is excluded by the persistence conditions.

E+ removes the remaining intuition. In the complete actual history, $B$'s pre-mortem stages and any post-mortem stages are all equally actual constituents of $\mathfrak R^*$ (C22–C28, C47). Gappy existence is temporally disconnected location of one object, not a ceasing followed by a making of something new. The picture on which a gap is fatal — the object goes out of existence, so the next thing is a fresh start — is presentist. Under eternalism nothing "goes out of" existence; an object has the temporal location it has, connected or not. The identity fact across the gap is a fact in $\mathfrak R^*$, known by ACO (C11) without constituting it, as ACO knows any other identity fact.

So the coherence question is settled in the ontology's own terms: given simple persistence and E+, one individual with temporally disconnected location is coherent. What is not thereby settled is how the later stage comes to be that individual's, which is the question of Step 3.

## Step 3: the divine act and de re targeting

$G$ individuates each actual conscious subject (C122); ACO tracks identity facts and does not make them (by the same logic as C10 and C30). Re-realization is an exercise of $P_{OEC}$ or a further creative act (C393) whose immediate effect is that an identity unit is realized at $t^+$. The question is whether that act can have $B$ *de re* as its target rather than a qualitative specification that any replica would satisfy.

Under simple persistence the answer is yes, for two reasons. First, $B$ is not a qualitative type; C53 already distinguishes de re identity of actual agents from the qualitative types available for merely possible ones. $B$, being actual, has de re identity in the model. Second, the divine act is not a description that something must fit; it is an exercise directed at what $G$ individuates. The act makes the later stage actual; it does not decide which individual it is by comparing candidates, because there is no independent later candidate to compare. The later stage is $B$'s because the act that makes it actual is $B$'s re-realization, and there is no further identity-making fact.

This is where CH21's question whether $G$'s knowledge fixes identity or only tracks it receives its answer: knowledge tracks; the re-realizing act makes the later stage actual as $B$'s; nothing constitutes $B$'s identity beyond $B$. The account does not add haecceities; it declines to add a criterion, and it says why the ontology never needed one.

## Step 4: the tests

| Test | Result under simple persistence and E+ |
| --- | --- |
| two candidates equally continuous with $B$ | At most one is $B$; continuity does not decide, because it never constituted identity. The identity fact obtains and is known by ACO. Finite observers cannot tell; that is an epistemic limit, not a metaphysical indeterminacy. |
| re-realization with partial psychological continuity | Coherent; psychological continuity is the normal realization of a persisting subject's memory and character, and a re-realized $B$ with partial continuity is $B$ with damaged or restored memory, as within life. Full continuity is neither necessary nor sufficient. |
| persistence of a bearer with no realized capacities | Coherent only on realization models where the identity unit is not identical to its base (non-reductive embodied, dual-aspect, hylomorphic, dualist). Under reductive models there is no bearer to persist; only the re-realization route remains. The session does not decide between routes. |
| replica made by $G$ | A replica is a numerically distinct identity unit instantiating $B$'s qualitative profile. It is not $B$, and $G$, who individuates both, knows it is not. The objection assumes that $G$'s act can only target a qualitative profile; Step 3 denies this. |
| fission (a brain divided, both halves sustaining a subject) | Simple persistence says at most one resulting subject is $B$, or neither; there is a fact, and it is not fixed by symmetry of continuity. The result is counterintuitive only if one assumes continuity constitutes identity. Fission remains the hardest test and is recorded as such. |
| Parfit: identity is not what matters | The project's anchors are subject-indexed: this subject's valence, this subject's sourcehood, recognition of this subject. If what mattered were continuity, sourcehood would attach to chains and contrastive authorship would fork; the agency model excludes this. Identity is what matters for a source, because a source is not a chain. |

## Step 5: consequences for the realization models

C219 left five realization models open. The persistence result constrains them without selecting one:

| Model | Compatibility with simple persistence | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| reductive organismic or neural | fails: identifies the subject with its base or organization, so $Ident$ is constituted by $PhysCont$, contradicting the de re identity the freedom model needs and failing the replica and fission tests | `disfavored` |
| non-reductive embodied subject | compatible if the subject's identity is not constituted by its base | live |
| dual-aspect | compatible if the common bearer is the identity unit | live |
| hylomorphic | compatible; the subsistent form is the identity unit | live |
| substance dualism | compatible; the non-physical subject is the identity unit | live |

The mind–body branch remains deferred, but with a new constraint: any realization model must treat the subject as an identity unit whose persistence is not constituted by continuity relations. This constraint follows from libertarian sourcehood and whole-agent attribution, not from the afterlife question, and would stand even if continuation were rejected.

## Step 6: what remains open

1. The choice between the persistence route and the re-realization route, which depends on the realization model.
2. Whether an identity unit can exist wholly unrealized, which the persistence route requires.
3. Fission at the created level, where simple persistence gives a determinate but epistemically inaccessible answer.
4. Whether the realization base's material profile is the same or a different instance after re-realization; S45's constraint requires only that the same identity unit instantiate $\Pi_{phys}$ again, not the same instance.

None of these reopens the coherence of continuation; each concerns its mode.

## Adversarial tests

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Simple persistence is haecceitism under another name | Haecceitism posits a primitive thisness property; the present view declines to analyze identity and derives that refusal from what sources must be. It adds no entity. |
| Deriving persistence from libertarian sourcehood is circular | The derivation runs from C53, C212, and C33, which were adopted for reasons independent of persistence; if libertarian sourcehood is rejected, the derivation lapses, and the ledger says so. |
| Eternalism makes every scattered object one object | No; scattered location is coherent, not sufficient. Which stages belong to one subject is a fact of $\mathfrak R^*$; eternalism removes the presentist reason for thinking a gap is fatal, not the need for the identity fact. |
| “There is no criterion” is a refusal to answer | It is the answer the project's commitments entail, with an argument for why a criterion was never needed; Merricks defends the same position independently of theology. |
| Fission refutes the simple view | Fission is where every view pays; the simple view pays with epistemic inaccessibility, reductive views with indeterminacy or with arbitrary non-branching clauses. |
| The account lets $G$ make anyone into anyone | No; $G$ can re-realize $B$ or create a distinct unit, and cannot make a distinct unit be $B$, because identity is not made by any act. |

## Literature and novelty notes

Cited from memory; not verified online in this session.

- Trenton Merricks, “There Are No Criteria of Identity Over Time” (*Noûs*, 1998): the non-criterial view defended without theological premises; the closest precedent for Step 3.
- Roderick Chisholm, *Person and Object* (1976); Richard Swinburne in Shoemaker and Swinburne, *Personal Identity* (1984); E. J. Lowe, *Subjects of Experience* (1996): the simple view of persistence.
- Derek Parfit, *Reasons and Persons* (1984): psychological continuity and the claim that identity is not what matters; Sydney Shoemaker for the reductive alternative.
- Peter van Inwagen, “The Possibility of Resurrection” (1978), and Dean Zimmerman, “The Compatibility of Materialism and Survival” (1999): the materialist versions of the gap problem, which the present result shows to be artifacts of reductive persistence.
- David Wiggins, *Sameness and Substance Renewed* (2001), for substance-based persistence; Aquinas on the subsistent soul as identity unit.
- Stanford Encyclopedia entry on personal identity for the survey.

Classification: the simple view and its rivals are established; the project-specific elements are the derivation of simple persistence from libertarian sourcehood, de re modal identity, and whole-agent attribution, the eternalist dissolution of the gap intuition, and the de re targeting account of re-realization. No novelty claim.
