# Session: Intentional content and reference: CH14's remaining parts

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S89
**Primary question:** S38 and S57 fixed the modal status of imagined content and left open the ontology of content, reference to fictions and impossibilities, and the causal efficacy of thought (C534; open question 13). What kind of reality do these have, given the embodied subject of S37 and the ground of necessary structure of S88?
**Dependencies:** S38 (C343–C354, its five categories); S57/S60 (C534, C542); S05 (C53–C54); S34 (C284–C286); S36–S37 (C310–C321, C330–C338); S88 (C706–C707); CH14
**Origin:** the question-driven track's next item after S87–S88 (development map item 22; open question 13)
**Target depth:** F2 for the partition of act, vehicle, satisfaction condition, and referent, and for the treatment of the four hard cases; the intentionality bridge is left at F3 beside phenomenality

## Outcome

An act of imagining or thinking has three actual constituents and one that need not be actual. The **act** is a temporal manifestation of a power of the embodied subject (C334). Its **vehicle** is the representational organization the act realizes, which is actual and located in the subject's realization base. Its **satisfaction condition** is a specification, complete or partial (C347), that an item would have to meet for the act to be fulfilled; it is objective, because specifications are instantiable profiles, and by S88 those are ways $G$'s being can be participated (C706), not members of a third realm. The **referent**, when there is one, is the actual item that meets the satisfaction condition under an anchoring relation; when nothing actual meets it, there is no referent, and the act is about a specification. This is immanent-content actualism with objective satisfaction conditions, and it closes CH14's remaining parts at F2.

The hard cases are handled by the same four items. Imagining a merely possible individual is having a vehicle whose satisfaction condition is a profile plus an assigned label; there is no possibile to which the act is related, since a merely possible individual has no identity ground (C53). Thinking an impossibility is having a vehicle whose satisfaction condition has no admissible completion but whose parts are separately satisfiable, which is why it can be entertained and why it cannot be fulfilled (C347). A fiction is a specification under stipulation with an actual authorial provenance; fictional characters are individuated by that provenance, which is why two readers think of the same Holmes without there being a Holmes. Divine knowledge of all this is already provided for: the act, vehicle, and the fact that the act has the content it has are actual and fall under ACO; the admissibility of the satisfaction condition falls under MKO; what the imagining is like for the subject falls under $ASA$; and there is nothing further to know, because there is no possibile (C54). Thought is causally efficacious through its vehicle; content-involving explanation is constitutive, not a second cause (C335), so there is neither epiphenomenalism nor overdetermination. What remains is the intentionality bridge, how an embodied organization comes to bear a satisfaction condition at all, and it is the same F3 burden as phenomenality (C337–C338), not a burden CH14 adds.

## Step 1: the four items

For an act $i$ of subject $A$ at $t$:

| Item | Symbol | Status | Ground |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| act | $i$ | actual, temporal (C312, C352) | manifestation of a power of the whole embodied subject through its realization base (C334) |
| vehicle | $V(i)$ | actual | the representational organization the act realizes; part of the subject's exercise, not a second agent (C334) |
| satisfaction condition | $Sat(i)=\sigma$ | objective, not an event | a specification, complete or partial; an instantiable profile or a description built from such profiles; grounded as in C706 |
| referent | $Ref(i)$ | actual if any | the actual item meeting $\sigma$ under the act's anchoring relation |

S38's minimum, $Img(A,i,p,t)\rightarrow Actual(i,t)\land Rep(i,p)$, is preserved: $Rep(i,p)$ is $Sat(i)=\sigma_p$. What the session adds is the separation of vehicle from satisfaction condition, so that “content” no longer names two things at once (the actual organization and the objective condition), and the location of the objective condition in profiles rather than in a realm of propositions. Nothing here needs an abstract proposition as a further entity: the satisfaction condition is a specification, and specifications are what S38's category 5 already contained, now grounded by S88.

## Step 2: reference

$Ref(i)$ is the actual item that meets $Sat(i)$ under the act's **anchoring relation**: for perception and memory, a causal-historical relation to the item; for descriptive thought, satisfaction of the description by exactly one actual item; for $G$, actual subject acquaintance (C284). Two consequences follow.

First, reference is a relation to actual items only. When no actual item meets $\sigma$, the act has no referent and is about $\sigma$. This is not a defect of the act; it is what being about a specification is. The project's actualism (C349) already denies concrete possibilia, and C53 denies that merely possible individuals have identity grounds; so there is nothing for a non-actual-directed act to be related to but its own satisfaction condition.

Second, the anchoring relation does the individuating work that intuition assigns to “the object.” Two acts are about the same actual item when their anchoring relations converge on it, whatever their descriptions; two acts about non-actual specifications are about the same specification when their satisfaction conditions coincide, and about “the same individual” only in the sense that they share a label or a provenance (Step 4).

## Step 3: the possible and the impossible

**Merely possible individuals.** Imagining “a particular person who could have existed” is having a vehicle whose satisfaction condition is a profile together with an assigned label (Kung's assigned content; S38). The label adds no constitutive detail and no identity ground, so the act is about the profile, and “this possible person” is a manner of speaking. C53 and C54 already say the corresponding thing for $G$: MKO knows possible types, powers, and relations, not ungrounded individuals, and omniscience is not limited by the absence of a truth that has no truthmaker. The account explains why de re modal thought about actual individuals is different: an actual person has an identity ground, and an act anchored to her can represent her otherwise than she is (C53's de re alternatives).

**Impossibilities.** Thinking a square circle is having a vehicle whose satisfaction condition has no admissible completion (C347, C353). The condition is representable because representation requires a vehicle with a satisfaction condition, not a satisfier, and it is entertainable because its parts, square and circle, are each satisfiable; what fails is their conjunction. Under S88 this has an exact gloss: there is no participable profile for the conjunction (C706), and the subject's act, which is actual, latches onto two profiles at once and a conjunction that is not one. This also explains why impossibilities can seem possible on inspection (C354): the vehicle presents the parts, and the absence of a completion is not itself presented.

## Step 4: fictions

A fiction is a specification under a **stipulation operator**: $Fic(\sigma,h)$, where $h$ is the actual authorial history that introduced $\sigma$. Three things follow.

- *Individuation.* A fictional character is individuated by provenance, not by a possibile: readers think of the same Holmes because their acts inherit their satisfaction conditions from the same actual history $h$, not because there is a Holmes they are all related to. This is why a character can be inconsistent across a corpus without ceasing to be one character: the provenance is one, the specification need not be admissible.
- *Truth in fiction.* “In the stories, Holmes lives on Baker Street” is a truth about $\sigma$ as stipulated in $h$, together with the import rules the practice of fiction supplies (what is carried over from the actual world by default). It is an actual truth, since $h$ and its stipulations are actual, and it falls under ACO.
- *Reference.* Fictional names have no referent and are about $\sigma$ under $h$; where a fiction uses an actual item (London), the act is anchored to that item for that part of $\sigma$ and stipulated for the rest.

Fiction therefore requires no fictional objects and no non-existent objects, and it requires exactly what the account already has: actual acts, actual vehicles, objective specifications, and actual histories.

## Step 5: divine knowledge of thought

$G$ knows every actual thought completely and knows nothing that is not there to be known. The act, its vehicle, and the fact that it has satisfaction condition $\sigma$ are actual and belong to $\mathfrak R^*$ (C47, C352); ACO has them. Whether $\sigma$ is admissible, and what its completions are, MKO has (C355). What the act is like for $A$, $ASA$ has (C284–C286). Whether there is an individual that $\sigma$ would be, there is no truth to know when $\sigma$ is not anchored to an actual individual (C53–C54). This closes a question CH14 raised without a separate divine-psychology module: divine knowledge of imagination is divine knowledge of actual acts and of modal structure, already provided for.

## Step 6: causal efficacy

The act is a manifestation of a power of the whole subject through its realization base (C334); it is actual and temporally located; it is therefore a cause in the ordinary sense, whatever its target (C352 already says this). The question CH14 left was whether *content* is efficacious or idle. The answer is that the vehicle is what has the satisfaction condition, and the vehicle is what the subject's power manifests; explanation by content (“she reached for it because she took it to be the key”) is constitutive explanation of the act's organization, and constitutive and causal explanation are non-competing typed explanations of one process (C335). There is no second cause, so no overdetermination; and the satisfaction condition is not idle, because it is not a separate item that would have to do causal work: it is what the efficacious organization is an organization *for*.

What this does not explain is how an embodied organization comes to have a satisfaction condition at all. That is the intentionality bridge, and the project places it where it placed phenomenality: a fundamental power of certain organized subjects at F2 (C337), with a subject-level dual-aspect account as the strongest deeper bridge (C338). Whether intentionality reduces to phenomenality, or the converse, or neither, is an F3 question this session does not enter.

## Adversarial tests

### Is a satisfaction condition just an abstract proposition under another name?

It is a specification, and the project already had specifications (S38's category 5) and has now grounded them (S88). What is refused is a further entity, a proposition, over and above the specification that the vehicle is organized to satisfy. If someone calls specifications propositions, nothing changes except the word, provided the realm is not reintroduced (C708).

### Does the account make thought about the non-actual second-rate?

It makes it about specifications rather than about objects. That is the right result: the thought that there might have been a tenth planet is a thought about a profile, and it is true or false according to admissibility (C528), not according to a planet.

### Does provenance-individuation make fictional characters actual?

Their provenance is actual; they are not. A character is a specification with a history, and the history is what one refers to when one says the character was “created” by an author: the author created the stipulation, not a being.

### Does divine knowledge of my imagining violate its privacy or my freedom?

$ASA$ is factive access, not origination (C287); imagining is not a settlement unless it issues in one; and what is known is what is actual. Nothing here changes S34's results.

### Does the account presuppose a solution to the intentionality bridge?

It presupposes that some acts have satisfaction conditions, which the project already presupposed in attributing representation to subjects (C310, C352). It locates the unexplained item exactly and does not disguise it.

## Literature and precedent

Recorded from memory; to be verified in the author's next literature pass. The vehicle/content distinction and satisfaction conditions follow Searle, *Intentionality* (1983); the treatment of fictional characters by provenance is close to Kripke's *Reference and Existence* (2013) and to Amie Thomasson's *Fiction and Metaphysics* (1999), differing in that no abstract artifact is posited, only a stipulated specification with a history; the denial of possibilia as relata is actualist in the manner of Plantinga's and Stalnaker's actualism, with the difference that specifications are grounded in the divine nature rather than in a realm of properties or propositions; the treatment of impossible contents by unsatisfiable conjunction of satisfiable parts follows Kung's diagnosis of assigned content (S38). The constitutive/causal non-competition is S37's. No novelty claim.
