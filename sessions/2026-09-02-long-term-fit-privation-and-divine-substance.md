# Session: Long-term preference, epistemic sacrifice, privative evil, and divine substance

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S28
**Primary question:** Can the axiological interface positively favor the long term, replace the excessive information condition, clarify objective fit through due-good privation, and route the question of divine substance into the ontological spine?
**Dependencies:** C162–C197, S24–S27; C84–C103, S14 and S17
**Target depth:** Local F2 repair of the interface and F1 reprioritization of divine constitution

## Four authorial corrections

1. Long-term preference was an adequacy test for the theory of goodness, not merely permission to count future subjects. The account must positively explain why durable or transpersonal goods can defeat transient benefits. The project need not construct or endorse every component of strong longtermism.
2. “Informed sacrifice” is too demanding and poorly suited to action under uncertainty. Belief, trust, hope, and faith can orient sacrifice even when information is incomplete, false, or absent.
3. The privation theory of evil may capture the formal relation between evil and good, including the theological image of hell as absence of God, if its limitations are handled.
4. The challenge bank must ask what $G$ is made of and how matter can originate from whatever kind of substance or reality $G$ is.

## From future standing to defeasible long-term preference

Temporal non-discount supplies standing but does not yet satisfy the author's test. Add a comparative principle. Let $x$ and $y$ be eligible responses, where $x$ preserves or realizes anchors across a longer horizon or for a transpersonal beneficiary field, while $y$ supplies a conflicting transient benefit:

\[
LTP(x,y;B,C,\tau)
\Rightarrow
x\triangleright_{B,C,\tau}y.
\]

$LTP$ obtains provisionally when:

1. $x$ preserves, restores, or enables substantial future instances of $P$, $S$, or $R$;
2. $y$ is primarily transient, consumptive, or limited to a nearer perspective;
3. $x$ does not require a disproportionate grave misfit or destruction of present bearers;
4. the agent sincerely believes, trusts, hopes, or has faith in the long-term connection without culpably disregarding accessible counterevidence;
5. no nearer alternative preserves the long-horizon good with materially lower cost.

This is a genuine preference, not mere eligibility. It explains why species continuity, education, stewardship, research, institutional preservation, parenthood, and sacrifice for descendants can defeat short-lived consumption or pleasure. The preference is defeasible because a distant label does not make a project good, and speculative future scale cannot automatically license certain grave present harm.

The project remains open to conclusions associated with stronger longtermism when they follow from $LTP$, actual scale, probability, and the anchors. It does not adopt a complete strong-longtermist doctrine in advance or make its literature part of the present construction burden. Long-term preference is the internal adequacy condition; systematic longtermist theory is a later comparison and exposition task.

## Replace information with epistemic orientation

Sacrifice does not require knowledge of success or possession of a specified quantity of information. Replace “informed” with a more modest profile:

\[
EpStance(A,s,g,E_A)=
\langle Belief,Trust,Hope,Faith,Care_E\rangle,
\]

where $E_A$ is the evidence and counterevidence accessible to $A$, and $Care_E$ is the degree to which $A$ responsibly attends to what could reasonably be considered. Belief, trust, hope, and faith need not all occur, and none entails certainty.

The minimum agential condition is:

\[
OrientedInGoodFaith(A,s,g)
\land
\neg CulpablyDisregards(A,E_A).
\]

Good faith here means sincere practical orientation toward the believed good, not theological faith specifically and not guaranteed correctness. A parent can sacrifice for a child because the parent trusts, hopes, or believes that the act protects the child, even without predictive knowledge. Faith is especially relevant where the outcome exceeds the agent's control and the commitment itself partly constitutes the relationship.

Three evaluations must then be separated:

| Evaluation | Depends primarily on | Can survive failure? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| agentic or faithful worth | sourcehood, sincere orientation, commitment, and non-culpable treatment of accessible reasons | yes |
| objective fittingness | whether the act really realizes or protects an undefeated anchor | sometimes, even when the intended result fails for external reasons |
| causal success | whether the intended good actually obtains because of the act | no |

A false but non-culpable belief can support courage, fidelity, or loving intention while failing to benefit its target. A lucky action can succeed without displaying epistemic or agentic excellence. Information remains useful as defeasible evidence and for responsibility; completeness, truth, or even substantial availability is not a universal prerequisite for sacrifice.

## A clearer anatomy of $Fit_*$

$Fit_*$ should not mean merely “is good.” It has five typed components:

1. **bearer eligibility:** $B$ actually has, or conditionally could instantiate, the relevant conscious, agentive, or relational anchor;
2. **directed profile:** the anchor has characteristic modes of realization, exercise, development, frustration, and corruption;
3. **apt response:** $x$ is an objectively pertinent and proportionate way to realize, protect, restore, or answer one of those modes; this does not require that external circumstances permit final success;
4. **scope:** bearer, context, causal role, and temporal horizon are explicit;
5. **defeat:** generic goodness additionally requires that the local fit not be defeated by grave misfit elsewhere in the relevant scope.

Formally:

\[
Fit_*(x,a,B,C,\tau)
\quad\text{iff}\quad
Eligible(a,B,C)
\land Directed_a(B,C)
\land AptResponse(x,a,B,C,\tau).
\]

This makes $Fit_*$ informative: a judgment can be challenged by denying the bearer, the anchor, its direction, the causal contribution, or its scope. $Fit_*$ remains pro tanto; undefeated fit belongs to generic $Good$.

In ordinary language, $x$ fits an anchor when it is an appropriate way of sustaining or answering how that kind of bearer, capacity, or relation can be realized rather than damaged in the stated situation. The initial profiles are:

| Anchor | Directed realization | Characteristic corruption or privation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $P$ — conscious valence | conscious integrity and the possibility of non-destructive experience; protection from grave involuntary suffering | agony, experiential disintegration, or loss of the conditions for conscious flourishing |
| $S$ — sourcehood | effective exercise and development of the subject's own reason-responsive authorship | coercion, manipulation, incapacitation, or replacement of authorship |
| $R$ — truthful relation | truthful recognition, reciprocity where possible, fidelity, and non-instrumental regard | deception, betrayal, exclusion, or treatment of a subject merely as an object |

These profiles give $Fit_*$ more content than “whatever is approved.” They remain defeasible: they do not yet settle every trade-off, and the word “directed” still carries the irreducible claim that realization is normatively preferable to corruption. That is the exact burden to be grounded, not concealed.

The residual normative burden is now localized in $Directed_a$: why does a capacity or relation have a due or proper realization rather than merely statistically common outputs? Three grounding options remain:

| Ground of directedness | Advantage | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| primitive necessary aptness | formally economical | leaves normativity brute and may sit uneasily with divine ultimity |
| constitutive teleology of powers and relations | makes fit arise naturally from the kinds already in the ontology | must distinguish flourishing from mere biological function or successful pathology |
| essential rational grounding in $G$ | preserves ultimity and connects value to divine reason rather than arbitrary choice | must derive polarity from established divine features without calling them good in the premise |

The preferred route is a provisional hybrid: the ontological profiles of $P$, $S$, and $R$ determine what realization and corruption amount to, while $G$'s necessary rational and relational ground supplies the ultimate basis of their objective polarity. This is clearer than one unexplained global predicate, but the final non-circular bridge remains unearned.

## Due-good privation as the formal account of evil

The strongest usable form of privation theory is not “every absence of good is evil.” It is absence or corruption of a good **due** to a bearer, capacity, or relation:

\[
Priv_a(B,C,\tau)
\quad\text{iff}\quad
Due_a(B,C,\tau)
\land
\neg Realized_a(B,C,\tau).
\]

$Due_a$ is supplied provisionally by the directed anchor profile. A stone's lack of sight is not evil because sight is not due to its profile; blindness can be an evil for a creature whose visual capacity should be realizable. This condition prevents privation from counting every unrealized possible enhancement as evil.

Prefer the following hybrid privation thesis:

> Evil as evil is the absence, frustration, or corruption of due objective fit; positive states and events can be its bearers, experiences, causes, or vehicles.

Pain can be a fully positive phenomenal occurrence while its badness consists in experienced damage, loss of due integrity, frustrated functioning, or grave misfit in $P$. A coercive act positively occurs while corrupting $S$; a lie positively occurs while depriving $R$ of truthful recognition. This preserves the insight that evil has no independent rival essence without denying the reality or causal force of suffering and harmful action.

The thesis refines S27. $Misfit_*$ now ranges over privation, frustration, and corruption of due fit. It does not force every token evil into the ontology of a literal absence.

## Hell as absence of God

The project's ontological omnipresence rules out one literal reading. If $B$ exists because $G$ grounds the created order, then $B$ cannot lack all ontological relation to $G$ while continuing to exist:

\[
Exists(B)
\Rightarrow
Present_O(G,B).
\]

A coherent “absence of God” must therefore concern a stronger personal or axiological relation:

\[
HellLike(B)
\Rightarrow
Present_O(G,B)
\land
\neg Communion(G,B),
\]

perhaps together with a radical privation of truthful recognition, reciprocity, or freely accepted participation in $R$. This fits the earlier distinction between ontological presence, personal availability, exercised relation, and communion. It also permits self-exclusion or failed reciprocity without implying that $G$ stops knowing, grounding, or being causally available to the subject.

This is only a coherence model. The project does not thereby assert an afterlife, hell, punishment, permanence, or the cause of the privation.

## New ontological challenge: what is $G$ made of?

The ordinary question is legitimate because it can expose several different burdens. It must be decomposed before it can be answered:

1. **composition:** does $G$ have constituents, metaphysical parts, aspects, or powers?
2. **substrate:** is there a stuff or subject in which divine properties inhere?
3. **kind:** is $G$ a substance in the same sense as created substances, an analogous substance, or another ontological category?
4. **material relation:** if $G$ is non-material, how can material substance originate without a material input?
5. **continuity:** does creation produce numerically distinct matter, transform divine substance, emanate from it, or manifest it?

Calling “made of” a category mistake would be a conclusion requiring argument, not a first response. An external substrate threatens $G$'s ultimity; internal constituents raise questions about unity and grounding; an absolutely non-composite account must still give positive content to divine powers and explain cross-category production.

This challenge joins, rather than duplicates, the existing OEC/PMC problem. OEC says that $G$ can originate a distinct concrete effect without a prior material patient. PMC objects that concrete effects require material causes. The new question asks what the producer is and whether any relation between divine and created substance can supply, replace, or make unnecessary the alleged material continuity.

The next ontological pass should therefore compare four families:

- non-composite non-material source plus OEC;
- structured but non-material divine substance with powers;
- *ex deo* transformation, emanation, or ontological continuity;
- external or co-fundamental material substrate.

## Literature and novelty notes

- [Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 48](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1048.htm) distinguishes privation from mere negation by requiring the absence of a naturally due good. The project borrows this constraint without importing Aquinas's full teleology, convertibility of being and good, or divine simplicity.
- The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on faith](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/faith/) maps faith across belief, trust, hope, practical commitment, risk, and action under uncertainty. This supports treating epistemic orientation as a profile rather than as possession of information.
- The [entry on fitting-attitude theories of value](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2023/entries/fitting-attitude-theories/) shows that fittingness is an established normative primitive and also exposes why invoking fit does not by itself reduce normativity.
- The [entry on divine simplicity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/divine-simplicity/) situates “what is God made of?” within established disputes over material-form, act-potency, existence-essence, and other kinds of composition. The project has not adopted that doctrine.

The due-good constraint, faith/trust distinctions, fittingness, privation, divine simplicity, and *ex nihilo*/*ex deo* alternatives are established. The local synthesis connects them to $Fit_*$, sourcehood, E+, ontological versus personal presence, and OEC/PMC. No novelty claim is made.
