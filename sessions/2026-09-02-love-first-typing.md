# Session: Love: a first typing under the anchors, and what the affective route would require

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S91
**Primary question:** The author asks whether love means anything beyond biology, whether love between persons and love of $G$ are one relation, whether $G$ loves each subject in particular, and whether $G$ loves itself (CH30). What does the ledger already fix about these, and what would Route 3, divine affective regard, add and cost?
**Dependencies:** S27 (C187–C189); S23 (C146–C149); S20 and S34 (C121–C125, C284–C291); S48 (C462–C465); S52 (C497–C505); S72 (C581); S87 and S90 (C698–C704, C718–C722); C321 (empirical bridges); C471
**Origin:** CH30, opened in S90 by the author's questions
**Target depth:** F1–F2 typing; four accessible answers; Route 3 developed to the point where its cost is exact, not adopted

## Outcome

Love is typed as a standing disposition of a whole agent, directed at a *particular* subject, that is fit toward all three of that subject's anchors: willing and, where possible, serving the subject's good ($P$), respecting and fostering what is the subject's own to do ($S$), and recognizing the subject truthfully as the subject it is ($R$). It has three components: volitional (willing the good), recognitional (true regard), and affective (felt regard, a valence in the lover keyed to the beloved's state). The first two are fixed by what the ledger already contains; the third is what biology supplies in us and what Route 3 asks about for $G$.

On this typing the four questions have answers of different strength. Love means more than biology because biology explains the origin and realization of the capacity, not what makes a loving response fitting; fit is objective (C188), and love directed at the dead, the distant future, or $G$ has objects biology has no use for. Love of persons and love of $G$ are one genus with an asymmetric species: toward $G$ the serving and protecting components have no work, since $G$ has no misfit and no welfare, and what remains is truthful recognition, uptake of the relation, and delight, which belongs to the experiential register. $G$'s love for each subject in particular is earned in its volitional and recognitional components: $G$ knows each subject as that subject (C284), is available to each (C122), preserves each one's agency (C115, C501), and, after S90, makes each one actual for its own sake and favors the closure of each one's horizon (C719); what is not earned is exercised communion, which is co-produced (C124), and affective love. $G$'s self-love is, in the volitional and recognitional components, exactly what C462 formalizes: $G$'s dispositions are fit toward what $G$ is, and $G$'s self-knowledge is true recognition; an affective component is not attributed, and the Trinitarian thesis that love requires another within $G$ is not addressed (CH01's fourth variant).

Route 3 turns out cheaper than S87 estimated. Affective regard can be modeled as an executive intrinsic mode (C581) of two established powers, actual subject acquaintance and volitional love: the occurrent aspect of being acquainted with a valenced state while willing that subject's good. That adds no new power kind and no constitutive basis, so it preserves $PP_G$ and $MCS$; what it requires is the stronger acquaintance model on which acquaintance with a valenced state has a valence-like aspect for the acquainter (C288's vicarious phenomenal representation), and it costs impassibility only in the weak sense that $G$ has contingent occurrent aspects, which S72 already admits, not in the strong sense that $G$ is acted upon or altered in its constitution. If adopted, it would make the $P$-disposition bearer-indexed and standing, like the dispositions toward $S$ and $R$, and would earn $GoodOrient_P$ and with it $G_3$ by C462's mechanism. It is not adopted here; its exact cost is recorded for the author.

## Step 1: typing love

\[
Loves(x,y):=Standing(x)\land Particular(y)\land\bigwedge_{a\in\{P,S,R\}}FitDir_a(x,y),
\]

where $FitDir_a(x,y)$ says that $x$'s dispositions relevant to $y$'s anchor $a$ stand in $Fit_*$ toward it for $y$ (the bearer-indexed relation of C189), $Standing$ says the disposition is not a token response but a settled orientation of the whole agent (C64), and $Particular$ says it is indexed to $y$ and not to $y$'s type. Three components are distinguished within it:

| Component | Content | Ground in the ledger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| volitional | willing $y$'s good and, where possible, serving or protecting it | $Fit_*$ toward $P$ and $S$; C188–C189 |
| recognitional | true regard for $y$ as the subject it is, including what its states are like for it | $Fit_*$ toward $R$; C187, C284–C286 |
| affective | felt regard: a valence in $x$ keyed to $y$'s state | in finite subjects, the welfare profile (C146) and embodiment; for $G$, open (C465, C703) |

Love is not one anchor's fit but all three at once toward one subject. This is why using someone, however warmly felt, is not love (fails $S$ and $R$), why cold benefaction is not love (fails the affective component, and often $R$), and why sentiment without regard for what is the other's own to do is not love (fails $S$). The typing is a consequence of the anchors, not a new primitive.

## Step 2: love and biology (P61)

Biology explains why finite subjects have the capacity: attachment, pair-bonding, kin care, and the affective apparatus that realizes felt regard. Under the project's rule for empirical results (C321, development-map rule), this explanation enters through a bridge premise: it constrains the actual realization of the capacity and says nothing about what makes a loving response fitting. Fit is an objective relation between an item and a bearer's anchors (C188); whether one's regard for a subject is apt to what that subject is, is theirs to do, and feels is not settled by the history of the apparatus. Two further facts show the surplus. Love takes objects biology has no use for: the dead, subjects a century away (C192), $G$. And the same biological apparatus realizes both love and its counterfeits; what separates them is fit, which the apparatus does not supply. So love's *capacity* is biological in origin for us, its *content* is objective, and its *significance* beyond biology is exactly its fit: to love someone is to respond aptly to what they are.

## Step 3: love of persons and love of $G$ (P62)

Both fall under $Loves$, so they are one genus. The species differ because $G$ has no misfit to be protected from, no welfare to be improved, and no sourcehood a creature could foster: the serving and protecting parts of the volitional component have no work toward $G$. What remains toward $G$ is the recognitional component, truthful regard for $G$ as the source of everything and of oneself, which is what the register calls adoration (P58); the volitional component in the only form it can take, consent to and uptake of the relation $G$ makes available (C124), and willing what $G$ wills, which after S90 includes willing the good of creatures for their own sake (C719); and the affective component, delight in $G$'s presence, which the ontology locates in the experiential register (C471) without deciding whether it is relation to $G$ or to created structure grounded in $G$. Toward persons, all three components have full work. The asymmetry is the one C295–C296 already fix for communication: the relation is real without parity. So the answer to “is it the same thing” is: the same kind of thing, done under a difference the other party makes.

## Step 4: $G$'s love for each subject (P63)

Take the components in turn for $G$ toward an actual subject $A$.

- *Recognitional.* $ASA(G,A)$ is direct, factive, subject-indexed access to $A$ and to what $A$'s states are like for $A$ (C284–C286). This is true regard for $A$ as $A$, in particular and not as an instance of a type.
- *Volitional, toward $S$.* The agency-preservation presumptions (C115, C501) are toward each agent's sourcehood at both levels; $G$'s address never overrides (C466).
- *Volitional, toward $P$.* After S90, $A$ is made actual for $A$'s own sake and the closure of $A$'s own horizon is favored (C719); every permission concerning $A$ is covered and never uses $A$ (C718, C699).
- *Availability.* $G$ is personally available to $A$ (C122).

Since $Fit_*$ is bearer-indexed (C189) and $ASA$ is subject-indexed, these dispositions are toward $A$ in particular. So volitional-recognitional love of each subject in particular is earned: $G$ loves each one, in the sense that $G$'s standing dispositions toward each one are fit toward all three of that one's anchors. Two things are not earned. Exercised communion, reciprocal presence, is co-produced and cannot be supplied by $G$ alone (C123–C124): $G$ loving $A$ does not entail $A$ being in a relationship with $G$. And the affective component is Route 3. The caveat S90 fixed also holds: the $P$-directed part is a favoring reason, so “$G$ wills my good” is earned and “$G$ guarantees my good” is the register's belief ($G_3$, C721).

## Step 5: $G$'s self-love (P64)

Applied reflexively, the recognitional component is $G$'s self-knowledge, which ACO contains (C47) and which is true. The volitional component toward $S$ and $R$ is what C462 formalizes: $G$ necessarily instantiates the kinds from which those anchors take their fit conditions, and $G$'s dispositions are fit toward them by construction; there is no divine misfit to be repaired, so self-love in $G$ has no serving component and consists in the fit of $G$'s dispositions toward what $G$ is. This is the content of the classical thesis that $G$ wills its own goodness necessarily, restated without simplicity. The affective component is not attributed, for the same reason as in Step 4. The Trinitarian claim that love, as self-gift, requires another *within* $G$ is not addressed: it needs the person/being ontology CH01's fourth variant sets aside (C588, C689).

## Step 6: what Route 3 would require, exactly

S87 costed Route 3 as passibility, an account of divine valence, and C288's stronger model. The typing allows a sharper estimate. Affective regard is a valence in the lover keyed to the beloved's state. For $G$, model it as an **executive intrinsic mode** (C581): the occurrent intrinsic aspect of the joint exercise of two established powers, $ASA$ with a valenced state of $A$ and volitional love of $A$. S72 already admits executive modes as contingent token variation that preserves the essential power profile and adds no constitutive basis (C581, C584). So affective regard needs no new power kind, preserves $PP_G$ and $MCS$, and is impassibility-violating only in the weak sense S72 already accepts: $G$ has contingent occurrent aspects. What it does need is C288's stronger acquaintance model: that acquaintance with a valenced state has, for the acquainter, a valence-like aspect (vicarious phenomenal representation), so that the executive mode is *felt* regard and not merely a token of willing. That model is live and unadopted.

If adopted, the consequence for the P-gap is the one S87 stated: the $P$-disposition becomes bearer-indexed and standing, part of what $G$ is toward each subject rather than a favoring reason among others, and C462's mechanism yields $GoodOrient_P$, hence $G_3$. The author's decision was to develop this route rather than adopt it; the session records that its cost has shrunk to one model choice (C288) and that its gain would be the guarantee the register now holds as faith.

## Adversarial tests

### Is the typing just the three anchors renamed?

It adds two things the anchors do not: particularity and standing. Fit toward a type or a policy is not love; fit toward this subject, as a settled orientation, is. Both are needed for the answers above, and both are already in the ledger's vocabulary (C189's bearer index; C64's whole agent).

### Does “$G$ loves each subject” overstate a favoring reason?

The volitional-toward-$P$ component is a favoring reason (C719) and is reported as such; the recognitional and $S$-directed components are not favoring reasons but standing dispositions already earned. Love in the volitional-recognitional sense is earned; its guarantee is not, and P63 says so.

### Does modeling affect as an executive mode trivialize passibility?

It locates exactly what the passibilist wants: that $G$ is not indifferent, that $G$'s occurrent life varies with creatures. It refuses what the impassibilist fears: that $G$ is altered in constitution or acted upon. Whether the middle position is stable is the residual, and it turns on C288.

### Is self-love without another coherent?

In the volitional-recognitional sense, yes: it is the fit of an agent's dispositions toward its own nature and its true self-knowledge. Whether love in the fullest sense requires another is the Trinitarian question, and it is set aside, not denied.

## Literature and precedent

Recorded from memory; to be verified in the author's next literature pass. The volitional account of divine love as willing the good of the other is Aquinas, ST I q.20, and the analysis of love as union and benevolence is q.20 a.1 with I-II q.26–28; the tripartite typing is close to Eleonore Stump's account of love as desire for the good of the beloved and desire for union (*Wandering in Darkness*, 2010, ch. 5); the bearer-particularity of love follows Harry Frankfurt, *The Reasons of Love* (2004); the biological-origin versus objective-fit distinction is the project's bridge rule applied to attachment theory. The affective-mode proposal for $G$ sits between classical impassibility and the passibilism of Moltmann, *The Crucified God* (1972), and Fiddes (1988); its nearest analytic neighbor is the “qualified passibility” of Richard Creel, *Divine Impassibility* (1986). The Trinitarian argument that love requires another is Richard of St Victor, *De Trinitate* III. No novelty claim.
