# Session: Other universes — possible worlds, cosmological multiverses, and plural created orders

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S51
**Primary question:** Do other universes exist, and what does the ontology say about each of the things that phrase can mean?
**Dependencies:** C03–C05, C08, C22–C28, C33, C47–C49, C57, C78–C83, C104–C111, C183–C184, C358–C362, C384, C388–C389, C422–C424, C429–C434, S01–S05, S40, S43–S44, S49–S50
**Origin:** Author's question of 2026-09-02; taken before the consolidation pass because it is contained and interlocks with S49–S50
**Target depth:** F1 distinctions with local F2 notation; one bridge claim about physical interpretation

## Starting position

“Other universes” names at least three different things: possible worlds in the modal sense, cosmological multiverses in the physicist's sense, and further created orders in the sense the architecture itself uses when it speaks of a post-mortem domain (C381, C384). The ontology has resources for all three and different verdicts for each. The session separates them, applies the existing commitments, and records what is coherent, what is excluded, and what is unknown.

## Definitions

| Term/symbol | Definition used in this session |
| --- | --- |
| world $w$ | A complete way things could be, represented as a pointed modal structure (C49); a representation of compossible manifestations, not a concrete item |
| physical universe $U$ | A maximal causally connected spacetime domain within a created order; “universe” in the physicist's sense |
| created order $\mathcal C_i$ | A created domain specified by some $S_i$ and produced by $OEC^+$; may contain one or many physical universes; has its own time |
| $\mathfrak R^*$ | Complete actual reality: $G$, every actual created order, their histories, interventions, and dependence relations (generalizing C47 from one $\mathcal C_0$ to a set) |
| cosmological multiverse | Many physical universes within one created order: inflationary bubbles, cyclic epochs, or branches of one dynamics |
| plural creation | More than one created order actualized by $G$ |
| theistic plenitude | $G$ actualizes every eligible (or every undominated) structure |

## Sense 1: possible worlds

The ontology already answers this. Possible histories represent compossible manifestations of powers and constraints and are not additional concrete eternalist blocks (C49); possibility is grounded in necessary constraints and powers (C48, C429), not in a plurality of concrete worlds. So other possible worlds are not other universes. They are ways this reality could have been, and what makes them possible is the definition of S50, not their existence somewhere. Concrete modal realism is excluded, not as incoherent, but as a posit the ontology never needs and the weak explanatory preference (C358) counts against: it multiplies concrete existents to do work that constraints and powers already do.

\[
Poss(w)\;\not\Rightarrow\;Concrete(w).
\]

## Sense 2: cosmological multiverses

A cosmological multiverse is internal to one created order. Whatever mechanism generates bubbles, epochs, or branches is a feature of $S_{phys}$; the universes it generates instantiate $\Pi_{phys}$ under the same specification. Three consequences follow from commitments already in force.

**Empirical, not metaphysical.** Whether the actual order contains one physical universe or many is a question for physics through explicit bridges (C183–C184). The ontology is neutral and does not predict either answer.

**Orthogonal to the ground question.** A multiverse does not reduce contingency and does not bypass the necessary ground. The stopping-point analysis (C360–C362) applies to the multiverse as a whole: it is contingent, and its generating structure is either brute, necessary and necessitating, necessary with chance, or created. The level argument (C389) adds that a multiverse-generating mechanism is itself a consequence of $S$ and cannot govern the origination of $S$. And the project's motivation never rested on fine-tuning (C03, R02), so the multiverse's role as an alternative to design is not an alternative to anything the project claims. The popular contest between “multiverse” and “God” is, in this architecture, a category mistake: one is a hypothesis about the contents of the created order, the other about its ground.

**One bridge claim: branching at settlement.** Everettian many-worlds interpretations make every quantum outcome actual in some branch. The ontology can accommodate branching for non-agentive events by letting $H^*$ be the branching structure. It cannot accommodate branching at the events that realize an agent's settlement: libertarian settling actualizes one of the originable alternatives (C08, C33, C57), and an event in which every alternative is actualized in some branch has no contrastive settling and no single actual outcome. Hence:

\[
Settles(A,a;\{a,b\})\;\Rightarrow\;\neg\big(Actual(a)\land Actual(b)\big),
\]

which is incompatible with universal branching at the realization of settlement. The claim is a metaphysical constraint on physical interpretations, not an empirical prediction: interpretations of quantum mechanics are underdetermined by the evidence, and the project records that it disfavors universal Everettian branching for the same reason it disfavors reductive persistence (C413), namely that the subject's settlement must have one outcome. If universal branching were ever established as the uniquely viable interpretation, the bridge would make it a genuine defeater of C08; the ontology accepts that exposure.

## Sense 3: plural created orders

This is the sense the question most likely intends and the one the architecture treats most seriously, because it already uses it. S43 typed continuation as possibly a further creative act producing a post-mortem domain within $\mathfrak R^*$ (C384), and S50's creative plenitude says every independently possible created specification is within $G$'s capacity (C433, conditional). Nothing limits $G$ to one creative exercise: S49's settling selects within the undominated set, and selecting more than one member is itself an option in the settling space. So plural creation is coherent and within divine power:

\[
\Diamond\,\exists S_1\neq S_2\,[Actualizes(G,S_1)\land Actualizes(G,S_2)].
\]

What would such orders be? Each has its own specification, time, and history; each is grounded by the same $G$, who is atemporal with respect to all of them (C14) and ontologically present to each (C91, generalized). They are causally disconnected from one another unless $G$ relates them by an intervention that spans orders, which is coherent under relational immanence. Their subjects are all individuated de re by the same $G$ (C416) and all fall under universal personal availability (C122). Divine uniqueness is untouched: plural orders have one ground, not several (C80–C81), and a second necessary agent is excluded by $MUNA$ (C432). ACO's domain generalizes without strain, since C47 already made $\mathfrak R^*$ exceed one history.

Whether any such order exists is a different question, and the honest answer has three parts:

1. **Not excluded, not entailed.** No commitment excludes plural orders, and none entails them. The favoring reasons of S49 (variety, expression, more finite otherness and relation) would favor them; no requiring reason demands them.
2. **Unknowable by finite means.** Causally disconnected orders leave no trace in ours; there is no empirical route, and experiential reports are not evidence for the architecture (C369). The status is therefore open in a stronger sense than usual: not merely undecided, but undecidable from inside one order.
3. **Not theistic plenitude by default.** The stronger thesis that $G$ actualizes every eligible or undominated structure is coherent but not preferred. It would require a requiring reason for maximal variety, which the project lacks (C108: no maximizing norm), and it would relocate the contrastive settling of S49 from “which structure” to “all rather than some,” which is a further exercise the reasons do not compel. Precedents that reach plenitude do so from a perfection or must-create-the-best premise the project has declined (R03).

## Adversarial tests

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Rejecting concrete worlds while accepting plural created orders is inconsistent | No; concrete worlds would be non-created, self-existent totalities, excluded by the createdness clause and by sole ultimity; plural orders are created and grounded |
| A multiverse explains fine-tuning and removes the need for God | The project never used fine-tuning; the multiverse is contingent and its generator is within $S$; the ground question is untouched |
| Disfavoring Everett is metaphysics dictating to physics | Interpretations are metaphysics; the ontology states its constraint and its exposure honestly, and it does not deny any empirical result |
| If $G$ can create every possible order and has reasons favoring variety, $G$ creates them all | Favoring reasons do not require; C108 denies a maximizing norm; plenitude is coherent, not preferred |
| Plural orders make “the universe” meaningless | “Universe” keeps its physical sense; “created order” and “complete actual reality” do the ontological work |
| The question is idle because unanswerable | It is answerable in two of its three senses and honestly open in the third, with the reasons for openness stated |

## Literature and novelty notes

Cited from memory; not verified online in this session.

- David Lewis, *On the Plurality of Worlds* (1986): concrete modal realism, the contrast case for sense 1.
- Max Tegmark's mathematical-universe and Level IV multiverse: plenitude at the impersonal stopping point (C360); Everett's relative-state interpretation and David Wallace, *The Emergent Multiverse* (2012), for sense 2's branching.
- Klaas Kraay, “Theism, Possible Worlds, and the Multiverse” (2010), and Timothy O'Connor, *Theism and Ultimate Explanation* (2008), for theistic multiverses reached from perfection or must-create premises; the project's refusal of a maximizing norm is the reason it stops at coherence.
- Robin Collins and the fine-tuning literature, for the debate the project declines to enter (C03, R02).

Classification: established families; the project-specific elements are the three-way partition tied to the ontology's own modules, the settlement-branching bridge constraint, and the diagnosis that plenitude would need a requiring reason. No novelty claim.
