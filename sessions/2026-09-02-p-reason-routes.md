# Session: The P-reason routes, and how much of the P-gap the established profile already closes

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S87
**Primary question:** C526 records two candidate routes to a P-directed creative reason and adopts neither. Can either be derived from established commitments rather than added; if not, what is the weakest addition that would close the P-gap, what would it cost, and how much of the gap is already closed without it?
**Dependencies:** S56 (C521–C527); S48 (C462–C465); S49 (C472–C479); S50 (C480–C486); S27–S28 (C187–C195); S34 (C283–C291); S52 (C497–C505); S72 (C577); C55–C60, C96, C109, C460; the accessible answers on goodness, suffering, and the reason for creation

**Target depth:** F2 for the decomposition and the route tests; adoption of a P-reason is not attempted (C324; the author's faith content is C479)

## Outcome

The P-gap is not one gap. $GoodOrient_P(G)$ decomposes into three conditions on permitted suffering: that none is gratuitous, that no bearer is a mere means to others' goods, and that every bearer's suffering is covered within the bearer's own horizon. The second is already earned: it follows from the earned orientation toward truthful relation, since $R$ forbids treating a subject as a vehicle (C477). The first is half earned: every divine permission is a reason-guided settlement, so no suffering is permitted for nothing, but a reason need not be a cover, so non-gratuitousness in the ledger's sense is not derived. The third is not earned at all, and it is exactly what the accessible register believes.

Of C526's routes, the second (regard internal to truthful relation) yields cognition only, as S56 suspected; a conative variant of it, with the explicit premise that adequate recognition of a subject counts the subject's anchors, would deliver the first condition but not the third. The first route (otherness as for-the-sake-of) delivers the third, and it does so only in requiring mode; in favoring mode it yields a presumption, not closure. A third route exists that S56 did not list: divine valence. Under C462's own mechanism, $G$'s profile is fit toward the anchors $G$ instantiates; $G$ instantiates sourcehood and relation but is attributed no valence, which is why $P$ is the anchor that does not follow. Attributing affective regard to $G$ would close the gap by the same route as $S$ and $R$, at the price of divine passibility. None of the three is adopted. The weakest addition that closes all three conditions is the first route in requiring mode, and its cost is exact: by C478 it entails an extended horizon for every bearer whose life ends in uncovered suffering, which is C485's conditional continuation. The register's belief is therefore located to the row: to believe that $G$ is good in what we feel is to hold the first route in requiring mode, with continuation as its consequence.

## Step 1: three conditions, not one

Write $Suff(s,B)$ for a permitted suffering $s$ of bearer $B$, $Cover(r,s)$ as in C473, $Vehicle(B,s)$ for the case in which $s$ is permitted for the sake of goods of bearers other than $B$ with no cover within $B$'s horizon, and $HCover(s,B)$ for a cover within $B$'s own horizon (C477). Then $GoodOrient_P(G)$, as C473 and C477 already use it, is the conjunction:

\[
\begin{aligned}
G_1&:\ \forall s\,\forall B\,[Suff(s,B)\rightarrow\exists r\,Cover(r,s)]\quad\text{(no gratuitous suffering)}\\
G_2&:\ \forall s\,\forall B\,[Suff(s,B)\rightarrow\neg Vehicle(B,s)]\quad\text{(no bearer a mere means)}\\
G_3&:\ \forall s\,\forall B\,[Suff(s,B)\rightarrow HCover(s,B)]\quad\text{(bearer-level closure)}
\end{aligned}
\]

$G_3$ entails $G_1$ and $G_2$; $G_1$ entails neither of the others. S49 treated $G_1$ as the content of $GoodOrient_P$ and $G_3$ as the bearer-level remainder that policy covers leave open. Separating $G_2$ is the step that matters, because it is the one the established profile reaches.

## Step 2: what the established profile already gives

**$G_2$ is earned.** $GoodOrient_R(G)$ is earned (C463): every established divine disposition relevant to truthful relation is fit toward it. C477 states, as part of what the $R$ anchor requires, that a bearer's suffering may not be treated as a mere vehicle for others' goods. Permitting $s$ for the sake of other bearers' goods with no cover within $B$'s horizon is exactly that treatment. So a disposition to permit such an $s$ would be a misfit toward $R$, which $GoodOrient_R$ excludes. Hence:

\[
GoodOrient_R(G)\rightarrow G_2.
\]

This is the first $P$-relevant disposition the architecture earns, and it is earned from $R$, not from any regard for valence. It has content: the policy-level covers of S49 (source, regularity) protect the sourcehood and truthful relation of every agent, $B$ included, so a bearer covered only at policy level is a participant in what the policy protects and not a mere vehicle; what $G_2$ excludes is permission of $B$'s suffering *for others* with nothing for $B$.

**$G_1$ is half earned.** Divine settling is reason-guided (C109, C460), and a permission is not a mere non-occurrence: where $G$ is aware ($ASA$, C284) and able (C472's definition requires it), non-prevention is intentional refraining, which is an action with an eligible alternative (C577). Reason-guided agents act for reasons (C56, C60: the project's terminus is reason-guided, not brute, exercise). Hence every permission has a reason:

\[
Suff(s,B)\rightarrow\exists r\in\mathcal R_G\,[r\ \text{is a reason for}\ Permits(G,s)].
\]

But a reason is a cover only if it targets an anchor and the misfit prevention would produce is undefeated relative to $s$'s (C473). A reason targeting a non-axiological feature (intelligibility, variety, expression; C521) has no fit-standing and cannot defeat a misfit under C163 and C189, so a suffering permitted for it is uncovered. Nothing in the established profile excludes such permissions. So what is earned is that no suffering is permitted for nothing; what is not earned is that every reason for permission is a cover.

**$G_3$ is not earned.** Policy covers leave the bearer-level remainder (C477); nothing established closes it (C478).

## Step 3: the routes

**Route 2 (regard internal to truthful relation).** $ASA$ gives factive, subject-indexed, qualitatively discriminating access to every actual subject's states (C284–C286). Recognition of $B$ as a subject therefore includes knowing what $B$'s states are like for $B$. This is cognitive regard, and S56 was right that it does not supply conative orientation: capacity and knowledge do not supply a reason for exercise (C96), and the project separates the epistemic from the agentive relation throughout (C287). The route closes nothing by itself.

A conative variant can be stated with its premise explicit. Call it **Route 2′**: *adequate recognition of a subject as a subject counts the subject's anchors among the considerations that can defeat and be defeated*. Under Route 2′, a permission whose reason has no fit-standing subordinates $B$'s misfit to something that is not a good for anyone, which is a failure of recognition and so a misfit toward $R$. Route 2′ therefore delivers $G_1$: every reason for permission must be a cover. It does not deliver $G_3$: a cover for others' goods that leaves $B$'s horizon unclosed is not a failure to count $B$'s anchors, only a failure to close them. Route 2′ is an addition to the content of $R$; it is natural, and C187's own motivation for including $R$ (irreducible other-regard) leans toward it, but it is not entailed by C477's letter.

**Route 1 (otherness as for-the-sake-of).** Read the creation-level reason of C105 as *making other subjects actual for their own sake*. A subject made for its own sake is one whose life, as it is for that subject, is a target of the reason; so the reason's target includes $P$ and the bearer's own horizon. This delivers $G_3$, and with it $G_1$ and $G_2$. But the mode matters (C521):

- In **favoring** mode, Route 1 orders eligible structures and interventions by how fully they realize bearers' horizons, without excluding those that leave some unclosed. It yields a presumption toward closure, defeasible by other favoring reasons. It does not yield $G_3$.
- In **requiring** mode at the architecture and specification levels, Route 1 filters: no eligible total history leaves a bearer's suffering uncovered within the bearer's horizon. This yields $G_3$.

So the only reading of Route 1 that closes the gap is the requiring one, and its consequence is exact. For a bearer whose embodied life ends in uncovered suffering, closure within its own horizon requires that the horizon extend beyond that life (C478). Route 1 in requiring mode therefore entails, as an admissibility condition on every eligible history containing such bearers, that those bearers continue (C480, C485). Continuation ceases to be merely conditional on $GoodOrient_P$; it becomes a filter on what $G$ could have created at all, given the reason. This is a strong claim, and stating it is the point: the belief the register already holds has this content and no less.

**Route 3 (divine valence).** S48's grounding of $Fit_*$ in $G$ (C462) works differently for the three anchors: $G$ *instantiates* the kinds fixing the fit conditions of $S$ and $R$, and only *grounds* the kind fixing those of $P$, because divine valence is not attributed. The established dispositions toward $S$ and $R$ are fit toward those anchors because $G$ is itself a source in truthful relation: the profile is fit toward what $G$ exemplifies. The absence of $P$-orientation is the image of the absence of divine valence. If $G$ were attributed affective regard toward creatures, valence-like states directed at what their lives are like for them, then $P$-orientation would follow by the same mechanism, without any of the reason-profile additions above. This is the classical route, love in the affective sense, and its costs are the classical ones: divine passibility, an account of divine valence (C465 already names it as a requirement of omnibenevolence), and the stronger model of acquaintance as vicarious phenomenal representation (C288), which the project holds as live but does not adopt. The route is recorded because it explains *why* the gap has the shape it has; it is not adopted.

## Step 4: the weakest closure and its cost

| Addition | Delivers | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Route 2′ (recognition counts anchors) | $G_1$ | a strengthening of $R$'s content; no consequence for continuation |
| Route 1, favoring | a presumption toward $G_3$ | none beyond abductive attribution; closes nothing |
| Route 1, requiring | $G_1,G_2,G_3$ | continuation of every bearer with uncovered suffering, as an admissibility condition (C478, C485) |
| Route 3 (divine valence) | $G_1,G_2,G_3$ by C462's mechanism | passibility; an account of divine valence; C288's stronger model |

The weakest addition that closes all three is Route 1 in requiring mode. Route 3 closes them too but changes the divine profile rather than the reason profile, at higher cost. Route 2′ is cheap and closes only $G_1$; it could be adopted alone, and would leave the register's belief exactly where C479 put it, at $G_3$.

None is adopted here. Adopting one is the author's decision, since each is an abductive attribution at C105's evidential level (S56), and since the register's faith content is the author's (C479, C557). What the session fixes is the map: which condition each route buys, at what price, and that $G_2$ needs no purchase.

## Adversarial tests

### Does deriving $G_2$ from $R$ smuggle valence into relation?

No. $G_2$ says nothing about $B$'s valence being good or bad for $G$'s purposes; it says $B$ may not be used. C477 states this as part of $R$ already, and S49 used it to show that policy covers cannot close the bearer level. The session only notices that the same clause is a positive disposition the profile has.

### Is “every permission has a reason” trivial, given that $G$ could permit by policy without token attention?

The policy is itself a reason-guided settlement (the requiring reason $r_S$ of C523–C527), so token permissions under it have a reason: the policy's. The half-earned status of $G_1$ is not that some permissions are reasonless but that the reason may fail to be a cover. The threshold residue of C474 is the live case: the gravest tokens, preventable without collapsing the policy, are permitted for some reason, and the profile does not say it is a cover.

### Does Route 1 in requiring mode make creation of sufferers impossible unless continuation is guaranteed?

Yes, and that is its content. It filters eligible histories to those in which every bearer's horizon closes. Since the actual order contains bearers whose embodied lives end uncovered, adopting the route commits one to their continuation as a condition of the actual order's eligibility, not as a hope. Anyone who finds that too strong should hold Route 1 in favoring mode and accept that $G_3$ is then not closed, or hold Route 2′ and accept $G_1$ alone.

### Does Route 3 conflict with $MCS$ or $PP_G$?

Not as such: affective regard would be an aspect (C492), not an ingredient, and an essential one. It conflicts with impassibility, which the project has not adopted either. Its burden is an account, not a contradiction.

### Could $G_3$ be reached from $S$ rather than $P$, via the agency ecology?

The ecology conditions (C505) require a real field of consequences and opportunity for self-formation, which are $S$-targets; a bearer whose agency was fully respected can still end in uncovered suffering. $S$ does not reach $G_3$.

## Consequences for the accessible register

The answer “¿Es Dios bueno?” can now say one more earned thing: $G$ does not use anyone's suffering as a means to others' good, and this follows from the orientation toward truthful relation, not from any assumption about valence. The answers on suffering and on the reason for creation already state the belief with the content C479 fixes; the session gives that content its row. Both revisions are shown to the author.

## Literature and precedent

Recorded from memory; to be verified in the author's next literature pass. The decomposition of the goodness condition into non-gratuitousness, non-instrumentalization, and bearer-level (“person-centred”) closure follows the shape of Marilyn McCord Adams's insistence in *Horrendous Evils and the Goodness of God* (1999) that a theodicy must defeat horrors within the sufferer's own life, and of Eleonore Stump's *Wandering in Darkness* (2010) on the sufferer's own good as the relevant benefit; the non-instrumentalization clause is Kantian in form and is standard in the literature on “means” objections to greater-good theodicies. William Rowe's formulation of gratuitous evil is the source of $G_1$'s target. Route 3's costs are those of the impassibility debate (Paul Fiddes, *The Creative Suffering of God*, 1988; Thomas Weinandy, *Does God Suffer?*, 2000). The project-specific element is the derivation of $G_2$ from the truthful-relation anchor rather than from benevolence, and the location of the register's belief at one row with its exact consequence. No novelty claim.
