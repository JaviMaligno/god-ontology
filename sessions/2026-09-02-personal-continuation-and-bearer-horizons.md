# Session: Personal continuation and bearer horizons

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S38
**Primary question:** Can the ontology support an extended bearer horizon — the numerically same conscious subject existing after the cessation of its embodied realization base, with undefeated fit — and what would that require of E+, ACO, divine power, the embodied subject of S29, and the privative model of S28?
**Dependencies:** C15, C22–C28, C47, C53, C71–C75, C98–C103, C122–C124, C183–C184, C208–C209, C212–C221, C301–C303, S28–S29, S37
**Origin:** Author's instruction of 2026-09-02 to proceed with CH16
**Target depth:** F1 with local F2 notation; no afterlife doctrine is asserted

## Starting position

S37 derived that, for a bearer $B$ whose life ends in uncovered suffering, closure of the P-gap requires undefeated $P$-fit within a horizon extending beyond $B$'s life (C302). The requirement arrived as a dependency, not as a doctrine. This session asks whether the architecture can carry it, and refuses in advance two shortcuts: settling personal identity by stipulation, and treating continuation as established because the argument needs it.

The author's inherited line is thin here. U16 mentions, from the traditions, that humans can choose or not choose God and that hell is the representation of not choosing; S28 turned that into a coherence model of hell-like absence as privation of communion, explicitly without asserting an afterlife. C21 requires the project to discover how far the argument reaches before comparing with religious concepts.

## Step 1: what continuation must be, given S37

Three conditions follow directly from C301–C302:

1. **Same bearer.** Transpersonal continuity (C181), memory in others, descendants, replicas, or a qualitatively identical successor do not satisfy the requirement, because the remainder is bearer-indexed and $R$ forbids substituting a vehicle for the subject.
2. **Fit, not mere existence.** Survival in a hell-like privation (C208) would not close anything; $ContHorizon$ requires undefeated $P$-fit for $B$, and for $S$-capable bearers also availability of $R$.
3. **Where.** E+ makes later states of $B$ as actual as earlier ones. The ACO domain $\mathfrak R^*$ already exceeds the created spatiotemporal history $H^*$ (C47), so continuation need not be located in the same spacetime; it must be located somewhere in complete actual reality.

## Step 2: coherence under the open realization models

S29 left the realization of the unified subject open (C219). Each model yields a distinct route and a distinct identity burden:

| Realization model | Route available | Identity burden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| reductive organismic or neural | re-realization only: the same organism or functional organization made to exist again | gappy existence of one individual; the replica problem |
| non-reductive embodied subject | re-realization with a new base, or persistence if the agent-level bearer can outlast its base | whether agent-level powers can exist unrealized or be re-realized as the same bearer |
| dual-aspect | persistence if the common bearer is not exhausted by its physical aspect | specifying the bearer that persists |
| hylomorphic | persistence of the subsistent form, incomplete without matter; completion by re-embodiment | whether a form individuated by matter can persist without it |
| substance dualism | persistence of the non-physical subject | interaction and individuation burdens already recorded |

The result is not that continuation is established, but that no open realization model excludes it, and that each pushes the burden to the same place: $Ident(B,B^+)$. The reductive model carries the heaviest burden, because it must secure identity across a gap through organization alone.

C53 is relevant and does not block the route: actual agents retain de re identity across histories in the model, and $G$ individuates each actual conscious subject (C122). A re-realization of *that* individual is therefore not a modal-knowledge problem; it is an identity-criterion problem. The project does not adopt primitive haecceities to solve it.

## Step 3: divine role and power

Typing the divine relation to continuation:

| Option | Status in the architecture |
| --- | --- |
| part of $S$: subjects persist by their created nature | unsupported; no structural or empirical premise indicates it, and the embodied regime shows base-dependence |
| intervention: a token or general act of sustaining or re-realizing $B$ | coherent under C15 and C72; non-continuous dependence means $G$ need not have been keeping $B$ in being moment by moment, so a further act is an additional exercise (C71) |
| further creative act: a post-mortem domain within $\mathfrak R^*$ | coherent under OEC (C98–C100) and C47 |

Given qualified omnipotence (C74–C75), continuation is within $G$'s power if and only if it is coherent, that is, iff $Ident(B,B^+)$ can be satisfied. Nothing here entails that the power is exercised (C71). The typing also shows that continuation is a *divine* act in every route except a persistence route on a dualist or dual-aspect bearer, and even there the further arena is created.

## Step 4: the empirical bridge

The robust evidence is that in every observed case conscious life depends on brain function and ends with it. Under C183–C184 this constrains the actual embodied regime. It becomes a defeater of continuation only through a bridge of the form:

\[
NoObserved(PCon\ \text{without}\ RB)\;\Rightarrow\;\neg\Diamond\,PCon\ \text{without this}\ RB,
\]

which is the non-detection inference R27 forbids, and which every realization model except the reductive one rejects as a matter of what the subject is. Honesty cuts the other way too: no evidence supports continuation, re-realization is unobservable in principle, and near-death and similar reports are not admitted as evidence for the architecture (C292). Continuation is therefore empirically undefeated and empirically unsupported; its status is fixed by metaphysics and by the P-gap, not by data.

## Step 5: what the ontology does and does not establish

No established premise entails $Continues(B)$. The only support internal to the project is conditional:

\[
GoodOrient_P(G)\;\Rightarrow\;\forall B\,[\text{uncovered }s\text{ for }B\Rightarrow ContHorizon(B)].
\]

So the belief in divine goodness toward valence and the belief in continuation stand or fall together, and both are held under C303 as faith with stated content. The session neither strengthens nor weakens that conditional; it shows that the consequent is coherent, within divine power, and empirically undefeated, so the conditional does not collapse into a contradiction. That is the honest maximum at F1.

## Step 6: consequences if continuation obtains

Two consequences follow from anchors already in force and are recorded as preferred:

1. **Universality.** Universal personal availability (C122) and the $R$ anchor make a continuation offered only to the uncovered a misfit toward the others. If continuation obtains at all, it is available to every bearer, not only to those the argument needed it for.
2. **Co-production, not imposition.** For $S$-capable bearers, post-mortem communion is asymmetrically co-produced (C124): $G$ makes it available and contributes to it, but does not override sourcehood to impose it. This is why S28's privative model remains coherent as the negative case: a bearer may not accept the relation. For bearers without $S$-capacity, such as an infant who died in pain, $P$-fit can be given directly, and the co-production condition does not apply.

The second consequence connects the argument back to U16: the choice of God or not, which the author took from the traditions, appears here as a consequence of sourcehood rather than as an imported doctrine.

## Literature and novelty notes

Cited from memory; not verified online in this session.

- Peter van Inwagen, “The Possibility of Resurrection” (1978), and Dean Zimmerman, “The Compatibility of Materialism and Survival: The ‘Falling Elevator’ Model” (1999): the identity burden under materialist re-realization; the replica and gappy-existence problems.
- Lynne Rudder Baker, *Persons and Bodies* (Cambridge, 2000): a constitution view of persons compatible with re-embodiment; nearest to the non-reductive embodied route.
- Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I.75–76 and 89: subsistent but incomplete soul, completion by resurrection; the hylomorphic route.
- Richard Swinburne, *The Evolution of the Soul* (Oxford, 1986): the dualist persistence route.
- Derek Parfit, *Reasons and Persons* (Oxford, 1984): why identity may not be what matters; the project's bearer-indexed fit resists that move but must answer it in CH17.
- John Hick, *Death and Eternal Life* (1976), and Marilyn McCord Adams, *Horrendous Evils and the Goodness of God* (Cornell, 1999): continuation as required by a theodicy that respects the individual sufferer; closest precedents for the conditional of Step 5.
- Stanford Encyclopedia entries on personal identity and on afterlife, for the survey.

Classification: established problem families; the project-specific elements are the typing of continuation as intervention or further creative act within $\mathfrak R^*$ under non-continuous dependence, the mapping of routes onto the S29 realization models, and the derivation of universality and co-production from the anchors. No novelty claim.
