# Session: Divine knowledge of a subject as a subject

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S34
**Primary question:** Does ACO's propositional completeness exhaust knowing an actual conscious creature as a subject, or does divine knowledge require acquaintance or irreducibly second-personal relation?
**Dependencies:** C10–C14, C28, C47–C54, C90–C94, C121–C126, C134–C139, C242–C243, C270, CH03, S05, S20, S22, S29
**Target depth:** local F2 closure of CH03

## Result in one sentence

ACO remains complete with respect to every truth about actual reality, but the preferred divine epistemic profile adds **actual subject acquaintance** $ASA(G,A)$ as a direct, factive, subject-indexed mode of access to every actual conscious subject. $ASA$ neither identifies $G$ with $A$ nor requires $G$ to token the same phenomenal state, and it remains distinct from reciprocal second-personal presence, which requires actual participation by the creature.

## Preserve ACO's exact role

ACO was deliberately defined by scope:

\[
ACO(G)\leftrightarrow
\forall p[TrueIn(p,\mathfrak R^*)\rightarrow K(G,p)].
\]

It says that no truth about complete actual reality is unknown to $G$. It does not define every epistemic relation by which $G$ knows. The distinction is between:

- **epistemic scope:** which truths and actual relata are cognitively available;
- **epistemic mode:** whether access is descriptive, inferential, representational, indexical, or directly acquainted.

Adding acquaintance therefore does not retract propositional completeness or add a previously missing truth. It denies only that a list of known propositions exhausts every cognitively significant relation to a subject.

## Four increasingly strong claims

| Level | Content | Current status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| propositional exhaustiveness | $G$ knows every truth about $A$ and its actual states | accepted through ACO |
| de re subject recognition | $G$ knows which actual individual is the bearer and source of those states | required by ACO plus the ontology of subjects |
| actual subject acquaintance $ASA$ | $G$ has direct, factive access to $A$ and to the first-personal organization of $A$'s actual states | preferred addition |
| reciprocal second-personal presence | $G$ and $A$ stand in actual mutual awareness, address, or shared attention | stronger and contingent/co-produced |

These levels prevent “knowing as a subject” from oscillating between mere identification and full interpersonal communion.

## Actual subject acquaintance

Let $c$ be an actual conscious state borne by $A$. Define:

\[
\begin{aligned}
ASA(G,A)\leftrightarrow{}&Actual(A)
\land ConsciousSubject(A)\\
&\land DirectEpistemicAccess(G,A)\\
&\land \forall c[ActualStateOf(c,A)\\
&\qquad\rightarrow GraspsAsPresentedTo(G,c,A)].
\end{aligned}
\]

The relation has at least five constraints.

### 1. Factivity

There is no concrete acquaintance with a merely possible subject:

\[
ASA(G,A)\rightarrow Actual(A).
\]

$G$ may know a possible subject-type, its organization, possible experiences, and possible originating powers through MKO. The numerically particular subject and its token first-personal life become available only through actuality.

### 2. Subject indexing

$G$ does not merely know that pain, joy, intention, or deliberation occurs. $G$ grasps the state as belonging and being presented to this subject:

\[
GraspsAsPresentedTo(G,c,A)
\not\equiv
GraspsAsPresentedTo(G,c,G).
\]

This protects the difference between “$A$ experiences red” and “I experience red” without requiring $G$ falsely to self-ascribe $A$'s state.

### 3. Qualitative discrimination

If two conscious states differ in their phenomenal or first-personal character, perfect subject acquaintance distinguishes them even if a coarse third-person description does not:

\[
PhenDiff(c_1,c_2)
\rightarrow Distinguishes_{ASA}(G,c_1,c_2).
\]

This is a constraint on adequacy, not yet a complete metaphysics of qualia.

### 4. Source discrimination

When $A$ originates a libertarian choice, $G$ grasps both its content and its source-role:

\[
Originates(A,q)
\rightarrow KnowsSourceAs(G,q,A).
\]

Acquaintance does not transfer origination to $G$. It strengthens the distinction between knowing or enabling a choice and supplying its contrastive settlement.

### 5. Non-identity

Direct epistemic access does not entail numerical identity, constitution, or experiential duplication:

\[
ASA(G,A)
\not\Rightarrow
G=A
\lor ConstituentOf(G,A)
\lor Tokens(G,c).
\]

This fits constitutive transcendence and relational immanence. Directness is a relation, not a claim that every creaturely mental state is literally a state of $G$.

## Atemporality and actuality

For a temporal subject, a state comes to occur at $t$ and may change what that subject can know. For atemporal $G$, the entire actual state and its relation to $A$ lie within the complete history:

\[
ActualStateOf(c,A,H^*)
\rightarrow AtemporallyAcquainted(G,c,A,H^*).
\]

The dependence is logical or grounding-based:

\[
Actual(c,A)\Rightarrow KnowableByASA(G,c,A),
\]

not a temporal sequence in which $G$ waits, observes, and learns. This is the same structure already used for ACO.

Crucially:

\[
\neg Actual(A)\Rightarrow\neg ASA(G,A).
\]

Before actuality in explanatory order, MKO can represent the possible type and alternative states, but there is no actual token perspective to be acquainted with. Once $A$ and its free act belong to $H^*$, ACO knows every truth about them and $ASA$ directly accesses their subject-indexed actuality. No pre-actual selection counterfactual is introduced.

## Does $G$ have to feel the creature's experience?

Three interpretations must be distinguished:

1. **exact cognitive discrimination:** $G$ perfectly distinguishes and understands the token phenomenal character as presented to $A$;
2. **vicarious phenomenal representation:** $G$ has a divine state representing what $A$ experiences from $A$'s perspective;
3. **token sharing:** numerically the same phenomenal state belongs to both $A$ and $G$.

$ASA$ requires the first. The second is a live stronger model. The third is not entailed and would require an additional account of shared state identity, subject boundaries, passibility, and possible divine–world overlap.

This restraint matters. Requiring $G$ to feel every pain as its own may confuse perfect knowledge of a state with possession of that state. Denying every direct qualitative access, however, would leave a gap whenever phenomenal differences outrun the selected third-person vocabulary. $ASA$ occupies the middle position: perfect direct grasp without automatic token identity.

## Relation to imagination

Human empathy often uses imagination to simulate another person's perspective. Divine $ASA$ need not be inferential simulation because $RI$ places every actual subject within direct epistemic reach. Nevertheless, the comparison exposes a new ontological question: what kind of reality belongs to an imagined image, thought, scenario, or merely represented possibility?

That issue is registered as CH14 rather than solved here. At minimum, future work must distinguish:

- the actual mental act or process of imagining;
- its intentional content;
- any represented but non-actual object or scenario;
- the modal possibility, impossibility, or indeterminacy of that content;
- the causal efficacy of the actual imaginative episode.

An actual thought is part of $H^*$ even when its represented content is non-actual. The actuality of the representation does not actualize what it represents:

\[
Actual(Imagines(A,S))
\not\Rightarrow Actual(S).
\]

This is likely to connect CH14 with MKO, the metaphysical definition of $Poss_C^{ind}(S)$, intentionality, and the ontology of consciousness.

## Second-personal relation

Let $2P(G,A)$ involve actual mutual orientation, such as reciprocal awareness, address, response, or shared attention. Then:

\[
2P(G,A)\rightarrow ASA(G,A),
\]

but:

\[
ASA(G,A)\not\Rightarrow 2P(G,A).
\]

$G$ can know an unconscious, unaware, resistant, or non-responsive subject directly. A reciprocal relation requires some fitting creaturely capacity and actual participation. This aligns with S20:

- $ASA$ belongs to divine epistemic access;
- $UPA$ is the capacity to enter direct personal relation;
- exercised address or regard depends on divine action;
- reciprocal shared attention is asymmetrically co-produced;
- communion adds evaluative and volitional conditions.

Therefore irreducible second-personal knowledge is not required for minimal omniscience. It is required for certain actual forms of interpersonal presence whose content cannot be supplied unilaterally by $G$ while remaining reciprocal.

## Later author ratification and asymmetry

The author explicitly accepts $ASA$ and prefers leaving the exact realization of mutual interaction open. S35 adds that reciprocity need not imply equal communicative capacities. $G$ may comprehend $A$ exhaustively while $A$ receives only partial, accommodated, analogical, or mediated content concerning $G$. Genuine divine address and genuine creaturely uptake can therefore constitute asymmetric communication without collapsing into either unilateral awareness or equal understanding.

## Revised divine epistemic profile

Do not represent the profile as a set-theoretic union of items of one type. It is a structured tuple of complementary roles:

\[
K_G^{total}=\langle ACO_P,MKO,ASA\rangle,
\]

where:

- $ACO_P$ gives complete propositional knowledge of actual truth;
- $MKO$ gives structural and power-grounded modal knowledge without pre-actual creaturely selection truths;
- $ASA$ gives direct subject-indexed access to actual conscious subjects and states.

The subscript on $ACO_P$ makes its propositional role explicit in this comparison; it does not replace the established name ACO elsewhere.

This profile is complete in scope while plural in epistemic mode. It also avoids claiming that $G$ has human sensory organs, neural simulation, memory, or temporally acquired empathy.

## Derivation and evidential status

$ASA$ does not follow from ACO by definition. It is preferred through a cumulative argument:

1. actual conscious states include first-personal organization not exhausted by coarse physical or behavioral description;
2. ACO gives complete truth but was neutral about non-propositional access;
3. $G$ is already modeled as a conscious subject with intellect;
4. $RI$ and $OP_A$ place every actual subject within immediate epistemic reach;
5. a direct acquaintance relation explains subject-sensitive knowledge more economically than an internal simulation for every creature;
6. no identity, causal authorship, or temporal-learning consequence follows.

The result is preferred at local F2 rather than demonstrated. A thorough reductionist account on which all cognitively significant access reduces to propositional knowledge remains a coherent alternative. It is less satisfying for the project's intended personal knowledge because it treats the mode of access as epiphenomenal or eliminable.

## Literature and novelty notes

- [Travis Dickinson, “God knows: acquaintance and the nature of divine knowledge”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034412517000324) develops divine knowledge through acquaintance and argues that theories focused only on scope neglect the nature of divine knowing. S34 adopts the scope/mode distinction but applies acquaintance specifically to actual subjects within ACO's actuality conditions.
- [Linda Zagzebski, “Omnisubjectivity”](https://www.baylor.edu/content/services/document.php/39971.pdf) argues that omniscience includes a perfect grasp of every creature's first-person perspective and models it through perfect empathy without identity with the creature. S34 uses her problem and several distinctions but does not require a copied phenomenal state.
- [Eleonore Stump, “Theology and the Knowledge of Persons”](http://hdl.handle.net/20.500.12153/4536) distinguishes knowledge of persons from knowledge-that and emphasizes second-personal knowledge. S34 reserves reciprocal second-personal relation for a stronger co-produced level rather than making it a condition of unilateral divine knowledge.

Acquaintance, de se knowledge, phenomenal understanding, empathy, and second-personal knowledge are established fields. No novelty claim is made for those components. The potentially useful local synthesis is their actuality indexing inside E+, the tuple $\langle ACO_P,MKO,ASA\rangle$, non-transfer of agentive sourcehood, and separation of unilateral subject acquaintance from co-produced second-personal presence.
