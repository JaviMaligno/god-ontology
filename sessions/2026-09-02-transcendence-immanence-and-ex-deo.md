# Session: Constitutive transcendence, relational immanence, and *ex deo*

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Session ID:** S31
**Primary question:** How can $G$ be external to creation while omnipresent and able to intervene, and which sense of creation *ex deo* would genuinely entail divine–world overlap?
**Dependencies:** C35–C46, C68, C84–C94, C98–C103, C222–C239, S04, S14–S15, S20, S30
**Target depth:** Local F2 refinement before the qualified-power blocker analysis

## Result in one sentence

The preferred architecture combines **constitutive transcendence** with **relational immanence**: creation does not constitute $G$ and $G$ is not a member of the physical order, yet every created relatum lies within $G$'s immediate grounding, epistemic, and causal reach. This needs no substantial overlap. A strict *ex deo* model begins only when numerically divine reality constitutes, persists in, or becomes created reality.

## Four meanings of “external”

Calling $G$ external can express different claims:

| Sense | Content | Current status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| spatial externality | $G$ occupies another region outside the cosmos | rejected as the intended model; $G$ is fundamentally non-spatial |
| physical-category externality | $G$ is not a body, field, or system constituted under $S_{phys}$ | preferred |
| constitutive transcendence | creation does not constitute, contain, or explain $G$'s basic identity | preferred |
| relational remoteness | $G$ lacks immediate epistemic, grounding, causal, or personal relations to creatures | rejected |

The first sense is misleading because “outside” suggests a larger spatial container. The preferred formulation is:

\[
CT(G,\mathcal C_0)
\leftrightarrow
GroundPrior(G,\mathcal C_0)
\land \neg ConstitutedBy(G,\mathcal C_0)
\land \neg PhysicalMember(G,S_{phys}).
\]

This is **constitutive transcendence** ($CT$), not spatial distance.

## Relational immanence

S15 already defines:

\[
Present_O(G,x)
\leftrightarrow
GroundReach(G,x)
\land EpistemicAccess(G,x)
\land CausalAvailability(G,x).
\]

Call the resulting universal profile relational immanence:

\[
RI(G,\mathcal C^*)
\leftrightarrow
\forall x\in D(\mathcal C^*)\;Present_O(G,x).
\]

$RI$ adds no fourth causal force. It names the already preferred integration of grounding, ACO, and direct causal availability. Therefore:

\[
CT(G,\mathcal C_0)
\land RI(G,\mathcal C^*)
\]

is coherent. The predicates answer different questions: what constitutes $G$, and how $G$ relates to creation.

An intervention is an exercise of causal availability with respect to a created target. A non-spatial $G$ does not travel through a boundary or enter a region in order to act there:

\[
Intervenes(G,e)
\Rightarrow
ExercisesAt(G,P_I,e),
\]

where “at” indexes the created target of the exercise, not $G$'s spatial location. Directness excludes a necessary created intermediary; it does not imply identity or shared substance.

## Numerical distinction is not constitutive disjointness

S30 requires:

\[
G\neq\mathcal C_0.
\]

That condition alone does not exclude overlap. Two numerically distinct realities may share a constituent, or one may be a mode or proper part of the other. Define the stronger condition:

\[
\begin{aligned}
CCD(G,\mathcal C_0)
\leftrightarrow{}&
\neg\exists z[ConstituentOf(z,G)\land ConstituentOf(z,\mathcal C_0)]\\
&\land\neg ModeOf(\mathcal C_0,G)
\land\neg ModeOf(G,\mathcal C_0).
\end{aligned}
\]

$CCD$ is **creator–creation constitutive disjointness**. It does not deny grounding, causal dependence, resemblance, participation, presence, or personal relation.

$MCS(G)$ and $CCD(G,\mathcal C_0)$ are not equivalent. $MCS$ denies ontologically prior ingredients and an external bearer of $G$. It does not by itself exclude the asymmetric possibility that divine reality constitutes a contingent effect without that effect constituting fundamental $G$. The preferred $OEC^+$ model therefore adds $CCD$ explicitly rather than deriving it from simplicity.

## Four senses of *ex deo*

The expression “from God” can mark four progressively stronger relations:

| Level | Relation | Requires divine–world constitutive overlap? | Relation to the preferred model |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $ED_C$ causal source | $G$ causes the existence of $\mathcal C_0$ | no | entailed by $OEC^+$ |
| $ED_V$ virtual or exemplar source | $G$ knows $S$ and possesses power adequate to instantiate it | no | entailed by $OEC^+$ |
| $ED_P$ participatory derivation | created powers are dependent finite analogues of powers in $G$ | no numerical identity required | compatible; preferred for created agency where the analogy applies |
| $ED_S$ strict constitutive source | numerically divine reality constitutes, persists in, or becomes created reality | yes | live but disfavored competitor |

Formally, strict *ex deo* requires more than causation or specification:

\[
ED_S(G,\mathcal C_0)
\leftrightarrow
\exists d[
DivineToken(d,G)
\land (
Constitutes(d,\mathcal C_0)
\lor PersistsIn(d,\mathcal C_0)
\lor Becomes(d,\mathcal C_0)
)].
\]

Accordingly:

\[
ED_C\lor ED_V\lor ED_P
\not\Rightarrow ED_S.
\]

Calling every caused or power-contained effect *ex deo* would erase the substantive distinction under investigation. The project may say that creation is “from $G$” in causal, exemplar, and participatory senses, but reserves **strict *ex deo*** for $ED_S$.

## Refining $OEC^+$

The preferred full formulation now includes constitutive disjointness:

\[
OEC^+_{CCD}(G,\mathcal C_0,S)
\leftrightarrow
OEC^+(G,\mathcal C_0,S)
\land CCD(G,\mathcal C_0).
\]

This makes the model's creator–creation distinction explicit. It is compatible with:

- existential dependence of $\mathcal C_0$ on $G$;
- structural specification through $S$;
- finite analogous powers;
- omnipresence and direct intervention;
- personal availability and possible communion.

None requires a numerically divine ingredient in the creature.

## Does strict *ex deo* solve material production?

Not automatically. $ED_S$ establishes a constitutive connection, but $PMC_U$ demands a **material** cause. A non-material divine token that constitutes a material effect leaves a cross-category question unless an additional principle explains how that token qualifies as material input:

\[
ED_S(G,\mathcal C_0)
\not\Rightarrow
\exists m\,MaterialCause(m,\mathcal C_0).
\]

Strict *ex deo* directly answers $PMC_U$ only if:

\[
\exists d[
DivineToken(d,G)
\land MaterialCause(d,\mathcal C_0)
].
\]

That stronger version must reconcile divine materiality with $ASP_G$, $MCS$, unity, and the contingency of creation. It may therefore move the burden rather than remove it.

## Strict-overlap variants

| Variant | Description | Main burden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| partition or transfer | a divine constituent becomes or supplies created matter | divine loss, division, persistence, and whether the constituent was a prior part |
| contingent divine mode | the world is a contingent mode or manifestation of $G$ | whether modes are genuine divine reality, whether $MCS$ survives, and how creaturely sourcehood remains contrastive |
| world as divine body | created reality bears an organism-like relation to $G$ | spatiality, individuation, dependence, and whether embodiment changes divine constitution |
| whole-presence constitution | the undivided $G$ constitutes created entities without partition | how numerically one subject constitutes many distinct material bearers without collapsing identities |

These are not shown contradictory. They require different metaphysics and must not be grouped as one solution.

## Omnipresence and omnipotence

Omnipresence supports $RI$, not $ED_S$:

\[
OP_A(G)\not\Rightarrow ED_S(G,\mathcal C_0).
\]

It also does not exclude $ED_S$ without the added $CCD$ condition. A panentheist can accept pervasive presence plus overlap; the preferred model accepts pervasive presence plus disjoint constitution.

Qualified omnipotence similarly concerns the range of coherent divine actions, not a default substance-sharing relation. Possessing power to act immediately on every created relatum neither entails exercising every power nor entails the ability to make an essential divine identity cease to be itself. The next blocker analysis must ask separately whether a constitutively overlapping manifestation is:

1. coherent with $UPSS$ and independently established essential features;
2. within $G$'s power if coherent;
3. ever required by maximality;
4. relevant to creation, intervention, incarnation-like manifestation, or none of these.

The answer to the third question is presently no: omnipotence does not require exercising every coherent power or choosing an overlapping form of creation.

## Decisions

| Decision | Status | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Replace spatial “externality” with constitutive transcendence | preferred correction | $G$ is not located in an outer region; the relevant independence concerns constitution and physical category |
| Combine $CT$ with relational immanence $RI$ | preferred | It expresses strong transcendence and immediate presence without contradiction |
| Infer overlap from omnipresence or intervention | rejected | Immediate relation and direct action do not entail spatial occupation or shared constitution |
| Treat numerical distinction as constitutive disjointness | rejected | Distinct realities may overlap or stand in a mode relation |
| Add $CCD$ to the preferred full $OEC^+$ model | preferred refinement | It makes the intended creator–creation distinction explicit rather than smuggling it into $G\neq\mathcal C_0$ |
| Use broad causal, virtual, and participatory “from $G$” relations | accepted with typed terminology | They capture genuine dependence without material or substantial continuity |
| Reserve strict *ex deo* for $ED_S$ | accepted terminology | Only numerical divine constitution, persistence, or becoming creates the disputed overlap |
| Treat all strict *ex deo* variants as one model | rejected | Transfer, mode, body, and whole-presence accounts have different commitments |
| Claim strict *ex deo* automatically satisfies $PMC_U$ | rejected | A shared non-material divine reality is not yet a material cause |
| Exclude every strict *ex deo* model through $MCS$ alone | rejected | Direction of constitution matters; $CCD$ carries the preferred exclusion |
| Treat strict *ex deo* as contradictory | not established | It remains a disfavored alternative requiring substantial revision |

## Literature and novelty notes

- The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on omnipresence](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/omnipresence/) surveys knowledge, power, cause-of-being, spatial occupation, and world-as-divine-body accounts. This supports separating relational presence from occupation and constitution.
- [Daniel Soars, “Creation in Aquinas: *ex nihilo* or *ex deo*?”](https://doi.org/10.1111/nbfr.12603) shows why causal or virtual pre-containment can blur the terminology. The four-level distinction prevents that ambiguity from settling the ontology.
- Recent [grounding-panentheist work](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034412525100978) treats divine–world overlap as an additional grounding model. That is precedent for distinguishing relational dependence from constitutive overlap.

The transcendence/immanence distinction, causal versus constitutive dependence, panentheism, emanation, and world-as-body models are established families. The local four-level *ex deo* taxonomy and its integration with $Present_O$, $MCS$, $CCD$, and $OEC^+$ are organizational refinements. No novelty claim is made.
