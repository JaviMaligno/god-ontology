# Session: The P-reason — ground, target, uptake, and orientation

**Date:** 2026-09-03
**Session ID:** S75
**Primary question:** Do either of C526's routes supply a reason directed at conscious valence without assuming divine goodness, and what would such a reason actually establish?
**Dependencies:** C55–C67, C104–C113, C121–C126, C187–C205, C283–C296, C462–C465, C472–C479, C521–C527, S18, S27–S28, S34, S48–S49, S56
**Origin:** S74 returned the active trunk to C526; the author asked the project to continue from that priority
**Target depth:** selective F2 for the reason architecture; no complete ethics or theodicy

## Starting position

S56 exposed an exact absence. The established creative profile contains reasons directed at sourcehood $S$ and truthful recognition or relation $R$, but no reason directed at conscious valence $P$. C526 left two candidates:

1. read actual finite otherness as making subjects actual for their own sake;
2. derive regard for a valenced subject from truthful relation with that subject.

Neither candidate works as a bare entailment. Wanting an actual other need not mean wanting what is good for it, and knowing a subject's experience through $ASA$ need not motivate any response. The two routes nevertheless supply different parts of a stronger cumulative case once three distinctions are made.

## Step 1: a reason needs both a ground and a target

C521 types a creative reason as $\langle a,\mu,\ell\rangle$: target, mode, and level. That is sufficient while every reason is treated as grounded directly in its target. It is insufficient for C526's second route. A relation can ground a reason whose practical target is the welfare of one participant.

The fuller form is therefore:

\[
r=\langle g,a,\mu,\ell\rangle,
\]

where:

- $g$ is the **ground**: the fact, profile, or relation in virtue of which the reason obtains;
- $a$ is the **target**: what the response is directed at;
- $\mu\in\{favoring,justifying,requiring\}$ is its mode;
- $\ell\in\{creation,architecture,specification\}$ is its creative level.

The old triple remains valid shorthand whenever $g=a$ or the ground is not under discussion. The two C526 candidates can now be stated without ambiguity:

\[
r_P^{dir,str}=\langle Profile_P(S),P,favoring,\ell\rangle,
\qquad
r_P^{R,str}=\langle Profile_R(S),P,favoring,\ell\rangle.
\]

The second is genuinely P-directed even though its ground is relational. This does not reduce $P$ to $R$ or make every relation benevolent.

## Step 2: applicability, uptake, exercise, and orientation

S56 also moved too quickly from the existence of a P-reason to $GoodOrient_P(G)$. Four levels must be separated:

| Level | Predicate | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| applicability | $Applies(r,A)$ | an objective normative reason bears on agent $A$ |
| uptake | $Owns(A,r)$ | $A$ admits $r$ into its deliberative or standing reason profile |
| exercise | $Exercises(A,r,\alpha)$ | $r$ partly explains or shapes act or policy $\alpha$ |
| orientation | $GoodOrient_P(A)$ | every relevant disposition and act responds fittingly to $P$, so every permitted P-misfit has an undefeated cover |

Their order is one-way:

\[
GoodOrient_P(G)\Rightarrow Owns(G,r_P)\Rightarrow Applies(r_P,G),
\]

and, for an actual act,

\[
Exercises(G,r_P,\alpha)\Rightarrow Owns(G,r_P).
\]

None of the converses holds merely by definition. An agent can have a reason and ignore it; it can sometimes act from a reason without responding perfectly in every case; and a favoring reason can be defeated without disappearing. This correction prevents a thin P-reason from silently becoming omnibenevolence or a no-suffering guarantee.

## Step 3: the direct route supplies normative applicability

The direct route does not begin from an unexplained benevolent desire. It begins from the already preferred minimal axiological interface:

1. $P$ is an objective anchor rather than a report of preference (C187–C189).
2. Its directed profile distinguishes realization, protection, restoration, and corruption for a bearer (C203–C205).
3. A due but absent or corrupted P-good makes an apt response objectively pertinent. This is the admitted normative content of $Fit_*$, not a new divine attribute.

Define a response-scope condition:

\[
RespScope(A,B,x,C,\tau)
\]

when $A$ can identify the bearer and the P-relevant fact, can make an apt response, and stands in a causal, creative, or personal relation that makes the response pertinent rather than arbitrary interference. Let $PCase$ cover an obtaining realization, protection, loss, or corruption of P-fit. Then, conditional on the preferred $Fit_*$ account:

\[
PCase(x,B,C,\tau)\land RespScope(A,B,x,C,\tau)
\Rightarrow Applies(r_{P,B}^{act},A).
\]

$G$ satisfies the scope condition for every actual conscious bearer: $G$ grounds the bearer and arena, knows the bearer and its valence through ACO and $ASA$, is causally available, and is personally available. Therefore actual P-fit and P-misfit supply pro tanto normative reasons applicable to $G$.

This is a conditional derivation from $Fit_*$, not an assumption that $G$ satisfies the reasons it grounds or knows. R30 remains in force. It establishes applicability only.

## Step 4: otherness and relation support uptake

The otherness and relation routes cannot by themselves create the normative content of $P$. They instead address why that content belongs in $G$'s operative creative profile.

The established profile is not neutral between two contrastive hypotheses. $H_{participant}$ requires that participants have non-instrumental standing; it does not deny that other expressive, relational, or aesthetic motives may also operate.

| Hypothesis | Reading of finite subjects | Fit with the established profile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $H_{means}$ | subjects are only instruments, displays, or sources of interesting outcomes for $G$ | can explain their existence, but leaves agency preservation, universal availability, and the classification of otherness as an $R$-target as separate additions |
| $H_{participant}$ | subjects are sought as numerically distinct participants whose own sourcehood and standpoint matter | jointly explains analogous sourcehood, the non-scripted architecture, agency-preservation, truthful recognition, and personal availability |

C524 already treats actual finite otherness as an $R$-target rather than as a means to an unrecorded divine benefit. $H_{participant}$ is therefore the better integrated reading of the accepted profile. It is abductive, because an instrumental creator remains logically possible.

A valenced participant has a standpoint under which things can go better or worse for that very bearer. $ASA$ gives $G$ direct, factive access to that standpoint. If $G$ treats the subject as a participant while systematically refusing to let anything within that standpoint count as a reason, the profile fragments: the subject matters as a source and interlocutor but never as the bearer who undergoes the history. The simpler integrated extension is:

\[
H_{participant}\land Applies(r_{P,B}^{act},G)
\leadsto_{abd} Owns(G,r_{P,B}^{act}).
\]

At the structurally prior creative level, the parallel inference uses MKO's type-profile knowledge rather than a non-actual individual:

\[
H_{participant}\land Knows_M(G,Profile_P(S))
\leadsto_{abd} Owns(G,r_P^{thin,str}).
\]

Relation can also ground the uptake directly:

\[
r_P^{R,str}=\langle Profile_R(S),P,favoring,\ell\rangle.
\]

Here the relation is the ground and the participant's valence is the focus of response. The inference requires participant-regard; mere truth tracking or accurate observation remains insufficient.

## Step 5: the two routes are complementary

C526 presented the routes as alternatives. The stronger result is asymmetric combination:

\[
\underbrace{P+Fit_*}_{\text{normative content and applicability}}
+
\underbrace{R+finite\ otherness+ASA}_{\text{agent-specific uptake support}}
\leadsto_{abd}
Owns(G,r_P^{thin}).
\]

$r_P^{thin}$ has two logically ordered applications that must not be conflated:

| Context | Reason object | Individuation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| creative selection | $r_P^{thin,str}$ concerns $Profile_P(S)$: valence capacities, structural exposures, and available forms of protection or repair | type- and structure-indexed; no merely possible individual is treated as actual |
| obtaining created reality | $r_{P,B}^{thin,act}$ concerns an actual bearer's obtaining P-fit or P-misfit | de re and bearer-indexed through ACO/$ASA$ |

The creative reason may operate at creation, architecture, or specification level wherever modal P-profiles differ. It is not a maximizing rule, a total ordering, or a requiring reason that selects a unique world. It uses no pre-actual libertarian choice and attributes no welfare claim to a merely possible individual. Once a bearer is actual, its token reasons do not collapse into the earlier structural profile.

At the project's constructive standard, $Owns(G,r_P^{thin})$ is **preferred at F2**, at the same abductive evidential level as the organizing reasons of C105. The result is stronger than bare faith that valence somehow matters to $G$, but weaker than $GoodOrient_P(G)$.

## Step 6: what remains open

The thin reason does not establish:

- that it is requiring rather than favoring;
- that it defeats every competing $S$-, $R$-, or non-axiological reason;
- that this is the best possible world or that any best world exists;
- that aggregate welfare overrides bearer-indexed fit;
- that every actual suffering has a cover;
- that every bearer has an extended undefeated horizon;
- omnibenevolence, love, or affective divine valence.

The strong P-gap is therefore not erased. It is narrowed and relocated:

\[
\text{old gap: does any P-reason belong to the profile?}
\]

\[
\text{remaining gap: is the response to P undefeated and exhaustive in every actual case?}
\]

Under C473, the remaining claim is equivalent to the absence of gratuitous suffering. C479 and C485 retain their status: that strong claim, and the personal continuation it may require, remain faith with exact content rather than an earned result.

## Adversarial tests

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Objective valence automatically binds every possible agent | Rejected. $RespScope$ is required; applicability depends on epistemic, capacitative, and relational pertinence. $G$ satisfies it for actual creatures for independent reasons. |
| Grounding $Fit_*$ means $G$ must obey it | Rejected by the applicability–uptake–orientation distinction and R30. Ground, recognition, motivation, and satisfaction are different roles. |
| Truthful relation entails benevolence | Rejected. Cognitive recognition alone does not yield conative concern. The uptake inference uses the cumulative participant profile and is abductive. |
| Treating subjects as participants means fulfilling their desires | Rejected. P is not desire satisfaction, and $P$, $S$, and $R$ can conflict. The structural and actual reasons are pro tanto. |
| A creative P-reason reifies merely possible subjects or presupposes what they would choose | Rejected. Creative deliberation ranges over $Profile_P(S)$ and possible bearer types; de re bearer-indexing begins only after actuality, in parallel with $ASA$. |
| A P-reason makes suffering impossible | Rejected. A favoring reason may be defeated. What it excludes is complete normative indifference, not every P-misfit. |
| The existence of terrible suffering refutes even the thin P-reason | Not by itself. It is evidence against strong orientation and may be evidence against uptake; it defeats the thin thesis only if the best explanation is that valence did no work at any level. Apparently gratuitous suffering remains attended counterevidence. |
| “For their own sake” simply assumes the conclusion | Correct if used as a premise. Here participant-regard is the abductive interpretation supported by the whole $S/R$ profile, while the direct P norm comes from $Fit_*$ independently. |
| The new ground field changes every prior reason | It refines the representation. The old triple remains shorthand when ground and target coincide; no prior ordering or eligibility result changes. |

## Decisions

| Claim ID | Status | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C616 | `accepted refinement` | Creative reasons have a ground and a target; the canonical full form is $\langle g,a,\mu,\ell\rangle$, with C521's triple retained as shorthand when the ground is suppressed. |
| C617 | `accepted distinction` | Normative applicability, uptake, exercise, and good-orientation are distinct; their implications run from orientation toward applicability, not conversely. |
| C618 | `preferred conditional theorem` | Under the preferred $Fit_*$ account, P-fit or P-misfit supplies a pro tanto reason to any agent satisfying $RespScope$; $G$ satisfies that scope for every actual conscious bearer through grounding, ACO/$ASA$, causal availability, and personal availability. |
| C619 | `accepted limit` | Neither actual finite otherness nor truthful cognitive recognition alone entails conative P-concern. |
| C620 | `preferred abductive reading at F2` | The cumulative profile favors $H_{participant}$ over $H_{means}$: created subjects are sought as participants whose sourcehood and standpoint matter, not merely as instruments or displays. |
| C621 | `preferred at F2` | The direct and relational routes jointly support $Owns(G,r_P^{thin})$: its creative application is indexed to modal structural P-profiles, while its actual response instances are de re and bearer-indexed. |
| C622 | `accepted correction of C525` | Possessing or exercising one P-reason is insufficient for $GoodOrient_P(G)$; strong orientation additionally requires undefeated fitting response in every relevant actual case and therefore no gratuitous suffering. |

## Literature and novelty notes

**Author review (2026-09-03):** after comparison with austere relationalism, instrumental creation, and contingent divine self-binding, the author judged the integrated participant interpretation reasonable. C616–C623 are therefore accepted at their stated levels; this does not upgrade the explicitly open claims about exhaustive P-orientation or omnibenevolence.

- [Mark C. Murphy, *God's Own Ethics* (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198796916.001.0001) argues that God may rationally promote creaturely welfare without being governed by an automatic requiring norm to do so. This is a close precedent for a non-requiring P-reason, though Murphy's perfect-being framework and self-imposed-norm conclusion are not imported.
- [Niko Kolodny, “Love as Valuing a Relationship” (2003)](https://www.mit.edu/~shaslang/mprg/KolodnyLVR.pdf) distinguishes the ground of valuation from its focus and argues that a relationship can provide reasons to act in a participant's interests. S75 borrows the ground/target distinction, not Kolodny's theory of love or its application to $G$.
- [Stephen Darwall, “Respect and the Second-Person Standpoint”](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/62458/Darwall2004_Respect_and_the_Second-Person_Standpoint.pdf%3Bsequence%3D1) connects recognition of persons with second-personal authority. The project does not import equal reciprocal authority into its asymmetrical divine–creature relation; the source is a comparison point for the claim that recognizing a participant can have practical, not merely cognitive, implications.
- Aquinas's account of love as willing good to another is a stronger traditional route. It is not used because divine love has not been independently established and would make the desired conclusion a premise.

Classification: objective pro tanto reasons, relationship-grounded reasons, and distinctions between normative and motivating reasons are established. The local synthesis is the four-place creative-reason form, the complementary rather than rival treatment of C526's routes, and the exact partition of applicable P-reason, operative P-reason, and exhaustive P-orientation. No novelty claim is made before a systematic comparison.
