# Session: Thought, imagination, and non-actual reference

**Date:** 2026-09-03
**Session ID:** S76
**Primary question:** What kind of reality belongs to a thought or imaginative episode, its intentional content, and what it is about when the apparent object is actual, merely possible, fictional, impossible, or absent?
**Dependencies:** C47–C54, C134–C139, C212–C221, C282–C292, C324–C354, C506–C520, C528–C534, C542, CH14, S29, S34, S37–S38, S55, S57, S60
**Origin:** CH14 was banked by the author and became the active ontology-first priority after S75
**Target depth:** F2 working ontology of finite cognitive episodes, content, reference, fiction, and causal role; no complete philosophy of language, abstracta, or divine cognition

## Result in one sentence

The preferred working model is a **token-real, target-role account**: an actual thought is a temporally located manifestation of an embodied subject with an actually instantiated content-profile; that profile may purport to refer through a target-role without any corresponding entity, while actual reference requires an independently successful anchor-and-fit route, possibility is settled by `Adm_C`, public fiction is minimally grounded in actual representational artifacts and practices, and causal work is done by the actual subject through the contentful episode rather than by a non-existent target or a causally competing abstract content.

**Method direction added during the session:** the author explicitly requires accepted positions to remain actively revisable. If a later alternative offers materially better reasons, explanation, economy, coherence, or integration, the assistant should surface and compare it for authorial evaluation rather than preserve the current view by inertia or replace it silently. In this session, dependent abstract artifacts remain such a live revision route for fiction even though the dossier-role minimum is presently preferred.

## Starting point and exact residue

S38 already established:

\[
Img(A,i,p,t)\rightarrow Actual(i,t)\land Rep(i,p),
\]

without entailing that the represented target exists or is possible. S57 and S60 then settled the modal component: admissibility belongs to `Adm_C`, not to imaginability. Three questions remained compressed in `Rep(i,p)`:

1. What in the actual episode makes it contentful rather than a merely physical occurrence?
2. How can it be directed at something when nothing answers to it?
3. How can what is thought make a causal difference without turning a content or a fictional object into a second efficient cause?

The answer should fit the accepted embodied hylomorphic-powers model and constitutive-admissibility actualism. It should neither reduce content to a neural description nor populate reality with one entity for every thinkable combination of words.

## Step 1: the five-place partition

A cognitive episode is represented by:

\[
Cog(A,i,c,t)\rightarrow
Actual(A,t)\land Actual(i,t)\land Manifests(i,A,P_{cog})\land HasContent(i,c).
\]

Five roles must be distinguished:

| Role | What it is | Ontological status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| subject $A$ | the bearer and source of the episode | actual embodied individual |
| episode $i$ | this judging, imagining, remembering, planning, or supposing | actual temporal manifestation of $A$ |
| realization vehicle $v_i$ | the bodily and neural organization through which $i$ occurs | actual constituent/aspect of the episode's realization |
| content-profile $c_i$ | how the episode presents, organizes, and assesses a target | actually instantiated formal/relational feature of $i$ |
| purported target or referent | what the episode presents itself as being about | an actual entity only when reference succeeds |

The list does not add five substances. The subject and episode are concrete; vehicle and content-profile are analytically distinguishable aspects of one realized episode. The content-profile can be displayed as:

\[
c_i=\langle m,f,\rho,\alpha,\kappa\rangle,
\]

where $m$ is the intentional mode (believing, imagining, fearing, hoping, and so on), $f$ is the representational format (propositional, imagistic, narrative, practical), $\rho$ is a target-role, $\alpha$ is an anchoring profile, and $\kappa$ gives the relevant satisfaction, accuracy, or fiction-internal conditions. This tuple is an analytic normal form, not a claim that five separable mental parts exist.

The content-token is therefore real in the same modest sense that an organism's organization or an action's source-role is real: it is an instantiated aspect that helps make this event the kind of event it is. A purely neural inventory may be part of its realization description, but it omits the truth, accuracy, inferential, and aboutness relations by which the episode is a thought with this content.

## Step 2: content tokens and repeatable content

Different subjects and different vehicles can entertain the same proposition, image-schema, plan, or story. This does not require a causally active Platonic object. Introduce an equivalence relation:

\[
c_i\equiv_C c_j
\]

when the two token profiles match in the content-relevant mode, structure, target-role, anchoring constraints, and satisfaction profile at the selected grain. A **content type** is the repeatable structural pattern tracked by this equivalence. At F2 the type can remain an abstraction from token profiles or a formal equivalence class. Nothing here decides whether abstract types exist independently, depend on their instances, or are merely indispensable representational devices.

This modesty matters in two directions. Reducing content identity to vehicle identity makes the same thought impossible across different brains or media. Treating every content type as a separate concrete or causally active being confuses repeatability with agency. The actual token profile is enough for the ontology of this episode; the type does classificatory and semantic work.

External anchoring prevents the model from collapsing into descriptivism. Two internally indistinguishable episodes may concern different actual individuals because their causal, demonstrative, acquaintance, memory, or linguistic-historical routes differ. Conversely, two very different vehicles can reach the same referent. The anchor field $\alpha$ is therefore part of the wide content profile wherever de re thought is at issue.

## Step 3: target-role is not referent

The pivotal distinction is:

\[
PurportsRef(i,\rho)
\not\Rightarrow
\exists x\,Ref(i,x).
\]

$\rho$ is a position in the content: the individual, kind, event, or situation the episode purports to concern, characterized as far as the episode characterizes it. A role is not a shadow object. Successful actual reference requires an independent route:

\[
Ref(i,x)\leftrightarrow
Actual(x)\land RefRoute(i,x,\alpha_i)\land RoleFit(x,\rho_i).
\]

`RefRoute` is deliberately plural. It can be demonstrative or perceptual acquaintance, a causal or memory chain, participation in a public naming practice, or a sufficiently determining descriptive route. The definition does not assume that description alone fixes every referent.

This yields four important cases:

| Case | Target-role | Actual referent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| thought of this actual person | a role plus an appropriate de re anchor | the anchored person, if the route and fit succeed |
| hallucination or mistaken identification | an instantiated role and apparent anchor | none, or a different referent from the one purported |
| merely possible individual/type | a role satisfiable in at least one admissible specification | no non-actual concrete individual is added |
| impossible object | a well-formed enough role with no admissible satisfier | no object, although the episode and its structured failure are actual |

“Intentional object” can therefore be used harmlessly for the target-role of an act. It must not be read as existential quantification over an object. When an actual referent exists, it is not part of the mental episode; it is the relatum reached through the episode's reference route.

## Step 4: possible, impossible, and incomplete targets

The modal status of a role is inherited from S38/S57 rather than inferred from its being thought:

\[
PossSat(\rho)\leftrightarrow
\exists S\exists x\,[Adm_C(S)\land In_S(x)\land RoleFit_S(x,\rho)].
\]

For a partial target-role:

\[
PossSat^{partial}(\rho)\leftrightarrow
\exists \rho'\,[Extends(\rho',\rho)\land PossSat(\rho')].
\]

An impossible role is one for which no admissible completion has a satisfier. A merely incomplete role may remain unclassified because the subject has not fixed enough content to select among possible and impossible completions. In every case the token act and the fact that it carries that role are actual.

This also blocks the move from a merely possible role to a merely possible *individual*. A profile may be satisfiable by many numerically different individuals across admissible specifications. Without an actual de re anchor, the thought fixes a way an individual could be, not a hidden individual waiting to be actualized. Modal predication about an actual person is different: “Socrates could have escaped” remains de re because the actual Socrates supplies the anchor, although the escaping history is non-actual.

## Step 5: fiction adds an actual public artifact, not automatically a fictional being

Solitary supposition and public fiction should not receive the same ontology by default. A public fiction normally has actual authors, inscriptions or performances, transmission histories, interpretive practices, and a relatively stable dossier. Let:

\[
FicArt(F)\land Introduces(F,\rho_F)
\]

say that an actual representational artifact $F$ introduces and stabilizes a fictional target-role $\rho_F$. Then:

\[
In_F(P(\rho_F))
\]

states that $P$ holds according to the fiction. This is different from unrestricted actual predication.

The scheme parses familiar claims without a concrete Holmes:

- “Holmes is a detective” is fiction-internal: $In_F(Detective(\rho_H))$.
- “Conan Doyle introduced Holmes” concerns an actual author, artifact, and dossier-role.
- “Holmes does not exist” says that no actual concrete detective realizes $\rho_H$.

The artifact itself may be treated as a historically dependent abstract artifact, as in Thomasson's theory, or more minimally as a structured family of concrete texts, memories, performances, and practices. CH14 does not need to decide that general ontology of cultural artifacts. Its preferred minimum is **dossier-role realism**: the public representational organization is real and historically grounded; the represented detective is not thereby an additional concrete or non-existent entity.

This minimum may be reopened if literal cross-fictional and metafictional discourse cannot be paraphrased without quantifying over fictional characters. That semantic convenience would support dependent abstract artifacts, not Meinongian concrete-like possibilia and not one object for every private thought.

## Step 6: truth, accuracy, and mode

Not every content is a proposition and not every intentional episode asserts. A belief or judgment may be truth-apt; an image may be accurate or inaccurate; a question can be answerable without being true; a fantasy may be assessed for internal coherence; a plan can be executable or not. Therefore:

\[
HasContent(i,c)\not\Rightarrow TruthApt(c).
\]

Truth and accuracy are typed by $m$, $f$, and $\kappa$. For assertive content, actual truth requires satisfaction in the actual history. For fiction, the relevant operator is $In_F$. For empty singular thought, the ontology fixes that no actual referent exists but does not choose here among falsehood, truth-value gap, or a free-logical semantic treatment. That is a philosophy-of-language extension rather than a load-bearing ontological choice.

This explains why reference failure is not content failure. A subject can reason coherently from an empty name, fear an absent monster, or discover that the object of a search never existed. The episode's actual content-profile supplies inferential and practical organization even when its referential purport is unsatisfied.

## Step 7: causal efficacy without a ghost cause

An impossible monster can cause no event because there is no monster. Fear of it can, because the fear is an actual episode of an actual subject. Under the accepted hylomorphic-powers model:

\[
CausalContent(i,e)\rightarrow
Actual(i)\land Manifests(i,A,P_{cog})\land Contributes(A,i,e).
\]

The content-profile is causally relevant when it identifies the content-sensitive manifestation and enters the contrastive explanation of what the subject does. It is not a second efficient cause operating beside the neural realization. The organized subject acts through the embodied episode; neural, psychological, and personal descriptions identify realization, content, and source roles within one token causal process.

Three claims follow:

1. the non-existent target does no causal work;
2. an abstract content type, if admitted, need not push matter or duplicate the episode's cause;
3. changing the content-profile can change the kind of cognitive manifestation and the action it makes intelligible, even when no corresponding external object exists.

This remains conditional on the project's accepted F2 hylomorphic realization. A reductive physicalist may instead identify all causal work with lower-level physical properties, while a substance dualist owes an interaction bridge. CH14 does not independently settle that wider dispute; it shows that the working model has a non-duplicative place for content.

## Step 8: divine knowledge

The finite-episode model must not be transferred univocally to $G$. Human cognitive tokens are temporal and bodily realized. The established divine profile is atemporal and role-structured:

\[
K_G^{total}=\langle ACO_P,MKO,ASA\rangle.
\]

The CH14 distinctions nevertheless constrain what this means:

- ACO includes every actual fact about a creature's episode, content-profile, anchors, and success or failure of reference.
- $ASA$ gives subject-indexed access to the actual episode as the subject's; it does not make $G$ instantiate the same token, image, or quale.
- MKO tracks whether a represented role has an admissible satisfier and why an impossible role fails.
- Knowing a possible target-profile does not place $G$ in de re acquaintance with a merely possible individual.

No claim is made that $G$ uses neural vehicles, entertains successive mental representations, or depends on abstract propositions as objects. Applying mode/content/target distinctions to divine cognition is an analogical constraint on a later theory, not part of the finite realization model.

## Alternatives and comparative costs

| Family | What it gets right | Cost for this project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meinongian or reified non-existent objects | makes intentional direction look relational in a uniform grammar | adds objects for absences and impossibilities when a target-role already explains the episode; complicates inconsistency and identity |
| pure abstract-content realism | handles repeatability, propositions, and shared inference | not every content is propositional; the causal and bearer relation of a token still needs explanation |
| dependent abstract artifacts | explains literal talk about publicly created characters and their historical identity | plausible for public fiction but too strong as a universal account of every private supposition or impossible thought |
| pretense or fictionalist semantics | avoids fictional entities and explains truth-in-fiction through operators and practices | needs supplementation for ordinary non-fictional empty thought, hallucination, planning, and de re reference |
| reductive vehicle identity | locates the episode in the physical causal order | sameness of vehicle does not by itself state aboutness, misrepresentation, inferential role, or externally anchored reference |
| token-real target-role account | preserves actualism, empty intentionality, repeatability, external anchoring, and hylomorphic causation with one framework | leaves the ultimate ontology of content types and cultural artifacts open and must resist becoming disguised descriptivism |

The final family is preferred as the F2 working account. It combines a real token profile with ontological restraint about targets. It does not claim to have reduced original intentionality to non-intentional vocabulary.

## Adversarial tests

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Aboutness is a relation, so an object must exist at the other end | Rejected. Referential purport is an actual structured feature of the episode; actual dyadic reference obtains only when an independent route succeeds. |
| The target-role is just a renamed non-existent object | Rejected. A role is a position and constraint in an actual content-profile; it has no independent identity, career, causal powers, or concrete existence. |
| Descriptively identical thoughts must have the same referent | Rejected. $\alpha$ records demonstrative, causal, acquaintance, memory, or public-language anchoring, so wide content can differ without internal vehicle difference. |
| A merely possible role singles out one possible individual | Rejected absent an actual de re anchor. `Adm_C` may admit many satisfiers; a profile is not a bearer. |
| Impossible content cannot be genuinely thought | Rejected. The actual profile may combine constraints that no admissible completion satisfies; impossibility belongs to satisfaction, not to the existence of the token. |
| If contents are not independent entities, two people cannot think the same thing | Rejected. $\equiv_C$ supplies repeatability at an explicitly selected grain without requiring a second concrete bearer. |
| If a fictional character is not an object, no statement about it can be true | Rejected at the minimum level. Fiction-internal claims are evaluated under $In_F$ and historical claims concern the actual artifact and introduced role. Literal quantification over dependent abstract characters remains an admissible extension if paraphrase fails. |
| Mental and neural causes overdetermine behavior | Rejected within the selected hylomorphic model. They are content/source and realization descriptions of one subject's token exercise, not two independent sufficient causal chains. |
| The account naturalizes or explains intentionality completely | Rejected. It types the ontology and its dependencies; original aboutness remains a fundamental power of the conscious embodied subject at F2. |
| God's knowledge of an impossible object requires that object | Rejected. ACO knows the actual episode and MKO knows the unsatisfiability of its role; neither relation has an impossible entity as relatum. |

## Literature and novelty notes

**Author review (2026-09-03):** the author advanced to the next priority without correction and then authorized S77. Under the default-acceptance protocol, C624–C633 are accepted at their stated levels. The explicitly optional dependent-abstract-artifact extension and the deeper residual questions remain open.

- [Tim Crane, “Intentionalism” (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199262618.003.0029) distinguishes intentional mode, content, and object and argues that an intentional state's nature need not contain its object. This is the closest precedent for the session's initial partition; S76 adds the actualist target-role and anchoring machinery required by this project.
- [R. M. Sainsbury, “Intentionality without Exotica” (2009)](https://www.marksainsbury.net/uploads/9/7/2/9/97295048/rmsintentionality.pdf) defends intelligible, truth-evaluable content containing singular expressions without referents. The session uses the ontological possibility of referential purport without a relatum, while leaving Sainsbury's particular free-logic verdicts open.
- [Amie L. Thomasson, “Fiction, Modality and Dependent Abstracta” (1996)](https://www.amiethomasson.org/_files/ugd/63fc8a_b7987a44d0ab4ad188da8ae99f2257b4.pdf) treats fictional characters as created, multiply dependent abstract artifacts. S76 retains this as a live stronger account of public fiction but prefers actual artifacts plus dossier-roles as its minimum commitment.
- [William Jaworski, “Hylomorphism, Explanatory Practice, and the Problem of Mental Causation” (2020)](https://doi.org/10.2307/48570647) argues that hylomorphic structure permits mental and physical causal explanation to be different without competing. This is direct precedent for the causal non-competition used in C631, which remains conditional on the already selected S37 realization.

Mode/content/object partitions, empty reference, artifact theories of fiction, pretense, and hylomorphic mental causation are established families. The project-specific synthesis is their integration with actual-marker semantics, constitutive-admissibility actualism, embodied sourcehood, and ACO/MKO/ASA. No standalone novelty claim is made.
