# Session: Nothing, the meta-modal ground, and temporal identity

**Date:** 2026-09-04  
**Session ID:** S78  
**Primary question:** What ultimately grounds wholly uninstantiated constitutive profiles without circularly defining possibility through divine power, and what light do absolute nothingness and the onset of personal identity cast on that problem?  
**Secondary question:** Does material temporality make death, evil, or suffering necessary?  
**Dependencies:** C04–C05, C14, C21–C30, C52–C54, C183–C209, C223–C241, C264, C343–C355, C435–C456, C506–C520, C528–C542, C634–C644, CH18–CH22, S28, S38, S45, S53, S55, S57, S60, S77  
**Target depth:** F2+ bounded closure of the meta-modal burden; F1–F2 integration of the three new questions; no full theodicy or theory of time

## Result in one sentence

The preferred working position is **bounded constitutive primitivism**: type profiles and possible-world models are representations rather than non-actual concrete beings, while at least the alien-profile residue in their objective constitutive admissibility is presently treated as irreducible necessary structure; divine knowledge tracks it, divine choice selects within it, and divine power realizes eligible structures without any of those roles creating modal truth. Absolute nothing is correspondingly an absence expressed by a quantifier rather than a source, and a created person begins with its actual token career even though its identity facts and complete career are tenselessly true. Material temporal life requires change, but the stronger route from change to death, evil, or each token suffering fails without additional premises.

## 1. Four claims hidden in talk of “nothing”

The comic comparison between God and nothing contains one useful metaphysical point and several claims that must be separated.

### 1.1 “Nothing” is normally a quantifier, not a name

“Nothing exists in the room” has the logical form:

\[
\neg\exists x\,InRoom(x).
\]

It does not introduce an object named *Nothing*. Absolute nothingness, to the extent that it is intelligible, is the absence of every concrete being and every candidate source. A causal relation requires relata:

\[
Causes(y,x)\rightarrow Exists(y)\land Exists(x).
\]

It follows that absolute nonbeing cannot occupy the source position. “Nothing caused the world” is therefore malformed if *nothing* is meant absolutely. “The world has no cause” is well formed, but it is a different claim: it withholds a source instead of naming one.

This corrects the satire without losing its central pressure. Atheism is not belief in an entity called Nothing. A non-theist may instead accept a brute cosmos, a necessary impersonal structure, another ultimate ground, or suspension of judgment. Those alternatives must be compared with the agentic ground; they cannot be defeated by grammar alone.

### 1.2 Creation *ex nihilo* gives nothing no causal role

Within this project:

\[
CreatesExNihilo(G,x)ightarrow
Creates(G,x)\land
\neg\exists m\,MaterialInput(m,x).
\]

The phrase denies prior material; it does not say:

\[
Causes(Nothing,x).
\]

Our $OEC^+$ account is consequently creation **without a material patient by a positive agentive source**. It does not describe a transition in which a state or substance called nothing turns into matter. This is exactly why the question “how can something come from nothing?” has two readings: it is decisive against treating nonbeing as a producer, but it does not by itself prove that every actuality must have a cause. The project still uses C457's weak comparative demand rather than a strong principle of sufficient reason.

### 1.3 Non-observability does not create epistemic parity

Absolute nothing cannot be empirically observed: an observation already entails an observer, an episode, and some reality. $G$, if non-physical and not spatially located, is likewise not a physical object available to ordinary detection. That shared negative fact does not assign equal evidence or equal probability. Empirical invisibility is a limit on one method, not a positive argument for either ontology. The comparison must use explanatory reach, coherence, economy, experience, and the independent arguments for the relevant entities or absences.

### 1.4 Death is not a destination called nothing

If a subject finally ceases to exist, there is no post-mortem state of that subject called “being nothing,” and no location to which it returns. “Returning to one's creator, Nothing” works as satire, not as ontology. Under the project's preferred gappy-continuation model, a period during which the subject does not exist is likewise not a state experienced by the subject. The earlier and later intervals can belong to one career if the identity constraints of S45 hold.

### 1.5 Empty-world theses

The modal claim usually called metaphysical nihilism in the contemporary literature is at least:

\[
Nih_C:=\Diamond\neg\exists x\,Concrete(x).
\]

It permits abstracta while denying every concrete object in some world. The stronger absolute thesis is:

\[
Nih_A:=\Diamond\neg\exists x\,Entity(x).
\]

Both are claims about an empty domain, not claims that Nothing is an entity. Both are incompatible with this project's necessary concrete $G$:

\[
\Box Exists(G)\land Concrete(G)
\rightarrow\neg Nih_C\land\neg Nih_A.
\]

That conditional does not independently prove necessary theism. A rival may reject its antecedent, and $Nih_A$ carries additional semantic burdens about the status of worlds, truths, or logic. What the project can say positively is that its selected ontology has no concretely or absolutely empty admissible history: histories without creation still contain $G$.

## 2. Why the “nothing” distinction helps the meta-modal problem

S38 said that a wholly uninstantiated kind can have a type-level profile without a token of that kind. The remaining danger is to slide from this useful nominalization to a realm of profile-objects. The present distinction blocks that slide:

- saying that no token instantiates kind $K$ does not introduce a bearer called “the uninstantiated $K$”;
- saying that a $K$-profile is constitutively coherent records a necessary structural truth or real definition;
- a formal profile in our documents is a representation of that truth, not automatically its truthmaker;
- if the kind is actualized, a concrete bearer and its manifested powers are new actuality; they were not hiding in modal space.

The ontology must therefore answer four different questions:

| Level | Question | Present answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| representation | What do our formulae and models denote? | structured descriptions and model roles |
| truth condition | When is a specification possible? | the six clauses of $Adm_C$ |
| ontological commitment | Must every profile be an entity? | no; nominalizing a structural truth adds no bearer by itself |
| ultimate explanation | Why are these constitutive structures true rather than others? | partially grounded locally; alien-profile residue presently primitive |

## 3. Candidate grounds of uninstantiated profiles

### 3.1 Robust Platonic structures

On a robust Platonic reading, kinds, essences, propositions, or maximal structures exist necessarily and independently. It gives uninstantiated profiles determinate standing. It also adds an abstract ontology stronger than the project needs, and it leaves the familiar ultimacy question: why this necessary structure? Abstract constraints would not be concrete causal competitors to $G$, but the explanatory stopping point would plainly be plural.

### 3.2 Divine intellectualism or voluntarist conceptualism

A divine-concept account gives every profile an actual locus in $G$'s intellect. Its strongest versions make modal truth depend on divine intellectual activity or choice. That is not adopted here. It conflicts with the established direction:

\[
Adm_C(S)\rightarrow Knows_G(Adm_C(S)),
\]

and threatens to replace it with:

\[
Knows_G(Adm_C(S))\rightarrow Adm_C(S).
\]

The latter confuses perfect tracking with truthmaking, while a voluntarist version also lets choice help constitute the domain from which choice is supposed to select. A non-voluntarist divine-essentialism is less problematic and remains live below.

### 3.3 Reduction to actual powers

Actual token powers ground many nearby modal truths. An existing seed's power helps ground what it can grow into; an actual agent's originative power grounds its accessible alternatives. They do not exhaust the pre-creative or alien domain. Reducing every uninstantiated profile to $P_{OEC}$ would reintroduce the B1 circularity:

\[
Poss_C^{ind}(S)\leftrightarrow CanCreate(G,S),
\]

followed by an allegedly informative proof that $G$ can create every possible $S$.

### 3.4 Bounded constitutive primitivism

The preferred F2+ stopping point is:

\[
BCP:\quad
Adm_C(S)
\text{ is an objective necessary constitutive fact,}
\]

with the following distribution of ground:

1. logical, identity, and mathematical constraints are necessary structural constraints;
2. actual essences and powers ground the local manifestations of actual kinds and subjects;
3. global compossibility concerns whether the locally specified roles can be jointly satisfied;
4. created-dependence conditions locate any concrete realization downstream of an existential source;
5. the constitutive coherence of a wholly alien, never-instantiated profile is an irreducible structural fact unless a later account supplies a stronger ground.

“Primitive” here means a stopping point in explanation, not an object, substance, cause, mind, or second god. This view is actualist in its concrete ontology and realist about modal truth. It does not require truthmaker maximalism—the thesis that every truth must have a distinct entity that makes it true.

This stopping point is acceptable for the same methodological reason that the project currently permits primitive numerical identity, primitive agent causation, and fundamental powers: an incomplete but explicit primitive is preferable to a circular reduction. It is also bounded. Most modal claims remain explained through real definitions, powers, identity, and compossibility; only the deepest alien-profile remainder is left irreducible.

### 3.5 The stronger divine-essential route remains open

A stronger integration would say that $G$'s necessary, non-volitional nature grounds the full space of coherent created forms. This would preserve independence from divine will and actual choice. It is attractive because it would make $G$ ultimate with respect to modal structure as well as concreta.

It is not yet earned. $UPSS$, minimal constitutive simplicity, rational agency, and $P_{OEC}$ do not by themselves entail an open-ended formal content containing every alien profile. Defining that content as “whatever $G$ can create” repeats the circularity; adding it as an independent divine feature adds a substantial attribute still needing justification. The option remains the preferred direction for an eventual F3 comparison, while $BCP$ is the current working closure.

## 4. Identity: origin, time, and eternity

The question “where does identity come from?” contains four different uses of *from*:

| Sense | Question | Answer for a created person |
| --- | --- | --- |
| logical/constitutive | What makes identity identity? | numerical identity is the primitive relation $x=x$; it is not produced |
| causal | What makes this bearer actual? | $G$ is the existential ground; created parents and processes may be proximate causes |
| temporal | When does this individual first exist? | at the first stage of its actual token career |
| epistemic | How is it recognized? | finite knowers use continuity and profile; $G$ tracks it de re through $ASA$ |

Before a person's first actual stage there may be an admissible **person-type** or partial profile. There is not yet that merely possible person as a de re individual. Actualization does not take a waiting person from modal storage. It originates a concrete token career and with it determinate de re individuation. S45's claim that individuation is prior to the acts a subject originates concerns grounding order: the acts presuppose their bearer. It does not make the bearer temporally prior to its own first stage. This fixes the metaphysical relation without yet fixing a biological date: the exact first stage depends on when one unified organismic bearer satisfying the relevant individuation profile exists, and the present ontology has not derived that threshold.

Eternalism does not make the person everlasting. Suppose:

\[
Career(B)=\{B@t:t\in J_B\}.
\]

The complete career and the facts linking its stages are tenselessly actual in $H^*$. Yet:

\[
\exists t<t_0\,\neg ExistsAt(B,t)
\]

can still be true. $B$ exists at the times occupied by its career, not at every created time. Divine atemporal knowledge of the whole career changes neither its temporal extent nor its mode of existence.

This yields a precise answer:

- the **truth** that the stages are one individual is tenseless;
- the **bearer** begins with its first stage and may have a finite or gappy career;
- the bearer's identity is not a further thing that exists before, during, or after it;
- if $B$ is re-realized after a gap, the later stage is $B$ only if the one-career identity fact and S45's origin, essence, and non-branching constraints hold;
- a qualitatively exact successor remains a distinct individual.

The uninstantiated-profile question therefore cannot be solved by positing pre-existing individual identities. Modal space contains kind and role constraints; actuality supplies token individuation.

## 5. Change, time, death, and evil

The proposed route is powerful only after four theses are separated.

### 5.1 Temporal material life requires change

For life of the embodied kind already described—metabolism, perception, action, learning, development, and interaction—change is constitutive:

\[
TemporalEmbodiedLife(B)\rightarrow Changes(B).
\]

This is safe as a thesis about the selected created life-profile. It is not yet a theorem that every imaginable life, material order, or temporal reality must change.

### 5.2 Time requires change

Aristotle's relationist route treats time as the measure or number of change in respect of before and after. It fits much of the project, since created time is internal to $S$ and its history rather than a container in which $G$ waits. The entailment remains disputed: Shoemaker's global-freeze construction is the classic attempt to establish intervals of time without change, and later relationists dispute what the construction presupposes.

The project therefore may adopt:

\[
Time_{life}(B)\rightarrow Change(B)
\]

for temporal embodied careers, while leaving this framework-neutral universal open:

\[
Time(S)\rightarrow\exists x\,Change(x,S).
\]

### 5.3 Change entails death or evil

This does not follow. Some changes are gains, rearrangements, growth, discovery, or action without the loss of a due good. Even privation in the broad sense of no longer having a prior state is not automatically evaluative privation. S28's evil requires misfit relative to a due profile, bearer, context, and horizon.

A more defensible structural thesis is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&TemporalEmbodiment(S)\land StableSecondaryCausation(S)\\
&\land PluralVulnerableAgents(S)
\Rightarrow Exposure_S(Loss,Conflict,Harm).
\end{aligned}
\]

Embodied agents need bounded organization; interaction and stable common laws expose that organization to disruption; plural projects can conflict; autonomous agency can be misused. This supports **structural vulnerability**, not the necessity of every death, disease, cruelty, pain intensity, or distribution.

### 5.4 Structural exposure justifies each token suffering

This also does not follow. The correct modal levels are:

| Level | Claim | Present status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| absolute necessity | every material or temporal world contains evil and death | too strong; unsupported |
| profile-relative constitutive cost | realizing some selected goods under a specified order entails vulnerability or loss-risk | plausible and to be tested |
| nomological inevitability | actual laws make mortality or some harms unavoidable | partly empirical; needs a bridge |
| token inevitability | this particular suffering had to occur | generally unsupported |
| token permission | $G$ had adequate reason not to prevent this case | remains the P-gap of CH19 |

Structural vulnerability can improve the policy-level explanation in S49. It cannot dissolve the problem of evil, because $G$ may retain the capacity to prevent particular harms, choose different laws, or choose another admissible structure. The author's questions—why this pain, this intensity, this distribution—remain legitimate.

### 5.5 Perfection and “singularity”

No-change, perfection, physical singularity, and divinity must remain distinct. A physical singularity is a feature or breakdown of a physical model, not the metaphysical absence of differentiation or time. Changelessness by itself supplies no evaluative perfection. $G$ is atemporal relative to created time, but the architecture also attributes contingent selection, knowledge of actual created facts, and possible relational exercises to $G$. A changeless state resembles this model of $G$ in one respect only and cannot serve as its definition.

## 6. Adversarial tests

| Objection | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Atheists literally believe in Nothing | False as a general analysis. Brute reality, impersonal necessity, and suspension are genuine rivals; reifying a quantifier attacks a caricature. |
| If nothing cannot cause, God follows | Invalid. The result excludes nonbeing as a cause; it does not exclude a brute existent or an impersonal ground. The agentic preference still needs S47. |
| A primitive modal truth is a second necessary being beside $G$ | Only with a further ontology or truthmaker-maximalist premise. $BCP$ treats it as necessary structure, not a concrete identity unit or cause. |
| Necessary modal structure limits omnipotence | It determines whether a description is an eligible task. A contradiction or malformed essence is no task and supplies no opposing power. |
| Grounding modality in $G$ would be more economical | Potentially, but only if the grounding feature is independently characterized. Otherwise one hidden primitive is replaced by a circular or unexplained divine attribute. |
| If a person's complete career is eternally actual, the person existed before birth | Equivocation between tenseless actuality and existence at every time. The whole bounded career is actual; $ExistsAt(B,t)$ still varies with $t$. |
| If identity is not produced, creation cannot create an individual | Creation produces or grounds the individual. Numerical identity is the logical fact that this created bearer is itself, not an additional product. |
| Without death, there can be no change | Unsupported. Change need not be lethal or privative of a due good. Mortality needs biological, nomological, or profile-relative premises. |
| Once some vulnerability is necessary, actual suffering is justified | Invalid move from type-level exposure to token permission. CH19 remains open. |

## Literature and novelty notes

- [Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 45](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1045.htm) explicitly treats “from nothing” as the denial of presupposed matter rather than making nothing a cause, and denies that creation is an ordinary change. This closely precedes C647 without supplying the project's $OEC^+$ architecture.
- [Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* I, q. 48](https://www.newadvent.org/summa/1048.htm) distinguishes privation from pure negation and analyzes evil as absence of a due good in a subject. His wider argument from corruptibility to the perfection of the universe uses stronger teleological premises not adopted here.
- [Aristotle, *Physics* IV](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/physics.4.iv.html) defines time through the number of motion or change in respect of before and after. [Sydney Shoemaker, “Time Without Change” (1969)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2023892) gives the classic global-freeze countermodel, while [Denis Corish, “Could Time be Change?” (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031819109000199) argues that the countermodel presupposes an absolute-time framework a relationist may reject. These sources justify leaving the universal open.
- [Thomas Baldwin, “There Might Be Nothing” (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1093/analys/56.4.231) treats the possibility of a world without concrete objects as a serious modal thesis. It confirms that metaphysical nihilism is not the reification of an object named Nothing; the stronger absence of all entities requires a further distinction, and the project's necessary-$G$ answer is conditional on its own prior commitments.
- [Brian Leftow, *God and Necessity* (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199263356.001.0001) gives divine nature, mental activity, imagination, and choice extensive modal work while resisting a separate realm of possible worlds and abstract objects. It is the strongest nearby divine-ground competitor, but its voluntarist/intellectualist machinery is substantially thicker than C652 and conflicts with the project's present truth-tracking direction if imported wholesale.
- Finean essence-first modality, Vetter's comparison of essence and potentiality, Pruss's actual-power route, and Williamson's necessitism were already assessed in S38 and S57. $BCP$ is a conservative clarification of that existing hybrid, not a new general theory of modality.

The distinction between quantificational nothingness and a named object, privation theories, relationist time, modal primitivism, divine conceptualism, and simple or non-criterial personal identity all have established precedents. The potentially useful local contribution is the joint diagnostic: do not reify absence, uninstantiated profiles, or identity; distribute explanatory ground across the six $Adm_C$ clauses; and keep profile-relative vulnerability separate from token theodicy. No standalone novelty claim is made.
