## Definitions

| Term/symbol | Definition used in this session |
| --- | --- |
| $D(G,x)$ | $x$ existentially depends on $G$, directly or through the established grounding chain. No constitutive overlap is built into this predicate. |
| $B(G,x)$ | Proposed primitive: the numerically same undivided $G$ is a real constitutive basis of the distinct bearer $x$, not merely its producer or existential ground. This is a typed constitution relation, not ordinary parthood or membership in a physical inventory. |
| Essential constitutive tie | For an actual $ANDC$ bearer, $\Box(E(x)\rightarrow B(G,x))$. This is a proposed de re constraint additional to actual overlap; it is not derived from actual overlap alone. |
| Constitutive subtraction | A comparison of rival descriptions preserving dependency, finite profile, and source structure while omitting $B$. It is not an established possible intervention or a possible world without necessary $G$. |

Neither $B$ nor its notation supplies a reduction or proof of metaphysical possibility. “Whole” means that the relatum is $G$ itself rather than a detachable fraction; it does not mean that $G$ exhaustively explains every finite determination without the creaturely profile.

## Arguments and alternatives

### 1. Non-replaceability is not a discriminator

Premise 1: both principal $ANDC$ and the disjoint fallback posit existential dependence on the same ultimate $G$.

Premise 2: a disjoint account may also make that dependence essential to a particular creature; nothing in disjointness entails replaceable ultimate grounding.

Conclusion: even $\Box(E(x)\rightarrow D(G,x))$ does not establish $B(G,x)$. The inference from indispensable ground to shared constituent is invalid. Testing whether the creature survives the non-existence of necessary $G$ supplies no ordinary possible-world discriminator either.

This is an internal inferential limit, not a rejection of $ANDC$ or a claim that indispensable constitution is impossible.

### 2. A stronger assertion, with an explicit remaining cost

The positive candidate is $B(G,x)$: the concrete reality entering the constitutive account includes the very same $G$, without a transferred piece, copied divine token, or newly acquired finite divine property. Under $CCD$, $D(G,x)\land\neg B(G,x)$ holds. Under $ANDC$, $D(G,x)\land B(G,x)$ holds with $x\neq G$.

For two distinct creatures, $B(G,x)\land B(G,y)$ asserts a numerically common constitutive basis. $CCD$ instead permits their common ultimate ground while denying that this ground is a shared constituent. A shared basis does not give $x=y$, since neither $G$ nor the repeatable profile exhausts token individuation.

The explanatory contrast concerns what constitutes a bearer, not an extra empirically detectable ingredient. But stipulating that contrast does not yet establish its explanatory success: $B$ is still the relation requiring interpretation. This pass gives an explicit disagreement and inferential constraints, not a completed account of constitutive participation.

### 3. What the identity counterfactual would add

If the essential constitutive tie is adopted, an $ANDC$ bearer cannot persist as that very bearer while becoming wholly constitutively disjoint from $G$, even if $G$ continues to sustain a qualitatively matching result. If such a disjoint result is independently possible, it is another bearer or the proposed essential tie is false.

Two limits matter. Actual overlap alone does not entail essential overlap. Also, the project has not established that an otherwise matching disjoint result or a transition to it is possible. The conditional therefore specifies a commitment of the stronger hypothesis, not an independently verified contrastive witness. A disjoint rival with essential divine grounding can retain its own bearer throughout continued dependence; that observation still does not prove the proposed constitutive essentialism.

### 4. Local model and its exact evidential ceiling

Take a three-object domain $\{g,x,y\}$ with distinct objects. Interpret $D$ as $\{(g,x),(g,y)\}$ and $B$ as the same ordered pairs; leave $B(x,g)$ and $B(y,g)$ false. Assign divine predicates to $g$ and finite profiles to $x,y$, with their own power and act predicates. This satisfies the restricted relational conditions of shared basis, asymmetry, distinctness, and non-inheritance. A second structure keeps every predicate except $B$ fixed and interprets $B$ as empty. It satisfies the corresponding disjoint description.

Thus the restricted grounding description does not logically entail the extra constitution predicate. Conversely, the ease of adding or deleting $B$ exposes the explanatory burden: the structures by themselves provide no reason to regard it as more than an uninterpreted addition. They are not complete models of the ontology, witnesses to independent $Adm_C$, or proofs of either metaphysics. No creative capacity is inferred from them.

The same limit applies to freedom. One can represent two admissible careers with the same finite profile and different source-indexed settlements. This blocks a formal entailment from profile to unique career in the restricted description; it does not derive genuine sourcehood merely by assigning a predicate.

## Adversarial tests

| Test | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Does a common $G$ make all creatures one individual? | No such inference follows. Token identity, unity, $\Omega_x$, and the non-branching career remain distinct requirements; the common basis alone is insufficient. |
| Does $B(G,x)$ transfer $x$'s finitude, pain, or acts to $G$? | Only with an added inheritance rule. The proposal denies generic inheritance, but still owes a positive account of how finite powers are realized; typed predicates alone do not settle that burden. |
| Does a changing creature change $G$ intrinsically? | Not by logical entailment from a relational tie. The candidate locates finite changes in $x$ and keeps $PP_G$ fixed; this is a coherence condition, not a derived mechanism. |
| Does whole-career constitution script the creature's choices? | It must not: $\Phi_x^C$ constrains admissible careers while actual settlements retain their source. Knowledge or tenseless actuality of $H_x^*$ is not sufficient contrastive divine production of its acts. |
| Does a common eternal basis determine onset or secure recreation? | No. No first member of the life-set is assumed; a common $G$ cannot distinguish returning $x$ from a duplicate without the existing identity and gap conditions. |
| Does constitutive continuity solve physical or phenomenal production? | No. A non-material shared basis leaves the finite-realization bridge to be explained, as S71 already recognized. |
