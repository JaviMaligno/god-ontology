# Session: The criterion sharpened: norms relative to a reader and norms relative to living, and five tests

**Date:** 2026-09-05
**Session ID:** S97
**Primary question:** The author objects to S96 on two points: “life is a text” is an unmotivated premise, since not everything meeting the stated conditions is a text, and the mark “one form among many” is weak, since erosion also yields one form among many and whether an outcome is one among many is the contingency of the fact, beyond our knowledge. He accepts the whole-versus-parts line and the “works versus says” distinction, and asks for the criterion sharpened and then tested. What is the criterion, exactly, and does it survive the tests?

## The criterion, restated

Within the created order, for an item $x$:

1. If $x$ is right or wrong relative to a practice of subjects (a norm relative to a reader), then $x$ has derived content and a mind is upstream. This is necessary: derived content requires original content, and original content is borne only by acts of subjects (C626–C631, C735).
2. If $x$ is right or wrong only relative to the self-maintenance of a system (a norm relative to living), the structure with its history suffices in principle; a mind is not required, and not excluded. Function is what a generative structure yields (C107, C380).
3. If $x$ is a response to a reason, something that counts in favor of it (C63), an agent is required, since processes respond to causes and not to reasons.
4. If $x$ is neither, the structure explains.

For the order as a whole, none of these applies, because there is no further structure and no practice; the comparison is between stopping points, and the agentic one is preferred because the order is contingent and an impersonal necessary ground must either necessitate it or leave it to chance, law, or plenitude (C458–C460).

Why Rosetta and not DNA, said plainly: the stone's marks need a reader to have their norm, and a reader is a subject; the sequence needs only to live, and living is what the created structure produces. That is the whole difference, and it does not require calling life a text or denying that life is organized.

## Five tests

1. **The genetic code's arbitrariness.** Codon assignments could have been otherwise, and the ID literature treats this as convention-like. Arbitrariness relative to chemistry does not make a norm relative to a reader; it makes the assignment contingent history, fixed by tRNA chemistry and selection, not by agreement among subjects. The fidelity of translation matters relative to living, not relative to a reader. Verdict: norm relative to living; passes.
2. **Animal signals** (alarm calls, the bee dance). If the signalers are subjects (C61 applies to animals as far as they meet it), the signals have derived content from their acts and a norm relative to a reader among them; if not, the signals are functional. The criterion gives the right dependence: content follows subjects, not complexity. Passes.
3. **A hammer.** Functional norm produced by a mind. Shows the asymmetry: a norm relative to living or use does not require a mind but is compatible with one; the criterion never says “functional, therefore no mind,” only “functional, therefore no mind is required.” Passes.
4. **The origin of life.** The first self-maintaining system is where the norm relative to living first appears, and the question is whether the generative structure yields it. That is empirical, and the project abstains without a bridge (C321). What the criterion adds is that the question is correctly posed as “does the structure suffice?” and not “is a mind needed?”: even if a first system required direct divine action, that would be a particular intervention within sparse direct action (C380), not evidence of a norm relative to a reader. The project's default, that the structure suffices, is a default under $GSDA$, not a result. Passes, with the open item recorded.
5. **Fine-tuning of constants.** Not within the order; the constants belong to the structure. The criterion sends it to the stopping-point comparison, and the project declines the probabilistic version (S58) in favor of contingency plus the necessitation dilemma. Passes, and shows why the “one among many” mark was wrong: an improbability estimate over structures is exactly what the project does not need.
