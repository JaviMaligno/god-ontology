## The ink analogy, made exact

Three things are run together when one says the brain is the machinery of reasoning, and the analogy separates them. With a written sum: that the sum is true does not depend on the ink; that it is written does; and that the writer did it correctly is a relation between what was written and what is true, not a property of the ink. With the brain: that what I think is valid does not depend on my neurons, because validity is a relation among satisfaction conditions (C624–C633) grounded in necessary structure (S78, S88); that I think it does depend on them, because a thought is a manifestation of a power of the whole subject through its realization base (C334); and that I reason well is that my act tracks the valid relation, a constitutive fact about the act's organization (C335), not a physical property, although it occurs only in something physical. “Being the machinery” therefore means being the realization base through which the subject exercises the power (C334), which is neither a second thinker inside (C332) nor what makes the sum true. The disanalogy with ink is that ink does nothing, while the brain is the subject's own exercise; the analogy holds for the truth of the content and fails, rightly, for the occurrence of the act.

## The asymmetry about machines

The author is right that the register was too cautious in one direction. That speaking like a person does not imply thinking like one is certain, not a cautionary remark: the criterion for thinking concerns the source (C734), and no output entails a source. What remains undecided is the converse: that the system does not think. The epistemic situation is asymmetric, and P66 now says so.

## The book metaphor

The metaphor is the author's accessible form of the project's first commitment (C01): a sufficiently rich scientific description contains explanation in the ordinary sense and does not thereby explain why fundamental reality has its structure. Laws are regularities within the order; they hold inside the book and cannot be what the book comes from, exactly as conservation laws hold within the order whose origin is in question (C490). It is also the register's form of the rejection of “God of the gaps” (R02): the gap is not inside the book. The metaphor is added to P03 as the author's.

## The author's position on machines (added the same day)

The author leans to the view that no existing machine thinks, while not knowing where the threshold lies at which one could say that a machine does. The ledger records the leaning as a preference (C740) and says where the threshold is, not what it is: not in output, but in whether an organization is a unified bearer with the powers of C61 whose consciousness participates in its acts (C138). For a machine this is the same onset question the project faces for organisms, which needs an empirically constrained eligibility profile and a psychophysical power principle (C333) that the project does not have; phenomenal and intentional powers are fundamental powers of certain organized subjects at F2 (C337), and which organizations bear them is the F3 gap (C717). P66 carries the leaning as the author's, shown in the reply.
