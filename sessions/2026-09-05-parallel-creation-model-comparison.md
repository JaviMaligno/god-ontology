## Definitions and shared baseline

Let $D$ be $OEC^+_{CCD}$: God originates and sustains distinct bearers without being their constituent. Let $E$ be relational $ANDC^{\Phi}$: the same undivided God also constitutes distinct bearers through the still-primitive $B$ relation. These are candidate models of actual creation, not claims that God lacks the capacity to create in the other way.

Both retain the preferred necessary agent, non-material divine profile, contingent creation, real created subjects, finite form, numerical identity, secondary causes, creaturely settlements, E+, ACO/ASA, and the independently qualified power account. $E$ adds a common divine constitutive basis while denying divine depletion, identity collapse, and automatic property or act inheritance. $D$ denies that basis without denying immanence.

Four readings of “something of God in us” must be separated:

1. **Origin and dependence:** our existence is from and sustained by God. Both models affirm this.
2. **Derived resemblance or participation:** finite knowledge, agency, or other powers may express created analogues of divine capacities. This is compatible with both; origin alone does not prove every proposed resemblance or goodness claim.
3. **Immediate personal presence:** God knows and can relate to the subject without spatial distance. Both can use the established ASA/availability account. Actual communion still requires its own conditions and participation.
4. **Numerically divine constitution:** divine reality itself constitutes the creature. This is $E$'s distinctive claim and $D$'s denial.

None of the first three entails the fourth. Strict overlap need not mean a detachable divine fragment, and disjointness need not mean a remote or absent God. No claim of an immortal divine particle is adopted.

## One matched finite-power test

Fix one finite subject $x$, a deliberative episode, reasons for $a$ and $b$, the finite power profile, and the actual settlement of $a$. This is a comparison of explanatory roles, not a claim that every branch-specific modal profile can be realized by the same individual in both models.

**Common premise:** the creature genuinely contributes the contrastive settlement. God's root production, sustaining role, and complete knowledge do not exhaustively replace that contribution. This is the inherited agency commitment, not a new proof of libertarian freedom.

**Under $D$:** God originates a subject with its own finite power; the subject exercises it. The explanatory stopping point includes genuine origination and genuine finite sourcehood. The fact that we already admit agentive powers makes this intelligible within the project, but does not deduce divine cross-category production from ordinary human action.

**Under $E$:** God originates and constitutes the subject; the finite power still belongs to $x$. Three interpretations are tested:

- If $x$'s settlement is numerically a divine settlement, the interpretation fails inherited source separation in that episode.
- If $B$ only guarantees existence while finite form and sourcehood do all the remaining work, no explanatory gain over $D$ has yet been earned. $E$ may still express a substantive constitutive preference; it is not thereby logically false.
- If $B$ grounds the reality of the finite power while the power and exercise remain genuinely creature-owned, $E$ has its strongest candidate bridge. It must positively explain this contribution and why it does not reduce to existential grounding. This is the branch's open constructive task.

This tests both routes against the same episode and reveals the discriminator. It does not close either model's fundamental power or phenomenal realization problem.

## Preferences, costs, and switching conditions

The comparison is abductive and coherence-based, not deductive. Simplicity means fewer unsupported explanatory commitments for the same explanatory work, not fewer words, symbols, or objects by stipulation. $E$ still recognizes distinct creatures, so a common basis does not automatically reduce its entity count. Its attraction is constitutive continuity, not established numerical economy.

Do not automatically count origination and constitution as two independent mechanisms in $E$: its strongest version may describe one creative act under both roles. If that unity earns a single intelligible bridge, the economy comparison must credit it. The present local disadvantage concerns the unexplained constitutive role in the current account, not a theorem that every ex-deo theory must contain one more primitive than every disjoint theory.

| Weight or preference | What it favors and what must be accepted |
| --- | --- |
| Explanatory economy and clearly distinct subjects | Currently favors $D$: accept originative production without shared substrate and fundamental finite sourcehood as stopping points. This is not a mechanism or proof of actual creation. |
| Constitutive continuity as intrinsically more intelligible or satisfying | Favors $E$: accept a primitive asymmetric basis relation plus an account of finite differentiation and source ownership. The author has not yet confirmed that the desired divine presence requires this strongest reading. |
| Personal closeness, meaning, direct knowledge, relation | Does not currently select between them; $D$ can retain these through already-established relations. |
| Consciousness, personal survival, or an answer to evil | Selects neither merely by invocation. Any advantage requires the missing bearer-, phenomenal-, or permission-specific bridge. |

**Current proposal:** $D$ is the baseline for economy; $E$ is the active continuity candidate. Neither is the settled overall choice. Develop them together on the next load-bearing test rather than automatically assigning every new gap to $E$.

Favor $E$ more strongly if its constitution bridge explains finite power, perspective, or individuation with less total unexplained structure than $D$, while preserving the shared constraints. Favor $D$ more strongly if the desired presence is adequately expressed without overlap and $B$ remains explanatorily redundant. A newly established contradiction would defeat the affected variant rather than merely shift weights. The author may prefer constitutive continuity even under residual underdetermination, but that preference must remain distinct from achieved explanation.
