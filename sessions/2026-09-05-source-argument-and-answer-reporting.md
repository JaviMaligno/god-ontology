# Session: Source arguments and accessible-answer reporting

**Date:** 2026-09-05  
**Session ID:** S81  
**Primary question:** What must be shown to the author when literature affects the reasoning or an accessible answer changes?  
**Dependencies:** U18 conversation rule, C324–C325, S46, S62, S79, `method/research-method.md`, `method/accessible-track.md`  
**Target depth:** operational rule; persistent across sessions

## Result in one sentence

Any source that does inferential work must be accompanied in the same reply by its exact argument, use, assumptions, limits, and assessment; any accessible answer added or changed must likewise be shown in that reply as an exact question-and-answer or exact delta.

## 1. Why the previous provenance rule was insufficient

The repository already required exact sources and tracked whether citations verified a claim. That did not guarantee that the author saw the reasoning for which a source was being invoked. A named example could therefore appear to oppose a thesis even when the example targeted only a narrower claim, as happened with the initial use of Shoemaker in S78.

The missing control is conversational and inferential. Provenance answers “where did this come from?”; the new rule also answers “what is the argument, exactly, and what work is it allowed to do here?”

## 2. Inferentially active sources

A source is inferentially active when it supports, rejects, limits, extends, or helps derive a project claim. Each such use requires disclosure of:

1. exact source and inspected portion;
2. premises, inferential move, and conclusion;
3. target project claim and argumentative role;
4. assumptions imported and declined;
5. scope limits, live replies, and project assessment.

Three voices remain separate: the source's argument, the project's inference, and the author's choice. Metadata, an abstract, or an example can bear only the content actually inspected. A source that has not been inspected closely enough for reconstruction cannot change claim status or priority.

A historical name or position mentioned only as vocabulary, precedent, discovery pointer, or broad contrast is non-inferential background. It is labeled as such and does not need a full reconstruction because it is not allowed to carry a conclusion.

## 3. Exact accessible-answer reporting

When `writing/accessible/preguntas-en-lenguaje-comun.md` gains an answer, the same reply must reproduce the exact question, complete answer, and status. When an existing answer changes, the exact changed passage is sufficient if its place and replacement are unambiguous. Adding an unanswered question to the bank must be reported explicitly as question-banking without an answer.

This turns the earlier author-review loop into a per-change rule. The repository preserves the answer; it never substitutes for the conversational review by which the author understands and directs it.

## Decisions

| Claim ID | Status | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C677 | `accepted authorial method rule` | Every inferentially active source must be disclosed in the same reply through exact source, reconstructed argument, project use, imported assumptions, limits, and assessment. |
| C678 | `accepted evidential control` | A source not inspected closely enough for the claimed argument may not alter commitment status or priority; metadata and abstracts support only their explicit content. |
| C679 | `accepted conversational reporting rule` | Every accessible answer added or changed must be shown to the author in the same reply as the exact question and full new answer or an unambiguous exact delta, with status; banking alone must also be stated. |

## Literature and novelty notes

No literature did inferential work in establishing these rules. They are direct authorial requirements prompted by the project's own S78–S79 correction. Shoemaker is mentioned only as the internal history of the failure, not as a new premise.
