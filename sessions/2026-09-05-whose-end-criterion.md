# Session: Whose end: the criterion restated after the software objection

**Date:** 2026-09-05
**Session ID:** S98
**Primary question:** The author objects that “code written to be read by someone” defeats the purpose of programming: software is written so that the software works, and is maintainable and scalable, not necessarily to be read, and increasingly it is written by language models, which are no one in the metaphysical sense. He keeps “it is enough to live” and rejects the rest. What is the mark, if not a reader?
**Dependencies:** S97 (C744–C745); C61, C63; C107, C331, C380; C626–C633, C735
**Origin:** the author's second review of the criterion
**Target depth:** F2 restatement; one accessible delta

## Source-argument disclosure (C677)

No source did inferential work. Kant's distinction between natural ends, which are cause and effect of themselves, and external purposiveness (*Critique of Judgment* §§64–65) is the classical form of the distinction below; named as background, not inspected.

## The criterion, restated (C746)

Within the created order, for an item $x$:

1. If $x$ is correct or incorrect relative to an end that is neither $x$'s own nor its system's own, an agent whose end it is lies upstream, necessarily; a proximate producer that is no one (a press, a model) does not break the inference, since its competence and the end are derived. Meaning is the case where the end is to say.
2. If $x$ is correct or incorrect relative to its own system's self-maintenance, its end is its own; self-maintaining wholes have intrinsic ends by their organization (C331) and are what the generative structure yields (C107, C380). No agent is required within the order, and none is excluded.
3. If $x$ is a response to a reason, an agent is required (C63).
4. Otherwise the structure explains.

For the order as a whole the comparison remains between stopping points (C458–C460).

Why the stone and the program point to someone and the sequence does not: the stone and the program are correct relative to ends that are someone else's; the sequence is correct relative to the organism's own living. It is enough to live.

## Tests, re-run

The five tests of C745 pass unchanged, and the software case is added: a program, whoever or whatever typed it, has an agent upstream because its end is someone's. A self-replicating program whose only norm were its own persistence would, on this criterion, be a case of clause 2, and that is the right result: if something maintained itself for its own sake it would be alive in the relevant sense, and only the structure would be required for it within the order.
