# Session: Dual-aspect episodes and subject unity

**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Session ID:** S88  
**Primary question:** What does a subject-level dual-aspect account add to the accepted embodied phenomenal-power model?  
**Dependencies:** bearer and phenomenal unity; causal relevance of experience  
**Starting ledger items:** C134–C139, C212–C220, C310–C338, C435–C446, C680–C685, C700–C717

## Starting position

The author endorses the route for further development. Under U18, S87's proposed divine-ground integration stays at its proposed status. The accepted baseline remains one embodied power-bearing subject with a qualified phenomenal primitive; God is the ultimate source within preferred disjoint creation. Neither creative power nor non-sufficient divine determination of free decisions is reopened.

**Proposed result:** prefer a limited dual-aspect refinement of that baseline: an embodied conscious episode is one subject-owned process with physical and phenomenal dimensions, not a pair of independently acting processes. This refines the ontology of manifestation but does not eliminate the phenomenal primitive. Its explanatory gain over the existing baseline is modest, because that baseline already avoided a second subject and competing bodily causes.

## Three claims often compressed into “two aspects”

| Claim | Content | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| two descriptions | a physical description and an experiential description are available | alone insufficient: different descriptions can concern correlated but different things |
| one episode/process | the bodily realization and phenomenal occurrence belong to one actual psychophysical process of the subject | proposed substantive refinement |
| full derivation | physical vocabulary or laws alone entail and explain every phenomenal fact | not established and not included in the refinement |

“Aspect” means a real dimension of the episode, not a component, inner observer, mere manner of speaking, or literal hidden spatial interior. “Physical” need not mean only what an observer can currently measure, and “phenomenal” need not mean that the subject can accurately report or reflect on the episode. The distinction concerns the episode's constitution, not merely perspectives of observers.

## Local token schema

For an actual embodied conscious episode, let $x$ be its bearer and $e$ the relevant integrated process. Use:

\[
Bearer(e,x)\land PhysicalProfile(e,p)\land PhenomenalProfile(e,\phi).
\]

The profiles describe or represent properties of the episode; they are not further concrete subjects or detachable ingredients. The phenomenal description and the relevant bodily-process description concern $e$. This does not identify an entire organism, a local neural component, and an experience as one object. The physical profile concerns the appropriate realization process at its proper scope, which still needs substantive specification.

The positive claim is stronger than $Correlates(e_{physical},e_{mental})$: the proposed realization does not have two numerically separate events needing a further transmission channel. It is weaker than deriving $\phi$ from $p$. No universal one-to-one physical/phenomenal mapping, physical supervenience theorem, or modal exclusion of phenomenally different physical duplicates is established merely by this schema.

Not every physical process is an experience. Not every conscious episode is a choice. Not every episode of a persisting subject must be consciously present to it, and the whole bearer is not identical to its current stream. No microexperiences or universal consciousness are introduced.

## One bearer is not yet one phenomenal field

Separate three unities:

1. **Bearer unity:** one organized individual persists and bears the capacities.
2. **Phenomenal unity:** contents are jointly present within an experiential field, rather than merely exchanged as information.
3. **Agentive integration:** some contents and processes can participate in one subject's practical appraisal, control, or settlement.

The accepted organismic and identity account supports the first. The existing agency account constrains the third. Neither by itself derives the second or establishes a fixed count of phenomenal fields for every possible condition of an organism. Nor does the one-bearer model require perfect access to every concurrent or past experience.

The proposed anchoring direction is bearer-first: the field is a manifestation belonging to an independently individuated subject, not a construction that first sums little subjects and then declares their sum a person. This retains fundamental numerical identity and the onset-neutral unity–origin–career account. It avoids adopting a micro-subject combination theory, while leaving the positive bearer-to-field bridge explicit.

### Boundary filter

For the current embodied realization, use independently described organismic organization as a candidate anchor: an immanent career of self-maintenance, coordinated regulation, and integrated capacities. These features must be assessed without defining them as whatever has consciousness. They are constraints on the bearer account, not a sufficient physical criterion for phenomenality or a universal definition excluding other possible conscious kinds.

Strong communication, synchronization, complexity, or shared goals alone do not establish a new bearer. Two otherwise distinct subjects exchanging all their reports do not thereby have a demonstrated common first-person field. This is an inferential limit, not a theorem that every possible collective subject is impossible. Conversely, one organism's numerical identity alone cannot settle every question of simultaneous phenomenal unity.

Pairwise information links are therefore not licenses for transitive merging of subjects or experiences. If a proposed bridge declares an arbitrary aggregate conscious merely because its components exchange information, it owes an additional unity argument rather than inheriting one from “dual aspect.”

## Causality: token unity is useful but insufficient

In a schematic episode, a subject experiences pain, appraises it as a reason for requesting help, and decides whether to ask. Under the proposed account, the pain's bodily realization and lived unpleasantness are dimensions of one process of that subject. This is a conceptual example, not an empirical claim about a specific mechanism, reflex, or experiment.

One token process need not compete with itself as a second independent cause. But:

\[
Causes(e,q)\land PhenomenalProfile(e,\phi)
\not\Rightarrow RelevantAsPhenomenal(e,\phi,q).
\]

A property can belong to an efficacious event without being relevant to its efficacy. Therefore the non-epiphenomenal role of experience needs a further condition: in an episode presented as consciously guided, the subject's lived content must enter practical appraisal or control in a way attributable to the subject, rather than accompany a separately completed decision. This extends C215–C216 without requiring every feeling to be a free or behaviorally efficacious act.

The physical and phenomenal descriptions can refer to the same reason-sensitive process. That gives a candidate for non-competitive relevance without postulating an additional bodily push. It does not by itself prove that phenomenal properties do the required work. A complete account must connect the lived character to the power's role instead of identifying efficacy solely with report or an independently sufficient subpersonal selector.

### Dependence test and its limits

Across independently admissible variations in the same kind of subject, changes in the episode's lived significance should be tracked in the relevant appraisal/control profile where the theory claims consciously guided action. The same behavior need not always result: several motives may compete and an agent may resist a reason. No deterministic function from pain to choice is required.

Do not demand removal of the phenomenal dimension while keeping the complete constitutive episode fixed: the model treats that dimension as belonging to the episode's nature, so such subtraction cannot be assumed possible. But forbidding that subtraction is not sufficient evidence for causal relevance either. Lawful covariation with control could still support a rival account. This test supplies an explanatory burden and possible comparisons, not a completed causal proof or an empirical protocol.

If an independently established prior process sufficiently settles a choice before any relevant conscious contribution, the episode fails the existing conscious-agency condition. Token identity is not an exemption from that defeater.

## Divine grounding

God originates an independently admissible order whose creaturely power profiles include these psychophysical manifestations. Ordinary capacities and episodes can be realized through created processes. The bearer of $\phi$ is $x$, not God; the divine knowledge of it is not that very experiential token. No return to strict ex deo or universal direct divine episode production follows.

God's conscious nature provides the already-preferred ultimate conscious source; it does not derive which finite bodily profile has which phenomenal character, how many perspectives obtain, or where an onset threshold lies. Those are constraints on the positive created profile proposed in S87. Independent admissibility is not replaced by divine will.

## What has been gained, and what has not

| Issue | Bare qualified-power baseline | Proposed dual-aspect refinement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| bearer | one embodied individual | same bearer; no additional subject |
| realization relation | bodily and phenomenal roles require further specification | one psychophysical manifestation token with real dimensions |
| causal competition | non-competition already allowed | a concrete token-unity interpretation, still owing property relevance |
| phenomenal existence and quality | qualified primitive | still qualifiedly primitive; no derivation from nonphenomenal structure |
| unity | organismic and agency constraints | bearer-first anchoring plus an explicit distinction from phenomenal-field unity |
| divine role | ultimate source of finite capacities | same role; no new divine constituent or universal intervention |

The argument for provisional preference is internal and abductive: retain the one-bearer model, real bodily manifestation, and real experience; one psychophysical episode integrates those commitments without an independent second event or subject. Its cost is a constitutive identity/aspect claim and an unfinished bearer/quality/relevance bridge. It is not a newly discovered proof of consciousness or a completed replacement for the existing phenomenal primitive.

A baseline defender can say that “one subject with phenomenal powers” already contains everything gained here. The reply is limited: the refinement explicitly selects one manifestation-token account from the more open realization descriptions, so it rules out a merely paired-event reading. If that clarification adds no further constraints in later work, it should remain a useful specification rather than be promoted as a deeper explanation.
