# Session: Bodily integration and a conditional phenomenal bridge

## Starting position

The author explicitly endorses S89 and asks to continue along that route. Retain one embodied subject, distinct bodily and experiential processes, differentiated pain/suffering/decision roles, and joint manifestation as a qualified phenomenal primitive. God is the ultimate ground within preferred disjoint creation. None of those commitments is reopened by merely acknowledging its explanatory limit.

S37 already required an independently constrained eligibility profile and a psychophysical power principle (C333). Naming such a principle again would not be progress. This session specifies one candidate episode-level bodily condition, identifies the exact extra premise needed to move from it to experience, and separates that conditional explanation from both a derivation of phenomenality and a sufficient determination of action.

**Proposed working result:** develop a scoped conditional manifestation account. It offers a constraint-bearing route for the current embodied model, not a claim that this route is already established or universally necessary.

## What makes the bodily candidate more specific?

The proposed route has three structural requirements:

1. Several processes contribute actively to an evolving common regulatory state during the relevant episode.
2. That state feeds back into their subsequent processing, so their roles involve a shared causal organization rather than mere storage or transmission.
3. Participation is selective: the proposed organization identifies which processes actually contribute during the episode, without including every process or signal connected to the body.

The common state may be distributed; it is not a spatial meeting point, a second subject, or an identification of bodily state with lived experience. Counterfactual questions about changing a contribution and its effects on common regulation can describe this bodily organization without first asking whether anyone experiences it.

This is more restrictive than “information passes between these parts,” but it is not enough for consciousness. A constructed regulator could satisfy these structural requirements without the project having established that it feels anything. That logical possibility for the description exposes the missing bridge; it is not a theorem that every such regulator lacks experience.

The route is not required to involve language, reflective awareness, a current choice, or overt movement. Nor does it require every pair of processes to communicate directly. Conversely, the existing subject boundary must be independently justified: the candidate does not turn two communicating subjects into one bearer by pooling their inputs.

## Argument and exact point of import

1. The accepted model attributes phenomenal capacity to an embodied subject and distinguishes having that capacity from manifesting it.
2. Bodily organization is already treated as relevant to manifestation, but bare ownership or connectivity leaves the participation boundary unspecified.
3. Active common regulation supplies a candidate participation condition stated without phenomenal vocabulary.
4. Nothing in that structural description alone asserts that the participating contents are jointly experienced.
5. A phenomenal conclusion therefore requires a further premise relating the subject's capacity and that bodily condition to joint presentation.
6. With that premise explicitly supplied, the model conditionally constrains joint presentation while preserving bodily/experiential distinction.

Steps 1–3 motivate the candidate internally; they do not prove that it is the actual route. Steps 4–6 identify the logical burden and conditional consequence. The preference for developing this route is architectural and provisional: it gives the existing power principle an independently describable candidate input and exposes where the additional commitment enters.

The principle is a rule governing how a power manifests, not another entity or an additional subject. Relative to S37, the added substantive hypothesis is the proposed scope and content of the rule, not the mere existence of a psychophysical relation.

## Local F2 schema

Fix a candidate embodied realization route and an independently individuated bearer x. Let:

- K: x has the relevant phenomenal capacity and satisfies the route's eligibility assumptions.
- R: the specified bodily processes satisfy the active-integration condition.
- J: the associated contents are jointly presented to x in this episode.
- B: the candidate bridge condition is included as a true premise of the local model.
- A: one of two response alternatives is selected.

The candidate premise is:

\[
B\land K\land R\ \longrightarrow\ J.
\]

B records the added substantive bridge assumption; it is not established by defining it. The schema is a scope test of that assumption rather than a derivation of the bridge.

### Structural underdetermination

Without B, K and R alone leave J unconstrained. Two interpretations can agree on the stated bearer/capacity and bodily conditions while assigning different values to J:

| K | R | B | J | Local interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | joint presentation |
| 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | no joint presentation of the relevant contents |

These are countermodels to a consequence from the stated structural premises. They do not establish two metaphysically possible worlds with identical complete physics, determine whether experience occurs separately in the second interpretation, or prove the absence of a stronger real-world law. The unspecified physical and psychophysical theory might rule out one interpretation.

### Field sufficiency is not response sufficiency

Under B, K, and R, J must be true in the local schema. Both A=0 and A=1 remain permitted because the field premise specifies no response selector.

This verifies only that the bridge condition itself does not entail the chosen response. It does not prove that the complete antecedent state leaves alternatives open, that either assignment has actual libertarian sourcehood, or that experience is causally efficacious. Those remain governed by the existing agency account and C215–C216/C336. It is compatible with C135–C137 for the occurrence or organization of an experience to be determined while a response is a candidate free act.

### One-way scope

The proposal supplies one sufficient route conditional on B and K. It does not assert:

\[
J\longrightarrow R
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\neg R\longrightarrow\neg J.
\]

No universal route necessity, unique field count, maximality rule, or transitivity rule for all overlapping contents follows. Another bodily route could realize joint presentation. A full absence claim would need independently supported necessity or exclusion of alternative routes.

## Adversarial tests

| Test | Result and remaining burden |
| --- | --- |
| Mere connection or synchronized outputs | Does not by itself establish active common regulation; even the stronger R needs the bridge premise |
| Constructed regulator satisfies R | Forces the account to retain eligibility, phenomenal capacity, and bridge scope; no inference to universal consciousness |
| Two subjects exchange reports | Existing separate bearer identities remain; communication alone licenses no new bearer or common experiential field |
| A process stops participating while the bearer persists | Distinguishes participation from identity. It changes the candidate R profile; a phenomenal consequence additionally needs the bridge and control of alternate routes |
| One route is disrupted but another remains | A sufficient-route proposal cannot infer loss of consciousness from absence of R alone |
| Jointly experienced contents do not issue in a report or movement | Report and motor expression are separate targets; their absence alone does not refute this phenomenal claim |
| Same experienced reasons, different responses | Compatible with the field schema, without being a proof of freedom or a completed causal theory |
| Add a psychophysical premise to any convenient structure | The danger is real: the actual rule must earn its scope through evidence and comparison; formal consistency alone is insufficient |

These are conceptual tests. No case study, experiment, medical fact, or observation of a particular person is imported.

## Alternatives and assessment

**Stop at S89's bare joint-manifestation primitive:** remains coherent and costs fewer specific assumptions. It explains less about selective bodily participation. S90 is worth pursuing only if its candidate can earn constraints independently; otherwise the proposal should be demoted to an illustration of the older power account.

**Identify joint experience with the functional organization:** would remove the extra bridge by definition or identity claim, but that is not established here and would reopen the author's S89 correction. Its availability is not a new reason to reverse the working preference.

**Add a separate subject or divine intervention for each field:** neither follows from the structural gap. A separate subject would still need a body–experience relation; invoking God for each episode would add a causal topology not required by the retained GSDA account.

**Preferred next elaboration:** the conditional route above. The gain is limited but concrete: a candidate physical participation condition, an explicit bridge assumption, a distinction between field and action sufficiency, and tests that prohibit stronger conclusions from the one-way rule. Actual realization and evidential maturity remain open. This partially develops C732; it does not close the mind–body problem.

God remains the ultimate ground of an independently admissible created power profile. This neither turns the finite experience into a divine constituent nor derives its organization from God's consciousness. The ordinary manifestation route belongs to the creature.
