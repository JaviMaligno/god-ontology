## Proposed distinctions

| Role | Local meaning | Decision relation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bodily process | The relevant bodily occurrence or activity, distinguished from its experience | Can condition, enable, constrain, or participate in the episode; not every such condition is a consciously apprehended reason |
| Felt pain | The painful experiential content, rather than the bodily occurrence simply renamed | May enter deliberation as an aversive motive without sufficiently selecting the response |
| Suffering | The subject's affliction, provisionally distinguished from felt pain; its scope is not exhausted by a deliberately adopted judgment | May involve lived significance and other affective content; it is neither defined as voluntary nor erased by accepting a cost |
| Decision | The subject's settlement within an embodied episode, with potentially conscious and non-conscious contributions | Can direct responses and actions with different relations to bodily conditions and lived experience |

These are conceptual role distinctions for this model, not an empirical taxonomy. Pain and suffering may overlap in an episode; distinct concepts do not establish separable components in every case. Calling pain experiential here is a proposed use of vocabulary, not an equation of pain with suffering or an assumption that the author used “dolor” in a technical sense.

A coarse decision episode can contain several distinct processes. Saying “one episode” in that ordinary aggregative sense is compatible with this correction; it must not silently restore S115's stronger identity of bodily realization and phenomenal occurrence.

## Endurance, reasons, and causal determination

Reconstruct the local argument:

1. The author treats enduring pain as an available response.
2. Pain can count in favor of stopping or seeking relief.
3. A reason can count against an act without sufficiently determining that the subject will avoid it (C56).
4. Therefore a model of endurance must allow pain to matter without invariably compelling avoidance. The stronger non-necessitating-reason model comes from the independently retained C56, not from endurance alone.

This excludes the claim that pain invariably compels avoidance. It does **not** establish either of the stronger conclusions:

- that the entire antecedent situation fails to determine the response;
- that bodily and experiential processes are numerically distinct.

A deterministic countermodel could include another motive or control process that sufficiently fixes continuing. Conversely, distinct bodily and experiential processes could be related by deterministic laws. This is a logical countermodel, not an endorsed realization theory or an empirical account. Identity and determinism remain separate under C327. The preferred process distinction is grounded here in the author's explicit correction and retained as the working account, not presented as a deduction from endurance.

Nor does “motive” exclude every causal contribution. The relevant exclusion is sufficient determination of the free token by antecedents independently of subject-level origination, not all influence. The occurrence of a pain or suffering may itself be involuntary or determined while the response remains a candidate free act (C135–C137).

## Differentiated contributions and return routes

The author's positive point is stronger than a bare list of distinct items: they participate differently in one subject's life.

A conceptual episode can be described as bodily conditions, lived pain and affliction, appraisal of competing reasons, settlement, and ensuing action. These are roles, not a universal linear physiological pipeline. Some can overlap in time, and appraisal can alter later experience before the final choice. Bodily processes are not a second chooser; experience is not an inner observer.

Candidate return routes include attention or appraisal directed toward what is experienced and action directed toward bodily conditions or the surrounding situation. The account must permit different efficacy and timing across these routes. It does not yet establish a mechanism, an available intervention in every case, or that deciding is enough to produce the desired change.

For consciously guided action, the lived content must contribute to the subject's appraisal or control before an independently sufficient settlement (C215–C216). Distinctness does not automatically defeat causal exclusion: a future positive theory must specify how the links work and why their effects are not independently fixed without the claimed conscious contribution. Common ownership alone supplies neither a mechanism nor causal independence.

Endurance is compatible with continuing to hurt or suffer. Agency over a response does not entail complete control of feeling, and failure to end suffering is not evidence that the person chose it. This protects the existing valence anchor and permission burdens; it supplies no new theodicy.

## Advancing the accepted unity question

A proposed refinement of C337 is that an embodied subject's phenomenal capacity can manifest several contents **jointly**. In a conceptual example, pain and a consciously apprehended reason for continuing may be lived together while remaining different contents and involving different processes. Not every motive must be conscious or co-present, and no inner spectator is added.

Distinguish:

- **Ownership:** both experiences belong to the same individual.
- **Practical integration:** both can affect that individual's appraisal or control.
- **Joint presentation:** the contents are lived together in one experiential field.

Ownership does not entail simultaneous presentation: a subject's earlier and later experiences can both be its own. Practical comparison can employ memory, reports, or dispositions and therefore does not alone entail joint phenomenal presence of the original experiences. Two subjects exchanging reports likewise provide no sufficient inference to a common field.

The proposed joint manifestation supplies an explicit phenomenal relation within the qualified-power baseline. It allows plurality within unity and makes process identity unnecessary for the intended unity claim. **It remains primitive at this stage.** Defining joint presentation is not deriving it from organization, and merely attaching the term to a subject adds no independent explanation of where and when it occurs.

The working choice is provisional: retain the phenomenal capacity and its jointly manifested content rather than assert that shared access explains all unity. Its cost is the undeveloped bearer-to-field bridge. A stronger account must add constraints independently of “whatever is jointly experienced”; the present proposal does not meet that stronger burden. No universal transitivity rule for pairwise co-presentation, fixed field count for every condition, or organismic threshold is inferred.

## Architecture and alternatives

The accepted subject is one embodied bearer with real powers. Distinguishing its bodily and experiential processes does not by itself establish two substances; substance dualism remains an alternative with its existing additional burdens. S115's token-identity account remains an open alternative, with its previous preference withdrawn. C335's conditional non-competition model remains valid as a compatibility claim but cannot establish the identity now withdrawn.

God remains the ultimate source of independently admissible finite powers. The creature bears and manifests its own experience through ordinary created capacities. Disjoint creation, GSDA, independent admissibility, and the non-sufficient divine determination of free choices remain at their current status. Process differentiation and primitive joint manifestation neither require a divine constituent nor derive finite phenomenal unity from divine consciousness.
