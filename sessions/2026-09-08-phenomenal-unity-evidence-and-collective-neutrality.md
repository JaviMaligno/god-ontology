## Starting position and author direction

The author agrees that a substantive unity explanation is needed, explicitly neither rejects nor endorses collective consciousness, and authorizes continuing the proposed evidence inquiry.

Retain S90 at its proposed conditional status under U18. The collective case is integrated as a boundary test of the same inquiry, not promoted to an independent research program. No source or lack of evidence may silently turn the author's neutrality into rejection.

The actual embodied subject remains the preferred baseline. However, the human organismic account must not become a universal definition excluding every other possible bearer. Conversely, neutrality does not establish that any collective has a phenomenal perspective.

## Three interpretations to distinguish

| Interpretation | Claim | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coordinated subjects | Several subjects exchange information or cooperate | That the collective experiences anything as a subject |
| Information enters an existing subject | A participant's own experience or decision incorporates transmitted content | That the original experiential tokens are shared or a further bearer appears |
| Collective phenomenality | A phenomenal perspective belongs to the collective itself | How its bearer is individuated or related to participant perspectives |

The third is the strong interpretation used for the author's open question. It need not be a numerical merger of the original subjects. A further bearer with persisting participants, or another explicitly explained ownership relation, remains a possibility to assess; neither coexistence nor replacement is asserted.

The question is not whether a collective is “really a unity” as opposed to an individual. If there were a collective phenomenal subject, its unity would also need explanation. The contrast is between functional cooperation and phenomenal subjecthood at whichever level is claimed.

### Source 1: mediated collaboration

**Exact source:** Linxing Jiang, Andrea Stocco, Darby M. Losey, Justin A. Abernethy, Chantel S. Prat, and Rajesh P. N. Inspected the [author-text PDF, arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.08632), Results, Discussion, and Methods, especially pp. 2–6; publication metadata and corresponding indexed journal text were cross-checked. The full PMC browser endpoint was blocked; the PDF supplied the inspected methods and results.

**Source argument:** P1: decisions from two senders reach a receiver through an EEG/TMS interface with task feedback. P2: five triads perform the task above chance. C: the interface supports mediated collaborative problem solving.

**Project role:** C741 limits what this result establishes about C734–C738. The measured endpoints concern communication and task performance; the study does not supply a test distinguishing a collective field from coordinated individual processing.

**Imported assumption:** task performance and the documented channel design support the communication interpretation within the experiment. **Declined assumption:** effective or direct brain communication entails shared experiential tokens or collective phenomenality.

**Assessment and limits:** evidence for collaboration is retained. Collective consciousness is neither established nor refuted. The implementation's binary signals, receiver role, small sample, and task-specific design constrain generalization. We do not claim that this study directly instantiates or tests every component of S90's R.

## Consequence for the candidate bridge

The evidence makes S90's burden sharper without verifying or falsifying its exact R-to-J link.

1. A common response or successful collaboration can be explained by transmission and receiver processing. Those outputs alone do not select a collective-field interpretation.
2. Ability to respond to individual contents and ability to compare them are distinct measured targets. One cannot silently stand for the other.
3. Neither target is identical by definition to jointly lived experience.
4. Therefore a bridge assessment must say which evidence concerns bodily coordination, which concerns practical access, and which bears on co-presence, and then defend the inference connecting them.

This is a project reconstruction using C134/C720 and the inspected task designs. It does not infer that experiences are inaccessible to evidence or that all interpretations are equally supported. It identifies why the particular measures cannot carry a stronger conclusion unaided.

**Revision of scope:** active shared regulation remains a candidate physical condition, but its current breadth does not individuate a subject. Describing feedback more richly can still leave the ownership and phenomenal relation open. The collective comparison exposes that gap; it does not show that every feedback network is conscious or that no collective could be.

## Proposed convergent evidence standard

Use different evidential routes and ask whether their combination discriminates the claimed unity from live alternatives:

- **Experiential report:** ask about contents lived together, their relations, and changes in presentation. Distinguish this from merely reporting that two inputs occurred or repeating a received description. Reports are fallible and may be inaccessible in some candidates.
- **Flexible relational performance:** test comparisons and relations across contents, including unfamiliar combinations. This constrains access and integration, but an engineered or social information channel can also support performance.
- **Controlled dependence:** compare changes to candidate integration with changes to transmission, sensory access, memory, response demands, and common inputs. Preserving component performance while changing relational performance would narrow the functional explanation; it would not alone count phenomenal fields.

These are proposed assessment dimensions, not a ready clinical protocol, an instruction to perform interventions, or validated universal consciousness tests. No invasive work or experiment is undertaken.

The same explanatory demand applies to bodily and collective candidates: make the ownership claim explicit and show why the evidence favors it over coordination. The available evidence and methods need not be identical. Established organismic organization supplies real support for a bodily bearer; it is not by itself a theorem of phenomenal unity.

The standard must remain usable: absence of direct third-person access is not grounds for requiring impossible certainty. Conversely, relabeling a convenient behavioral score as consciousness would make the conclusion circular.

## Adversarial limits and alternatives

| Temptation | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| A network solves a task, so it is one subject | Cooperation and receiver integration remain alternative explanations |
| A comparison fails, so there are two subjects | Access, task, or response limitations remain alternatives |
| A report says “one”, so no other field exists | Report is evidence, not exhaustive introspective certification |
| No collective field was measured, so none exists | Absence of a discriminating test does not settle the author's open possibility |
| Human organismhood defines all possible subjects | The actual bearer account is not a universal exclusion rule |
| Every account needs a bridge, so all are tied | Differences in evidence, explanatory scope, and cost still matter |
| A collective must erase its participants | This is an additional structural claim, not part of collective phenomenality by definition |

The endorsed embodied-power model remains preferred. Process distinction, the phenomenal primitive, God's ultimate role, disjoint creation, and GSDA retain their status. No divine shared field, token sharing, or universal intervention follows from collective neutrality.

## Accessible-answer report

New question: **¿Podría existir una conciencia colectiva?**

Complete answer:

> No lo afirmo ni lo descarto. Varias personas pueden compartir información y actuar coordinadamente; la cuestión adicional es si el conjunto tiene una experiencia propia. Que una persona reciba información de otras tampoco demuestra que haya aparecido un sujeto colectivo. Para aceptar esa posibilidad como algo real necesitaría razones sobre quién vive esa experiencia y qué la hace una unidad. Esa exigencia también se aplica a mi explicación de la conciencia individual. Por ahora, la cuestión queda abierta.

Status: open, with explicit authorial neutrality; the explanatory distinction is proposed. This question is answered at that limited status, not merely banked. No other accessible answer is changed; add its provenance row.
