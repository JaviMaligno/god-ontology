## Preferred continuity proposal

Propose understanding an unfolding experience as an ongoing manifestation that can update its contents, prominence and lived orientation. Continuity need not mean keeping the same inventory of contents.

Three targets must be distinguished:

1. **Persistence of the subject:** who continues to exist.
2. **Jointness within a presentation:** what is lived together at a specified stage.
3. **Continuity across change:** how an unfolding manifestation continues through successive configurations.

These targets are connected but not interchangeable. The same subject can be considered across a stipulated interruption of experience. Joint presentation at several separate stages does not yet establish an unbroken experiential transition between them.

For the embodied route, the candidate is continuing exercise of the phenomenal capacity through an ongoing, updating organization. Earlier organization conditions later activity, while the lived manifestation continues. Bodily causal continuation alone does not prove the last clause: the temporal psychophysical relation remains part of what must be justified.

This is a proposal about how continuation could be realized. Defining uninterrupted manifestation does not identify its actual boundary or show that a particular physical process realizes it.

## Different kinds of change

| Change | Local description | What remains to assess |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Redistribution of attention | A content already lived in the background becomes prominent, or conversely | Whether it was already present; attention can also introduce or alter content |
| Modification of lived framing | What is presented changes in felt significance or relation | Whether this occurs within a continuing manifestation |
| Incorporation or replacement | A content enters, leaves, or is replaced in what is lived | A changed inventory does not by itself establish interruption or a new subject |
| Interruption and later resumption | An experiential interval is stipulated absent between presentations | The subject and information can persist without filling the experiential gap |

These are model distinctions, not a clinical diagnostic scheme. Some changes can occur together.

For example, in a hypothetical episode a painful content could cease to be focal while remaining jointly lived with a goal-related orientation. It could instead disappear or be replaced. A changed focus alone does not decide which description is true. The account continues to distinguish pain from suffering and grants no general voluntary control over either.

## Local F2 check: overlap is not global co-presence

Let F0, F1 and F2 list selected contents stipulated jointly present at three indexed stages of a toy account:

\[
F_0=\{a,b\},\qquad F_1=\{b,c\},\qquad F_2=\{c,d\}.
\]

Adjacent stages share a content:

\[
F_0\cap F_1=\{b\},\qquad F_1\cap F_2=\{c\}.
\]

Yet no listed stage contains both a and d. The union contains four labels, but it is not another stipulated simultaneous presentation. Therefore connected overlap across stages does not establish that all contents were ever jointly present.

The labels are content types used for this local inference. Repeating b at two stages does not settle token identity. The sets are not a complete ontology of experience and do not replace S123's lived modes or relations.

The example does not prove actual experiential continuity either: the selected stages leave their transitions unspecified. A continuity account needs more than matching adjacent content lists.

A second diagnostic is equally limited: presentations with matching content types before and after a stipulated experiential gap do not make the gap disappear. This does not infer a gap from a failed report; the absence is part of the hypothetical specification.

The result is a bounded logical constraint, not a neuroscience finding or a theorem about possible field counts.

## Positive integration and remaining bridge

The account can permit change within continuity because what is proposed to continue is an exercise, not an immutable list. This preserves a role for both focal and background contributions while allowing some contents to be incorporated or lost.

Experiential uptake can affect subsequent appraisal and bodily organization without every such update creating a new subject or sufficiently settling a free act. Conversely, a causal influence across time is not automatically an uninterrupted experience: information or a disposition can link distinct episodes.

The next precise burden is the temporal bridge: what warrants an unbroken phenomenal transition rather than merely causally connected presentations? It must discriminate ongoing manifestation from interruption/resumption without using report continuity, content overlap, or unchanged ownership as sufficient criteria.

The existing scoped bridge from bodily organization to joint manifestation at a stage does not, by itself, provide a full account of transitions between stages. This is a specification burden on the preferred model, not a reason to reset its comparison with the bypass rival.
