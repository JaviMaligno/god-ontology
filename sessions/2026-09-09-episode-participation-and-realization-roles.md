## Preferred proposal

Participation is an active role in an organized exercise of the subject's capacity. A bodily process is a candidate episode realizer when it performs part of the operation that forms, maintains, differentiates, or regulates the relevant bodily configuration during the episode.

This is a criterion for membership in the proposed bodily realization base, relative to an independently specified organization. It is not yet a sufficient criterion for consciousness or an independent discovery of that organization. The bridge from eligible bodily organization to lived joint manifestation remains an additional commitment under C333/C735.

“Realizer” does not mean a bodily fragment is literally a piece of felt experience. The bodily process and the experience remain distinct under C726. Nor are already conscious micro-subjects combined to create the individual; the working bearer is the independently individuated subject whose power is exercised.

## Role distinctions

Classify a process by its role in a specified episode and time interval, not permanently by its anatomical location or object name.

| Role | What it contributes | What that alone establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Antecedent formation | Develops or configures a capacity before this exercise | Causal history |
| Input | Supplies a signal or value used by the current operation | Information available to the operation |
| Enabling support | Supplies resources or conditions for operation | Ability to continue operating |
| Realization participation | Performs an operative part of forming, maintaining, or regulating the organized configuration | Membership in the candidate realization base |
| Output or report | Expresses or transmits a result | A downstream effect or access route |

These are nonexclusive roles. The same process can supply an input and help organize it; a report-related process can feed back into the ongoing organization. If it does, its contribution must be assessed over that interval rather than dismissed as an output by definition. A support process can also have a differentiated organizing role.

Being part of the living subject is not identical to participating in every experiential episode. Conversely, an externally located component is not excluded from every conceivable realization solely by location. The present proposal concerns the preferred embodied route and does not establish an extended subject.

## Three requirements for a candidate participation claim

### 1. Specify a current operation

Identify the bearer, the relevant interval, and the bodily operation in terms of states, signal distinctions, interactions, and regulatory effects. Do not identify the operation merely as “whatever makes the experience one”.

The interval can include maintenance and feedback. Participation does not require a new signal at every instant: a maintained state or constraint can perform an active role. A dormant capacity with no operative contribution is different.

### 2. Identify the process's contribution to that operation

Show which role the process actually occupies in forming, preserving, differentiating, or regulating the configuration. Explain its dependencies on other roles and the dependencies they have on it.

For the S90 route, participation is situated in the common regulatory organization. Reciprocal regulation belongs to the organization as a whole; every component need not directly exchange signals with every other component. A local feed-forward stage can participate in a larger recurrent operation.

A supply of information or resources does not establish this further role simply because removing it would stop the operation.

### 3. Distinguish changes in organization from changes in its inputs and support

Compare changes to the candidate role or its relations while preserving relevant input values and enabling conditions as far as the model permits. Ask whether the organization itself is altered, whether an alternative component takes over, and whether the apparent effect belongs only to a downstream report.

This is a way to assess a participation claim, not a universal intervention test. Some constituents are redundant; some interventions have several effects; some distinctions cannot be held fixed independently. The argument must disclose those limits instead of counting every disruption as evidence for membership.

These requirements make the bodily claim more specific without defining bodily organization in terms of consciousness. They do not resolve which actual organization realizes a field, its unique boundary, or whether an overlapping candidate also matters.

## Conceptual example

Imagine an abstract signal-processing arrangement with a supply of operating resources, two incoming labels, an operation that jointly arranges their assignments, and an output channel.

The source of one label can change the result by changing the input. The resource supply can stop the whole operation. The arrangement operation can change how the same incoming labels are related while input values and available resources are held fixed. An output channel can change what is communicated while the arrangement remains intact.

The example distinguishes causal roles; the arrangement is not thereby conscious. In the bodily hypothesis, an independently supported bridge would have to connect a specified eligible arrangement to how contents are lived together.

A conditional prediction follows for a refined candidate: if an intervention changes a realization-relevant relation while eligible inputs, support, and experiential access survive, the bridge should specify what difference in joint presentation follows. S90's broad sufficient-route schema does not yet give that prediction. In particular, disrupting one sufficient route does not prove experience disappears if an alternative route remains.

## Counterexamples and revisions

| Tempting criterion | Counterexample | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everything necessary belongs to the experiential realization | In the abstract example, the resource supply is necessary without performing the arrangement role | Necessity alone does not establish that role |
| Every participant must be individually indispensable | Two active components can redundantly perform a role | Lack of a removal effect does not prove nonparticipation |
| Replaceability proves a component was only external support | Another component can take over the same operative role | Replacement can change the token occupant while preserving the role |
| Preserving the abstract role proves preserved experience | Bodily eligibility or substrate-sensitive features might differ | Role equivalence alone supplies no psychophysical equivalence theorem |
| Every member needs direct reciprocal influence on every other member | A feed-forward local stage can belong to a recurrent whole | Apply the organizational requirement at the appropriate level |
| Any reciprocal information exchange constitutes one subject | A hypothetical conversation can contain mutual adjustment without its description identifying a further bearer | Feedback alone does not establish collective experience |
| All reporting is external to realization | A reporting activity can regulate an ongoing episode or a later one | Distinguish role and interval rather than excluding a process class |
| All realized contents must be reportable | Organizational participation is defined without requiring verbal access | Preserve S93's distinction between experience and report |

The replacement claim is deliberately limited. It licenses neither that any device could replace a bodily component nor that functional duplication preserves the same experience or subject.

## What the proposal explains and what it costs

The proposal gives a positive account of the bodily side: a subject exercises a power through coordinated operative contributions. Its explanatory advantage over an undifferentiated causal chain is that it distinguishes the current exercise from its history, resources, incoming signals, and subsequent expression.

Its cost is explicit. It requires a real organization and an eligible phenomenal capacity, plus a bridge specifying their manifestation. Calling an organization integrated does not derive phenomenality, numerical field count, or a unique maximal realization boundary. Organization belongs to the existing one-subject account; it adds no inner observer.

The strongest remaining objection is that the description could identify an integrated information-processing operation without identifying a phenomenal realizer. That objection is correct as a limit of the organizational description alone. C337 already accepts a qualified phenomenal primitive. The present choice is to refine the conditions of its manifestation without claiming a reduction from nonphenomenal structure.

This remains an abductive architectural proposal, not a new empirical result. Its limited scope does not suspend the author's greater credence in individual consciousness.

## Integration with creation and agency

Preferred disjoint creation allows the actual bodily organization, capacity, and manifestation to belong to the creature. God remains the ultimate ground of these finite powers; no divine constituent is needed to specify the current participation roles. Ex deo by itself supplies no more precise membership or manifestation rule.

The present pass adds no proof for either creation branch. It retains their ranking and the distinction between necessary divine grounding and sufficient selection of every free act.

Distinguishing bodily realization from lived experience also leaves a specific causal burden: why and how the lived content contributes to the subject's appraisal and response. Saying only that the whole subject acts does not answer it. Conversely, treating bodily and experiential processes as distinct does not establish two independently sufficient producers or a second inner agent.
