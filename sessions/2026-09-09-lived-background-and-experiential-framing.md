## Correction to the previous framing

S95 distinguished current experience from standing influences, but its exposition could suggest that a goal outside focal thought must be outside experience. That inference is not licensed.

Distinguish:

- **Focal attention:** what is prominent or explicitly considered.
- **Phenomenal presence:** what belongs to how the situation is currently lived, including a proposed nonfocal mode or orientation.
- **Causal or formative contribution:** what influences the occurrence, organization, or course of the experience.

Lack of focal attention alone establishes neither absence from experience nor hidden phenomenal presence. Similarly, a formative influence alone does not establish that the influence itself is experienced.

The current proposal allows a goal to appear in experience as an explicit thought, as a lived orientation, or through a felt effect whose underlying goal is not itself represented. A goal can also influence processing without a current lived manifestation. These are episode-relative possibilities, not an empirical classification of all goals.

## A goal and its manifestation are different explanatory items

Distinguish the persisting goal or disposition, its current operation, and any current phenomenal manifestation. These items must not be collapsed merely because they are attributed to one subject.

| Case | Candidate description | Phenomenal claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Explicit goal | “I want to finish this” is presently considered | The represented goal is focal content |
| Lived background orientation | The situation is experienced as progress toward something valuable, without reciting a goal proposition | A directed or evaluative mode can belong to the present experience |
| Felt effect without representation of the source | An influence produces a felt tone, but the particular goal responsible is not thereby presented | The felt effect is present; the source's full content does not follow |
| Influence without current lived manifestation | A disposition affects processing or appraisal without the relevant goal or a distinctive felt contribution being present | Influence alone does not establish phenomenal membership |

These are descriptive roles, not mutually exclusive permanent compartments. One goal can move between roles, and several causes can support one lived orientation. A background orientation can be only partially articulate.

In particular, experiencing urgency or significance does not establish which particular goal produced it, that every part of a plan is represented, or that the goal's complete causal history is present.

## Conceptual example

Compare two imagined episodes involving a broadly comparable bodily discomfort. In one, the discomfort is lived as a cost within a valued effort; in another, it is lived as an obstacle lacking that significance.

The proposed difference can concern how the situation is experienced, not only a later explicit judgment about it. The goal need not occur as a separate sentence in inner speech for its orientation to be felt.

This is a conceptual illustration, not a claim about measured pain, identical complete bodily states, or a demonstrated intervention. The broader bodily and psychological realization can differ. Pain and suffering retain their distinction, and neither is defined as a voluntary interpretation. A valued goal does not guarantee relief or control over suffering.

The example also permits a weaker alternative: the goal might affect only a subsequent judgment while the earlier felt character remains unchanged. Which description applies to an actual case needs evidence; a plausible vignette does not decide it.

## What this adds to the unity proposal

Refine C731's joint manifestation to permit both currently presented contents and the lived relations or modes through which they are presented. An experienced orientation can shape a whole situation without being an additional independent item alongside every sensation.

The proposed field is therefore sensitive to more than a list of focal objects. A felt background can participate in the same manifestation as what is prominent. This is compatible with distinct contents and bodily processes; it does not require a second observer or another subject for the background.

The inference is conditional: when a background orientation is actually jointly lived with a focal content, it belongs to that manifestation. Mere simultaneity, shared ownership, causal influence, or common practical use does not establish the jointness.

No new mechanism, unique boundary, field count, or necessary relation between attention and consciousness is derived. The point is to refine what the existing manifestation hypothesis must accommodate, without defining every formative cause as part of the field.

## Linking framing and efficacy

The author-preferred C766 applies to the lived configuration, not merely to explicitly formulated thoughts. A candidate temporal sequence is:

1. Current goals and bodily organization help shape how the situation is lived.
2. That lived configuration contributes to subsequent appraisal or control through the proposed uptake relation.
3. Appraisal, attention, or action can then alter bodily organization or the operative goal, changing how the situation is lived later.

These are successive or partly overlapping roles. The proposal does not postulate an instantaneous self-causing cycle, a universal linear pipeline, or a voluntary choice behind every background orientation.

Each link has its own burden: the realization of the felt frame, the efficacy of the lived configuration, and the return route that can modify later experience. None follows automatically from the others.

The sequence joins the equally active research fronts. It permits a subject's experience to have a changing organization and to matter causally, while preserving the distinction between experience and its bodily realization. It does not entail that appraisal sufficiently settles the free response. C216 and the limits of control over suffering remain in force.

## Assessment standard and actual gain

Treat reports of lived orientation as defeasible evidence, not an infallible record of every earlier moment. Being able to articulate a goal now does not establish that it was phenomenally present before attention turned to it. A changed response alone does not identify a felt change.

The positive gain is a more adequate target for the unity account: contents can be jointly experienced through a felt organization that is not exhausted by focal items. It also sharpens causal uptake: a lived orientation can be a candidate contributor, while the full set of its causes need not be part of what is lived.

This remains a provisional F1 specification. It does not establish which actual goals are experienced in which way, a clinical effect, or an empirical theory of attention. No named external source does inferential work in this session. The terms are local explanatory vocabulary, with no historical priority or novelty claim.
