## Cosmology: broaden the positive case

### Exact sources and reconstruction

Inspected on 2026-09-11; the arguments and limitations are disclosed in the conversational reply.

**K. A. Olive and J. A. Peacock, Particle Data Group, “Big-Bang Cosmology,” revised August 2025**, [review](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-bbang-cosmology.pdf), §§22.1, 22.1.3–22.1.5, 22.2.3, 22.3.7, 22.4.1–22.4.2.

- Premises: expansion, the thermal microwave spectrum, and primordial abundance estimates constrain one hot expanding history; the deuterium-based baryon density agrees with the microwave-background estimate.
- Inference: their convergence supports the standard hot Big-Bang account. Within the six-parameter cosmological model, integrating its expansion history gives approximately 13.8 billion years.
- Role: adds the affirmative evidential case omitted from S100's short response. It does not merely repeat ignorance of an earlier phase.
- Imported assumptions: large-scale homogeneity/isotropy, general relativity, specified matter/radiation/vacuum content, nuclear physics, and the selected expansion model. The review records a lithium discrepancy; the project does not claim perfect agreement for every abundance.
- Limit: the age is model-conditioned. Extrapolating the classical matter/radiation solutions to zero scale factor produces a finite past boundary and divergent density; this is not an observed regular event at which four independently known substances appeared. The ultimate extension or termination is a further question.

**S. W. Hawking and R. Penrose, “The singularities of gravitational collapse and cosmology,” Proc. R. Soc. A 314 (1970), 529–548**, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspa.1970.0021); inspected original-paper OCR in [this reproduction](https://www.scribd.com/document/536666765/The-singularities-of-gravitational-collapse-and-cosmology-Stephen-Hawking), §1, §3 theorem, focusing argument, corollary (3.20)–(3.23), and proof (pp. 529–532, 538–546). The uploader's AI summary is not evidence.

- Premises: chronology, convergence/energy and generic-curvature conditions, and a suitable trapped set.
- Inference: if causal geodesics were complete, focusing would produce conjugate points; the trapped-set and causal argument yields a maximizing-geodesic contradiction. Hence causal geodesic completeness fails.
- Role: strengthens the classical singularity case beyond exact symmetry.
- Scope: general, not special, relativity; the stated conditions are not universal axioms of every conceivable physical regime. The corollary itself does not guarantee infinite curvature or one simultaneous beginning of all matter, energy, space, and time. Its direction and global interpretation require the appropriate cosmological application. Quantum completion is not provided.

These are not equal-weight competitors to the observed hot history. The distinction is between that history's strong support, its standard finite-age classical description, and an unconditional statement about all physical reality.

### An internal special-relativity countermodel

Flat spacetime with metric ds² = dt² − dx² − dy² − dz² and coordinates over all real values admits an inertial observer with x = y = z = 0 and proper time equal to t over the whole real line. Thus special relativity alone does not force a finite past. This is a counterexample to the unrestricted implication, not a proposed model of the actual universe or a rebuttal of BGV's conditional result.

Geometry and matter-energy dynamics are coupled in relativistic cosmology. That does not identify them as one substance or establish their simultaneous metaphysical creation. Finite duration need not contain a regular first instant; S80's boundary distinction remains relevant.

### Follow-up: the author identifies Borde–Guth–Vilenkin

**Arvind Borde, Alan H. Guth, and Alexander Vilenkin, “Inflationary spacetimes are not past-complete,” Phys. Rev. Lett. 90, 151301 (2003)**, [original text, arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/gr-qc/0110012), re-inspected §§I–IV, especially equations (8)–(11), on 2026-09-11.

- Premises: a classical spacetime admitting the relevant null or non-comoving timelike geodesic and a continuously defined congruence along it; a positive past-average expansion condition. Present expansion alone does not establish that condition over the entire past.
- Inference: the kinematically defined expansion is the derivative of a nonnegative function F of relative motion. Integrating gives integral H dτ ≤ F(γ_f), finite for the relevant reference observer. Consequently H_av Δτ ≤ F(γ_f): maintaining a strictly positive past average precludes unlimited past proper time (timelike) or affine length (null).
- Conclusion and positive role: such geodesics are past-incomplete; inflation satisfying the condition cannot by itself supply a past-complete cosmic history. This supports the author's appeal to a substantive past-boundary result and identifies C760's missing reference.
- Assumptions and limits: the argument does not require Einstein's field equations, homogeneity, isotropy, or energy conditions. Its use of local relativistic kinematics helps explain the special-relativity association, but special relativity alone is insufficient. It neither establishes a curvature divergence nor identifies the boundary with absolute creation of all physical reality; §IV explicitly leaves boundary physics to be supplied. No alternative boundary model is adopted here merely because one is possible.

## Non-participation without explicit rejection

S100's inference limit was valid but addressed a stronger question than the author's main point. Explicit communion requires participation; it need not be absent only because the person knowingly says no.

Distinguish:

- believing that God exists;
- seeking or consciously opening oneself to a relationship, possibly under uncertainty;
- presently participating in explicit communion;
- a settled will to refuse even under a different epistemic situation.

A believer can refuse. Someone who dismisses God's existence may not seek such a relationship at all, so present explicit non-participation needs no further explicit refusal. An agnostic can nevertheless express conditional openness. The selected intentional-content account already permits desire directed at a possibly existing referent without certainty that it exists.

Therefore the project accepts the author's practical point without equating absence of current uptake with hostile, informed, culpable, or eternal refusal. The author's expectation about “the vast majority” is recorded as an unverified population judgment; neither that percentage nor the rarity of counterexamples is established or needed by the ontology. No new sociology survey was undertaken.

## Privation is not automatically pain

The author's distinction follows from the separate anchors of C187–C189:

1. Non-realization of a possible greater good: an opportunity or enrichment is absent.
2. Privation of a due good: an evaluative misfit under C206.
3. Felt suffering: an occurrent negatively valenced experience.

The first does not by itself entail the second; neither entails the third without an additional psychophysical or evaluative bridge. Lower joy than an alternative is not equivalent to misery, and loss need not be consciously felt. The privative analysis of pain does not make every privation painful.

A diagnostic profile can retain pleasure, agency, and interpersonal recognition while lacking freely accepted communion with God. God remains the bearer’s ground and available addressee. The selected premises contain no implication from that missing communion to pain. This establishes non-entailment, not the actual existence of such a post-mortem condition, its complete goodness, or its permanence.

Accordingly the author's proposed hell-like condition may be less fulfilled without being torture, distress, or a total absence of good. C758's painful-eternity challenge is retained only conditionally if suffering is independently introduced; it is not an objection already instantiated by this proposal. Whether the missing good is due, and how that bears on divine goodness, remains a narrower evaluative question.

## Consequence for the continuing survival task

If a subject can continue with or without accepted communion, then the survival/identity bridge and the relation bridge must be evaluated separately. A lack of explicit faith cannot silently become a proof of personal extinction, nor can willingness to commune manufacture the identity link between an earthly and later bearer.

For the preferred re-realization route, three requirements remain:

- the later subject is this same individual under independently admissible token identity, rather than a matching replacement;
- that subject has a realization supporting further conscious life;
- its later exercise of relation remains its own.

This integration removes the hidden dependency “first choose God, then thereby establish who survives.” It does not solve the remaining primitive cross-gap identity question. The next comparison remains renewed realization versus uninterrupted survival of the bearer, without adding an immortal soul solely to label the gap.
