## Arguments and alternatives

### 1. Exact fit does not establish causal provenance

For a finite recorded sequence of discrete choices, indexed by decision episode, let the observed action at index i be a_i. Define q(a given i) to be 1 when a = a_i and 0 otherwise. This is a valid conditional probability representation of every recorded result. It stores the history; it neither predicts a new decision nor demonstrates that q produced the original decisions. No simplicity, explanatory compression, or generalization follows. Using the episode index avoids assuming that identical reduced descriptions of a situation always produce the same action.

Premises: the construction uses the actual sequence; the construction supplies no facts about the original causal process. Conclusion: perfect retrospective fit does not by itself establish causal provenance. A non-degenerate model that merely assigns positive probability to the history makes a weaker claim still: compatibility is not guaranteed reproduction.

The unrestricted statement that no probability function can represent human decision outcomes therefore fails in this finite, discrete, retrospective sense. Whether an independently specified model can predict or explain relevant decisions, with which inputs and at what level, is a different question. Predictability is not identical to antecedent causal determination; independently sufficient antecedent settlement would, however, defeat libertarian freedom for the token under C336.

### 2. Probability and conscious agency are different axes

A distribution states probabilities under conditions. It does not alone state whether there is a subject, what the subject experiences, or whether its apprehended reasons participate in settlement. A non-degenerate chance law may be part of a complete stochastic causal account without necessitating the individual outcome; non-necessitation is not itself agency. Conversely, lawful probabilities may describe tendencies of agents without replacing their exercise.

C55–C64 supply the positive model: a unified subject apprehends considerations and efficaciously originates an act for reasons. This is a substantive preferred ontology, not a deduction from fitting failure or indeterminism. A conscious subject may also choose to delegate a result to chance; the choice to use the procedure and its random result must be distinguished. Consciousness, randomness, and libertarian authorship cannot be treated as exhaustive opposing classes.

### 3. Description, causal explanation, and ultimate explanation

Retain C01's qualification: mathematical and probabilistic accounts can explain dependencies, mechanisms, distributions, and differences in likelihood when connected to the relevant system. They do not thereby explain why fundamental reality has that structure, and a distribution over outputs alone does not establish conscious sourcehood. Rejecting every probabilistic explanation would demand more than the author's central argument warrants and would misstate the existing framework.

### 4. Matching behavior and the metaphysical difference

Identical recorded behavior is weaker than identical responses in every possible interaction; either is weaker than identity of all physical and organizational properties. None may be silently substituted for another.

If two hypotheses assign identical likelihoods to every outcome of every admissible physical test, those tests cannot discriminate between them. This is conditional and methodological; it does not prove that a conscious and a non-conscious realization of that pair are metaphysically possible. Nor does it make the evidence generally irrelevant: weaker behavioral matching can still leave internal investigation and interventions discriminating.

For the preferred embodied model, the positive difference would concern bearer, phenomenal powers, and the role of the subject in producing the act. Holding the complete power-conferring organization and psychophysical principles fixed while varying consciousness requires a further argument. A causal difference can be conceptually distinguished without yet establishing a universally undetectable implementation. No impossibility or possibility theorem about physically identical non-conscious duplicates is adopted.

For the user's language-model example, reason conditionally: even if a device selects an output by a probability-based procedure, the procedure alone neither establishes nor excludes a conscious bearer. A claim of decision in its output is not independent evidence sufficient to establish authorship. No technical claim about a particular current model or decoding implementation is needed here.

### 5. Prospective prediction, foreknowledge, and actuality

Grant hypothetical perfect prospective accuracy, rather than defeating the example by returning to retrospective fit. Premise 1: accurate prediction or knowledge represents what the subject actually does. Premise 2: truth of that representation alone supplies neither a causal route from the representation to the act nor an independently sufficient antecedent cause of the act. Conclusion: even exact predictive success does not, by itself, establish causal determination or instantiate the subject's consciousness. Practical predictive success is compatible with the distinction and is not downgraded to mere useless description.

Three things must remain separate: the prediction's truth, why the predictor can know or predict, and what produces the action. Additional evidence about the latter two can matter greatly. A demonstrated antecedent mechanism sufficient independently of the subject would trigger C336; a correct prediction alone does not demonstrate such a mechanism. Communicating a prediction can influence the subject, but this needs that extra causal route and is not an entailment of knowing.

S02's inspected actual-marker account and C10–C14/C22–C25 provide the existing ACO integration. In each admissible history the content of divine knowledge tracks the actual act. If the actual act were different, the corresponding knowledge content would differ; the subject does not have to make true divine knowledge false to have alternatives. This varies the actual history and its knowledge together, rather than holding atemporal knowledge of A fixed while asking for B. The dependence is a truth-grounding relation, not a temporal message from the future. Knowing A implies that A is true; it does not by itself imply that A is necessary or that knowing causes A.

This is conditional on the preferred E+/ACO and sourcehood architecture. It neither demonstrates the possibility of a universally infallible temporal physical predictor nor restores rejected pre-actual truths selecting an act for every unrealized circumstance (C13). Statistical forecasting is not ACO; the relevant common point is representation versus causal production.

### 6. Unconscious decision and the necessary ground

C62 and C213–C218 already distinguish non-deliberative conscious action, unconscious contributions to one embodied episode, and wholly unconscious action. In an operational vocabulary, selection under information and control can be called a decision without settling conscious or libertarian agency. A wholly unconscious token is not a conscious decision by definition; whether it can be genuinely libertarian remains C218's open question, not a new priority.

The relation to C05 is comparative: chance and the preferred agent can both be non-necessitating. The agent hypothesis adds subjecthood, reasons, and authorship; it does not win merely because a probability formula is abstract, since the proper rival is a concrete impersonal reality with stochastic powers. The opponent may still argue that the agent's primitive exercise adds no explanatory content beyond chance. C460's preference and its standing burden remain; no proof of God or promotion of C05 follows.
