## Starting position and correction

The author explicitly promotes theoretical construction, objects to the carpenter analogy's ambiguous assignment of roles and external material, and retains the joint-experience discussion while judging its progress modest. Accept the correction. C660 remains the preferred principal creation hypothesis; C684's finite-form refinement remains proposed. The present development is also proposed, not author-endorsed merely because its construction was requested.

The precise inherited hypothesis is finite-form asymmetric non-depleting constitution: the same undivided G constitutes distinct dependent creatures; finite organization and powers belong to them; no created relatum constitutes the underived divine subject. It is neither the entire ex-deo family nor divine partition, transformation, or intrinsic creature-modes of G.

The intended analogy assigned BOTH originating and constituting roles to G. There is no third material alongside G. That mapping should have been explicit. Because ordinary carpentry additionally needs external material and transforms it, withdraw the analogy as an image of the creative operation. Its two-role distinction alone survives.

No pre-existing external material is required by the disjoint fallback either: G can be proposed to originate a new dependent concrete reality without being its constituent. The author's objection defeats the literal carpentry picture; absence of prior material alone does not select strict constitution.

## Positive proposal

Adopt provisionally, for examination, the following interpretation of finite-form ANDC:

1. The terminal concrete constituent of a creature is the numerically same G, not a newly created underlying stuff external to G. This concerns constitution, not just the ultimate cause of the constituent.
2. Creation nevertheless adds real individuals and actual finite determinations: organization, embodied relations, powers, and their manifestations. These are real additions to what exists. The proposal is not that nothing new is real.
3. Finite form is the actual organization/power-bearing determination of the creature. It is neither a detachable substance combined with G nor the abstract profile used to describe it. There is no independently existing empty creature into which a form is later inserted. The individual and its concrete organization exist together; no first temporal instant is assumed.
4. Powers belong to the resulting finite unity. A subject's pain and settlement are manifestations of that subject's powers. The common constitutive base is not itself assigned every predicate of every constituted bearer.

The new commitment under examination is **common terminal constitutive base plus real created determination without a separate underlying created substrate**. S82 gave the roles; this interpretation fixes a constitutive inventory and rejects a substrate in addition to G beneath each finite determination. Created bodies and their parts remain real and can constitute one another proximately. The proposal concerns where their constitutive explanation ultimately terminates; it does not eliminate physical structure.

“Form” cannot mean only external labeling or restriction of access. A view in which only G acts while creatures merely appear as viewpoints fails the inherited own-source requirement. Likewise, giving a description an index does not cause a new subject to exist.

## Local interpretation and its limits

Let B(x) name the terminal concrete constitutive base of a created bearer in this candidate. B is not a function for its producer, its immediate material parts, or its numerical identity. It is a substantive proposed constitutive relation summarized as a function for this bounded model; no claim that every ontology admits a unique such base is imported.

For distinct illustrative finite bearers a and b, the candidate allows:

```text
B(a) = G = B(b)
a != b; a != G; b != G
FiniteOrganization(a), FiniteOrganization(b)
Owns(a, e_a); Owns(b, e_b)
```

The same assignment does not license substituting a for b in ownership predicates. Absolute identity still has its ordinary substitution rule; equality of B-values is simply not equality of its arguments. Nor is constitution ordinary proper parthood. The model therefore owes a non-mereological constitution relation, and cannot borrow familiar composition rules selectively when they would make G a proper spatial part of each person.

In a contrasting disjoint interpretation, G originates both bearers and their created bases d_a and d_b, while B(a)=d_a, B(b)=d_b, and neither base is G or contains G as constituent. Both interpretations can include lifelong dependence and perfect divine knowledge. They disagree on a constitutive fact: whether their terminal constitutive analysis reaches the same concrete relatum or distinct created relata. Disjoint creation need not always give every person a different base; the two-base example is only one rival model. The decisive contrast is divine versus non-divine constitution, not the number of human bodies or independent ultimate causes.

This shows an intelligible truth-condition difference once a constitutive interpretation is granted. Merely assigning B-values is not evidence for that interpretation or a metaphysical construction of it. C686 is narrowed into a definite identity-of-base claim and a definite relational burden; it is not closed by the notation.

## Why posit this, and at what cost?

The argument is comparative and conditional:

- Given the preferred divine ground and the author's preference for real continuity, seek a constitutive account in which G is genuinely present as constituent.
- If finite individuals can be constituted from one undivided base through real finite determination, no separate underlying created stuff is needed to explain their concrete reality.
- This offers unity of constitutive explanation while retaining multiple created subjects. It provides a reason to develop this candidate, not a deduction from causality or divine agency.

The economy is specific: fewer distinct terminal constitutive bases, not fewer real individuals, fewer facts, or necessarily fewer primitives overall. The price is that non-mereological one-to-many constitution and power ownership are fundamental here. The disjoint rival can reasonably claim that producing distinct dependent realities is clearer and needs no shared-base bridge. An intrinsic-mode rival offers another interpretation, but would reopen C684's restriction on where finite states belong and the divine-power analysis; it is not silently substituted for ANDC.

Fundamentality need not make a relation empty. Its operands, constitution-versus-production role, allowed identity pattern, finite real additions, and constraints on power ownership can be specified. Nevertheless, these specifications are not yet a deeper account of why that relation is possible. If the author finds it unintelligible even after this interpretation, repeating “primitive” supplies no additional reason to prefer it.

## Adversarial tests

**Where is novelty located?** In the created bearer and its actually instantiated finite organization/powers, not in a new intrinsic state of G as the divine subject. The constitutive fact involves both G and the creature; it is relational for G and essential to the creature. Calling a relational fact real does not require making it an intrinsic divine state. Whether such a fact can do the constitutive work remains the principal metaphysical cost.

**Is this intrinsic finitude of G under another name?** No such inference is included. It would follow only from an additional rule transmitting every intrinsic predicate of the constituted to its base. The candidate rejects that rule and keeps ordinary numerical identity intact. Its burden is to justify constitution without that transmission, not to derive an arbitrary contradiction and dismiss it.

**Is form an external material?** A real created determination is not pre-existing stuff for God to acquire. It adds facts and real finite bearers, so “everything is simply the unmodified G with no real addition at all” is not this account. Form is not a second independently existing ingredient. The shared base by itself is insufficient for a particular creature.

**Does creation script choices?** No. Organization and powers fix capacities and admissible developments, not a unique selected act. G's constituting a source and the source's settling an act remain different contributions. This uses C685 rather than deriving libertarian freedom from shared constitution.

**Does shared base yield shared experience?** No pooling rule follows from equality of bases. In the example, e_a can be pain and e_b a color experience with different owners. A joint field requires an actual unified bearer/episode beyond common constitution. This is a consistency constraint inherited from S105, not a new solution of phenomenal unity. Ownership remains primitive at this point; no subject-counting theorem is claimed.

**Does the eternal base preserve the person?** A stable base does not by itself preserve or restore the finite individual it constitutes. Erasing the finite determination while retaining G does not leave the whole person. B(a)=B(b) cannot decide which, if either, is a continuation. CH22's cross-gap burden remains.
