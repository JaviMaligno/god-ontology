## Internal argument and source disclosure

C230 posits actualization of a bearer of powers; C232–C233 distinguish that explanatory stopping point from a demonstration of its possibility. The argument used is:

1. Both candidates assert that actual finite beings, with their own natures and powers, depend on G.
2. The shared G alone does not determine which finite individuals or powers exist; a merely repeated label or divine description is not their actuality.
3. Shared constitution consequently also needs effective origination of finite determination. That need is not removed by denying a separate created substrate.
4. Therefore the productive novelty burden can be developed once, without initially deciding whether G is also the creature's constituent.

These internal sources support unifying the research task. They do not supply a completed creative mechanism or demonstrate that either complete interpretation is true. The inherited assumptions are real creation, distinct finite bearers, own powers, divine dependence and agency. No external source, empirical result or historical authority does inferential work; novelty is unassessed.

## Positive common content

The proposed common explanation is: G can make an actual finite order exist, containing individuals whose organization and powers are genuinely theirs. For a subject, its capacities can be exercised by that subject; constituting those capacities does not settle all its later acts. For non-conscious creation the same general task concerns the appropriate finite organization and powers, without adding subjectivity universally.

This actualization concerns the individual with its instantiated determination together. It does not first posit an empty individual and then add a description to it. Nor does finitude require taking an already omnipotent creature and subtracting powers: what is actualized is a finite power profile from the outset, with no privileged temporal first instant implied. This locates the finite character in the real effect. It does not yet derive why that effect is possible.

Specification, effective actuality, and the resulting bearer are explanatory roles, not a claim of three separate acts. The divine ground, secondary causes and finite agent contributions keep their inherited distinct roles; no fresh direct divine action for each derivative thing is introduced. Knowledge of a possible profile alone cannot do the productive work. A fundamental productive capacity remains the current stopping point and the common object of investigation.

“Substance” must not switch senses unnoticed: the same constitutive base is compatible with distinct individual bearers in the ANDC proposal, whereas numerical identity with G would erase the created other. “Nature” here names the finite kind and powers to be explained; it is not an extra detachable ingredient. This brief typing keeps the common task intact without making terminology decide the fork.

## What a shared justification transfers

Let E abbreviate only the common claims of actual finite novelty, divine dependence, preservation of G and created powers. Let S assert the disputed constitutive sharing. Schematically the candidates contain E and S, or E and not-S, together with their further constraints.

An argument for E whose premises do not depend on S can contribute to both candidates. It does not thereby establish E-and-S or E-and-not-S. An argument that essentially invokes shared constitution likewise cannot be transferred unchanged to the disjoint reading. Shared difficulty is not a theorem that every successful solution transfers.

This is a dependency discipline, not a new generative mechanism, complete theory definition or proof of global equivalence. Work on the common explanation has priority while interpretation-specific support remains insufficient. The fork reopens when a proposed explanation requires or excludes S, or when an independently motivated consequence separates the interpretations.

## Correct the economy comparison

S107 described a local relational-economy advantage for disjointness. Qualify that claim. More predicates in a description do not by themselves mean more fundamental operations, powers or independently posited relations. A single creative activity could have productive and constitutive descriptions. Conversely, declaring both descriptions to concern one activity does not itself reduce the constitution relation.

An extra cost must be shown as an irreducible commitment and compared with the alternative's actual explanation. Until then, neither counting “creation” plus “constitution” nor merging their labels establishes the economy verdict. The precise shared-constituent claim stays open; its unresolved burden no longer mandates a duplicate research programme.
