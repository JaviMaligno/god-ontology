## Positive content before qualifications

The intended claim is that G itself supplies the constitutive reality of finite creatures as well as their originating ground. The question is not only who brings a creature about, but what reality is constitutively present in the creature. The same underived G is the proposed answer; it is not merely a second being displaying the same qualities.

An ordinary wood/object or clay/statue analogy can make the role intelligible: a maker produces an object, while the wood or clay is present as what constitutes it. The positive feature to retain is that constitutive role, not just a remote producing action. This is an explanatory analogy, not evidence that G is material or that ordinary material constitution instantiates ANDC. Division, reconfiguration, and ordinary mereology belong to the familiar example; a non-dividing, non-depleting divine analogue still needs its own account.

This specifies the proposed shared relatum as G itself, not an abstract property called existence or an unspecified similarity. It does not establish the coherence or actuality of a relation in which the undivided G constitutes multiple finite subjects while their intrinsic powers remain their own. S104 was entitled to report the preference but not to imply that naming the shared relatum explained the relation.

## Why three proposed tests do not settle the difference

Let D stand for actual creaturely dependence and production by G. Let S stand for the additional real common-constituent relation intended by strict ex deo. The stronger model asserts D and S. A theory that accepts D but denies S remains the existing constitutively disjoint fallback.

1. **Dependence throughout existence:** a creature's every stage may depend on G even in the disjoint model. Hence this consequence does not discriminate S. This does not require a new direct divine act at every moment; S41's sparse-action account remains intact.
2. **No creature without G:** essential dependence can be admitted without shared constitution. Moreover, G's necessity makes a simple absence-of-G counterfactual a poor test without a specified counterpossible semantics. No such semantics is introduced.
3. **Resemblance or capacities originating in G:** a cause can produce a distinct bearer with its own powers on the fallback account. Finite agentive resemblance therefore does not by itself prove S.

Similarly, perfect divine knowledge is compatible with both models. Non-replaceability can be a desired constituent constraint, but under sole ultimate grounding it does not alone distinguish a constituent from an essential ground.

The result does not refute S or erase C660's authorial rationale. It identifies which familiar consequences cannot establish the stronger claim. A useful next account must give positive content to the shared constitutive relation itself or identify a consequence grounded in that content, rather than merely appending S to D. The present pass does not select divine modes, transferable parts, or a new substance ontology to force closure.

## One experience versus coordination: primary source

**Tim Bayne and David J. Chalmers, “What is the Unity of Consciousness?” (2003)**, published in A. Cleeremans (ed.), *The Unity of Consciousness: Binding, Integration, Dissociation*. Inspected the [authors' text](https://consc.net/papers/unity.html), §§1–3, especially §2's subject/subsumptive unity distinction and §3's joint-access/joint-experience analysis.

- Premises: jointly available contents are characterized by access for report, inference, and control; a jointly lived state is itself phenomenal and includes the relevant experiences as aspects. Merely assigning states to the same subject gives a trivial sense of unity.
- Inference: a conjunction of contents or states does not establish, by definition alone, a conscious whole that experiences them together.
- Conclusion used: joint access and phenomenal unity are different explanatory targets. This supplies positive content to C780's question instead of restating “one subject” as the answer.
- Imported assumption: there are phenomenal states and meaningful contrasts in what is jointly lived. These fit the project's C134/C337.
- Declined imports: their universal necessary Unity Thesis, detailed neurological interpretations, a physical implementation criterion, or a deduction of diachronic subject identity. §§2–3 analyze synchronic unity; they do not solve survival or divine constitution.
- Assessment: adopt the local distinction as a useful explanatory constraint. It is a conceptual contribution, not empirical proof that a group can or cannot be conscious.

## Internal extension: communication does not transfer phenomenal ownership

Consider one subject who jointly hears a note and feels warmth. In the stipulated unified case there is an actual experience of both together. Now consider two subjects, each with one experience, exchanging fully accurate reports. Each may know both contents while the original episodes remain separately undergone. Adding an accurate representation of the other's state is not, without an additional bridge, adding the other's actual episode to a common lived whole.

This is a non-entailment construction, not a claim that no conceivable connection could yield joint experience. Increased communication may matter to realization; a new joint phenomenal state would be an additional fact to establish. If a new group-level bearer existed, numerical identity alone prevents declaring two still-distinct original persons both identical with it. Coexistence, termination, and replacement require separate accounts; group behavior does not settle them.

Represent the local target as:

JointPhen(e1,e2) iff some actual phenomenal episode E includes e1 and e2 as jointly lived aspects.

This formulation fixes the phenomenon to explain. It does not produce E from a network diagram or reduce ownership to an information-sharing measure. Pairwise links are not automatically closed into one universal field. A temporally ordered succession is also not the same as one simultaneous experience, and absence of an occurrent field does not establish annihilation of a subject.

Applied to G, C284–C287 permit exact acquaintance with another's experience without self-ascribing the other's episode. Sharing a constitutive ground likewise supplies no demonstrated rule for pooling experiential ownership. Thus the coordination branch clarifies what distinct finite subjects would require under ANDC while leaving the constitutive relation itself unsettled.
