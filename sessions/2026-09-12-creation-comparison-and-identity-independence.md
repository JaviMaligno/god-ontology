## Universal scope and three different meanings of “divine”

Let C be the concrete created domain admitted by the ontology, not abstract truths, fictional referents, or uninstantiated possibilities. Let S(G,x) mean genuine divine constitution. The universal candidate asserts:

```text
For every x in C: Depends(x,G) and S(G,x).
```

The disjoint candidate asserts dependence for every x in C and denies S(G,x). Universal scope does not require every object to have an identical proximate composition: bodies, organisms, and their parts can have different created organization while the ultimate constitutive claim has one scope.

Premises: if a feature follows from the general creation relation, every member satisfying that relation has it; consciousness is not among the premises of that general relation. Therefore restricting this constitution to conscious subjects requires an additional creation rule and an independently justified selection criterion. It cannot be obtained by appealing again to the common creation relation. A hybrid remains possible to investigate, but is unmotivated here and adds a boundary burden.

Distinguish:

| Sense | Scope | Does it have degrees here? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Divine origin/dependence | Every concrete creature in both models | Not a quantity of deity |
| G as actual constituent | Every concrete creature conditionally under universal ANDC; none under the disjoint candidate | No supplied scalar quantity: the same undivided G is the claimed relatum |
| Resemblance in a specified capacity | Only bearers actually having that capacity | Differences can concern particular powers; no single scale of divinity or moral worth is derived |

Freely received personal communion is another relation, not a measure of constituent quantity. Human or other subject-level agency can be a specific resemblance within either creation model. Shared constitution does not entail universal consciousness without a further premise transferring that predicate. This non-entailment does not explain why or when consciousness exists, which remains a shared realization burden. Nothing here establishes which non-human beings are conscious.

## Identity: positive ordinary account and bounded independence

Parallel read-only analysis examined the existing identity ledger and sessions; the following is an internal synthesis, not a new externally sourced theory.

Ordinary persistence has a positive causal explanation in the inherited organismic account: an actual living unity maintains and transforms its own organization. In reproduction or copying, information or components from one unity contribute to bringing another into operation. These can both be causal processes; what differs is whether the processes maintain the existing organized life or originate another. This is not a reductive criterion for every imaginable case. The unity boundary remains substantive, and no functional description alone proves consciousness.

For the comparative argument, hold fixed finite organization, origin, powers, episode ownership, and causal-organizational continuity in two candidate interpretations. Vary only whether the ultimate constitutive relation includes G. The current identity rules appeal to those fixed finite facts and fundamental identity, while C790 already denies identity transfer from a common base. Therefore the available identity reasons do not change solely with the base choice. This is a bounded independence result, not a proof of universal translation or full metaphysical equivalence.

Stress tests in both interpretations:

- Maintaining the organism through material or autobiographical change can preserve its career; exact components and complete memories are unnecessary under the inherited view.
- An original and a functioning duplicate are distinct units. Removing the original does not, merely by removing competition, turn reproduction into its continuation.
- In fission, identity with both distinct successors would identify the successors by transitivity. Selecting one or neither needs a further basis; fission is not evidence that numerical identity branches.
- An interval without experiences can preserve the bearer. A genuine interval without the bearer requires the additional cross-gap admissibility already distinguished in S102.

A concrete potential discriminator is an independently justified finite support exclusive to the subject that preserves its unity beyond its current bodily realization. ANDC might propose a persistent finite determination in relation to G; disjoint creation might propose a persisting created support. Either must establish its reality, exclusivity, efficacy and fit with one embodied subject. Naming a stored form or remembered description does not do this.

If that support preserves the subject throughout, it explains uninterrupted persistence, not a genuine interval of nonexistence. This prevents the same proposal from being credited for two different survival routes. No such support is adopted or inferred here. A stronger identity model could favor a creation candidate if derived from its independently supported resources at lower added cost.
