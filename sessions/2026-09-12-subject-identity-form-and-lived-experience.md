# Session: Subject, identity, form, and lived experience

**Date:** 2026-09-12  
**Session ID:** S103  
**Primary question:** What do subject, identity, and form mean, and how can experience shape someone without constituting a transferable identity?  
**Dependencies:** the accepted one embodied power-bearing subject; numerical identity and the limits of persistence/recognition criteria.  
**Starting ledger:** C134–C138, C297–C300, C310–C315, C330–C334, C435–C450, C506–C519, C680–C685, C765–C770.  
**Depth:** F1 definitions with bounded F2 distinctions and counterexamples. The deeper constitution and survival bridge remains open.

## Author direction and current preference

The author explicitly promotes definition of identity and form as the prerequisite of further survival work. He observes that much of his own experience is no longer remembered, distinguishes experience from its effect on identity, and offers a tentative body/conscious agency/experience analogy with Son/Father/Spirit. He explicitly declines to force that analogy and permits other useful approaches.

Retain S102 at its proposed status under U18. Accept the direction and the scope of the autobiographical observation, without turning “memories do not determine who you are” into the stronger claim that knowledge or memory never shapes the person. The analogy is recorded as non-inferential author imagery; it neither establishes a three-part subject nor imports a doctrine of the Trinity. No trinitarian claim is assessed here.

The preferred positive account remains one concrete embodied individual with a real unifying organization and powers. Its experiences are its states or processes; its free acts, when present, are exercises of the whole subject. No detachable identity-object is added. The present correction is that saying “identity is fundamental” does not finish defining the other terms or explaining the individual's persistence conditions.

No definition alone proves that an entity instantiates it, that a bodily organization realizes phenomenality, or that a human bearer can survive death. Logical identity being primitive also does not prove C436's substantive metaphysical non-reduction thesis. The project retains that preferred thesis on its existing arguments; it does not derive it from the notation x=x or use the logical rules to prove an immortal simple substance.

## Experience, memory, and self-formation

The author's forgetting observation supports a bounded argument:

1. In the ordinary same-person history under discussion, some experiences are no longer recalled.
2. Therefore retaining every memory is not necessary to count that life as the same individual's.
3. Under C441/C768, copying a memory profile is not sufficient either.

The first premise uses the author's ordinary reidentification of himself, not a memory-independent proof of all diachronic identity. Forgetting alone does not refute every sophisticated continuity theory and says nothing about the empirical incidence of total memory loss. It does explain why an inventory of remembered contents cannot be our definition.

Three different things can obtain: an episode occurred; it contributed to later dispositions; it is now recalled. In the conceptual model a past experience may influence a disposition without being recalled, may be recalled without controlling present action, or may belong to the history without a currently detectable trace. These are diagnostic possibilities, not a surveyed empirical generalization that all experiences permanently alter the subject.

The positive thesis is that experience and choice can partly form the individual's changing way of being. C300 already gives earlier acts a role in later governing states. Their history belongs to the subject; the subject need not be reduced to a list of those acts or an archived inventory of episodes. “Shapes identity” is apt for the biographical profile, not for manufacturing a new numerical individual after every change.

“Not a component” needs the same care. An episode is genuinely something occurring in the concrete subject's life, and may partly constitute its state at that time. The proposal excludes treating it as a separable identity-carrying part. It does not remove experience from the subject's reality or make it merely an external description.

## One subject, differentiated roles

Body, consciousness, agency, and experience are not four items of one logical type. Embodiment concerns the concrete subject's realization and constitution; conscious capacity concerns what it can undergo; agency concerns its role in originating acts; experience concerns an actual exercise or state. Experience and consciousness overlap rather than automatically furnishing two independent constituents.

The author's threefold image can be retained as embodiment, conscious/agentive capacities, and lived formation. This is a heuristic view of one subject, not three agents or a discovery of three substances. A relation to G belongs to the person; no God-facing inner part is inferred. Consciousness alone does not establish libertarian freedom, and relation to G does not individuate a subject from all others who share that relation.

## What form explains, and what it does not

Form is explanatory when it states how the concrete organization integrates constituents and powers into a living whole, and which changes preserve or destroy the relevant unity. The kind profile, the actual organization at a time, and the repeatable description of that organization must be distinguished.

Let Q_x(t) represent the current qualitative/biographical state, U_x(t) the actual unifying organization, and K_x the independently warranted kind-essential constraints. These symbols represent different questions, not three extra entities. Learning may change Q_x(t), and may change aspects of U_x(t), without settling that x has ceased. K_x must not be defined as every feature the individual currently has, nor may its survival conditions be derived merely from wanting a post-mortem continuation.

A reproduction of the same organizational description in y does not establish that the actual bearer x continues. Conversely, differences in present organization need not imply replacement if they fall within the bearer's admissible changes. The positive preferred ordinary account uses one integrated life, its actual token origin and an immanent non-branching career. Calling these “x's” records the ultimate identity relation; it does not reduce that relation to resemblance.

## Bounded tests and progress on survival

### Change without logical contradiction

The conjunction “x is fearful at t1” and “x is not fearful at t2” does not contradict numerical identity: the predicates differ in temporal qualification. Substitutivity applies in the same respect. Identity does not require a frozen character or one unchanging list of states. These logical rules apply to any bearer, not specially to conscious agents; different kinds can nevertheless have different persistence profiles.

### Minimum profile and limits

An adequate continuation proposal must state a concrete finite bearer, the unity of its own states/powers, and its origin-compatible non-branching career. When further conscious life occurs, it must include actual first-personally undergone episodes. This is a typed adequacy profile with irreducible phenomenal and identity clauses, not a sufficient observable algorithm that turns matching structure into the same subject.

Removing a particular memory or changing character does not by itself remove the bearer on the selected view. Removing occurrent experience need not do so either (C315). Loss of the current bodily organization requires further analysis; loss of every specified finite bearer and all its unity/powers leaves only an unfilled role, not an explained survivor. Neither the bare-role objection nor this profile proves that all alternative realization is impossible.

This sharpens S102: the next test should ask whether a proposed transformation conserves a finite subject's unity or creates a new unity with matching features. Primitive identity and form both remain useful, but neither can serve as a verbal certificate of survival. The existing preferred gappy possibility is retained; no full criterion, actual continuation, or temporal-connectedness requirement is inferred.

### Recognition is not identity production

The author requires a later subject to be ontologically the same, not merely persuasive or similar. Take “recognizable as the same” in that ontological sense without adding that an earthly observer must have an infallible identification procedure.

P40 contained an outdated sentence that made divine acquaintance guarantee the success of re-realization. C447/S100–S102 already reject that inference. The revised answer states that divine knowledge tracks the identity fact and the admissibility of continuing x must be established independently. This is repair of an inconsistency with existing commitments, not a new demotion of ASA or the continuation preference.
