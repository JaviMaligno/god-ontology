Premises: UPA states God's ability to recognize, receive an address and respond, not that every person can recognize an actual invitation. Reciprocal relation requires a creature's own contribution. Supplying information or reasons need not install evaluative standards or settle the response. A person can fail to participate without an informed refusal, and a missing relation need not be felt as pain.

Conclusions: (a) distinguish ontological presence, divine availability, recognizable access from the person's side, and actual reciprocal participation; (b) clarity of existence is not identical to imposed communion; (c) neither nonbelief nor absence of feeling establishes culpable resistance; (d) the relational problem can arise independently of painful absence.

Role: these are positive distinctions and a correction of P57/P35, not a complete explanation of the observed distribution of belief. Imported: the preferred agent/knowledge/sourcehood framework, not demonstrated divine perfection or an empirical estimate of nonresistant nonbelief. Assessment: retained structural explanation with an open practical-access burden. The asymmetry result means a finite person need not comprehend God exhaustively to receive something genuine.

## Primary source 1: Schellenberg's challenge

**Exact source:** J. L. Schellenberg, “Divine Hiddenness: Part 1 (Recent Work on the Hiddenness Argument),” author's manuscript for *Philosophy Compass* 12 (2017), DOI 10.1111/phc3.12355, [author-hosted PDF](https://www.jlschellenberg.com/uploads/8/5/6/1/8561683/divine_hiddenness--part_1.pdf). Inspected §I, manuscript pp. 1–3; the explicit/implicit relation discussion on pp. 5–6; and §IV's clarification of variable experience and openness, pp. 7–9. The page identifies itself as a manuscript preceding the corrected published version.

**Reconstructed argument:**
1. Perfect divine love entails continuing openness to personal relationship with every finite person capable of it.
2. Such openness excludes a capable person's nonresistant lack of belief in God's existence, because that person cannot consciously reciprocate toward God as God while lacking the relevant recognition.
3. Some people are nonbelievers without resisting such relationship.
4. Therefore no perfectly loving God exists; exclusion of God without qualification additionally assumes that God must be perfectly loving.

The target is an opportunity for conscious reciprocal relation, not constant intense feeling, a spectacular public sign or already completed communion. Variation in experience within a relationship is compatible with the proposed openness.

**Role:** C924 introduces a serious objection to claims of always accessible loving relationship and limits the appeal to freedom in P57. Assumptions imported conditionally are perfect love, the openness/recognition bridge, relevant capacity, and nonresistant nonbelief. The project does not assert that this paper independently demonstrates all these premises, import its ultimate-person definition as our starting definition, or infer the impossibility of every necessary agent.

**Assessment and live replies:** our UPA is weaker than the disputed universal recognizable-access claim, so the contradiction does not follow from UPA alone. But replacing practical accessibility with mere power to communicate does not answer the challenge to the project's preferred recognition/participant orientation. The belief requirement and absolute always-openness premise can be debated; tentative or implicit engagement may count as some relation. Even then, whether a capable willing person has adequate access remains a real question. No diagnosis of every nonbeliever as resistant is adopted. The article's secondary reports of other authors and empirical studies are not independently used as evidence here.

## Primary source 2: van Inwagen's proposed direction

**Exact source:** Peter van Inwagen, “What Is the Problem of the Hiddenness of God?”, in Daniel Howard-Snyder and Paul K. Moser (eds.), *Divine Hiddenness: New Essays* (Cambridge University Press, 2002), pp. 24–32; [chapter scan](https://www.andrewmbailey.com/pvi/Hiddenness.pdf). Inspected the distinction between general presence and special evidence in the dialogue, pp. 25–29, and the defense/theodicy distinction and concluding advice, pp. 30–32.

**Reconstructed argument:**
1. Wanting a person to believe does not entail indifference to why or how that belief forms.
2. God might also value features of that person's response or character which a particular route to belief would undermine.
3. Therefore a divine preference for belief is compatible with withholding some particular signs; a defense can investigate such reasons without claiming to know the actual divine explanation.

**Role:** C925 supplies a conditional candidate for differentiated disclosure, not a solution to every case of nonresistant nonbelief. Import the possibility of multiple relevant ends and reason-sensitive formation; decline to import a demonstrated ranking of any actual person's unbelief over belief, a theological salvation account, or a proof that clearer recognition generally undermines free response.

**Assessment and limits:** van Inwagen explicitly does not construct a complete defense or theodicy in this essay. His point bears most directly on particular methods of producing belief, not on the absence of any adequate recognizable route. A project explanation must therefore distinguish what is withheld, from whom, why it matters, and why another less controlling disclosure would not secure the same good. A speculative compatible story is weaker than an explanation of the actual distribution.

The two sources do not establish a settled literary solution. They expose the challenge and one bounded response strategy. No novelty claim or exhaustive literature review is made.

## Constructive assessment within this framework

The positive proposal is **recognizable invitation with preserved authorship**. A creator who values finite others and truthful relation has, within those preferred premises, a reason to enable them to recognize whom they may address. The person's response must remain their own. Those ends can align: information can make a choice more informed without fixing which choice is made.

This is a proposed defeasible reason, not a theorem that every capable person must always believe. The actual policy of revelation, its timing and its evidential forms are not supplied by the current model. Universal good-orientation and no overriding reason would require further support.

The proposed human role of understanding does not repair the gap by itself. Knowledge of God's existence need not eliminate inquiry into creation, moral development, doubt about other matters or free relationships. An open hypothesis about a mission cannot establish that ignorance of the creator is a necessary condition of that mission.

Nor do shared divine constitution or disjoint creation settle access. Common grounding and even common constitution do not themselves supply an epistemically recognizable address to an individual. This conclusion transfers across the two open creation interpretations because it does not rely on their disputed constituent relation.

The framework's contribution is thus a precise joint burden: preserve the subject's authorship **and** explain the availability or absence of a usable relational opportunity. Its preferred recognition of finite subjects makes hiddenness more pressing than it would be for an indifferent necessary cause. Avoid invoking the absence of proven perfection as a complete escape from that comparative pressure.

## Bounded formal checks

Use local predicates:
- O(G,x): ontological presence/dependence.
- A(G,x): the UPA capacity for direct recognition and address.
- E(G,x,t): an adequate recognizable opportunity for the capable finite subject at t.
- R(x,G,t): actual reciprocal participation.
- N(x,t): the subject is not resisting such participation.

A local interpretation with O and A true and E/R false satisfies the minimal presence/capacity claims. Thus O or A does not alone establish E. Another interpretation with E true and R false captures recognized invitation without accepted communion. The inference E -> R therefore requires a further premise absent from our definitions. These are bounded vocabulary checks, not proofs that every stipulated complete history is metaphysically admissible.

The stronger hiddenness challenge concerns a bridge like perfect love plus capacity plus N -> E, together with actual failures of E. Our preferred relational reasons favor E conditionally but have not established that universal bridge or an exhaustive defeating-reason account. This is why the result remains partial.

A manifestation that independently settles the response would violate sourcehood, whatever its emotional intensity. An intensely felt experience need not independently settle it, and a clear belief about existence does not entail imposed love, trust or obedience. Concrete mechanisms of influence still require assessment; clarity alone is not the measure.

## Correction of C470 and adjacent answer

“Absence of experience refutes nothing in the model” is too broad if read as an evidential immunity. Retain the narrower result: absence of a felt episode alone does not deductively refute ontological presence or a communication capacity. It may count against further claims predicting accessible self-disclosure, and persistent nonresistant nonbelief can challenge the relational profile. C470 is refined visibly, not discarded as if its original capacity/exercise distinction were false.

P35's inference from no feeling to no relation is also too strong. Absence of a particular felt manifestation does not establish the absence of every possible relational contribution. Correct it without explaining all religious experience psychologically or treating anyone's lack of feeling as blameworthy.
