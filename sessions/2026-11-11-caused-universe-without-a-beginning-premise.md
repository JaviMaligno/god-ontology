## Arguments

### A. Four arguments that do not use a beginning

1. **Contingency.** The universe could have failed to exist or been otherwise. What could have been otherwise does not account for itself. So its account lies in something that could not have failed to exist. An eternal universe would be just as contingent.
2. **Present dependence.** Things have their existence and powers on loan now, not only at a first moment. A chain in which every member only passes on what it receives needs a member that has it of itself, however long the chain. This is a vertical relation, not a sequence in time; it matches the project's reading of creation as dependence (C586).
3. **Whole and parts.** Explaining each state by an earlier one does not explain why there is the series at all, or this series. An infinite past changes nothing.
4. **Consistency of explanation.** Nowhere else is “it just is” accepted while an explanation is available; inquiry treats brute fact as a last resort. Exempting the universe alone is special pleading, and a general licence for brute facts would undercut science. This is the same parity as C949: the reasoning that sustains science and moral truth also sustains asking for the universe's cause.

### B. Why the universe is not the uncaused first cause

The author's exception in S130 was a universe that is itself the uncaused first cause. An uncaused terminus must be necessary: it could not have been absent or otherwise. The universe shows the marks of the opposite. It changes, it is composed, and its laws and constants have particular values that could have been different, as the author argued earlier in the project. A necessary being with arbitrary particulars would need the very explanation it was introduced to end.

### C. Why the cause is a mind

By the necessitation lemma (C458) a necessary ground cannot necessitate a contingent world, or the world would be necessary too. So the world came from it in a way that could have gone otherwise, and the candidates are chance or choice (C459). Choice explains why this world and for what reasons (C460). The moral route of S130 reaches a mind independently (C952), and so does the trust in reason. These are separate lines that agree.

### D. The beginning as a further, separate line

Retained from S100–S101: expansion, relic radiation and primordial abundances give the standard model a finite age (C759); Borde–Guth–Vilenkin bounds the past of an on-average expanding spacetime (C760). The literature adds two philosophical arguments: that an actual infinite of past events cannot exist, and that a series formed by adding one event after another cannot complete an infinity. C752's scope statement is unchanged. The beginning strengthens the case and is not its foundation.
