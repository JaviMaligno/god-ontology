## Author position

The author does not in general accept macroevolution. He holds that there are many arguments against it, that it relies on patches which depend on philosophical stance and not on evidence, and that it is mainstream only because naturalism is the mainstream philosophical position.

## Definitions

| Term | Definition used in this session |
| --- | --- |
| $D$ descent | All living things are related by common ancestry. A claim about pattern. |
| $M$ mechanism | Mutation and natural selection, extrapolated from change within populations, suffice to produce new organs, body plans and higher groups. A claim about cause. |
| $N$ unguidedness | The process had no purpose or direction from a mind. A metaphysical claim. |

“Macroevolution” in dispute usually bundles the three. The inspected critical sources aim almost entirely at $M$ and $N$.

## Findings

### 1. Unguidedness is philosophy, not a result

Methodological naturalism is a rule about what science may invoke; by its own definition it says nothing on whether anything beyond nature exists. “Random” in biology means that mutations do not arise in response to the organism's needs, not that the process is undirected. Calling the history of life unguided is therefore an addition from metaphysical naturalism. On this point the author's claim holds without qualification, and it is the point the project needs (C732, C949).

### 2. Excluding design by rule is also a matter of method

Whether a design inference counts as science is disputed, and even if it does not, that would not show the inference defective: science need not exhaust legitimate conclusions from empirical data. The design argument is an inference to the best explanation of the same kind the project uses elsewhere.

### 3. The sufficiency of the mechanism is disputed inside biology

- Darwin stated the tests himself: his theory “would absolutely break down” if a complex organ could not have been formed by numerous slight modifications; the absence of finely graded intermediates is “the most obvious and serious objection”; and the sudden appearance of animal groups in the lowest fossil strata is a “much more serious” difficulty, which he met by appeal to the imperfection of the record.
- Paleontologists since the 1970s have rejected “extrapolationism”, the view that large-scale evolution is nothing but accumulated small-scale change; stasis and rapid change are treated as real features of the record and not as gaps.
- Müller, in the Royal Society's journal, argues that the standard theory bypasses the origin of phenotypic novelty and complexity and that the usual reply, that small-scale mechanisms are consistent with large-scale phenomena, has little to do with the actual problem. Futuyma replies in the same issue that the principal tenets stand and that natural selection remains the only known cause of adaptation; he grants that the synthesis lacked a theory of the origin of phenotypic novelty.
- Critics outside the mainstream press the same point further: Behe's irreducible complexity; Meyer's argument from the Cambrian appearance of body plans and the rarity of functional protein sequences; a public statement by several hundred scientists sceptical that mutation and selection account for the complexity of life.

So $M$ is not a closed question, and the author is right that confidence in it outruns the evidence. Two limits are stated to him: the mainstream revisers such as Müller propose further natural mechanisms and give no support to design; and the mainstream verdict on the design critics' specific claims is strongly negative.

### 4. Descent is where author and assistant differ

The inspected critics do not chiefly attack $D$. Behe lists common descent among the operative factors and holds that design of some systems does not exclude them; Meyer's paper speaks of the origin of form “during the evolution of life” and targets the mechanism; the public statement concerns mutation and selection only. The assistant's assessment, labelled as such, is that $D$ is well supported by independent lines of evidence and is not held merely because of naturalism, since theists and design proponents accept it. The evidence for $D$ has not yet been examined in this project beyond an encyclopedia summary, so this remains the assistant's standing assessment and not an inspected result. The author's general non-acceptance is recorded. The disagreement is left open and is not load-bearing.

### 5. The author's claim about the mainstream

Correct for $N$ and for the exclusion of design by rule. Supported in part for $M$, where the standard account is defended as the only naturalistic candidate. Not shown for $D$.

## Author follow-up: what common descent rests on

The author can in some sense agree with common descent as common design by one designer plus microevolution, and objects that if it rests only on genetic or physiological similarity it is open to the pan-and-pot reply: similar things need not share an ancestor.

Result shown to the author:

1. **The objection holds against similarity (C970).** It answers the arguments from overall likeness, the tree-building assumption that similarity reflects relatedness, and the step in the chromosome 2 argument that identifies the fused chromosomes with ape chromosomes. The fusion itself shows only that human ancestors once had one more chromosome pair.
2. **The discriminating evidence is of another kind (C971):** the same apparently functionless marks at the same positions in different species, as the same misprint in two books indicates copying. Its force depends on the marks being functionless and their position not being otherwise determined; both premises are disputed, and the functionless premise has been eroding.
3. **Descent and design are compatible (C972).** The remaining question is separate making versus making by modification.

The assistant's standing assessment that the shared-mark evidence favours descent is kept and labelled; it is no longer stated as resting on “independent lines” without qualification, since several of those lines reduce to similarity.

## Closing pass on the two premises of C971

At the author's request the literature on insertion position and on function was read.

1. **Position (C973).** The premise holds. Integration preferences are regional and virus-specific. A natural test exists: one retrovirus entered the chimpanzee and gorilla lineages independently, and its insertions fall at different places in at least 95.8% of 287 mapped cases; the overlapping regions resolved to the base pair were distinct insertions.
2. **Function (C974).** The premise fails as a general claim. Some inserts are regulatory, syncytins are essential, and the functional fraction of the genome is unsettled. The critics' charge that arguments from uselessness were overstated is sustained. The argument for descent survives without the premise, on integration scars and graded decay.
3. **Assessment (C975).** Shared inserts favour modification over separate making for primates including humans. Universal common descent is not established by this evidence. The author's decision is pending and nothing in the project depends on it.
