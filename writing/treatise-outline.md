# Proposed Treatise Outline

Working language: English. Research notes may remain bilingual. The outline deliberately separates the original motivation from the ontology that later emerged.

## Preface: Scope and epistemic posture

- A constructive ontology rather than a proof packaged in advance
- Personal commitment, rational inquiry, and the role of faith
- Why novelty is not the governing aim

## Part I — Motivation: Description, explanation, and the limits of science

1. **When Explanation Is Rich Description**
   - Ordinary and technical senses of “explain”
   - Prediction, mechanism, unification, and causal description
   - Why gravity does not end the explanatory regress
2. **What Science Presupposes and What It Does Not Decide**
   - Regularity, induction, mathematics, and intelligibility
   - Methodological success versus metaphysical entailment
3. **The Question Beyond the Model**
   - Why fundamental structure invites interpretation
   - Brute facts as a live stopping point

## Part II — Method: Constructing rather than importing an ontology

4. **Standards of Metaphysical Comparison**
   - Coherence, economy, explanatory reach, fit, and fruitfulness
   - Deduction, abduction, preference, and faith
5. **Necessity, Possibility, and Ground**
   - Modal vocabulary
   - Conceivability and its limits
   - What grounds possibility?
6. **The Adversarial and Provenance Method**
   - Load-bearing objections
   - Ancient and contemporary sources
   - Decisions, open questions, and cautious novelty claims

## Part III — From contingent reality to a necessary agent

7. **Contingency, Brute Facts, and Regress**
8. **Could Physical or Mathematical Reality Be Necessary?**
9. **Why Agency Enters**
   - Impersonal production, randomness, and agent causation
   - O'Connor and the contrastive explanation of contingency
10. **How Far Is the Necessary Agent from God?**
    - Creator, intellect, knowledge, power, goodness, and personhood as separate steps

## Part IV — The minimal ontology

11. **Generative Creation**
    - Structure $S$, laws, constants, boundaries, and primitives
    - Minimal/uniqueness-determining specifications
12. **Creaturely Agent Causation**
    - Libertarian branches and truthmakers
    - Why indeterminism is not yet freedom
13. **Asymmetric Co-production of History**
    - Divine, physical, and creaturely causal roles
14. **Actuality and Possible Histories**
    - Avoiding unnecessary reification of possible worlds
    - The grounding of modal space

## Part V — Knowledge, time, and freedom

15. **Actuality-Complete Omniscience**
    - Minimal definition
    - Modal and counterfactual extensions
16. **Agent-Causal Eternalism and Three Kinds of Indexing**
    - The complete actual history
    - Temporal, perspectival, and actuality indexing
    - Open-future semantics as a controlled alternative
17. **The Fixity Problem**
    - Truth, knowledge, necessity, causal determination, and freedom
18. **Atemporal Priority and Temporal Experience**
    - Whether any sequence exists outside creaturely time

## Part VI — Divine action

19. **Creation as Intervention Zero**
20. **A Taxonomy of Intervention**
    - Boundary, law, state, circumstance, and agency
21. **Miracle and Secondary Causation**
22. **Providence as Architecture**
    - Control without exhaustive pre-programming
    - Response and intervention under atemporality

## Part VII — Attributes not yet earned

23. **Unity and the Uniqueness of the Ground**
    - One ground of the contingent order versus one necessary being simpliciter
    - Independence, non-composition, individuation, and explanatory unity
24. **Can Omnipotence Be Derived?**
25. **What Could Omnipresence Mean Outside Space?**
    - Individual presence versus distributed coverage
26. **Goodness, Nature, Choice, and Reasons**
    - Are reasons prior to the agent?
    - Can reasons be plural without arbitrariness?
27. **Agency, Development, Suffering, and Evil**
    - Human judgment versus total-order evaluation
    - Whether robust agency requires difficulty

## Part VIII — Comparison and evaluation

28. **Naturalist and Mathematical-Structural Alternatives**
29. **Classical Theism, Molinism, Open Theism, and Timeless Models**
30. **What the Model Explains and Where It Stops**
31. **Established Elements, New Synthesis, and Remaining Work**
