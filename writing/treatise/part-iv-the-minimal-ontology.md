# Part IV — The Minimal Ontology

*Consolidation draft, first pass (S59, 2026-09-02). Same conventions as Part III: bracketed identifiers refer to the commitment ledger; every conclusion carries its kind of support. Part III established a necessary agent, $G$; this Part states the least that must be true of creation, of created agents, of their joint history, and of possibility for the architecture to stand.*

## 11. Generative Creation

### What is created

$G$ does not create events one by one. What $G$ creates is a **generative structure**, written $S=\langle L,C,B,O\rangle$: laws, constants, boundary conditions, and ontological primitives, the kinds of things there are and the powers they have [C07]. Within that structure, histories unfold by ordinary causation and, where the structure includes agents, by their originating acts. Creation is the actualization of the arena, not the scripting of what happens in it. The exact specification of $S$ can include agents and their powers while leaving more than one complete history admissible, provided the specification does not covertly encode how the powers are exercised [C34].

### How it is created

Creation is **originative existential causation**: a causal exercise whose immediate terminus is the existence of a numerically distinct order, not the modification of a prior patient [C98–C99]. The strengthened model, $OEC^+$, has the underived power-structured subject of Chapter 10 contingently exercise a specific power, $P_{OEC}$, under the specification $S$, producing an order that is numerically distinct from $G$, constitutively disjoint from $G$ — sharing no constituent and standing in no mode relation — and dependent on $G$ for its existence [C230–C231, C248]. Knowledge supplies the specification; the exercised power supplies the production. The explanation terminates in a fundamental power and its exercise, not in a mechanism composed of earlier material states, and the project accepts this terminus because it already accepts fundamental powers for agency and for modality [C232]. Formal consistency of the model is not a proof of its metaphysical possibility [C233]; what follows raises its support above coherence.

### Matter

The objection that a non-material cause cannot produce matter presupposes an account of matter the ontology does not hold. Matter is not a stuff handed from cause to effect. To be material is to instantiate the **material profile** that the physical component of $S$ specifies for material bearers: spatiotemporal location or extension, field quantities, conserved magnitudes, dispositional roles [C487]. Physics itself describes particles as excitations of fields, mass and energy as interconvertible, and conservation as a consequence of symmetries; nowhere does it posit a transferable substrate. Under the profile account the question becomes how a cause can make something instantiate a profile, and the answer is power plus specification.

The residual worry is the principle that every concrete caused existence requires a material cause. Its evidential support is intraworld transformation under conservation laws. But conservation laws are consequences of $S$ and govern processes within the histories $S$ admits; they cannot govern the origination of the order that entails them without $S$ governing its own origination [C488]. The principle's only remaining route is a priori, and the weak explanatory preference treats such intuitions as comparative costs, not premises [C489].

More than this can be said. Sole ultimity, the contingency of the material order, the necessitation lemma, and the physical immateriality of $G$ together entail that the origination of matter has a non-material ground; with constitutive disjointness this entails that matter had no material cause [C490]. The dilemma is exact: matter is either contingent, in which case creation without prior matter follows, or necessary, in which case it is a co-fundamental substrate beside $G$, which rejects sole ultimity and reinstates the impersonal stopping point of Chapter 8 [C491, C238]. Creation *ex nihilo* denies a prior patient; it does not treat nothing as a source [C236].

### From God

The order is “from $G$” in three accepted senses — causal source, exemplar source, and participatory derivation of finite powers from divine ones — and not in the strict sense on which a numerically divine reality constitutes, persists in, or becomes the created effect [C245–C247]. The strict sense is coherent and disfavored; its non-destructive variants are coherent with divine non-spatiality only under a principle on which a mode may bear the material profile while its subject does not, a principle nothing in the project motivates [C494].

### Why it is created

Creative reasons are typed by their target, their mode, and their level. The **mode** is favoring, justifying, or requiring: requiring reasons filter which structures are eligible, favoring reasons order the eligible ones partially, justifying reasons make an option intelligible without preferring it. The **level** is the choice to create at all, the kind of architecture, or the exact specification [C521]. At the level of creation, reasons justify and favor: creation and non-creation both remain live [C111]. At the level of architecture, the organizing reasons require: given that $G$ creates for actual finite otherness and for finite analogues of its own sourcehood, every eligible structure enables autonomous agents, and a generative structure follows rather than a scripted history [C105, C523]. At the level of specification, plural favoring reasons leave more than one undominated structure, and $G$ settles on one by the same reason-guided exercise that Chapter 9 described. Reasons explain eligibility and attraction; the settling explains the contrast [C522, C109]. The project does not claim to know the actual divine motive; it claims that the profile makes creation intelligible [C113].

Typing the reasons by the anchors of value that Part VII develops shows their targets: the agency of created subjects and truthful relation with them. No established reason is directed at what created subjects feel. That absence is the P-gap of Chapter 10 seen from its source [C524–C525]. Two routes to such a reason are visible — reading otherness as being for the other's own sake, and regard internal to truthful relation — and neither is adopted here [C526].

A reason for agency is not a reason to make everything an agent. Non-agentic domains supply a shared arena, regularities that make evidence and deliberation possible, real consequences, secondary causation, and the conditions of embodied agency [C107]. They are required by the same reason that requires agents.

### Possibility and plenitude

Which specifications are possible is not defined by what $G$ can do. A created specification is possible when it is *constitutively admissible*: well formed; consistent under logical, identity, and mathematical constraints, which includes admitting at least one complete history; its kinds satisfy their real definitions; its laws and changes fit the manifestation profiles of their bearers; its components can obtain jointly in one order; and its basis has a coherent dependent profile [C344–C348, C542]. None of these clauses mentions divine power or imaginability [C355, C274]. Possibility so defined licenses, for a complete root specification, a possible root instantiation [C373]. The range of creation is then reached by a bridge rather than assumed: whenever a fundamental created order exists its ultimate origin is the same $G$ [C360]; a root created basis has no existentially prior created producer, so $G$ originates it directly [C369–C370]; and that origination is agentic, a selected exercise of the essential originative power [C371]. Hence *qualified creative plenitude*: every essence-compatible admissible root specification is within $G$'s creative capacity, where essence-compatibility may exclude only descriptions incoherent about the agent itself [C372, C375–C376]. The result is preferred at F2 and inherits the abductive status of modal ultimity. It concerns capacity, not actuality: whether $G$ has actualized more than one order is coherent, within power, and undecidable from inside any one of them [C539]; that $G$ actualizes every eligible order would need a requiring reason the profile lacks [C540].

What grounds the structure the criterion appeals to is a further question, and the project answers it in the same spirit in which it answered the question of a second concrete source. The logical and identity truths hold in virtue of the agent's nature and of every dependent by its dependence, since a dependent shares the form of being of its source; and a constitutive profile is admissible exactly when it is a way the agent's being can be participated by a dependent [C620]. Mathematical structure is the structure of possible plurality so grounded. The structure is therefore not made, since no exercise produced it and a willed necessity would be contingent [C619]; not apart from the agent, since it is the agent's way of being as shareable; and not ours, since finite knowers abstract it from a created order made according to it [C625]. The criterion of possibility keeps every clause and contains no reference to the agent; the thesis says why the criterion holds, not what it says, and it sits upstream of every claim about creative range [C621]. It is preferred abductively, with primitive necessity as the coherent fallback and an independent realm of necessary truths rejected for the brute concordance it would leave between what the realm permits and what the agent's nature admits [C622]. The objection that the agent's nature is itself described with logic reads grounding as production; a nature is not prior to itself, and a truth held in virtue of it is neither caused by it nor presupposed by it [C623].

## 12. Creaturely Agent Causation

### Freedom

At least some created agents have libertarian, agent-causal powers: they originate acts among genuine alternatives, and the act is theirs [C08]. Indeterminism is not freedom; a random outcome is not an exercise. The realized act must be grounded in a conscious agent's exercise of an originating power, guided by reasons that do not entail it [C57, C60]. The project does not require an antecedently sufficient contrastive reason for every free selection, for the reason given in Chapter 9: the requirement is determinism restated.

### Consciousness

Four things travel under “consciousness”: phenomenal experience, access of contents to use, self-representation, and participation in originating action [C134]. Freedom is token-relative: an act is consciously free when embodied sourcehood includes causally relevant conscious access before or during its settlement [C215–C216]. Consciousness need not be the first event in a decision; it must participate non-epiphenomenally before the option is irrevocably fixed.

### The embodied subject

Action belongs to the unified, temporally persisting embodied subject, not to consciousness, the brain, or the body treated as an inner chooser [C212]. A decision is an **episode** — preparation, conscious access, settlement, execution, retrospective ownership — in which neural preparation does not by itself locate settlement, and a readiness signal is not a completed decision [C213–C216]. The empirical findings that motivate the objection are respected through explicit bridges: they show timing dissociations in arbitrary movement, not that the body deterministically completes reasoned choices before consciousness can contribute. Were sufficient pre-conscious settlement established for a token, that token would not be consciously free [C217]. The realization of the subject is no longer left open at the working level: the trunk accepts, at F2, a unified embodied power-bearing subject whose organization supports real conscious and agentive powers, under a thin hylomorphic interpretation on which the form is neither a second spatial component nor a second substance [C330–C332]; reductive identity remains live and non-preferred, and the deeper phenomenal bridge, for which a subject-level dual-aspect account is the strongest candidate, remains open [C328, C337–C338].

### Identity

The trunk's account of identity is canonical here and is stated in full in Part VII [C435–C456]. Five questions are kept apart: synchronic individuation, numerical identity, diachronic persistence, reidentification, and survival of what matters [C436]. Numerical identity is absolute and fundamental, not reducible to qualitative, material, psychological, or causal similarity, because every proposed reduction presupposes the individuals it compares and the relations it offers can branch where identity cannot [C435–C437]. For embodied subjects, persistence is explained by an anchored hylomorphic-organismic career: one living power-bearing continuant, individuated by its integrated organization, persisting through a non-branching immanent career while matter, consciousness, memory, and character change; the pre-conscious organism and the later agent are one individual, and “person” marks a capacity or phase [C438–C446]. The question-driven track reached the same conclusion from the agency model, and its result is retained as the refinement that explains why *sources* in particular must be identity units: the freedom of this chapter requires the same agent in the history it lived and the history it could have lived, and those histories diverge in every continuity, so what makes the agent the same is prior to any continuity [C506, C556]. A qualitative replica is a distinct subject and need not act as the original does [C513]; reidentification by finite knowers is evidence and never constitution, and $G$ identifies by direct acquaintance, which tracks the fact [C436, C518].

### The diachronic level

Token freedom at every moment does not make a life the agent's own. A curator who selects contexts can leave every choice free and still make every path lead to the same place; another can leave the paths open and install the standards by which the agent chooses. Meaningful agency therefore has a second level: **diachronic authorship**, that the shape of a life is contrastively the agent's, and **evaluative authorship**, that the agent's standards are outputs of its own reflective exercises rather than of cumulative influence that bypasses evaluation [C497–C500]. Education, argument, and truthful recognition are rational influence and are how finite agents become evaluators at all; manipulation is a-rational influence accumulated until the standards are someone else's [C499]. Manipulation damages the authorship of the same persisting subject; it does not make a new one [C520]. These distinctions constrain providence in Part VI and give the accessible answer to whether a manipulated person is free: at each moment, often yes; as the author of a life, to that extent, no.

## 13. Asymmetric Co-production of History

The actual history is constituted jointly by $G$ and by created causes, and the constitution is asymmetric [C09]. Five relations must be kept apart, and no transitivity is assumed across them: existential grounding, structural constraint, ordinary event causation, agent origination, and intervention [C32]. $G$ grounds the existence of every agent, power, and arena; that grounding does not make $G$ the author of what the agents do. A created agent supplies the contrastive explanation of why the history contains its act rather than an available alternative, while $G$ remains the ground of the arena in which either was possible [C33]. The label for this division of explanatory labor is **asymmetric co-production**: $G$ sets up the game and keeps it in being; creatures make real moves that $G$ does not make for them.

The structural test is direct. If $S$ includes genuine originating power and does not encode its exercise, then two admissible histories can share their entire past up to a moment and differ in what the same agent does at that moment. Exact specification of laws, boundaries, and powers therefore does not entail a unique complete history; adding the actual outcome to $S$ would erase the distinction between structure and contribution the model exists to draw [C31, C34]. $G$ can actualize the arena and the powers without selecting the complete qualitative content of what results.

Divine action within the arena — intervention — is typed by causal target and is the subject of Part VI. Two consequences belong here because they follow from the co-production model alone. Divine knowledge of a free act is not prior, in the order of explanation, to the act's obtaining; the act grounds its truth, and the truth is what is known [C30]. And divine control of an agent's deliberative context does not entail control of the resulting act: context and response are distinct contributions to one history [C42].

## 14. Actuality and Possible Histories

Possible histories are not additional concrete blocks alongside the actual one. They represent compossible manifestations of powers and constraints, and the representation may be set-theoretic without granting its members concrete existence [C49]. Modal knowledge is grounded in necessary constraints — logical and identity constraints, consequences of specified mathematical structure, necessities from $G$'s established essence — together with the powers of $G$ and of created agents [C48, C52]. Concrete modal realism is excluded as a posit that multiplies existents to do work constraints and powers already do [C536].

The actual history bears an **actual marker** that is grounded by the history that obtains; the marker designates, it does not select [C29]. The project's temporal model is **agent-causal eternalism**: one complete actual history exists tenselessly, and created agents make genuine originating contributions within it [C22]. Three kinds of indexing must be kept distinct: temporal location at a creaturely time, perspectival availability to a creature or frame, and actuality indexing of divine knowledge to what obtains [C23]. Evaluating whether a creature could have done otherwise holds the causal past, capacities, and circumstances fixed while the history bearing the actual marker differs; the modal structure and the totality of divine knowledge need not change, and no temporal state of $G$ is implied [C26–C28].

What divine knowledge does not contain is as important as what it does. There are no pre-actual truths about what a merely possible libertarian agent would choose, and so no knowledge of them; knowledge that an agent *could* originate an act under conditions is admitted, knowledge that it *would* is excluded [C13, C50]. Merely possible agents are known as types; actual agents retain de re identity across the histories that diverge at their choices [C53, C519].

Possibility itself, for a created specification, is constitutive admissibility as Chapter 11 stated it [C344–C348]; imaginability is defeasible finite evidence of it and never its constitution [C354]. Other possible worlds are not other universes. Physical universes within one created order — bubbles, epochs, branches — are an empirical matter internal to $S$ and orthogonal to the ground question of Part III [C537]; one constraint holds, that the events realizing an agent's settlement do not branch into all their alternatives, since settling actualizes one [C538]. Plural created orders, each with its own specification, time, and history, grounded by the one $G$, are coherent, within divine power, and undecidable from inside any one of them [C539]. The complete actual reality that divine knowledge is indexed to may therefore contain more than one order; it contains, in any case, $G$, whatever $G$ has created, its histories, its interventions, and the relations of dependence among them [C47].
