## 23. Unity and the Uniqueness of the Ground

Part III ended with an audit of what had been earned, and Part IV with a being that is one, necessary, and underived. This Part takes the attributes one at a time and says, for each, how far the argument reaches and where it stops. It begins with unity because every later derivation quantifies over one agent, and the project must say why.

**Three theses, not one.** Three uniqueness claims are kept apart [C78]. *Ground uniqueness* ($U_G$) says that exactly one being is the ultimate ground of the total contingent order [C80]. *Divine-agent uniqueness* ($U_D$) says that, given the agentic stopping point of Part III, there is exactly one necessary agent that is that ground [C81]. *Unrestricted uniqueness* would say that only one necessary entity of any category exists, and the project does not claim it: necessary abstracta and derivative necessary items are left open, and monotheism, as the project understands it, does not require their denial.

**The abductive case.** The route to $U_G$ is cumulative rather than demonstrative. One integrated contingent order invites one ground; existential sovereignty over everything created leaves no created relatum outside the ground's reach; explanatory economy counts against a second ultimate; and the qualified maximal power of Chapter 24 makes two independent bearers of it a source of possible conflict, reciprocal restriction, or an unexplained necessary concordance [C83]. Three plural-ground models are tested against this: partitioned grounds, each responsible for part of the order; joint grounds, jointly responsible for all of it; and redundantly total grounds, each sufficient alone [S13]. Each fragments the explanatory stopping point or multiplies concrete ultimates without an independently established need. None is declared incoherent. The result is preferred, and it is the preference that Part VI's modal extension inherited: conditional modal ultimity extends actual uniqueness across every possible created order by the same abductive considerations, and with the same revisability [C360–C362].

**How the agent is individuated.** A question that classical treatments rarely ask is what makes this agent *this one*. The answer is not a qualitative profile, since no profile individuates a subject [C515]. The agent is individuated by necessary existence together with the sole-ground role; its identity across possible exercises is presupposed by every de re modal claim about it and by the power bridge of Chapter 24, and no qualitative principle supplies it [C516]. An exact divine duplicate is incoherent, because exactness would have to include both created dependence and underived ultimate-ground status [C266]. Whether a dependent qualitative near-copy could be created remains open [C267].

**What is not used.** Aquinas's routes to unity through simplicity and perfection are conditional resources, not premises [C82]. The project does not adopt absolute simplicity (Chapter 25) and does not infer perfection (Part III), so it cannot borrow their consequences; it records them as places where a stronger architecture would reach further.

## 24. Can Omnipotence Be Derived?

**What the question is.** Qualified omnipotence is the power to perform every action metaphysically coherent for the agent under necessary constraints and an independently established divine essence [C74]. Two points fix the question before any derivation. A possible state of affairs is not automatically an action the agent can coherently perform as its originating agent [C256]; the domain quantifies over well-formed tasks attributable to the agent [C255]. And an alleged essential limit qualifies the domain only if the essential feature is established independently of the incapacity and of omnipotence itself [C263]. The second is the anti-circularity rule that governs every later step: nothing may be defined into the essence in order to protect the attribute, and nothing may be smuggled out of it.

**Range and strength.** Omnipotence requires both maximal *range* over eligible tasks and sufficient *strength* or control over the contribution assigned to the agent [C257]. Strength was settled early: strong divine production of a token outcome is distinct from enabling a creature to originate it, and inability to determine a free act while preserving it as free is no limitation [C76, C258]. Range is where the work lies, and the project divides it into two claims. *Created-order maximality* ($OMP_C$) says the agent has maximal power over every coherent created target; *unrestricted qualified maximality* ($OMP_Q$) says it has power over every coherent action at all [C261–C262]. The first is better supported than the second, and the difference is worth keeping: the first is defeated only by an essence-compatible incapacity, the second also by a non-created concrete limiter that independently exists [C265]. Blockers are classified so that each can be tested: incoherent tasks are not blockers; role-mismatched tasks (asking the agent to be the creature's source) are not blockers; created relata cannot be ultimate blockers of their own ground [C259]; abstract and logical constraints are not agents competing in power [C264]; internal limits must pass the anti-circularity rule; and a non-created concrete blocker remains the live defeater [C265]. The absence of a blocker does not supply a power [C278]; the project does not argue from the absence of limits to unlimited power.

**The universal-range problem.** Cross-category evidence, originative power, variable specification, and typed intervention, favors maximal created-order power without entailing a universal range [C260]. The trunk therefore decomposed the range into four components and set out to earn each [C272]: creative plenitude over root specifications, constitutive control over what exists, history-sensitive intervention relative to a fixed past, and a final exhaustion condition for unrestricted $OMP_Q$. The first three are now supported at F2, and Part VI carried the second and third in detail. The summary here is what Chapter 10's audit lacked.

*Creative plenitude* was reached by a modal-source bridge with four premises [C273]: independently grounded created possibility, invariant ultimate source, essential originative power, and agent-relative accessibility. Each had a circularity to avoid. Possibility could not be read as whatever the agent can actualize [C274]; it received the constitutive-admissibility criterion of Part IV [C344–C345]. Source invariance could not be inferred from actual uniqueness alone [C275]; it received the conditional modal ultimity of Chapter 23 [C360]. Originative power could not be declared content-neutral by fiat [C277]; patient-independence, specification fidelity, and universal manifestation range were separated, and the universal range was derived from the modal architecture rather than from the power's lack of a patient [C367–C368]. And accessibility could not be witnessed by a world where the outcome merely happens; the witness must contain the same necessary agent intentionally performing the act through its essential power [C374]. With a root created basis defined without mention of the agent [C369], the direct-root lemma [C370] and the agentic-origin principle [C371] yield *qualified creative plenitude*: every essence-compatible admissible root specification is within the agent's creative capacity [C372, C375]. The result is preferred at F2 and inherits the abductive status of modal ultimity [C376]. *Constitutive control*, in its positive, persistence-sensitive, and annihilative ranges, and *history-sensitive intervention*, conditional on local prefix composability, were argued in Part VI [C401, C418]. The three named created-order ranges are thereby supported [C419].

**Closing the current action domain.** Every divine task is written with its agent, patient, contrastive result, preservation profile, and other source roles [C420]. A *pure created-directed* task is one whose divine contribution terminates in something created without essentially modifying the agent [C421]; every such task has root, type-outcome, or fixed-prefix form, so the three created ranges exhaust it at F2 [C422–C423]. Identity then removes pseudo-tasks that would eliminate the very agent said to complete them [C427, C451–C455], and S71 assigns relational whole-presence creation to the root range [C569–C570]. S72 closes the remaining self-executive fragment. Such an act is defined without a capacity predicate through the agent's direct executive sourcehood; its executive result requires no distinct bearer or causal patient and preserves $PP_G$ [C573]. Its independently earned grammar contains cognitive-attentional, rational-volitional, communicative-address, originative, and interventive manifestation kinds [C574]. Source, kind, content, positive or negative polarity, pure or mixed role, and preservation/compossibility conditions form $SEForm_G$; $SEAdm_G$ filters tokens before capacity is inferred [C575–C576]. Formal executive generality $FEG_G$ then covers every admissible token generated by that grammar [C578]. It does not infer every compatible manifestation from one exercise or introduce arbitrary new action kinds [C579]. Mere non-occurrence is not refraining [C577], divine states and relations are not automatically further actions [C428], and abstract necessities constrain task coherence without becoming resistant patients [C429].

**Intrinsic manifestation and the remaining limit.** An intrinsic mode is executive when it is the occurrent aspect of an established power's token exercise, preserves the bearer, and adds no new constitutive basis [C581]. A qualitative mode instead introduces a new quality, capacity, part, or substrate-state and needs its own ontology [C582]. This divides strict divine-mode constitution into $ED_{IM}^{exec}$ and $ED_{IM}^{qual}$ [C583]. When one independently admissible mixed specification makes an executive token both the divine mode and the substantive constituent of its root-created terminus, token unity plus $FEG_G$ and $CP_G^{elig}$ conditionally cover it; the qualitative case remains open [C584]. The cumulative result is *ontology-relative qualified omnipotence* $OMP_Q^{\mathcal O_*}$ at F2: every task licensed by the current ontology's kinds, relata, identity conditions, and source roles has a range assignment [C585]. The unrestricted claim remains stronger because a rival ontology may independently motivate a distinct non-created concrete relatum or a genuinely new action kind. If one of these limits the agent, the fallback is a bounded demiurge, maximal over its creation but limited by something it did not make [C265]. The first of these has now been tested. A non-created concrete limits the agent only if some contingent fact concerning it has no ultimate ground in the agent [C589]; under modal grounding coverage extended to every contingent fact and modal uniqueness of the underived source, no such limiter is possible [C590–C591]. What survives is an emanation, necessary and grounded in the agent, or an inert necessary concretum, and neither limits anything; both lie outside the preferred ontology because the preferred model makes every divine exercise contingent, not because of any claim about power [C592]. So the non-created-relatum condition on unrestricted omnipotence reduces to the abductive status of conditional modal ultimity's components, and the one remaining cross-ontology test is a genuinely new action kind [C594].

## 25. Immateriality and the Ground of Created Space

**Three theses.** *Created-space aspatiality* says the agent is not circumscribed by or constituted through any region of created spacetime [C84]. *Physical immateriality* says it is not a body, field, or physical system under the physical specification [C85]. *Absolute non-spatiality* says it has no fundamental spatial extension or external container in any order [C86]. The first two are earned conditionally from ultimacy and grounding asymmetry: what grounds spacetime is not located in it. The third is preferred abductively: nothing in the agent's agency, knowledge, or power profile requires a spatial arena or an intrinsic geometry, and higher-dimensional, intrinsically geometric, and ubiquitous-occupation alternatives add structure without explanatory return [S14]. None of the three entails full divine simplicity or the absence of metaphysical structure [C87].

**What the agent is made of.** The question is valid and decomposes into material, parts, substrate, constitutive profile, and category [C210]. The answers were given in Part IV and are summarized: no material input, no prior ingredients, no external bearer; a thin substance that is identity unit, bearer, and source; integrated role-distinct powers that are aspects rather than parts [C223–C228, C492]. This *minimal constitutive simplicity* does not entail the identities of absolute simplicity, and the project declines to infer them [C227, C87]. Subject-level and aspect-level descriptions of an exercise are not rival causes [C493].

**Transcendence and immanence.** Constitutive transcendence says that creation does not constitute or contain the agent's basic identity and that the agent is not a member of the physical order [C241]. Relational immanence says that every created relatum lies within the agent's immediate grounding, epistemic, and causal reach [C242]. The two are compatible, and their compatibility is the project's replacement for the picture of a God who is far away and occasionally arrives: direct intervention indexes a created target and requires no travel across a boundary [C243]. Numerical distinction between creator and creation does not by itself entail *constitutive disjointness*, which is an explicit further condition of the full production model [C244, C248].

**The burden of immateriality.** Physical immateriality owes a positive account of how a non-material ground produces material substances, and grounding must not merely rename the transition [C88]. The account has three parts. First, the production relation: an originative existential power exercised under a specification yields a numerically distinct bearer of powers without material transfer [C229–C236]; the transformation-based principle that every effect needs a prior material patient is distinguished from the universal principle that every material effect needs a material cause, and only the first is accepted [C235]. Second, the senses of “from God”: causal, virtual or exemplar, participatory, and strict constitutive derivation are separated; the first three are accepted and the fourth remains open [C245–C247, C254]. Strict *ex deo*, on which something numerically divine constitutes created reality, divides into partition, divine-mode, world-as-body, and whole-presence variants, all live and all disfavored [C251]; and it does not satisfy the material-cause demand unless it supplies a material cause, which is exactly what its proponents cannot say without attributing extension to the substance [C250, C494]. Third, matter itself: materiality is an instantiated profile fixed by the physical specification, not a transferable stuff [C487]; conservation laws are consequences of the specification and govern processes within the order, so they cannot govern the origination of the order that entails them [C488]; and with that the evidential base of the universal material-cause principle disappears [C489]. What remains is a derivation: sole ultimity, the contingency of the material order, the necessitation lemma of Part III, and physical immateriality entail that the origination of matter has a non-material ground; with constitutive disjointness this entails the denial of the universal principle, and without it the same follows unless the agent is material [C490]. Matter is either contingent, in which case it was originated without material input, or necessary, in which case it is a co-fundamental substrate and a second impersonal stopping point subject to the necessitation dilemma [C491]. Material-continuity alternatives remain live and disfavored [C89].

**The stopping point.** A fundamental power is an explanatory stopping point, not a mechanism. The one residue the account leaves, how an existential power operates beneath its exercise, is of a kind the ontology already accepts for created agency and is fewer and less fundamental than the residues of the alternatives [C496]. The project distinguishes formal coherence from demonstrated possibility throughout, and claims the first.

## 26. What Could Omnipresence Mean Outside Space?

**Ontological presence.** Presence to a thing combines immediate grounding reach, complete epistemic access, and direct causal availability [C90]. On that definition the agent is ontologically omnipresent throughout the actual created order [C91], and the same architecture accompanies every possible order it actualizes [C93]. Omnipresence is predicated of an individual subject: distributed coverage by several beings would not establish the omnipresence of any one of them [C79]. Empty regions are no exception, since presence is not occupation; the scope is ontology-neutral over whatever the created domain contains. Relational immanence neither entails nor excludes constitutive overlap [C252], so omnipresence is not an argument for strict *ex deo*.

**Personal presence.** Personal presence divides into five levels: ontological presence, personal availability, exercised regard or response, reciprocal shared attention, and loving communion [C121]. The agent is universally personally available to every actual conscious subject, on the strength of conscious agency, complete knowledge, subject acquaintance, and direct causal availability [C122]. Availability does not entail universal exercised attention, response, shared awareness, love, or communion [C123]; significant reciprocal presence is asymmetrically co-produced when it includes creaturely awareness, attention, or response [C124]. Divine address is typed as circumstantial or informational contribution or as bare regard, never as source override [C466].

**Knowing subjects.** Part V established the epistemic side: propositional completeness does not exhaust epistemic mode, and the agent has direct, factive, subject-indexed acquaintance with every actual subject without identity, constitution, or token sharing [C283–C288]. Reciprocal second-personal presence is stronger and requires creaturely participation [C290]; reciprocity requires participation by both, not equality of their capacities, so that genuine communication can be radically asymmetric in comprehension, precision, and range [C295–C296]. This is the ontological basis of the experiential register: an experience presenting the agent as present, addressing, or responding can be veridical of standing presence, veridical of exercised regard, or non-veridical, with different truthmakers in each case [C467]; it is defeasible first-person evidence for the subject and never evidence for the architecture [C468]; and absence of experience refutes nothing, since capacity is not exercise [C470]. Communion, the fifth level, is an evaluative and relational thesis and belongs to Chapter 27's branch, where it is deferred.

## 27. Goodness, Nature, Choice, and Reasons

**Scope.** The project develops a minimal axiological interface for the ontology, not a comprehensive ethics [C179]. Ontology has governing priority over ethics in the development order [C178]; the interface must cover good, evil, sacrifice, transpersonal continuity, and their relation to the agent, and it may defer moral-status theory, population ethics, maximizing longtermism, and complete conflict ordering [C180]. Good-for, excellence, impersonal value, fitting action, good agency, obligation, and omnibenevolence are distinct claims, and the chapter earns some and not others [C127].

**Good-for before good simpliciter.** The inquiry begins with the good of a bearer [C143]. Conscious subjects are the first scope of welfare bearers [C144]; the working welfare profile includes phenomenal valence, functioning, agency, epistemic contact, relationship, and diachronic integrity [C146]; pro tanto and all-things-considered good are distinguished [C145]; desire and pleasure are neither necessary nor sufficient, while sincere, reflective, non-manipulated endorsement is evidence and can itself be a component [C147]; multidimensional dominance yields some comparisons without a scalar [C148]; and assessment is temporally scoped [C149]. Finite welfare facts are asymmetrically co-grounded: the agent grounds bearers and arena, complete knowledge recognizes obtaining welfare facts, divine institution can alter contingent normative contexts, and value constitution is the open component [C150–C151].

**Construction and its limits.** The possibility that value is creature-constructed, and that the agent has no native evaluative standpoint, is taken seriously and typed [C155–C156]. Actual-attitude construction is insufficient as a complete theory [C158]; creature-only construction needs a principled restriction, since the agent too is a conscious autonomous agent [C157]; agency-constitutivist and hybrid accounts are the strongest constructivist candidates [C159]. Finite creatures are under a distinctive pressure to construct norms, from vulnerability, dependence, limited knowledge, coordination, and self-maintenance [C166]; the agent may lack that pressure while retaining normative capacity, which does not establish that it lacks a native norm [C167]. The project's resolution is a hybrid with objective anchors: good is an objective regulative standard that temporal subjects approximate fallibly [C168–C169]; anchors are objective, and personal, moral, and institutional specifications are constructed on them [C170].

**The anchors and the bridge.** The minimum anchor domain is conscious valence, agentive sourcehood, and truthful recognition or relation: $\mathcal A_{min}=\{P,S,R\}$ [C187]. One typed objective relation, *fit*, is the single axiological bridge from the ontology to value [C188]: pro tanto good and bad are fit and misfit relative to an anchor, bearer, context, and horizon, and generic good is undefeated fit across the relevant scope [C189]. Fit is refined into bearer eligibility, an anchor's directed profile, an apt and proportionate response, explicit scope, and defeat [C203]. The one primitive normative burden that remains is directedness, what is *due* to a bearer, capacity, or relation [C204]; the preferred hybrid grounds it in anchor profiles for realization and corruption and in the agent's necessary rational and relational ground for objective polarity [C205]. Evil is objective misfit, grave in its narrow scope [C190], formalized as absence, frustration, or corruption of due good rather than as absence of any possible good [C206]; positive states can be bearers or vehicles of privative evil without becoming a rival substance [C207]; moral and natural evil share evaluative content and differ in causal authorship [C191].

**Sacrifice, time, and the species.** Sacrifice is not good because it costs [C160]; its cost, its agentic quality, its effect on the agent's diachronic welfare, and its value for others are separate assessments [C161, C202]. Qualified stable commitments receive defeasible diachronic priority over transient pleasures [C162]; defeat can represent justified priority without a common scale [C163]. A fitting sacrifice is a sourceful, sincere, epistemically responsible, and proportionate response to an undefeated anchor, for the agent or others, potentially across a posthumous horizon [C194]; knowledge is not a universal prerequisite, and belief, trust, hope, and faith are distinguished with non-culpable attention to counterevidence [C200–C201]. Actual future bearers are eligible for the same fit relations without temporal discount [C177, C192]; the eternalism of Part V is what makes the future real enough for this. Species continuity is derivatively valuable insofar as it preserves conditions for future conscious life, sourcehood, relation, and inherited projects, and is not an unconditional or lexically supreme good [C181–C182, C193]. A positive defeasible long-term preference belongs inside the interface, stronger than temporal standing and weaker than strong longtermism [C186, C198–C199].

**Voluntarism and ultimity.** Five forms of theological voluntarism are distinguished [C140]; unrestricted choice voluntarism is live and disfavored [C129]; divine nature cannot be a standard merely by being labeled good [C131]. Objective normativity does not threaten divine ultimity, because an objective normative relation need not be an entity prior to the agent [C128]; a normative relation said to connect the agent to creation decomposes into existential grounding, value constitution, recognition, and institution [C141], and these are distinct divine roles from perfectly satisfying the standard [C196]. The preferred realization of fit is essentially grounded in the agent [C195], and the grounding is non-circular: the agent necessarily instantiates the kinds from which the sourcehood and relation anchors take their fit conditions and existentially and modally grounds the kind that fixes the valence anchor, all established without evaluative premises [C462]. Divine goodness does not require a unique optimal world, maximization, or necessary creation [C132].

**Reasons.** Creative explanation is layered across the choice to create, the kind of architecture, and the exact specification [C104]. The preferred organizing reasons are actual finite otherness and finite analogues of conscious sourcehood [C105]; a generative structure is preferred to a scripted history [C106]; reasons may be plural and only partially order eligible structures, with no assumption of a unique best or a maximizing choice function [C108]; the exact selection terminates in reason-guided settling [C109]; creation and non-creation remain live even with reasons favoring creation [C111]; and the project does not claim to know the actual reason [C113]. A creative reason is typed by target, mode, and level: requiring reasons filter eligibility, favoring reasons induce a partial attraction order, justifying reasons make an option intelligible [C521–C522]. The organizing agency reason is requiring at the architecture level, which is why every eligible structure enables autonomous agents [C523]. Physical features generated by a reason-supported structure are derivatively explained, which is explanatory coverage and not moral justification [C114].

**What is earned and what is not.** The established reason profile has targets $S$ and $R$ only [C524]; by typing, the agent's established profile is good-oriented toward sourcehood and truthful relation [C463]. This is earned within the architecture and is not nothing: a creator could have scripted, overridden, or withheld itself, and did not. It is not omnibenevolence, which would require universality over anchors, bearers, contexts, and acts, a no-misfit guarantee, and an account of divine valence [C465, C133]. The gap is exact: no established disposition or exercised reason is directed at conscious valence, at what created subjects feel [C464, C525]. Two candidate routes to such a reason exist, reading actual finite otherness as making subjects actual for their own sake, and regard internal to truthful relation with a valenced subject; each is an abductive addition at the evidential level of the reason profile, and neither is adopted [C526]. The project's stopping rule then applies: moral status, population ethics, aggregation, and applied ethics are deferred unless ontologically load-bearing [C197].

## 28. Agency, Development, Suffering, and Evil

**Where evil enters.** The problem of evil enters the architecture at the P-gap and nowhere else [C464]. It is not the classical inference from evil to non-existence, which does not run against an agent whose full goodness and unrestricted power are unearned; it is the question whether a good-oriented agent can permit created suffering without misfit toward conscious valence. Divine relations to a token suffering are typed as origination, arena grounding, permission, knowledge, and response; the agent is not the contrastive author of moral evil, natural suffering has derivative explanation from the structure, and permission is the load-bearing relation [C472]. Permission, cover, and gratuitousness are defined so that orientation toward valence entails that no actual suffering is gratuitous [C473].

**Policy-level covers.** Two covers are derived from the creation reason rather than imported. The *source cover*: a policy of preventing every harmful outcome of created choice would conflict with the creation reason, the agency ecology, and truthful relation, since a world in which choices never harm misrepresents what choosing is [C474]. The *regularity cover*: constant preventive intervention against natural suffering would erode the regularity that reason-guided sourcehood and truthful relation require [C475]. Both are policy-level: token prevention remains available, and both leave a threshold residue, why not the worst cases, to which the reply that any threshold would be arbitrary is recorded as contested rather than adopted [C474]. Part VI added the diachronic cost: a policy of repeated preventive selection of circumstances is itself a curator policy over the agent's field of consequences, and past some density it funnels [C504]. The thesis that robust agency requires difficulty is kept as an optional layer and is not used in the derivation [C476].

**The bearer-level remainder.** Policy-level covers do not close bearer-level misfit. The truthful-relation anchor forbids treating a bearer's suffering as a vehicle for others' goods; closure for a bearer requires undefeated fit within that bearer's own horizon [C477]. For many bearers the suffering is integrated within the life. For bearers whose lives end in uncovered suffering, closure requires an extended bearer horizon, and personal continuation thereby becomes a load-bearing question rather than a consolation [C478]. The belief that the agent's goodness extends to valence has the exact content that no actual suffering is gratuitous and that bearer horizons suffice; it is held as faith in the sense Chapter 27 gave that word, with apparently gratuitous cases as non-culpably attended counterevidence [C479]. Skeptical theism as a blanket move is refused, and so is the inference from an unseen cover to no cover; the same finite fallibility blocks both [C168].

**Continuation as a role.** Personal continuation is the numerically same conscious bearer existing after the cessation of its embodied base, with an undefeated fit horizon, located anywhere within complete actual reality [C480]. Transpersonal continuity, memory in others, descendants, and replicas do not satisfy the requirement, because the remainder is bearer-indexed [C481]. Continuation is coherent under every live realization of the subject, by persistence of a subject-level bearer or by numerical recreation of the same individual [C482]; it is within divine power as an intervention or a further creative act, an eligible source-neutral outcome under the causal distribution of Part VI, and capacity does not entail exercise [C483]. The empirical regime does not decide it: dependence of consciousness on the brain constrains the actual embodied regime and does not defeat continuation, and nothing supports it either; the inference from unobserved disembodied consciousness to its impossibility is the non-detection inference the method excludes [C484]. Continuation follows from no established premise; it follows conditionally from orientation toward valence, so that belief in divine goodness toward what creatures feel and belief in continuation stand or fall together [C485]. If it obtains, it is available to every bearer, and communion within it is asymmetrically co-produced rather than imposed; the privative model of hell-like absence, privation of communion and not of grounding, is its negative case [C486, C208–C209].

**Identity across the gap.** The trunk has since given identity its own module, and it is stated here as canonical [C435–C456]. Numerical identity is absolute and fundamental: every proposed reduction to qualitative, material, psychological, or causal relations presupposes the individuals it compares, and those relations can branch where identity cannot [C435–C437]. Kind-sensitive persistence profiles explain and constrain the ordinary career without replacing the relation: for human and animal subjects the preferred profile is anchored hylomorphic-organismic persistence, one living embodied power-bearing continuant, individuated by its integrated organization, persisting through a non-branching immanent career while matter, consciousness, memory, and character change [C438–C446]. The pre-conscious organism and the later agent are one individual, and “person” marks a capacity or phase [C444]. Ordinary continuity does not cross a genuine gap, but intermittent existence is not impossible: a complete admissible history may assign one individual a disconnected life-set, constrained by the identity fact, the same origin anchor, essence compatibility, non-branching, and one grounded career [C447–C450]. Numerical recreation is a preferred possibility at F2 and duplicate reconstruction a distinct task; no persisting carrier is required; the agent's knowledge tracks the identity fact and does not make it [C451–C453]. Fission has a rule: a unique continuer of the organismic career may be the original, symmetric branches are distinct successors, and a remote competitor cannot change a local identity fact [C445]. The question-driven track reached the same conclusions by another route, from the agency model, and its results are retained as the agency-side refinement: sourcehood, de re identity across histories, and whole-agent attribution require the identity unit to be prior to the history it produces, which is what the trunk's primitive identity is [C506]; and identity is what matters for a source, since the anchors are subject-indexed [C511]. What the branch gives up is the suggestion that a subject's ordinary persistence is nothing but being the same source; in this life it is also, and explanatorily, the career of one living organism [C517, C556].

**Human judgment.** The project keeps a rule against overreach in both directions. Not every humanly perceived evil is evil from the complete perspective [R06]; and that fact may not be used as a blanket excuse [C184]. What the architecture can say is what it has said: where the problem enters, which relations bear it, which covers are derived, what the covers leave uncovered, and what would have to be true for the remainder to close. Whether it is true is the faith of Chapter 27, with its content now exact.

**What the Part has earned.** Unity at the divine-ground level, with the individuation of the agent by role rather than profile; ontology-relative qualified maximality by a sequence of independently filtered ranges, and an honest statement of the unrestricted claim's cross-ontology burden; immateriality with a positive account of material production and a derivation of the non-material ground of matter; omnipresence as relational immanence and universal availability, with the experiential register placed on it; a minimal axiological interface with objective anchors and one bridge relation; a good-oriented profile toward freedom and relation; and the exact location of the problem of evil, with the covers it admits, the remainder it leaves, and the continuation it makes load-bearing. What it has not earned is what it has been careful to name: unrestricted omnipotence, absolute simplicity, perfection, omnibenevolence, and the orientation toward what creatures feel on which the last of these depends.
