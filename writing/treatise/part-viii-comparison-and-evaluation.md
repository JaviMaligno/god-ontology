## 29. Naturalist and Mathematical-Structural Alternatives

The project's rule is to discover how far its own argument reaches before comparing the result with any inherited position [C21]. Parts III through VII did the discovering. This Part does the comparing, and it begins with the alternatives the author found most attractive at the start, since the honest test of a position is against the rival one was tempted by.

**What naturalism offers.** Naturalism, as the project uses the word, is the thesis that the physical order, or the mathematical structure it instantiates, is the last thing there is: no ground beyond it, no agent beneath it. Its attractions are real and were felt. It is economical in kinds. It leaves nothing to faith. It has the success of the sciences on its side. And it can present the apparent simplicity of fundamental law as a reason to think the law self-sufficient, although the author's own remark stands against that last step: the simplicity of a mathematical symbol is a product of abstraction, and what it encodes is often very complex, so the elegance of a formalism is no evidence that what it describes needed no ground [U04].

**Where it pays.** Part III showed that naturalism is not one stopping point but three, and that each has an exact cost [C459]. *Brute contingency* accepts that the order exists, could have failed to, and has no account; it is coherent, and it is the lowest-ranked option under the weak explanatory preference that the sciences themselves exemplify [C457]. *Necessitarianism* makes the physical or mathematical structure necessary and its output necessitated; it is coherent, and it denies the contingency from which inquiry began, the alternatives that freedom needs, and the datum that decisions are ours [C461]. *Impersonal necessity with chance or plenitude* keeps the structure necessary and its output contingent, and must then say why this output: by chance, which leaves the contrast unexplained; by a selection law, which is itself either necessary, collapsing into necessitarianism, or contingent, reopening the regress; or by plenitude, which realizes every admissible output and answers “why this rather than that” with “both,” at the price of an ontology the size of the possible [C459]. The necessitation lemma is what forces the trichotomy: no necessary thing necessitates a contingent one [C458].

**The multiverse.** A cosmological multiverse, in any of its physical senses, is orthogonal to this comparison [C537]. It is internal to one created order, empirical through bridge premises, and one more contingent totality; its generating mechanism is a consequence of laws, not something prior to them. The project's motivation never rested on fine-tuning, so a multiverse removes nothing from it; and the popular dispute “multiverse or God” confuses what the created order contains with what its existence depends on. One bridge constraint is recorded: an interpretation on which every alternative is realized at the events that realize an agent's settling is incompatible with libertarian settling; branching elsewhere is not excluded [C538].

**Impersonal plenitude as a stopping point.** The strongest impersonal option is plenitude, because it is the only one that gives a contrastive answer. The project's reply is not that plenitude is incoherent; it is that plenitude buys its answer by realizing everything, and that a necessary agent buys the same contrastive explanation while realizing one order, at the price of one primitive: reason-guided settling that does not necessitate [C460]. The comparison is therefore between two primitives, a plenum and an author, and the project prefers the author for the reasons Part III gave: explanatory form, unification with the agency it needs anyway, and freedom. This is a preference, and its withdrawal conditions are on record: the incoherence of necessary agency, the demonstrated emptiness of reason-guided settling, or an impersonal model that grounds contingency and created agency at lower cost [C460, S09].

## 30. Classical Theism, Molinism, Open Theism, and Timeless Models

**Classical theism.** The earned profile is far more than a necessary cause and less than the God of classical theism, and the differences are exact. The project does not adopt absolute simplicity: its minimal constitutive simplicity, a thin substance with aspect-powers, does not entail the identities absolute simplicity asserts, and it declines to infer them [C87, C227]. It does not infer perfection from necessity or agency, and does not use perfection as a premise [R03]. It does not claim omnibenevolence [C465]. It has earned created-order maximality by construction and holds the unrestricted claim as belief with less support [C261–C262, C432–C433]. It holds omniscience in an actuality-complete form that declines counterfactuals of freedom [C11, C13]. And it earns unity, immateriality, omnipresence, and availability by routes that do not pass through the doctrines of participation, pure act, and simplicity on which the classical routes depend, recording those routes as conditional resources [C82, C279]. The relation to classical theism is therefore neither adoption nor rejection: it is a report of how far a bottom-up construction reaches, and it reaches most of the profile by different roads and stops short of the rest.

**Thomistic concurrence and deism.** Between universal immediate concurrence and deism the project takes a definite position. Ordinary later effects are proximately produced by created causes, not by the agent, although the agent could substitute for any eligible source-neutral outcome; this is grounding with sparse direct action [C380, C383]. It is not concurrentism, since the agent does not immediately co-produce every derivative token; it is not mere conservationism either, since the agent retains substitutive capacity and contributes actual interventions; and it is not deism, since the agent grounds the order, is causally available to every relatum, and may act within the history [S41]. The medieval precedents are recorded: Aquinas on secondary causes and on God's ability to produce their effects without them, and the minority position that defended genuine created causation without universal concurrence [S41].

**Molinism.** The project excludes exhaustive pre-actual counterfactuals of freedom, and so excludes the middle knowledge on which Molinism rests [C13]. The exclusion is not a limitation of omniscience but the absence of a truthmaker: where nothing grounds what a free agent would have done, there is no truth to know [C54]. Providence accordingly cannot proceed by consulting such truths; it proceeds by architecture and by response to actual conditions [C106, C549]. The corollary that two qualitatively identical agents in identical circumstances need not act alike marks the distance from the Molinist picture most sharply [C513].

**Open theism.** The open-future model is retained as a controlled comparison and not as a co-primary account [S01]. It preserves the literal, alethic reading of “not true yet” at the price of frame-relative truth; the project's eternalism accepts epistemic, causal, and modal openness and declines alethic openness [C25]. The difference is load-bearing in exactly one place, the fixity problem, which E+ must answer directly and which Part V answered by the actual-marker semantics rather than by denying future truth [C27–C30]. The conditions under which F would be reinstated are stated and have not been met [S01].

**Timeless models.** The nearest checked precedents are contemporary models that combine eternalism, divine foreknowledge, and strong causal libertarianism, and models of an eternal divine perspective with genuine creaturely input and non-absolute providence; an earlier precedent makes timeless divine knowledge depend on the act while treating the future contingent as indeterminate until it occurs [research map]. The project belongs to this family and differs from its members in the layering of its knowledge claims, actuality-complete knowledge as the minimum with modal knowledge and subject acquaintance as separable roles, in the asymmetric co-production of the history, and in the derivation of a good-oriented profile with an exact gap. Whether those differences are more than expository is a question the provenance pass must settle.

**Panentheism and strict *ex deo*.** Models on which something numerically divine constitutes created reality, whether by partition, divine mode, world-as-body, or whole presence, are live and disfavored [C251]; causal, exemplar, and participatory derivation from the agent are accepted, and strict constitutive derivation remains open for deeper comparison [C254]. Omnipresence, understood as relational immanence, neither entails nor excludes such overlap [C252]. The trunk's most recent classification places a strict non-destructive overlap among mixed constitutive tasks whose coherence depends on the identity account now promoted [C431, C434].

**Process theism and temporal models.** A temporal, changing, or passible agent is not adopted: the agent is atemporal with respect to creaturely time, and responsiveness is modeled without succession [C14, C549–C551]. What such models gain in relational vividness the project seeks instead through actual subject acquaintance and asymmetric communication, which give direct, discriminating access to every subject without making the agent a temporal participant [C284–C296].

## 31. What the Model Explains and Where It Stops

**Science's three roles.** Empirical science is an informative and defeasibly constraining route to knowledge of the actual created order, not the automatic definition of metaphysical categories [C183]. It plays three roles in the project. It is *input*: what physics says about matter shaped the account of materiality as an instantiated profile, and what neuroscience says about preparation and settlement shaped the embodied decision episode [C487, S29]. It is a *realization constraint*: the actual embodied regime of consciousness constrains what continuation could look like here without deciding what it could be elsewhere [C484]. And it is a *bridge-mediated defeater*: a scientific finding defeats an ontological claim only when an explicit bridge shows that the claim entails an empirically contradicted consequence [C184]. Each role is bounded by the same rule: science may inform metaphysical construction without becoming definitionally prior or a standing veto beyond its demonstrated domain [C185]. Current non-detection is not a metaphysical veto [R27].

**Operational success.** The motivation of Part I returns here as a result. Operational success, prediction and control, neither defines nor exhausts metaphysical reality. A description that predicts everything still leaves the question why its fundamental structure obtains, and Part III showed that this question has four answers, none of which the description supplies. This is not a complaint against science; it is the location of the boundary at which science stops describing and the present inquiry begins.

**What is explained.** The model gives contrastive, reason-guided, non-necessitating explanation of why a contingent order obtains, which impersonal stopping points cannot [C460]. It reconciles infallible atemporal knowledge with genuine alternatives without denying future truth [C27–C30]. It types divine action so that creation, sustenance, secondary causation, miracle, and providence are distinct without a temporal God [S04, C380]. It derives, from the reason for which agents were created, both a presumption against replacing their agency and the two policy-level covers under which suffering is permitted [C115, C474–C475]. It places the problem of evil at one exact point, the P-gap, and shows what would have to be true for it to close [C464, C478]. It gives materiality a positive account and derives the non-material ground of matter [C487–C490]. And it earns a divine profile that is one, necessary, underived, immaterial, present, available, knowing, powerful over its creation, and oriented toward the freedom of its creatures and truthful relation with them [C463].

**Where it stops.** The stops are as exact as the explanations, and the project counts listing them among its results. The agentic terminus is a preference, not a demonstration [C460]. Orientation toward what creatures feel is not earned, and with it omnibenevolence and the closure of the problem of evil [C464–C465]. Personal continuation follows from no established premise and is held conditionally [C485]. Unrestricted omnipotence awaits the exhaustion of self-executive types and a verdict on non-created concreta; what is supported is ontology-relative maximality over the created domain [C432–C433]. Absolute simplicity and perfection are neither adopted nor denied. The grounding of uninstantiated constitutive profiles is open [C350]. The identity of subjects across change and gaps has a candidate account and is the trunk's promoted module [C434]. Temporal passage is undecided. The exact ground of divine direct address and the veridicality conditions of experience remain at the level of typed distinctions [CH19–CH20]. None of these is hidden by a confident tone, and each is the kind of item the accessible register states as “I don't know.”

## 32. Established Elements, New Synthesis, and Remaining Work

**The honest classification.** The project's first precedent pass classified the architecture as a potentially useful synthesis of established components [research map]. That verdict stands after the later sessions. Contingent reality and a necessary ground, agent causation, eternalism with foreknowledge and libertarian freedom, the layering of omniscience, creation without material input, qualified omnipotence and its blockers, the free-will and natural-law covers, privation, fittingness, and the phenomenology of religious experience all have precedents, some ancient and some recent. The project has not found, and does not claim, a component that is new.

**What is project-specific.** What the pass records as distinctive is a set of integrations and compressions, each of which may prove expository rather than ontological: the actuality-complete formulation of omniscience with modal knowledge and subject acquaintance as separable roles; the necessary agent as contrastive explanation combined with asymmetric co-production of the history; the typed intervention taxonomy with creation as its zero case; the derivation of a good-oriented profile with an exact gap, rather than the assumption of omnibenevolence; the minimal anchor domain and a single fit relation as the axiological bridge; the permission-cover-remainder structure that makes continuation load-bearing; the modal-source bridge and its four components; the root-created basis and qualified creative plenitude; sparse direct action with source-neutral substitutability; bounded atemporal grounding as the route to annihilation; the created-action normal form; simple persistence derived from the agency model; and well-founded responsiveness. The project calls these syntheses and claims nothing more until the comparison is broader.

**Remaining work.** Three kinds of work remain, and the project distinguishes them. *Trunk items*: the identity module now promoted [C434]; the residual range questions, self-executive exhaustion and possible non-created concreta [C426, C430]; the two candidate routes to a valence-directed reason [C526]; the strict *ex deo* coherence question [C431]; the F3 burdens of consciousness [C341]. *Method items*: the dedicated literature, provenance, and novelty pass, including verification of the sessions that cited from memory; the formal appendices; the concordance that will replace the bracketed identifiers in this draft. *Expository items*: Parts I–II in the author's voice, the Spanish edition, and the continued revision of the accessible answers as the framework changes, under the rule that an answer is revised whenever a commitment it rests on changes status.

## 33. Argument and Experience

**Two registers, one ontology.** The project maintains two registers over one set of commitments. The argumentative register builds, tests, and records; the accessible register says the same things in ordinary first-person language and connects them with lived experience. The second is downstream of the first and may not claim more than the ledger supports, and it is question-driven: a plain question the ledger cannot answer becomes a research item [accessible track]. The author frames the two as communication: metaphysics, mathematics, and science communicate certain kinds of knowledge through logic; art, music, and religion communicate feelings and experiences; all of it is communication among subjects and with the agent [C547]. The framing is consistent with asymmetric communication, which requires participation by both and not parity of capacity [C295–C296], and with the complementarity of the registers [C469]. The image of an analytic left hemisphere and an experiential right one is used as a figure for the two registers and flagged as such; it is not an empirical premise [R181].

**What the argument establishes.** The ontology says that the agent is present to every created thing and personally available to every conscious subject, able to address and to be addressed [C91, C122]. It says that availability is not exercise, that reciprocal presence is co-produced, and that significant relation depends partly on the creature [C123–C124]. It says that divine address is typed as circumstantial contribution or bare regard and never as override [C466]. It does not, and cannot, have the relation it shows to be available. An argument can show that a door exists and is open; crossing it is what experience does [C469].

**Three ways an experience relates to its truthmaker.** An experience presenting the agent as present, addressing, or responding can be veridical of standing ontological presence, which holds for every actual subject whether felt or not; veridical of exercised regard, which requires an actual address or response; or non-veridical, while standing presence still holds [C467]. The partition does three things. It explains why an experience of presence in music or in silence can be of something that is always true rather than of something that happens only then. It separates the question whether the agent is present from the question whether the agent has addressed this subject. And it leaves the identification of exercised regard as hard as it is.

**Defeasible evidence without self-authentication.** Such experience is defeasible first-person evidence of presence or relation for the subject who has it; it is not self-authenticating, it can have a simpler explanation, and it is never evidence for the specific architecture [C468]. The two-sided rule is stated in the accessible register in one sentence: an experience is not nothing, and it is not less than nothing because it cannot be measured; and no experience, however intense, establishes anything the argument has to earn. Absence of experience refutes nothing, since capacity is not exercise and relation is co-produced; presence of experience entails no miracle beyond regard [C470]. The conditions under which an experience is veridical, and what defeats it, remain the register's open challenge [CH20].

**Art, music, and the anchors.** Aesthetic and religious experience engage two of the three anchors of value, conscious valence and shared attention on something not one's own, and are a non-argumentative mode of apprehension [C471]. In a world whose order and meaning are grounded in the agent, attending to beauty or meaning is attending to something whose ground the agent is. Whether that is already mediated relation to the agent, or relation to created structure grounded in the agent, is open, and the project leaves it open rather than deciding it for the sake of a tidier picture. What it does say is that the experiential register is a real way of attending to what the argument can only point at, and that the two registers, in the author's framing, are two languages for one relation: one says what can be known, the other what is felt and lived.
